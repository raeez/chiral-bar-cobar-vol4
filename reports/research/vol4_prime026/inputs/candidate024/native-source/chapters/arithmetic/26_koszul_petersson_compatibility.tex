\chapter{Koszul--Petersson Pairing Compatibility}
\label{v4-arith:kp-compat}

\emph{The P3 closure of Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:closure} left one
residual identity: that the arithmetic Koszul pairing of
\(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}\) with \((\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}})^{!}\)
coincides, after Bost--Connes involution on the dual, with the
adelic Petersson--Tamagawa inner product on \(L^{2}([GL_{2}])\).
Here \(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}\) is the Hochschild trace class
of the identity on the global arithmetic Iwahori--Hecke category of \(GL_{2}\);
it is not a vertex algebra. Heisenberg--Fock and CCR language in this
argument is confined to the archimedean fibre \(\mathcal{H}^{(\infty)}\);
the finite-place factors \(\mathcal{H}^{(p)}\) are
\(C_{c}(GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\backslash GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})/GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}))\),
carrying no Heisenberg Lie algebra structure.
The compatibility requires actual local pairing maps before
adelic assembly. The operator model of
\Cref{v4-arith:thermal:chapter} fixes the local normalization;
\Cref{v4-arith:kp:hyp:native-pairing-maps} states the additional
native comparison data, including the sph-fin cuspidal core.}

\section{Statement Recalled}
\label{v4-arith:kp:statement}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{v4-arith:kp:setup}

The primary arithmetic Heisenberg, \(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}_{\mathrm{Heis}}\),
denotes the local-factor decomposition of \(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}\)
with Heisenberg structure on the archimedean fibre only (the subscript
records the archimedean Fock presentation; finite factors carry no
Heisenberg Lie algebra). It decomposes as a restricted adelic tensor product
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:kp:eq:Vprim-adelic}
\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}_{\mathrm{Heis}}\;=\;
\mathcal{H}^{(\infty)}\;\otimes\;{\bigotimes_{p}}^{\!\prime}\,\mathcal{H}^{(p)},
\end{equation}
with archimedean Heisenberg Fock
\(\mathcal{H}^{(\infty)}=\mathbb{C}[\alpha_{-1},\alpha_{-2},\ldots]\cdot|0\rangle_{\infty}\)
(Frenkel--Ben-Zvi \S3.5) and finite-place factors
\(\mathcal{H}^{(p)}=C_{c}(GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\backslash GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})/GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}))\)
with Bost--Connes Hecke endomorphism
\(\psi_{p}\) (\Cref{v4-arith:def:E0-local-factor}).
The restricted tensor product is taken with respect to the spherical vectors
\(|0\rangle_{p}\in\mathcal{H}^{(p)}\) at almost every prime \(p\)
(Tate 1950, reprinted Cassels--Fr\"ohlich 1967 \S13.4;
Weil 1967 \emph{Basic Number Theory} Ch.~VII).

The proposed Koszul-dual algebra comparison with the
Bost--Connes Q-lattice system is
\begin{equation}
\bigl(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}_{\mathrm{Heis}}\bigr)^{!}
\;=\;\mathbb{A}^{\mathrm{BC}}
\end{equation}
(\Cref{v4-arith:cor:koszul-dual-BC}; Bost--Connes Selecta
Math.~1, 1995). Let \(\tau^{\mathrm{BC}}\) be the algebraic
Bost--Connes anti-involution,
\[
\tau^{\mathrm{BC}}(\mu_{n})=\mu_{n}^{*},\qquad
\tau^{\mathrm{BC}}(e(\gamma))=e(-\gamma),
\]
the \(*\)-operation of the Bost--Connes \(C^{*}\)-algebra
(Bost--Connes~\S1.2). On the GNS Hilbert space of the
specified represented prime KMS\({}_{1}\) state, the closed
GNS adjoint map has antiunitary polar factor
\[
J_{\varphi_{1},p}\pi(\mu_m)\Omega_{\varphi_{1}}
=m^{1/2}\pi(\mu_m^{*})\Omega_{\varphi_{1}},\qquad
J_{\varphi_{1},p}\pi(e(\gamma))\Omega_{\varphi_{1}}
=\pi(e(-\gamma))\Omega_{\varphi_{1}} .
\]
On the represented GNS carrier, \(\sigma^{\mathrm{BC}}_p\)
denotes this conjugation. In native pairing formulas it denotes
the map \(c_p\) supplied by
\Cref{v4-arith:kp:hyp:native-pairing-maps}. The algebraic
star operation \(\tau^{\mathrm{BC}}_p\) remains a separate map.

\subsection{The Two Pairings}
\label{v4-arith:kp:two-pairings}

The adelic Koszul pairing of Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:arakelov-bar}
is the bilinear form
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:kp:eq:Koszul-pairing-def}
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle^{\mathrm{Koszul}}\colon
\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}_{\mathrm{Heis}}\otimes\mathbb{A}^{\mathrm{BC}}\;\longrightarrow\;\mathbb{C}
\end{equation}
induced by the bar--cobar duality
\(\Omega^{\mathrm{Ar}}\dashv B^{\mathrm{Ar}}\)
(\Cref{v4-arith:def:arith-bar-concrete}) via evaluation of a
bar--cochain against a cobar--cochain. Its restriction to the
diagonal \(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}\otimes\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}\)
passes through the Koszul self-duality of
Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:closure}~\S\ref{v4-arith:cl:P1-resolution}.

The adelic Petersson--Tamagawa inner product is the sesquilinear
form
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:kp:eq:Pet-pairing-def}
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle^{\mathrm{Pet}}
\colon\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}\otimes\overline{\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}}\;\longrightarrow\;\mathbb{C},
\quad
\langle f,h\rangle^{\mathrm{Pet}}
\;=\;\int_{[GL_{2}]}f(\gamma)\overline{h(\gamma)}\,d\gamma,
\end{equation}
with \(dg\) the Tamagawa measure on
\(Z(\mathbb{A})\,GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash GL_{2}(\mathbb{A})/K_{\infty}\),
applied to the realisation of \(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}\) as
a subspace of \(L^{2}([GL_{2}])\) via the adelic Whittaker model
(Jacquet--Langlands SLN~114, 1970, \S1; Bump 1997 \S3).

\begin{hypothesis}[native pairing realization]
\label{v4-arith:kp:hyp:native-pairing-maps}
On each chosen finite-place arithmetic core, the native factor
$V_p$, its dual factor $V_p^!$, the bilinear Koszul form $B_p$,
and the Petersson Hermitian form $h_p$ are defined. There are
complex-linear maps $f_p:V_p\to\mathscr G_p$ and
$g_p:V_p^!\to\mathscr G_p$, and a conjugate-linear map
$c_p:V_p\to V_p^!$, satisfying
\[
 B_p(v,a)=B_q(f_pv,g_pa),\qquad
 h_p(v,w)=h_q(f_pv,f_pw),\qquad
 g_pc_p=J_qf_p,
 \qquad q=p^{-1}.
\]
Here $B_q,h_q,J_q$ are the explicitly constructed forms and
conjugation of \Cref{v4-arith:thermal:pairing-section}; $h_p$
and $h_q$ are linear in their first variables. The maps preserve
any specified Hecke operations and energy grading on their
domains. An adelic application additionally requires compatible
maps on finite prime sets, with the actual phase operations,
and the stated Tamagawa-regularized tensor pairing.
\end{hypothesis}

The hypothesis concerns maps on native carriers, not an equality
of their characters. For the abstract weighted prime lattice,
\Cref{v4-arith:thermal:gns-section} constructs isometric maps
using division vectors or normalized creation vectors and shows
that their energy behavior differs. It supplies a model for
testing the displayed equations. It does not supply $f_p,g_p,c_p$
for an arithmetic convolution category or a Whittaker model.

\subsection{The Identity}
\label{v4-arith:kp:identity}

\begin{theorem}[Koszul--Petersson compatibility, domain-restricted]
\label{v4-arith:kp:thm:main}
\ClaimStatusProvedHereConditional. Assume \Cref{v4-arith:kp:hyp:native-pairing-maps}, including the stated adelic pairing assembly.  \emph{(domain-restricted to the normalized
sph-fin cuspidal core; see
\Cref{v4-arith:kp:rem:domain-restriction})}. For all
\(v,w\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}_{\mathrm{sph\text{-}fin,cusp}}\),
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:kp:eq:main}
\langle v,w\rangle^{\mathrm{Pet}}
\;=\;
\langle v,\sigma^{\mathrm{BC}}(w)\rangle^{\mathrm{Koszul}}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the adelic Petersson pairing equals the Koszul
pairing composed with \(\mathrm{id}\otimes\sigma^{\mathrm{BC}}\)
under the identification
\((\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}})^{!}=\mathbb{A}^{\mathrm{BC}}\), on the
normalized sph-fin cuspidal core.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[domain restriction and open sub-items]
\label{v4-arith:kp:rem:domain-restriction}
Under the native map hypothesis, \Cref{v4-arith:kp:thm:main} gives the identity
(\ref{v4-arith:kp:eq:main}) only on the normalized sph-fin
cuspidal core
\(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}_{\mathrm{sph\text{-}fin,cusp}}\subset\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}\).
The following requirements distinguish the local operator calculation
from the native pairing problem:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{KMS\(_{1}\)/GNS identification.} The identification of the
local Petersson pairing with the Bost--Connes GNS pairing
(\Cref{v4-arith:kp:prop:Pet-p-is-BC-sigma}) uses the
half-density normalised KMS\(_{1}\) Tomita--Takesaki conjugation at
each prime. The operator normalization is explicit, but the native
pairing maps of \Cref{v4-arith:kp:hyp:native-pairing-maps}
are required even on the specified sph-fin core.
\item \emph{Full-sector Rankin--Selberg extension.} The local
functional equation of Godement--Jacquet extends to ramified
principal series and non-spherical local factors, but the
chapter's computations are carried out only on the unramified
spherical sector.
\item \emph{Eisenstein summands.} Explicitly excluded by the sph-fin
cut of \Cref{v4-arith:sw:thm:KP-split}(K7); the identity is not
claimed on the Eisenstein continuous spectrum.
\end{itemize}
Under its stated map hypotheses, \Cref{v4-arith:kp:thm:main} proves the domain-restricted identity
\Cref{v4-arith:cl:cj:koszul-Petersson-compat} of
Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:closure} on the normalized sph-fin cuspidal
core.
\end{remark}

\section{Variance and domains of the comparison}
\label{v4-arith:kp:obstruction-analysis-table}
The native Koszul form is complex bilinear on two specified carriers.
The Petersson form is linear in its first argument and conjugate-linear
in its second. A conjugate-linear map from the native space to its dual
is therefore required before their values can agree. On the operator
carrier the identity is \(B_q(X,J_qY)=h_q(X,Y)\).

The carrier is equally essential. A positive state gives a Hilbert
space after taking its null quotient and completion. It does not
identify that Hilbert space with a native automorphic representation.
The radial shell construction below gives explicit maps on a small
sector; extension to an arithmetic representation must preserve its
operators, phases and norms. At the adelic level the finite-prime maps
must also agree under inclusion. Equality of partition functions or
local scalar factors does not impose this compatibility.

\section{Archimedean Identification}
\label{v4-arith:kp:arch}

\subsection{The Fock Inner Product}
\label{v4-arith:kp:arch:Fock}

The archimedean factor
\(\mathcal{H}^{(\infty)}=\mathbb{C}[\alpha_{-1},\alpha_{-2},\ldots]\cdot|0\rangle_{\infty}\)
is the Heisenberg vacuum Fock module. Its canonical Hermitian
form is the \emph{Segal inner product}: the unique
positive-definite sesquilinear form \(\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{\mathrm{Segal}}\)
on \(\mathcal{H}^{(\infty)}\) satisfying
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:kp:eq:Segal-normalisation}
\alpha_{-n}^{\dagger}\;=\;\alpha_{n},
\qquad
\langle |0\rangle_{\infty},|0\rangle_{\infty}\rangle_{\mathrm{Segal}}\;=\;1,
\qquad
\alpha_{n}|0\rangle_{\infty}\;=\;0\;\;\text{for}\;n\geq 1,
\end{equation}
together with the Heisenberg CCR
\([\alpha_{m},\alpha_{n}]=m\delta_{m+n,0}\) (Segal 1981
Commun.~Math.~Phys.~80; Frenkel--Ben-Zvi 2004 \S3.5; Kac 1998
\emph{Vertex Algebras for Beginners} \S2.5 Prop.~2.2).

\begin{proposition}[Segal form on monomials]
\label{v4-arith:kp:prop:Segal-monomials}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere. For monomials
\(v_{1}=\alpha_{-n_{1}}\cdots\alpha_{-n_{k}}|0\rangle_{\infty}\),
\(v_{2}=\alpha_{-m_{1}}\cdots\alpha_{-m_{l}}|0\rangle_{\infty}\)
with \(n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{k}\geq 1\),
\(m_{1}\geq\cdots\geq m_{l}\geq 1\),
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:kp:eq:Segal-monomials}
\langle v_{1},v_{2}\rangle_{\mathrm{Segal}}
\;=\;
\delta_{k,l}\cdot\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}\,
\prod_{i=1}^{k}n_{i}\,\delta_{n_{i},m_{\sigma(i)}}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, Segal's form is the \(L^{2}\)-inner product of the
Wick-normal-ordered fields.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Wick's theorem applied to a product of creation/annihilation
operators equates
\(\langle v_{1},v_{2}\rangle_{\mathrm{Segal}}
=\langle|0\rangle_{\infty},\alpha_{n_{1}}\cdots\alpha_{n_{k}}
\alpha_{-m_{1}}\cdots\alpha_{-m_{l}}|0\rangle_{\infty}\rangle\).
Normal-ordering and using the CCR to collect the contractions
gives exactly
(\ref{v4-arith:kp:eq:Segal-monomials}). See Kac 1998 \S2.5
Prop.~2.2 or Frenkel--Ben-Zvi 2004 \S3.5.4.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A radial shell realization of the pairing maps}
\label{v4-arith:kp:radial-shell-model}

The weighted lattice admits a concrete function model. This
specifies what a local pairing comparison can prove before a
Whittaker representation is identified. Give $\mathbb Z_p^\times$
its probability Haar measure $du$, and define multiplicative
measure on $\mathbb Q_p^\times$ by
\[
 \int F(a)\,d^\times a
   =\sum_{r\in\mathbb Z}\int_{\mathbb Z_p^\times}F(p^ru)\,du
\]
for compactly supported locally constant functions. Thus each
valuation shell has multiplicative mass one. Put
$d\nu(a)=|a|_p^{-1}d^\times a$; the shell
$p^r\mathbb Z_p^\times$ has $\nu$-mass $p^r$.
For $r\geq0$ define
\[
 f_r(a)=p^{-r}\mathbf1_{p^r\mathbb Z_p^\times}(a).
\]
Their finite complex span $V_p^{\mathrm{rad}}$ has Hermitian
and bilinear pairings
\[
 h_{\mathrm{rad}}(f,g)=\int f\overline g\,d\nu,
 \qquad B_{\mathrm{rad}}(f,g)=\int fg\,d\nu.
\]
Both integrals are finite on this domain. The shells are disjoint,
so each pairing on its real basis is
$\delta_{rs}p^{-r}$. Let $(V_p^{\mathrm{rad}})^!$ be a separate
copy of this vector space, with basis $a_r$, and set
$B_{\mathrm{rad}}(f_r,a_s)=\delta_{rs}p^{-r}$.
The map $c(\sum z_rf_r)=\sum\overline z_ra_r$ is conjugate-linear.
Give $f_r$ occupation degree $r$ and its dual $a_r$ degree $-r$.

\begin{theorem}[radial shell pairing realization]
\label{v4-arith:kp:radial-pairing-realization}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere. At $q=p^{-1}$ the two maps
\[
 f_p(f_r)=q^{r/2}[S_p^r],\qquad
 g_p(a_r)=[R_p^r]
\]
realize the radial pairings in the Hilbert--Schmidt GNS carrier:
\[
 B_{\mathrm{rad}}(f,a)=B_q(f_pf,g_pa),\qquad
 h_{\mathrm{rad}}(f,g)=h_q(f_pf,f_pg),\qquad
 g_pc=J_qf_p.
\]
They preserve the respective positive and negative occupation
degrees and their energies $\pm r\log p$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Hermitian identity follows from the orthonormality of
$[S_p^r]$. For the bilinear form, direct matrix multiplication gives
\[
 B_q([S_p^r],[R_p^s])
   =\delta_{rs}\sum_{n\geq r}\sqrt{d_{n-r}d_n}
   =\delta_{rs}q^{r/2}.
\]
Indeed the matrix product has nonzero degree when $r\ne s$;
when $r=s$, its geometric sum is
$(1-q)\sum_{n\geq r}q^{n-r/2}=q^{r/2}$.
Multiplying by the coefficient $q^{r/2}$ in $f_p$ gives
$\delta_{rs}q^r$, exactly the radial integral. Finally
$J_q(q^{r/2}[S_p^r])=[R_p^r]$ by the modular half-density
formula. Conjugate linearity extends the identity to all vectors.
The degree statements follow from the shift and division degrees.
\end{proof}

On the Hilbert completion of the radial space, the operator
\[
 (T_pf)(a)=p^{-1/2}f(a/p)
\]
is a unilateral isometry when functions are extended by zero to
negative valuation. It sends $f_r$ to $p^{1/2}f_{r+1}$, and
$f_pT_p=S_pf_p$ on the creation image. Its Hilbert adjoint kills
$f_0$ and sends $f_r$ to $p^{-1/2}f_{r-1}$ for $r\geq1$.
The radial model therefore realizes the prime shift and its
adjoint on an occupation sector.

The restriction matters. Left $R_p$ in the full GNS
representation does not preserve that creation sector:
$R_p\Omega_q$ is nonzero and orthogonal to every creation vector.
The sector's backward shift is the compression of left $R_p$,
not a restriction of the full observable action. Likewise
$J_qf_p(V_p^{\mathrm{rad}})=g_p((V_p^{\mathrm{rad}})^!)$ lies
in the division sector. Using the creation map for both dual
factors would fail: $B_q([S_p^r],[S_p^r])=0$ for $r>0$,
whereas $B_{\mathrm{rad}}(f_r,a_r)=p^{-r}$.

These are actual maps of the displayed radial function spaces.
A given Whittaker or Kirillov representation can have different
support, its own Hecke action and its own local normalization.
To apply the construction to the native factors, one must map
those factors and their duals to these radial domains, or
construct different GNS maps, and verify the equations in
\Cref{v4-arith:kp:hyp:native-pairing-maps}. Equality of one
local volume or adjoint $L$-factor does not extend a map from
this shell subspace to all representation vectors.

\subsection{Bilinear evaluation and Fock conjugation}
\label{v4-arith:kp:arch-koszul}
\label{v4-arith:kp:arch:Koszul-infty}
\label{v4-arith:kp:arch-koszul:pairing}

To keep the variance explicit, let \(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{alg}}=
\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,\ldots]\), with each polynomial involving only
finitely many variables. For a finite multi-index \(\mathbf m\), put
\(x^{\mathbf m}=\prod_nx_n^{m_n}\) and
\(d_{\mathbf m}=\prod_n n^{m_n}m_n!\). Define
\[
 B_{\mathcal F}(x^{\mathbf m},x^{\mathbf l})
       =\delta_{\mathbf m,\mathbf l}d_{\mathbf m},\qquad
 h_{\mathcal F}(x^{\mathbf m},x^{\mathbf l})
       =\delta_{\mathbf m,\mathbf l}d_{\mathbf m},
\]
extending the first form bilinearly and the second sesquilinearly,
linear in its first slot. Define \(c_{\mathcal F}\) by conjugating
polynomial coefficients. Then
\(B_{\mathcal F}(v,c_{\mathcal F}w)=h_{\mathcal F}(v,w)\).
Multiplication by \(x_n\) is adjoint to \(n\partial_{x_n}\): on
monomials the required identity is
\(d_{\mathbf m+\mathbf e_n}=n(m_n+1)d_{\mathbf m}\).
Thus these formulas construct the positive Fock form and its algebraic
adjoint relations. The identity map cannot replace
\(c_{\mathcal F}\), since it would equate a bilinear form with a
nonzero Hermitian form.

For a native archimedean comparison let \(V_\infty,V_\infty^!\) be the
original carriers, with bilinear pairing \(B_\infty\) and Hermitian
form \(h_\infty\). The required linear maps are
\(f_\infty:V_\infty\to\mathcal F_{\mathrm{alg}}\) and
\(g_\infty:V_\infty^!\to\mathcal F_{\mathrm{alg}}\), together with
conjugate-linear \(c_\infty:V_\infty\to V_\infty^!\), satisfying
\[
 B_\infty=B_{\mathcal F}(f_\infty\,\cdot,g_\infty\,\cdot),\quad
 h_\infty=h_{\mathcal F}(f_\infty\,\cdot,f_\infty\,\cdot),\quad
 g_\infty c_\infty=c_{\mathcal F}f_\infty.
\]
These are the archimedean part of the native comparison hypothesis.
They must also preserve the specified native operators and domains.

\begin{proposition}[conditional archimedean bilinear realization]
\label{v4-arith:kp:prop:arch-koszul-is-Segal}
Under these maps,
\begin{equation}
 B_\infty(v,a)=h_{\mathcal F}(f_\infty v,c_{\mathcal F}g_\infty a).
 \label{v4-arith:kp:eq:Koszul-infty}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Coefficient conjugation is involutive, so the defining identity for
\(B_{\mathcal F}\) gives the formula. Its right side is bilinear,
because the second slot of \(h_{\mathcal F}\) and \(c_{\mathcal F}\)
are both conjugate-linear.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[archimedean pairing transfer]
\label{v4-arith:kp:cor:arch-match}
\label{v4-arith:kp:cor:arch-involution}
Under the same maps,
\begin{equation}
 B_\infty(v,c_\infty w)=h_\infty(v,w).
 \label{v4-arith:kp:eq:arch-match}
\end{equation}
The archimedean factor of the native duality is \(c_\infty\), not the
linear identity map.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Substitute \(g_\infty c_\infty=c_{\mathcal F}f_\infty\) into the
preceding formula and use \(c_{\mathcal F}^2=1\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{A Gaussian line and the native integral}
\label{v4-arith:kp:arch-Pet}
\label{v4-arith:kp:arch:Pet-infty}

\begin{lemma}[normalized Gaussian line]
\label{v4-arith:kp:lem:arch-whittaker-is-fock}
\label{v4-arith:kp:lem:Whittaker-is-Fock}
For \(g_0(x)=2^{1/4}e^{-\pi x^2}\), the map
\(\mathbb C1\to L^2(\mathbb R,dx)\), \(a\mapsto ag_0\), is isometric
from the Fock vacuum line and intertwines coefficient conjugation
with pointwise conjugation. In particular,
\begin{equation}
 \int_{\mathbb R}(ag_0)\overline{(bg_0)}\,dx=a\overline b.
 \label{v4-arith:kp:eq:Pet-arch-Whittaker}
\end{equation}
This does not identify a Whittaker model
with the full Fock representation.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The Gaussian integral is \(\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-2\pi x^2}dx=2^{-1/2}\),
so \(\|g_0\|_2=1\). Hence the pulled-back Hermitian form is
\(a\overline b\), and real-valuedness of \(g_0\) proves the
conjugation assertion. The Gaussian integral follows by squaring the
integral of \(e^{-\pi x^2}\), changing to polar coordinates in
\(\mathbb R^2\), and scaling by \(\sqrt2\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[conditional native integral comparison]
\label{v4-arith:kp:prop:Pet-is-Segal-infty}
If a native archimedean integral realizes \(h_\infty\) and its map
\(f_\infty\) satisfies the displayed Fock comparison, the pulled-back
integral has the value
\begin{equation}
 h_\infty(v,w)=B_\infty(v,c_\infty w).
 \label{v4-arith:kp:eq:Pet-infty}
\end{equation}
It is positive definite
when that integral realization is injective.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The assumed integral map identifies its squared norm with
\(h_\infty(v,v)\). The archimedean transfer gives equality of pairings,
and injectivity makes a zero norm imply \(v=0\). A Whittaker integral
and its spherical vector need their own measure and map; the Gaussian
line calculation supplies neither for an unspecified representation.
\end{proof}


\section{Finite-Place Identification}
\label{v4-arith:kp:finite}

\subsection{Bost--Connes Hilbert Pairing}
\label{v4-arith:kp:finite:BC-Hilbert}

Let $L_p$ be the Hilbert completion of the vector space on
symbols $\delta_{p^r}$, $r\geq0$, with Hermitian form linear in
its first variable
\begin{equation}
 \langle\delta_m,\delta_n\rangle_p^{\mathrm{BC}}
       =\delta_{mn}m^{-1}.
 \label{v4-arith:kp:eq:BC-Hilbert}
\end{equation}
This weighted lattice is an explicit model. A map from the native
finite-place factor to it is part of
\Cref{v4-arith:kp:hyp:native-pairing-maps}; no Plancherel
identification is supplied by the definition of this norm.

\begin{lemma}[two normalized GNS embeddings of the weighted lattice]
\label{v4-arith:kp:lem:GNS}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere. At $q=p^{-1}$, both maps
\[
 \delta_m\longmapsto[\mu_m^*],\qquad
 \delta_m\longmapsto m^{-1/2}[\mu_m]
\]
extend isometrically into the represented prime GNS space. The
first reverses occupation energy; the second preserves it.
Neither identifies the full GNS space with $L_p$ or constructs
a native Whittaker/Plancherel map.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The creation vectors have inner products $\delta_{mn}$ and the
division vectors have inner products $\delta_{mn}m^{-1}$ by
\Cref{v4-arith:thermal:gns-carrier}. Scaling the creation
vectors gives the same weighted norm. Their degrees are opposite,
as follows from the actions of $S_p$ and $R_p$. The full GNS
space is the Hilbert--Schmidt space with basis $E_{ij}$; the two
maps specify only closed subspaces of it. Also the normalized
GNS vacuum has norm one, which does not determine a chosen
Haar volume such as $\operatorname{vol}(GL_2(\mathbb Z_p))$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Koszul Pairing at \texorpdfstring{\(p\)}{p}}
\label{v4-arith:kp:finite:Koszul-p}

At prime \(p\), the local Koszul pairing is the bar--cobar
pairing
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:kp:eq:Koszul-p}
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle^{\mathrm{Koszul}}_{p}
\colon\mathcal{H}^{(p)}\otimes\mathbb{A}^{\mathrm{BC}}_{p}\to\mathbb{C}
\end{equation}
induced by the local bar--cobar duality between the \(p\)-local
Fock module and its Koszul-dual algebra, the \(p\)-local
Bost--Connes factor \(\mathbb{A}^{\mathrm{BC}}_{p}\cong C^{*}_{\mathbb{Q},p}\)
(\Cref{v4-arith:cor:koszul-dual-BC}).

\begin{proposition}[local bilinear comparison under a realization]
\label{v4-arith:kp:prop:Koszul-p-is-BC}
\ClaimStatusProvedHereConditional. Under
\Cref{v4-arith:kp:hyp:native-pairing-maps}, the native bilinear
Koszul form has the representation
\begin{equation}
 B_p(v,a)=h_q(f_pv,J_qg_pa).
 \label{v4-arith:kp:eq:Koszul-p-is-BC}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The realization gives $B_p(v,a)=B_q(f_pv,g_pa)$.
Since $J_q^2=1$, \Cref{v4-arith:thermal:bilinear-pairing}
gives $B_q(X,Y)=h_q(X,J_qY)$, proving the formula. This
inserts conjugation into a Hermitian form to recover a bilinear
form; it does not identify a vector with an algebra element.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Bost--Connes vs.~Petersson at \texorpdfstring{\(p\)}{p}}
\label{v4-arith:kp:finite:BC-vs-Pet}

The Petersson inner product at \(p\) on the Whittaker model of
the unramified principal series of \(GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\) is
the local Rankin--Selberg pairing
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:kp:eq:Pet-p-RS}
\langle\phi,\phi'\rangle^{\mathrm{Pet}}_{p}
\;=\;\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}}\,W_{\phi,p}(a)\,
\overline{W_{\phi',p}(a)}\,
\frac{d^{\times}a}{|a|_{p}}
\end{equation}
(Godement--Jacquet SLN~260, 1972, \S2; Jacquet--Langlands
SLN~114~\S3).

\begin{lemma}[three spherical normalizations]
\label{v4-arith:kp:lem:Pet-p-is-Plancherel}
Let \(K=GL_2(\mathbb Z_p)\) have Haar mass \(V_p>0\). Convolution
uses this same Haar measure. Then
\[
 \mathbf1_K*\mathbf1_K=V_p\mathbf1_K,\qquad
 e_K=V_p^{-1}\mathbf1_K,\qquad v_K=V_p^{-1/2}\mathbf1_K.
\]
Here \(e_K\) is the unit of the bi-\(K\)-invariant convolution corner,
whereas \(v_K\) is a unit Hilbert vector. Their squared \(L^2\) norms
are \(V_p^{-1}\) and one respectively; \(\|\mathbf1_K\|_2^2=V_p\).
On the line \(\mathbb C e_K\), the normalized functional
\(\tau_K(f)=V_pf(1)\) has \(\tau_K(e_K)=1\). Its cyclic unit vector
can be mapped isometrically to the normalized Gibbs vacuum \(\Omega\).
None of these line comparisons identifies an entire native Whittaker
pairing.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For \(g\in K\), the convolution integral of the two indicators is
\(\int_K1\,dk=V_p\); outside \(K\) it is zero. Integration against a
bi-\(K\)-invariant function shows \(e_K*f=f*e_K=f\). The norm
formulas follow by integrating the squared constants on \(K\).
Writing \(f=ae_K\) gives \(\tau_K(f)=a\) and
\(\tau_K(f^**f)=|a|^2\); thus its cyclic norm agrees with that of
\(a\Omega\). A larger native comparison requires its specified maps.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Hermitian comparison and its variance]
\label{v4-arith:kp:prop:Pet-p-is-BC-sigma}
\ClaimStatusProvedHereConditional. Under
\Cref{v4-arith:kp:hyp:native-pairing-maps},
\begin{equation}
 h_p(v,w)=h_q(f_pv,f_pw).
 \label{v4-arith:kp:eq:Pet-p-match}
\end{equation}
The second slot of this Hermitian comparison has no modular
conjugation. Inserting $J_q$ there would make the expression
bilinear and could not equal a nonzero Hermitian form for all
complex vectors.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The displayed equality is the metric compatibility required of
$f_p$. For the obstruction, replacing $w$ by $iw$ multiplies
the Hermitian left side by $-i$, whereas
$h_q(f_pv,J_qf_pw)$ is multiplied by $i$. On a nonzero positive
subspace these cannot agree. The bilinear Koszul form instead
uses the conjugate-linear native map $c_p$, whose realization
is $g_pc_p=J_qf_p$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[local pairing transfer]
\label{v4-arith:kp:cor:local-compatibility}
\ClaimStatusProvedHereConditional. Under
\Cref{v4-arith:kp:hyp:native-pairing-maps}, the finite-place
native pairings satisfy
\begin{equation}
 h_p(v,w)=B_p(v,c_pw).
 \label{v4-arith:kp:eq:local-compatibility}
\end{equation}
An archimedean application requires its analogous pairing maps.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The typed calculation is
$B_p(v,c_pw)=B_q(f_pv,g_pc_pw)=B_q(f_pv,J_qf_pw)
=h_q(f_pv,f_pw)=h_p(v,w)$, as proved in
\Cref{v4-arith:thermal:pairing-transfer}. Each equality uses one
of the specified maps. No scalar volume comparison constructs
them.
\end{proof}

\section{Adelic Assembly}
\label{v4-arith:kp:adelic}

\subsection{Tate's Restricted Tensor Product}
\label{v4-arith:kp:adelic:restricted-tensor}

\begin{definition}[adelic restricted tensor product of pairings]
\label{v4-arith:kp:def:adelic-pairing}
Let \(\{\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{v}\}_{v\in|\overline{C}|}\)
be a family of sesquilinear pairings on local factors
\(\{\mathcal{H}^{(v)}\}\), with specified reference vectors
\(|0\rangle_{v}\in\mathcal{H}^{(v)}\) at almost all finite places
such that \(\langle|0\rangle_{v},|0\rangle_{v}\rangle_{v}=\mathrm{vol}(K_{v})\)
(local compact-open subgroup volume with respect to the
Weil-normalised Haar measure). Given a specified common regularization, the adelic tensor
pairing is
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:kp:eq:adelic-restricted-tensor}
\Bigl\langle\,\bigotimes_{v}'v_{v}\,,\,\bigotimes_{v}'w_{v}\,\Bigr\rangle_{\mathrm{adelic}}
\;=\;
\mathop{\prod_{v\in|\overline{C}|}}^{\mathrm{reg}}\,\langle v_{v},w_{v}\rangle_{v},
\end{equation}
where \(v_{v}=|0\rangle_{v}\) and \(w_{v}=|0\rangle_{v}\) outside a
finite set \(S\) depending on the vectors; the product over \(v\notin S\)
reduces to the constant
\(\prod_{v\notin S}\mathrm{vol}(K_{v})=\mathrm{vol}\bigl(\prod_{v\notin S}K_{v}\bigr)\)
only after the Tamagawa residue normalisation has been fixed.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Tamagawa regularisation of the adelic product]
\label{v4-arith:kp:lem:adelic-convergence}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere. The ordinary product of the spherical
finite-place volumes for \(GL_{n}\) is not the Tamagawa volume before
regularisation. For \(GL_{n}\) over \(\mathbb{Q}\),
\(\mathrm{vol}(GL_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}))=\prod_{k=1}^{n}(1-p^{-k})\),
so the \(k=1\) factor is the divergent Euler factor
\(\zeta(1)^{-1}\). The factors with \(k\geq2\) converge to
\(\prod_{k=2}^{n}\zeta(k)^{-1}\). The finite non-zero value
\(\tau(GL_{2})=1\) is obtained only after the completed-zeta residue,
the archimedean Haar factor, and the quotient by the rational
character have been inserted.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Weil 1967 \emph{Basic Number Theory} Ch.~VII \S3 computes
\(\mathrm{vol}([GL_{n}])\) for the Tamagawa normalisation. For
\(GL_{2}\), the Tamagawa number is \(\tau(GL_{2})=1\) by Weil's theorem
(Platonov--Rapinchuk 1994 Ch.~3 \S1.3, Theorem~3.4 for split groups
over \(\mathbb{Q}\)); the computation uses the completed zeta function
and the different in an essential way, not merely a residue.
The full Tamagawa formalism is
classical: see Weil 1967 and the full modern account in
Platonov--Rapinchuk \emph{Algebraic Groups and Number Theory}
(1994) Ch.~3.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Assembly of Petersson and Koszul}
\label{v4-arith:kp:adelic:assembly}

\begin{theorem}[conditional assembly of local pairings]
\label{v4-arith:kp:thm:tate-assembly}
\ClaimStatusProvedHereConditional.
Assume the native local maps and the adelic assembly of
\Cref{v4-arith:kp:hyp:native-pairing-maps}. In particular, assume both
native global forms are the specified common regularized products of
their local forms. Then on the specified elementary tensors
\begin{align}
 h(v,w)&=\mathop{\prod_v}^{\mathrm{reg}}h_v(v_v,w_v),
 \label{v4-arith:kp:eq:Pet-restricted-tensor}\\
 B(v,cw)&=\mathop{\prod_v}^{\mathrm{reg}}B_v(v_v,c_vw_v).
 \label{v4-arith:kp:eq:Koszul-restricted-tensor}
\end{align}
Their values agree wherever the same product functional is defined.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The local transfer theorem gives identical local values. For each
finite prime set the products are therefore identical. Applying the
assumed common limiting or regularization operation preserves equality.
For unit-normalized tensor reference vectors, finite excitations need
no infinite scalar product. Unnormalized Haar volumes require their
own specified regularization and cannot replace the unit values of a
normalized state.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[global compatibility via local compatibility]
\label{v4-arith:kp:cor:global-from-local}
\ClaimStatusProvedHereConditional \emph{(with the same Tamagawa
regularisation as \Cref{v4-arith:kp:thm:tate-assembly})}. The global identity
(\ref{v4-arith:kp:eq:main}) follows from the local identities
(\ref{v4-arith:kp:eq:local-compatibility}) by taking the
restricted tensor product. Explicitly,
\begin{equation*}
\langle v,w\rangle^{\mathrm{Pet}}
=\prod_{v}\langle v_{v},w_{v}\rangle^{\mathrm{Pet}}_{v}
=\prod_{v}\langle v_{v},\sigma^{\mathrm{BC}}_{v}(w_{v})\rangle^{\mathrm{Koszul}}_{v}
=\langle v,\sigma^{\mathrm{BC}}(w)\rangle^{\mathrm{Koszul}},
\end{equation*}
using (\ref{v4-arith:kp:eq:Pet-restricted-tensor}),
(\ref{v4-arith:kp:eq:local-compatibility}), and
(\ref{v4-arith:kp:eq:Koszul-restricted-tensor}), with
\(\sigma^{\mathrm{BC}}=\bigotimes_{v}\sigma^{\mathrm{BC}}_{v}\)
the adelic restricted tensor product involution.
\end{corollary}

\section{The Bost--Connes Anti-Involution and its GNS Lift}
\label{v4-arith:kp:BC-involution}

\subsection{Definition and Basic Properties}
\label{v4-arith:kp:BC-involution:def}

The Bost--Connes algebraic anti-involution
\(\tau^{\mathrm{BC}}\) is the adelic extension of its local versions
\(\tau^{\mathrm{BC}}_{p}\). The local anti-involution at a prime \(p\)
is the conjugate-linear anti-automorphism
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:kp:eq:BC-involution-local}
\tau^{\mathrm{BC}}_{p}\colon\mathbb{A}^{\mathrm{BC}}_{p}\to\mathbb{A}^{\mathrm{BC}}_{p},
\quad
\mu_{m}\mapsto\mu_{m}^{*},\quad
\mu_{m}^{*}\mapsto\mu_{m},\quad
e(\gamma)\mapsto e(-\gamma).
\end{equation}
On the GNS Hilbert space of the represented prime Gibbs state,
constructed in \Cref{v4-arith:thermal:gns-carrier}, the corresponding
antiunitary is not \(\tau^{\mathrm{BC}}_{p}\) itself but
\[
J_{\varphi_{1},p}\pi(\mu_m)\Omega_{\varphi_{1}}
=m^{1/2}\pi(\mu_m^{*})\Omega_{\varphi_{1}},\qquad
J_{\varphi_{1},p}\pi(\mu_m^{*})\Omega_{\varphi_{1}}
=m^{-1/2}\pi(\mu_m)\Omega_{\varphi_{1}} .
\]
The factors are forced by
\(S_{\varphi_{1}}=J_{\varphi_{1}}\Delta_{\varphi_{1}}^{1/2}\). An archimedean extension requires its own conjugate-linear map.
The identity on a nonzero complex Hilbert space is linear, so it
cannot itself be the archimedean factor of an antiunitary tensor
map. Coefficient conjugation fixes a chosen real vacuum and gives
a possible antiunitary factor, but its native pairing compatibility
remains part of the adelic comparison.

\begin{lemma}[properties of the local anti-involution and its lift]
\label{v4-arith:kp:lem:involution-properties}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere. The local Bost--Connes anti-involution
satisfies:
\begin{itemize}
\item \(\tau^{\mathrm{BC}}_{p}\circ\tau^{\mathrm{BC}}_{p}=\mathrm{id}\)
(involutive; Bost--Connes~\S1.2).
\item \(\tau^{\mathrm{BC}}_{p}\) is \(\mathbb{C}\)-antilinear
(it is the \(*\)-conjugation of the \(C^{*}\)-structure).
\item The GNS lift \(J_{\varphi_{1},p}\) preserves the Bost--Connes
Hilbert pairing:
\(\langle J_{\varphi_{1},p}v,J_{\varphi_{1},p}w\rangle^{\mathrm{BC}}_{p}
=\overline{\langle v,w\rangle^{\mathrm{BC}}_{p}}\).
\end{itemize}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first two properties are immediate from the defining formulas
(\ref{v4-arith:kp:eq:BC-involution-local}): involutivity from
\((\mu_{m}^{*})^{*}=\mu_{m}\) and \(e(-(-\gamma))=e(\gamma)\);
\(\mathbb{C}\)-antilinearity from the \(*\)-conjugation structure.
The third follows from the Hilbert--Schmidt adjoint formula in
\Cref{v4-arith:thermal:modular}, with the half-density
factors displayed above.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Adelic Involution}
\label{v4-arith:kp:BC-involution:adelic}

\begin{definition}[tensor conjugation and native adelic comparison]
\label{v4-arith:kp:def:adelic-involution}
Fix an archimedean antiunitary $c_\infty$ preserving a specified
real vacuum. For a finite prime set, the operator
$c_\infty\otimes\bigotimes_{p\in F}J_{p,1}$ is defined on
complex tensor products by conjugating scalar coefficients.
With unit reference vectors it extends to the incomplete Hilbert
tensor product. Its transport to native adelic factors requires
the compatible maps of \Cref{v4-arith:kp:hyp:native-pairing-maps}.
\end{definition}

The archimedean identity map cannot replace $c_\infty$ on a
nonzero complex factor: applying a conjugate-linear map in one
factor and a linear map in another sends $(iv)\otimes w$ and
$v\otimes(iw)$ to opposite scalar multiples, although these
are the same tensor. Using anti-linear maps in every factor
respects the tensor relation. Coefficient conjugation in a chosen
real orthonormal basis supplies an archimedean antiunitary, but
its native pairing compatibility is additional data.


\begin{proposition}[conditional transport of conjugation]
\label{v4-arith:kp:prop:involution-conjugation}
Suppose a native conjugate-linear map \(c:V\to V^!\) and linear maps
\(I:V\to L^2(X,\mu)\), \(I^!:V^!\to L^2(X,\mu)\) satisfy
\begin{equation}
              I^!c(v)=\overline{I(v)}.
              \label{v4-arith:kp:eq:involution-conjugation}
\end{equation}
Then the transported native map is pointwise conjugation on the
specified images. This compatibility is an additional hypothesis on
native maps, not a consequence of local modular conjugation alone.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The displayed equality defines the transport on the images. On the
explicit operator carrier \(J_qX=X^*\), which exchanges row and column
indices and does not preserve the creation sector. Pointwise
conjugation of scalar functions consequently cannot be identified with
\(J_q\) without maps from the function spaces and their duals. The two
maps in \Cref{v4-arith:kp:radial-pairing-realization} provide exactly
this comparison on the radial sector.
\end{proof}

\section{Spherical-Vector Calculation}
\label{v4-arith:kp:spherical-calc}

To make the match between Koszul and Petersson pairings
concrete and verifiable at the level of numerical constants,
we compute both at the unramified spherical vector at a
single prime \(p\) and at the archimedean place.

\subsection{Haar mass and unit-state mass}
\label{v4-arith:kp:spherical-calc:Pet-p}
\label{v4-arith:kp:spherical-calc:Koszul-p}

\begin{lemma}[the compact-group volume]
\label{v4-arith:kp:lem:spherical-Pet-p}
Give \(M_2(\mathbb Q_p)\) additive Haar measure with
\(M_2(\mathbb Z_p)\) of mass one, and give \(GL_2(\mathbb Q_p)\) the
measure \(dg=|\det g|_p^{-2}d^4g\). For \(K=GL_2(\mathbb Z_p)\),
\begin{equation}
 V_p=\operatorname{vol}(K)=(1-p^{-1})(1-p^{-2}),
 \qquad \|\mathbf1_K\|_2^2=V_p.
 \label{v4-arith:kp:eq:spherical-Pet-p}
\end{equation}
The normalized vector \(v_K=V_p^{-1/2}\mathbf1_K\) has norm one.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The determinant has absolute value one on \(K\). Reduction modulo \(p\)
partitions \(M_2(\mathbb Z_p)\) into \(p^4\) equal-measure fibers.
An invertible matrix over \(\mathbb F_p\) has \(p^2-1\) choices of
first column and \(p^2-p\) choices of second column. The ratio of
these counts to \(p^4\) is \(V_p\). The two norm statements follow
by integration.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[the state vacuum and its scaled line]
\label{v4-arith:kp:lem:spherical-Koszul-p}
In the represented Gibbs GNS space, \(\Omega=[1]\) has norm one,
\(J_q\Omega=\Omega\), and \(B_q(\Omega,\Omega)=1\). The linear map
from the compact-group line to the vacuum line is isometric when
\[
             \mathbf1_K\longmapsto\sqrt{V_p}\,\Omega.
\]
For this explicitly scaled vector,
\begin{equation}
 B_q(\sqrt{V_p}\Omega,J_q\sqrt{V_p}\Omega)=V_p.
 \label{v4-arith:kp:eq:spherical-Koszul-p}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Unitality gives \(\|\Omega\|^2=\Phi(1)=1\). On Hilbert--Schmidt
operators \(\Omega=\sqrt{\varrho_q}\) is self-adjoint and
\(B_q(\Omega,\Omega)=\operatorname{Tr}\varrho_q=1\).
The positive real rescaling gives the stated isometry and pairing.
It does not change the normalized state or identify a whole native
Whittaker representation with the GNS space.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite products and adelic normalization}
\label{v4-arith:kp:spherical-calc:global}

\begin{proposition}[finite spherical-line comparison]
\label{v4-arith:kp:prop:global-spherical-match}
For a finite prime set \(F\), the tensor of the displayed line maps
is isometric, and
\begin{equation}
 \left\|\bigotimes_{p\in F}\mathbf1_{K_p}\right\|^2
 =B_F\left(\bigotimes_{p\in F}\sqrt{V_p}\Omega_p,
           J_F\bigotimes_{p\in F}\sqrt{V_p}\Omega_p\right)
 =\prod_{p\in F}V_p.
 \label{v4-arith:kp:eq:global-spherical-match}
\end{equation}
For the empty set the value is one. Along all primes the unnormalized
products tend to zero, whereas the normalized reference vectors all
have norm one. A native Tamagawa pairing requires its own global
measure and comparison maps; it is not the ordinary infinite product
of these unnormalized volumes.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Finite tensor inner products multiply. Since
\(0<V_p\le1-p^{-1}\), the infinite product is zero: otherwise its
reciprocal would bound all finite products
\(\prod_{p\in F}(1-p^{-1})^{-1}\), whereas these dominate every finite
harmonic sum once \(F\) contains all prime divisors of its indices.
The harmonic sums are unbounded. Normalized reference vectors instead
have squared norm one in every finite tensor product.
\end{proof}

\section{Analytic continuation of a realized pairing}
\label{v4-arith:kp:analytic-cont}
\label{v4-arith:kp:analytic-cont:poles}

The normalized state and its partition trace can have different pole
behavior. For \(|q|<1\),
\[
 Z(q)=\sum_{n\ge0}q^n=(1-q)^{-1},\qquad
 (1-q)Z(q)=1.
\]
The first scalar has a pole at \(q=1\), while the unit value of the
normalized functional extends to the constant one. Its vacuum projection
value \(1-q\) is also entire. Thus a pole of a partition function is
not automatically a pole of a normalized pairing.

\begin{proposition}[conditional continuation of the pairing identity]
\label{v4-arith:kp:prop:pole-matching}
Suppose the native and automorphic comparison maps give equal pairings
on a nonempty open parameter set. Suppose their scalar matrix elements
admit meromorphic continuations to the same connected complex domain,
using \((s_1,\overline{s_2})\) as independent variables for a
sesquilinear parameter family. Then those continued matrix elements
agree. In particular their Laurent principal parts agree wherever a
common local Laurent expansion is defined.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For each fixed pair of vectors, subtract the two meromorphic matrix
elements. On each coordinate neighborhood multiply by a common
holomorphic denominator. The result is holomorphic and zero on the
initial open set. The identity theorem, applied along overlapping
coordinate neighborhoods in the connected domain, makes it zero
everywhere. Dividing back proves equality of the meromorphic elements
and hence of their Laurent coefficients. The initial equality and
continuation hypotheses concern the actual pairings, not just their
partition scalars.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the pole-divisor requirement]
\label{v4-arith:kp:analytic-cont:no-exotic}
\label{v4-arith:kp:rem:no-new-residue}
A prescribed automorphic pole divisor transfers only after the native
trace or pairing map and its continuation are constructed. The local
Gibbs model cannot exclude additional native homological contributions
or determine their poles. The completed scalar ratios and their
removable singularities are computed separately in
\Cref{v4-arith:kms-gns:lem:Koszul-continued}.
\end{remark}

\section{The Main Theorem}
\label{v4-arith:kp:main-theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Koszul--Petersson pairing compatibility, domain-restricted]
\label{v4-arith:kp:thm:main-full}
\ClaimStatusProvedHereConditional. Assume \Cref{v4-arith:kp:hyp:native-pairing-maps}, including the stated adelic pairing assembly.  \emph{(domain-restricted to the normalized
sph-fin cuspidal core and using the Tamagawa regularisation of
\Cref{v4-arith:kp:lem:adelic-convergence}; see
\Cref{v4-arith:kp:rem:domain-restriction})}. The identity
(\ref{v4-arith:kp:eq:main})
\begin{equation*}
\langle v,w\rangle^{\mathrm{Pet}}
\;=\;
\langle v,\sigma^{\mathrm{BC}}(w)\rangle^{\mathrm{Koszul}}
\end{equation*}
holds for all
\(v,w\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}_{\mathrm{sph\text{-}fin,cusp}}\),
the normalized sph-fin cuspidal core of \(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}\).
Equivalently, the arithmetic Koszul pairing of the sph-fin
cuspidal core with
\((\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}})^{!}=\mathbb{A}^{\mathrm{BC}}\)
coincides, after Bost--Connes involution on the dual, with the
adelic Petersson inner product on \(L^{2}([GL_{2}])\) restricted
to the image of the sph-fin cuspidal core. Full-sector
compatibility and KMS\(_{1}\)/GNS identification beyond this core
remain open
(\Cref{v4-arith:kp:rem:domain-restriction}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For every place \(v\leq\infty\),
\Cref{v4-arith:kp:cor:local-compatibility} gives
\(\langle v_{v},w_{v}\rangle^{\mathrm{Pet}}_{v}
=\langle v_{v},\sigma^{\mathrm{BC}}_{v}(w_{v})\rangle^{\mathrm{Koszul}}_{v}\).
\Cref{v4-arith:kp:thm:tate-assembly} provides the restricted
tensor product assembly of both sides. The adelic involution
\(\sigma^{\mathrm{BC}}=\bigotimes_{v}'\sigma^{\mathrm{BC}}_{v}\)
is well-defined on the restricted tensor product
(\Cref{v4-arith:kp:def:adelic-involution}). Taking the product
over \(v\) of the local identities yields
\begin{equation*}
\langle v,w\rangle^{\mathrm{Pet}}
=\prod_{v}\langle v_{v},w_{v}\rangle^{\mathrm{Pet}}_{v}
=\prod_{v}\langle v_{v},\sigma^{\mathrm{BC}}_{v}(w_{v})\rangle^{\mathrm{Koszul}}_{v}
=\langle v,\sigma^{\mathrm{BC}}(w)\rangle^{\mathrm{Koszul}},
\end{equation*}
as in \Cref{v4-arith:kp:cor:global-from-local}.

The product or regularization is part of the stated assembly
hypothesis. If the two pairings additionally admit meromorphic
continuations on a connected common parameter domain and agree on a
nonempty open subset, the identity theorem extends their equality
there. A normalized tensor state does not supply this continuation.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[what the native hypothesis requires]
\label{v4-arith:kp:rem:non-circular}
The local Hilbert--Schmidt and radial shell formulas construct actual
pairing maps on their declared carriers. The native arithmetic identity
requires maps from the original arithmetic and automorphic objects,
compatible with the specified duality, operators and adelic phases.
The conditional theorem transfers the identity once those maps exist;
it does not establish their existence.
\end{remark}

\section{Consequence: P3 on the Sph-Fin Cuspidal Core}
\label{v4-arith:kp:P3-unconditional}

\begin{corollary}[P3 unitarity on the sph-fin cuspidal core]
\label{v4-arith:kp:cor:P3-unconditional}
\ClaimStatusProvedHereConditional. Assume \Cref{v4-arith:kp:hyp:native-pairing-maps}, including the stated adelic pairing assembly.  \emph{(domain-restricted to the normalized
sph-fin cuspidal core and to the Tamagawa-regularised pairing)}. On the normalized sph-fin cuspidal core
\(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}_{\mathrm{sph\text{-}fin,cusp}}\),
assume also an injective automorphic realization into the cuspidal
\(L^2\) space. Its pulled-back integral norm is a positive-definite Hermitian form
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:kp:eq:Pet-form}
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle^{\mathrm{Pet}}\colon\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}_{\mathrm{sph\text{-}fin,cusp}}\otimes\overline{\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}_{\mathrm{sph\text{-}fin,cusp}}}\to\mathbb{C}
\end{equation}
given by the adelic Petersson--Tamagawa inner product, and this
form is compatible with arithmetic Koszul self-duality via
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:kp:eq:Pet-Koszul-compat}
\langle v,w\rangle^{\mathrm{Pet}}
\;=\;\langle v,\sigma^{\mathrm{BC}}(w)\rangle^{\mathrm{Koszul}}.
\end{equation}
Full-sector P3 is not a consequence of the sph-fin cuspidal identity:
the KMS\(_{1}\)/GNS half-density is the operator-algebraic statement
of \Cref{v4-arith:kms-gns:thm:KMS1-GNS-BC-identification}, while the
ramified Rankin--Selberg and Eisenstein terms are separate domain
conditions (\Cref{v4-arith:kp:rem:domain-restriction}).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If \(I\) is the assumed injective realization, then
\(\langle v,v\rangle^{\mathrm{Pet}}=\|Iv\|_{L^2}^2>0\) for
\(v\ne0\). The native pairing identity follows from the local transfer
and the assumed adelic assembly. Positivity of this form does not
establish a unitary vertex-algebra action: adjoint relations for that
action require their own verification.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the native positivity requirement]
\label{v4-arith:kp:rem:P3-upgrade}
Injectivity is essential. Pulling back a positive Hilbert norm along a
linear map gives a positive semidefinite form whose null space is the
kernel of the map. It is positive definite precisely when that kernel
is zero. Local Gibbs positivity therefore cannot replace the native
realization hypothesis, even on a restricted cuspidal core.
\end{remark}

\section{Boundary}
\label{v4-arith:kp:summary}

The represented prime state has unit value, the modular conjugation has
creation factor \(m^{1/2}\) and division factor \(m^{-1/2}\) at
\(\beta=1\), and the bilinear operator pairing becomes Hermitian after
that conjugation. The radial shell maps realize these identities with
explicit norms and energy behavior.

The native Koszul--Petersson theorem requires its own pairing and
realization maps, including at the archimedean place. Finite spherical
line maps account for Haar volume by rescaling vectors; they do not
rescale the unit of a state. Infinite normalized tensor products,
Tamagawa regularization and analytic continuation are separate
constructions. The native comparison hypotheses must include whichever
of them the desired global pairing uses.

On an injectively realized cuspidal core, the pulled-back integral norm
is positive definite and the conditional transfer identifies it with
the Koszul form after native conjugate-linear duality. Establishing a
unitary chiral-algebra action, extending to the Eisenstein continuum,
and representing the arithmetic zero-divisor or Li trace remain further
mathematical requirements.
