\chapter*{Preface}
\markboth{Preface}{Preface}
\label{v4-ch:preface}

Arithmetic operations produce quantities that can also appear as
characters, partition functions or spectral traces. The subject of this
volume is the comparison before evaluation: the coefficient algebras,
modules, relative conditions and geometric operations that could explain
such an equality. Its source and target must be constructed, and the
map between them must preserve the structures consumed by later
operations. Equality of the final numbers is then a consequence with a
specified mathematical domain.

The prologue gives four reasons for retaining this information.
Gaussian integration preserves a relation complex while changing both
products and normalization. The cubic algebra has a strict differential
graded model with a nonzero triple product. A matrix coefficient lost
by an early trace changes a subsequent four-input evaluation. Positive
extension-counting forms and a reciprocal polynomial can coexist with
the wrong absolute values for a geometric weight-one operator. These
are calculations on explicit carriers; each identifies data that the
corresponding scalar has omitted.

Chapter~\ref{v4-ch:marked-realizations} defines comparisons preserving
an algebra, a module, a selected vector and a functional. It separates
objectwise existence, uniqueness and coherent choice. The coefficient
ring and its topology enter the definition: a finite torsion ring admits
no unital map to a characteristic-zero field, and taking an inverse
limit before rationalization is a different construction.

The algebraic core then retains intermediate coefficient states through
relative interfaces. Chapter~\ref{v4-ch:relative-coefficient-envelopes}
constructs their composition under the stated central-action and derived
tensor hypotheses. Chapter~\ref{v4-ch:connected-descent} identifies the
coring introduced by restriction of coefficients and proves descent
under explicit completeness and detection hypotheses. An empty
coefficient and a local unit have different carriers; an adjunction
counit need not identify a restricted composite with its original
interface.

Relative arithmetic classes also retain primitives. For a restriction
map \(f:A\to B\), a relative cocycle \((a,y)\) satisfies
\(da=0\) and \(f(a)=dy\). Multiplying two such classes can use
one or the other local primitive. Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:gluing:chapter}
constructs the two products and their integral comparison in every
arity, including the induced module and bimodule operations. No
division is needed. A restricted inverse limit over finite coefficient
rings preserves the comparison; an unrestricted product over word
lengths need not do so. Its interpretation as a derived tensor has its
own flatness hypothesis.

The comparison questions from the first three volumes retain their
specific invariants. For an automorphic product with seed constant
coefficient \(c_N(0)\), the weight identity
\[
 w_N=\frac{c_N(0)}2
\]
is one possible numerical comparison. Interpreting this weight as a
Borcherds--Kac--Moody invariant, written \(\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}\),
requires the actual denominator construction and its normalization.
Reading the same seed coefficient twice is not a second construction.
Likewise the affine expression
\[
 \kappa=\frac{\dim(\mathfrak g)(k+h^\vee)}{2h^\vee},
\]
for a simple Lie algebra \(\mathfrak g\), level \(k\) and dual
Coxeter number \(h^\vee\), is an invariant to compare in its stated
normalization. It is not, by itself, a screening-cohomology comparison.

For the Virasoro shadow model, the proposed comparisons involve the
rational functions
\[
 c_S=-\frac{218}{45},\qquad
 |\omega|^2(c)=\frac{c^2(5c+22)}{4(45c+218)},\qquad
 S_5(c)=-\frac{48}{c^2(5c+22)}.
\]
The pole and denominators specify excluded parameter values for these
formulas. If an actual function \(H\) satisfies \(H^2=t^4Q_c\),
then, on a domain where the quotients are defined,
\[
 U=(\log H)'-\frac2t=\frac{Q_c'}{2Q_c},\qquad
 U'+2U^2=\frac{Q_c''}{2Q_c}.
\]
The second identity follows by differentiating the first. Identifying
these functions with a shadow tower, a Borel invariant or an
operator-product coefficient requires the respective construction.
The four quantities \((K,K^\kappa,K_c,K_g)\), the conductor,
complementarity, central-charge and ghost-number invariants, likewise
have different definitions. Their comparisons, the determinant at
\(p=691\), and the eight-form Gritsenko--Cl\'ery family enter with
the source hypotheses stated for each object.

For the finite-type, freely generated finite-jet Khan--Zeng Poisson
vertex models, the shifted-cotangent Batalin--Vilkovisky construction
compares the classical master equation with the Hamiltonian condition.
The quantum master equation,
reflected weights, boundary vertex algebra and higher operadic lift
require additional analytic and geometric constructions. The
spectral-decomposition retract, Fulton--MacPherson boundary terms,
reflection conventions and the lift map belong to those hypotheses;
the classical calculation does not supply them.
The holomorphic--topological comparison also concerns the seven
presentations of the two-coloured chiral--topological Swiss-cheese
datum \(\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}\). The operadic, Koszul-dual,
factorization, BV/BRST and convolution descriptions require their actual
comparison maps. The Drinfeld-centre and derived-geometric descriptions
further require the specified enrichment and representability hypotheses.

On the Calabi--Yau side, a specified two-stage construction has the form
\[
 \Phi_d=\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathrm{ch}}_{\Sigma_{d-1},C}\circ
 \Phi^{\mathrm{FA}}_d.
\]
The first map produces the declared factorization object; the second
uses the chosen cycle \(\Sigma_{d-1}\), curve \(C\) and its
specialization data. Comparisons with a K3 Hall--Drinfeld object
\(\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}\), Joyce's vertex algebra and
Kontsevich--Pandharipande--Soibelman orientation data, the
Maulik--Nekrasov--Okounkov--Pandharipande invariants, or a framed
Lagrangian theory each require their own maps. The proposed K3
representation targets are the full rank-\(24\) Mukai module, a
Heisenberg--Mukai quotient, and an intermediate quantum vertex
\(W\)-algebra. Identifying any of these targets requires the
evaluation map, its preserved form and the specified
\(\mathfrak{so}(4,20)\) action. An \(S^3\)-framing likewise
belongs to the Lagrangian data. A proved specialization does not
identify all the other constructions with it.

The analytic arithmetic endpoint is the full signed Weil functional,
with both polar evaluations, the archimedean term and every prime power.
A positive state on an auxiliary algebra gives a realization of that
functional only after a product- and involution-preserving comparison
and its evaluation identity have been proved on the full test domain.
Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:w10-final:chapter} separates the stated arithmetic
comparisons from the remaining Weil positivity condition; its list is
not an exhaustion theorem for every possible realization problem.

The topology of a proposed spectral recipient can already exclude a
comparison. The graph norm of multiplication by \(t\) on
\(L^2(\mathbb R,dt)\) is
\[
 \|f\|_{\mathrm{gr}}^2
 =\int_{\mathbb R}(1+t^2)|f(t)|^2\,dt.
\]
Every continuous functional is integration against a measurable
function \(q\) with
\(\int |q(t)|^2/(1+t^2)\,dt<\infty\). Such a function is
locally integrable. If its distribution is supported on a locally
finite set, it must vanish, since that set has Lebesgue measure zero.
Put \(\xi(s)=\tfrac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\),
where \(\zeta\) is the Riemann zeta function. In particular the
graph dual cannot carry nonzero point or finite-jet residues on
\[
 \Gamma_\xi^{\mathrm{crit}}
 =\{\gamma\in\mathbb R:\xi(\tfrac12+i\gamma)=0\}.
\]
A spectral-flow construction using these residues needs a stronger
specified domain, such as a suitable rigged, Paley--Wiener, de Branges
or reproducing-kernel space, or a boundary theory with continuous
trace and jet maps. It must also retain multiplicities, the full
Hadamard divisor where needed, and compatibility with the post-Tate
quotient. The choice of recipient is therefore part of the arithmetic
comparison itself.
