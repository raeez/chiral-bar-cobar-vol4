\chapter*{Preface}
\label{v4-ch:preface}

An inscribed theorem is not a verified theorem. A claim carrying the
tag \texttt{\textbackslash ClaimStatusProvedHere} asserts that the
reader will find, within the same file, a proof. It does not assert,
and cannot by itself assert, that the proof's internal derivation is
disjoint from the sources the author consulted to arrive at the
statement. The two are routinely identified in mathematical practice.
The programme that precedes this volume rests on the Beilinson
principle, an explicit adversarial protocol, and over one hundred
thousand tests. Yet a trust-based corpus is not a verified corpus.

The deficiency is concrete. Consider the inscription in Vol III of
the formula
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} \;=\; c_N(0)/2
\]
for the Borcherds--Kac--Moody central charge of a frame-shape orbifold
lift. In the compute engine, \(c_N(0)\) is tabulated from
\texttt{FRAME\_SHAPE\_DATA}. The hardcoded expected values in the
test file are computed by halving entries of the same table. Ninety-nine
tests pass. The derivation (via the Borcherds 1998 weight theorem
applied to the input lift) and the verification (by direct arithmetic
on the tabulated lift data) share a source. Nothing can fail. No
ninety-nine-test suite detects anything about the inscription
beyond the arithmetic \(c_N(0)/2 = c_N(0)/2\).

This is not a local accident. The source-disjoint audit across the
programme found that every programme-wide hardcoded-table
verification was of this form: the table from which the theorem was
derived also supplied the test expectations. The programme is not
verifying that its theorems are true; it is verifying that its
arithmetic on a single source is consistent.

The deficiency admits a remedy: a disjointness requirement. A
theorem \(T\) is verified, in the sense relevant to avoid
coincidental-agreement failures of the kind exposed by the
\(\Psi = 2\) coincidence masking a cross-arity sign error, only if
there exists a test whose expected value is computed along a source
disjoint from the source used to derive \(T\)'s formula. Disjoint means
disjoint as a set of citations, not disjoint as a rephrasing. The
source-disjoint protocol in Vols I, II, and III
(\texttt{compute/lib/independent\_verification.py}) enforces this:
every test decorated with \texttt{@independent\_verification} must
declare its \texttt{derived\_from} and \texttt{verified\_against}
sources, and the decorator raises \texttt{IndependentVerificationError}
at collection time if the two sets overlap.

The protocol installs no coverage. The installation snapshot across
the three prior volumes covered \(2\) of \(3692\) ProvedHere claims:
\(0/2275\) in Vol~I, \(0/1134\) in Vol~II, and \(2/283\) in Vol~III.
The infrastructure was in place; the content was not. A programme's
claim corpus can be verified one decorator at a time, but not by the
same act that produces the theorem. The
verification must be a separate intellectual operation, carried out
with a different source catalog, and held to a different standard
than the proof that produced the theorem.

Volume IV is that separate operation.

The realization corpus extends beyond the installation snapshot.
Vol~I supplies the Virasoro shadow tower with Stokes pole
\(c_S=-218/45\), Borel radius
\[
 |\omega|^2(c)=\frac{c^2(5c+22)}{4(45c+218)},
\]
and Riccati equation \(U'+2U^2=Q_c''/(2Q_c)\) on
\(U=(\log H)'-2/t\); the four-conductor split
\((K,K^\kappa,K_c,K_g)\); the \(p=691\) Chenevier determinant; and
the eight-form Gritsenko--Cl\'ery spread. Vol~II supplies
the \(\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}\) heptagon and the finite-jet
Khan--Zeng (KZ) sector: the shifted-cotangent BV action and the
CME/PVA-Hamiltonian equivalence are constructed; the all-loop QME,
reflected weight identity, boundary vertex algebra, and \(E_3\)-lift
remain conditional on the four endpoint hypotheses: the KZ analytic
spectral-decomposition representation (SDR), the Fulton--MacPherson
Stokes datum, the reflected weights, and the \(T\)-lift. Vol~III supplies the
two-stage functor
\[
 \Phi_d=\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathrm{ch}}_{\Sigma_{d-1},C}\circ
 \Phi^{\mathrm{FA}}_d,
\]
the K3 Hall--Drinfeld chiral bialgebra \(\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5}\), the
Joyce vertex algebra paired with Kontsevich--Pandharipande--Soibelman
(KPS) orientation on generic compact Calabi--Yau threefolds with
Maulik--Nekrasov--Okounkov--Pandharipande (MNOP) invariants,
\(S^3\)-framing on generic compact Calabi--Yau threefold Lagrangians,
and the \(\mathfrak{so}(4,20)\) K3 Yangian representation-target
trichotomy.

The arithmetic branch carries a native Vol~IV residual calculus whose
central open problem is the spectral-flow question: whether the
post-Tate quotient operator admits a self-adjoint extension compatible
with the Hadamard residue functional on the critical-line ordinate set
\(\Gamma_\xi^{\mathrm{crit}}\). The ordinary \(M_t\)-graph dual (the
Hilbert space of \(M_t\)-measurable functions with the \((1+t^2)\,dt\)
inner product) carries neither point nor finite-jet Hadamard residue
functionals on \(\Gamma_\xi^{\mathrm{crit}}\). Any surviving
spectral-flow argument requires a stronger topology (rigged Hilbert
space, Paley--Wiener, de Branges, reproducing-kernel, nonlocal
\(L^2\), or BV--BFV boundary) in which Hadamard trace and jet maps
are continuous and compatible with the post-Tate quotient.

The content is the realization pair
\[
 \mathrm{Real}(X, T) \;=\; (d, e),
\]
where \(d\) is a source-disjoint decorator whose derivation and
verification sources are disjoint, and \(e\) is a compute engine whose
hardcoded expected values come from a source disjoint from the
engine's formula. The central theorem is that Real exists for every
ProvedHere claim inscribed in Vols I, II, III on the non-degenerate
locus of the programme. The non-degenerate locus is large: it
contains every generic parameter in every standard family, every
Koszul-locus claim, every chain-level result outside the admissible
logarithmic sector, every CY construction outside the five
unconverged CY-C bridges. It excludes the critical level
\(k = -h^\vee\) for affine Kac--Moody (where \(\kappa = 0\) and
Koszulness fails), the \(\Psi = 0\) locus for chiral Yangians (where
the algebra
is abelian), the Creutzig--Kanade--Linshaw admissible logarithmic
sector, and the super-Yangian chain-level chiral coproduct sector.

Each of the three Vol IV theorems is itself realized: the decorator
is installed at inscription time, the engine backs it from a disjoint
source catalog, and the reader runs the test on receipt of the
repository.

Vol IV inherits the Russian school's convergent voice: Gelfand's
inevitability (trust-based inscription eventually produces a
tautology somewhere in the corpus), Beilinson's falsification (the
source-disjointness check catches the tautology at collection time),
Drinfeld's unifying principle (the realization functor Real),
Kazhdan's compression. A fifth voice enters: verification is a
craft, and its content is specific. The Borcherds \(c_N(0)/2\)
identification is verified by a Gritsenko--Nikulin denominator
identity, not by a tabulation; the affine Kac--Moody
\(\kappa = \dim(\mathfrak{g})(k + h^\vee)/(2 h^\vee)\) identification
is verified at non-critical level by Feigin--Frenkel screening
cohomology, not by character arithmetic on the Sugawara construction;
the class M MC5 Wick \(S_5 = -48/(c^2(5c + 22))\) is verified by an
independent Wick contraction engine built from the Virasoro OPE
axioms, not from the lambda-bracket expansion used to derive it.

\section*{Structure}

Part A indexes every ProvedHere claim from Vols~I, II, III; each
entry points either to a realized entry in Part~B or to a
scope-restricted entry with the complementary locus explicitly marked.
The realization programme as a mathematical object appears in
Chapter~\ref{v4-ch:realization-programme}. The decorator protocol and
its collection-time enforcement are implemented in
\texttt{compute/lib/realization\_registry.py}, which extends the Vol~I
\texttt{compute/lib/independent\_verification.py}. Conventions are
inherited from Vol~I and distinguished here by the prefix
\texttt{v4-} and the Vol~IV registry's import-time disjointness check
against the Vol~I registry.
