\chapter{Comparison problems for the first three volumes}
\label{v4-ch:realization-formalism}

\section{Independent comparison}
\label{v4-sec:deficiency}

The constructions of the first three volumes give particular
comparison problems of the kind defined in
Chapter~\ref{v4-ch:marked-realizations}. Their coefficient categories,
modules, operations and evaluation maps must be compared before the
resulting scalar identities can be interpreted geometrically.

Fixing an invariant functor specifies what an evaluation retains.
A comparison on the original category can imply an equality of such
invariants. Recovering a comparison from that equality requires further
hypotheses on the functor; the character example of
Section~\ref{v4-marked:sec:characters} shows the failure when it
forgets extensions.

\section{The realization pair}
\label{v4-sec:realization-pair}

\begin{definition}[Comparison of invariants]
\label{v4-def:realization-pair}
Let \(\mathscr C\) be a category and \(I:\mathscr C\to
\mathrm{Set}\) a functor. A \emph{comparison of invariants}
is a pair \((d,e)\), where \(d\) consists of objects \(A,B\)
and a map \(f:A\to B\), and \(e\) consists of specified elements
\(a\in I(A)\), \(b\in I(B)\) with \(I(f)(a)=b\).
When a statement \(T\) in a family \(X\) asks for this
identification, write \(\mathrm{Real}(X,T)=(d,e)\) for a chosen
pair. The map \(f\) is part of the data. Neither its existence nor
its invertibility follows from the numerical values of \(a,b\).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Coherent comparison of a diagram]
\label{v4-thm:realization-formalism-definition}
Let \(J\) be a small category, let \(U:\mathscr R\to\mathscr D\)
be a functor between categories of realizations and retained data,
and let \(d:J\to\mathscr D\) be a diagram. For each object
\(j\), choose \(r_j\in\mathscr R\) and an isomorphism
\(\alpha_j:U(r_j)\to d(j)\). Suppose that every arrow
\(a:j\to k\) has a chosen lift \(r_a:r_j\to r_k\), with
\[
 \alpha_k U(r_a)=d(a)\alpha_j,\qquad
 r_{1_j}=1_{r_j},\qquad r_{ba}=r_b r_a.
\]
Then these data define a functor \(r:J\to\mathscr R\) and a
natural isomorphism \(Ur\cong d\). Conversely every such functor
and natural isomorphism supplies the displayed data. If \(U\) is
fully faithful, the chosen objects and isomorphisms determine all
the lifts and their identities uniquely.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The unit and composition identities are exactly the functor axioms;
the first equation is naturality of \(\alpha\). Conversely these
axioms hold for every functor and natural isomorphism. If \(U\) is
fully faithful, the map
\(\alpha_k^{-1}d(a)\alpha_j\) has exactly one lift to
\(\mathscr R\). Faithfulness then proves the unit and composition
identities by applying \(U\) to both sides. Objectwise existence
without these hypotheses is insufficient, as the nonsplit
\(BC_4\to BC_2\) example in
Section~\ref{v4-marked:sec:spaces} shows.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[A restricted family of comparison problems]
\label{v4-def:non-degenerate-locus}
Fix a category \(\mathcal I\) of specified parameterized constructions
and their specialization maps. Let \(\mathcal I_{\mathrm{nd}}\) be
its full subcategory obtained by omitting the critical affine level
\(k=-h^\vee\), the Yangian parameter value \(\Psi=0\), the chosen
admissible logarithmic sectors, the super-Yangian coproduct comparison,
and the five Calabi--Yau comparison constructions \(R_2,\ldots,R_6\)
described below. The meaning of the parameters and sectors is that of
the respective construction. This is a restriction of the comparison
problem; no existence or regularity theorem is included in the notation.
\end{definition}

The omitted cases need their own coefficient categories and maps.
Their omission gives no automatic comparison on the remaining objects.
In particular, a failure of one normalization at a critical parameter
does not prove failure of every realization at that parameter.

\section{Seven structural comparison problems}
\label{v4-sec:existence-platonic}

The seven families considered here concern Koszul reflection, the
coderived--contraderived correspondence, derived centres, higher-genus
obstructions, Hochschild cohomology, celestial boundary algebras, and
reconstruction from higher invariants. Each comparison must use the
hypotheses and the mathematical recipient of the particular source
statement. A common name for the families gives no common existence
theorem.

\begin{openproblem}[Structural realization comparisons]
\label{v4-thm:realization-existence-for-platonic-theorems}
For each specified object in one of these seven families, construct an
independent realization of the retained operations and its comparison
map. Determine its coefficient domain, its preserved structures, and its
compatibility with specialization. Where only scalar invariants can
presently be compared, determine which additional operations the
comparison would have to retain.
\end{openproblem}

For Koszul reflection, a filtered twisting-morphism construction and a
factorization-homology construction provide possible starting objects.
A comparison on an ordered Orlik--Solomon model would have to identify
their differentials and tensor conventions, and then prove the claimed
compatibility with gluing. Neither the existence of the two constructions
nor equality of their graded dimensions supplies this map.

For the coderived--contraderived correspondence, specify the curved
coalgebra, the two categories of modules, and the totalizations in the
adjunction. An abstract adjoint functor theorem can construct an adjoint
under its hypotheses; an equivalence additionally requires the unit and
counit to be equivalences on the stated categories. The chain-level
bicomplex and the categorical adjunction must be compared with these
conditions intact.

For a derived centre, a mapping-stack or shifted-symplectic construction
can serve as a comparison only after its stack, tangent complexes and
shift have been defined on the chosen objects. The required map must
identify the centre's operations and the specified duality involution.
A symplectic form on a mapping stack does not itself construct that
identification.

For a higher-genus obstruction expressed in terms of a Hodge class,
Riemann--Roch and curvature formulas can compute characteristic classes
of specified bundles on the universal curve. A comparison must identify
the actual obstruction class with that characteristic class, including
any term contributed by gluing different channels. The Hodge-bundle
calculation alone supplies neither identification.

For Hochschild cohomology, the Heisenberg, Virasoro and affine examples
have distinct candidate comparison complexes: Lie-algebra cochains,
BRST cochains and relative cohomology complexes. Each needs its own map
to the bar model. A proposed concentration statement must specify the
completed or bounded complex to which it applies and retain any
quotient or Koszul hypotheses. A finite-family calculation gives no
concentration theorem for every chiral algebra.

For a celestial boundary algebra, an operator-product calculation in a
holomorphic gauge theory gives local coefficients on its specified
background. To compare it with an independently defined boundary
algebra, construct the boundary fields, their allowed singular products
and the reduction map, and prove their compatibility with composition.
A matching \(w_{1+\infty}\) coefficient is an invariant of such a
comparison, not its construction.

For reconstruction from a sequence of higher invariants, the comparison
must show that the invariant functor distinguishes the permitted
objects and morphisms. A Calabi--Yau categorification or an identification
of numerical characters can give part of the data. One must still prove
convergence, recover the retained operations, and control automorphisms.
The realization groupoids of Section~\ref{v4-marked:sec:spaces}
separate existence from these uniqueness requirements.

\section{Objectwise comparisons and their scope}
\label{v4-sec:completeness}

\begin{remark}[Objectwise comparison criterion]
\label{v4-thm:realization-completeness-programme}
For a fixed statement, a comparison of evaluations exists when the
actual constructions and calculation in
Definition~\ref{v4-def:realization-pair} are supplied. Restricting the
parameter domain or changing the references does not supply them.
Even after they have been supplied for each object, a functorial
comparison still requires the lifted arrows and composition identities
of Proposition~\ref{v4-thm:realization-formalism-definition}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Realization boundary]
\label{v4-rem:completeness-claim-form}
A comparison of evaluations is weaker than a marked realization of the
algebra, modules, higher operations and state. The two-dimensional
modules in Section~\ref{v4-marked:sec:characters} have identical
characters and no module isomorphism. Thus even a complete list of the
chosen scalar comparisons would not establish all the structural
comparisons above.
\end{remark}

\section{Boundary strata}
\label{v4-sec:domain-restrictions}

The following are additional comparison problems for the omitted
sectors. They are not an exhaustion of all possible failures of
existence, uniqueness or continuity.

\subsection{Degenerate admissible logarithmic sector}
\label{v4-sec:ckl-sector}

The Creutzig--Kanade--Linshaw admissible logarithmic sector for
admissible representations of affine \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) at
\(k + 2 = p/q\) with \(\gcd(p, q) = 1\), \(q \geq 2\), lies outside the
chosen restricted family. A comparison in this sector must retain the
logarithmic representation structure. An argument using only a generic
semisimple model does not supply that comparison.

\subsection{Super-Yangian chain-level chiral coproduct}
\label{v4-sec:super-yangian}

FM230 absorbs the concrete computation of the chain-level chiral
coproduct for super-Yangians into the parity-balance super-Whitehead
lemma, conditional on an explicit chain-level comparison at specific
super-algebras (super-\(\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)\) at generic parameters).
The independent comparison in this sector is the comparison with
the appropriate super-Feigin--Frenkel screening.

\subsection{Vol III CY-C conditional bridges}
\label{v4-sec:cy-c-conditional}

Vol III gives six constructions to the quantum vertex chiral group
\(G(K3\times E)\): \(R_1\)
the CY-to-chiral framed assignment \(\Phi_3^{(\Sigma_2,C)}\); \(R_2\)
the Borcherds multiplicative lift; \(R_3\) the Mukai-lattice VOA;
\(R_4\) the Kummer construction; \(R_5\) the
\(\mathcal{N}=(2,2)\) sigma-model half-twist; \(R_6\) the BLLPR
Schur-limit construction. Only \(R_1\) is a \(\Phi_3\) application;
CY-C is the assertion that \(R_2\)--\(R_6\) identify as
\((\Sigma_2, C)\)-specialisations of one canonical Stage-1 datum.
\(R_1\) carries a proof of convergence at \(d = 2\) via the framed
Stage-1 output. The other five constructions carry
\(conditional\) forms tied to convergence hypotheses:

\begin{enumerate}
 \item \(R_2\) (Borcherds multiplicative lift): conditional on
 convergence of the Igusa cusp form product formula at genus 2
 against the \(R_1\) Stage-1 normalisation.
 \item \(R_3\) (Mukai-lattice VOA): conditional on identification of
 the Mukai-lattice OPE with the \(\Phi_3^{(\Sigma_2,C)}\) chiral output
 on the \(K3\times E\) slice.
 \item \(R_4\) (Kummer construction): conditional on identification of
 the Kummer-orbifold chiral data with the smooth \(K3\) chiral
 specialisation of the common Stage-1 datum.
 \item \(R_5\) (sigma-model half-twist): conditional on convergence of
 the \(\mathcal{N}=(2,2)\) half-twisted vertex algebra to the same
 \((\Sigma_2,C)\)-specialised Stage-1 output.
 \item \(R_6\) (BLLPR Schur limit): conditional on identification of
 the Schur-limit construction at the \((\Sigma_2,C)\) datum with
 the common Stage-1 specialisation.
\end{enumerate}

For \(R_2,\ldots,R_6\) the realization datum is conditional precisely
on the corresponding convergence isomorphism. Once that isomorphism is
proved, the same datum carries \(realized\).

\section{Boundary Problems}
\label{v4-sec:open-ends-precisely}

The following problems retain these particular omitted comparisons.
Their resolution requires the indicated maps, not only a choice of
reference or a numerical identity.

\begin{openproblem}[Admissible logarithmic realization]
\label{v4-open:admissible-realization}
Construct a Vol IV realization pair for every statement in Vols
I-III whose statement depends on admissible logarithmic
representations of affine Lie algebras at \(k + h^\vee \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}\)
with rational level. A functorial comparison must also supply compatible maps on
specializations, as in
Proposition~\ref{v4-thm:realization-formalism-definition}. The comparison
must preserve the Creutzig--Kanade--Linshaw logarithmic structure.
A semi-infinite cohomology construction is a possible comparison
recipient only after its coefficient category, extension data and
map from the chosen chiral construction have been supplied.
\end{openproblem}

\begin{openproblem}[Super-Yangian chain-level chiral coproduct]
\label{v4-open:super-yangian-chain-level}
Construct a Vol IV realization pair for every statement in Vol
I concerning the chain-level chiral coproduct of super-Yangians
\(Y(\mathfrak{sl}(m|n))^{\mathrm{ch}}\), outside the domain of FM230's
absorption into parity-balance super-Whitehead. A concrete super-
Feigin--Frenkel screening construction for \(Y(\mathfrak{sl}(2|1))^{\mathrm{ch}}\)
at generic parameters suffices as the seed.
\end{openproblem}

\begin{openproblem}[Five of six CY-C bridges]
\label{v4-open:cy-c-bridges}
Prove each of the five conditional CY-C constructions \(R_2\)--\(R_6\) of
Vol III (Borcherds multiplicative lift, Mukai-lattice VOA, Kummer
construction, \(\mathcal{N}=(2,2)\) sigma-model half-twist, BLLPR
Schur-limit construction) by proving
the specific convergence isomorphism attaching each construction to the
common Stage-1 \((\Sigma_2,C)\)-specialisation of \(\Phi_3\). Each
discharge is a standalone result: proof of the Igusa cusp form
product convergence against the \(R_1\) normalisation; identification
of the Mukai-lattice OPE with the framed Stage-1 chiral output;
identification of the Kummer-orbifold chiral data with the smooth
\(K3\) chiral specialisation; and the analogous equalities for the
sigma-model and Schur-limit constructions.
\end{openproblem}

\section{Structural Consequences Inherited from Vols I--III}
\label{v4-sec:architectural-sharpenings-inherited}

\subsection{The four-conductor split and the Koszul-self-dual trinity}
\label{v4-subsec:four-conductor-trinity}

Off the Koszul-self-dual locus the single Koszul conductor
\(K(\mathcal{A}) = -c_{\mathrm{ghost}}(\mathrm{BRST}(\mathcal{A}))\) of Vol~I
equation~(G1) separates into four distinct conductors: \(K\) (BRST
ghost charge), \(K^{\kappa} := \kappa+\kappa^{!}\) (Theorem-C
complementarity ceiling, with values
\((0,13,250/3,25/3,8)\) on
\((\mathsf G,\mathsf L,\mathsf C,\mathsf M,\mathsf B)\), the
\(\mathsf M\)-row value \(25/3\) conditional), \(K_c\)
(central-charge trace), and \(K_g\) (ghost-number anomaly). The trinity
\(K = K_c = K_g\) holds only on the Koszul-self-dual subcategory
admitting both BRST resolution and a Borcherds-lift input on a
Calabi--Yau target.

\noindent\emph{Independent comparison.} The four conductors are
tested against four independent sources: \(K\) against the BRST ghost
resolution, \(K^\kappa\) against the archetype rows of the landscape
census (Kac--Moody trace-form, Virasoro Zamolodchikov norm,
\(\beta\gamma\) contact coefficient, \(\mathcal{W}_N\) Drinfeld--Sokolov
reduction chain, \(K3\) Mukai pairing via Bruinier 2002 Heegner
reciprocity), \(K_c\) against Kac--Wakimoto 1988 central-charge tables,
and \(K_g\) against ghost-partition primary Euler-character
computations.
Disjointness of the four paths is the realization witness of the
four-conductor \emph{separation}; coincidence of three of them on
Koszul-self-dual witnesses the trinity.

\subsection{Post-Tate Hadamard support and the \texorpdfstring{\(M_t\)}{Mt}-graph dual}
\label{v4-subsec:post-tate-hadamard-meyer}

The arithmetic spectral-flow branch separates the critical-line

\[
 \Gamma_\xi^{\mathrm{crit}}
 = \{\gamma\in \mathbb R:\xi(1/2+i\gamma)=0\}
\]

the graph dual is represented through the resolvent chart by

\noindent\emph{Independent comparison.} The ordinary \(M_t\)-graph

closed condition arises from the analytic structure and is not
imposed by fiat.
