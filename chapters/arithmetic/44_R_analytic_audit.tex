\chapter{\(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\): Form, Difficulty, and Position in the Riemann Hypothesis Programme}
\label{v4-arith:R-analytic-audit}

Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:hpar-det} isolated three residuals for the
minimal completion path to the Riemann hypothesis: weight existence
(W), the Weil explicit formula at Gaussian test functions (H), and
the sharp moment asymptotic \(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\),
which asserts \(\mu_{2n}\asymp C^{n}n!^{2}\) unconditionally for the
symmetrised shifted-zero moment sequence of \(\xi\).
Remark~\ref{v4-arith:hpar-det:rem:residual-vs-RH} of
Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:hpar-det} characterises
\(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\) as ``strictly weaker than RH'' and
``classical-analytic''. The present chapter sharpens this
characterisation along three independent axes: form, implication
direction, and difficulty. The conclusions are (i) the form is
classical-analytic; (ii) \(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\) does not
imply RH; (iii) the difficulty is not classical but rather
density-of-zeros, a major open problem adjacent to RH in depth.
The tempting unconditional shortcut, which would read Carleman
divergence directly off the Bombieri--Lagarias upper bound, contains a
Stirling error; the corrected computation yields a convergent Bertrand
series. This is exactly why
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:hpar-det:prop:BL-carleman-partial}
records the Bombieri--Lagarias route as logically independent of the
determinacy estimate rather than as a divergence proof.

\section{The Four Claims of Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:hpar-det}}
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:statement}

Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:hpar-det} makes four claims about
\(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\), stated here in their exact form
for independent assessment.

\begin{claimnaive}[Form of \(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\)]
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:claim-1}
\(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\) is classical-analytic in form:
a two-sided moment asymptotic
\(C_{-}^{n}n!^{2}\le \mu_{2n}\le C_{+}^{n}n!^{2}\) on a regularised
positive measure associated to the symmetrised shifted zeros of
\(\xi\).
\end{claimnaive}

\begin{claimnaive}[Strict weakness of \(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\) relative to RH]
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:claim-2}
\(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\) is strictly weaker than RH in the
implication direction: an off-line-zero configuration with subleading
\(2n\)-th moment contribution is logically compatible with
\(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\), so
\(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\not\Rightarrow\mathrm{RH}\).
(\Cref{v4-arith:hpar-det:rem:residual-vs-RH}.)
\end{claimnaive}

\begin{claimnaive}[Unconditional partial Carleman divergence via Bombieri--Lagarias]
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:claim-3}
The unconditional Bombieri--Lagarias upper bound
\(\mu_{2n}\le (2\pi)^{-4n}(2n)!(\log n)^{4n}\) combined with the
Conrey~1989 lower bound
\(\mu_{2n}\ge c_{\mathrm{Conrey}}(2\pi)^{-4n}(2n)!(\log n)^{-4n}\)
produces an unconditional Carleman sum lower bound of the shape
\(\sum_{n}(\log n)^{2}/\sqrt{n}\) which \emph{diverges}; hence
Carleman's determinacy criterion holds up to a logarithmic deficit.
(\Cref{v4-arith:hpar-det:prop:BL-carleman-partial},
\Cref{v4-arith:hpar-det:cor:unconditional-gap}.)
\end{claimnaive}

\begin{claimnaive}[Summary: classical-analytic, not RH-equivalent]
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:claim-paraphrase}
Combining \Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:claim-2} and
\Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:claim-3}, Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:hpar-det}
summarises that \(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\) is ``classical
analytic, not RH-equivalent'', because (a) the unconditional
Bombieri--Lagarias route gives a partial Carleman divergence without
RH, and (b) the remaining logarithmic gap between
\((\log n)^{4n}\) and \(1\) is an analytic-number-theory open
problem not RH-equivalent.
\end{claimnaive}

The following sections assess
\Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:claim-1}--\Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:claim-paraphrase}
independently.

\section{The Stirling Error in the Carleman Computation}
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:stirling-error}

\Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:claim-3} asserts that the unconditional
upper bound \(\mu_{2n}\le (2\pi)^{-4n}(2n)!(\log n)^{4n}\) yields a
Carleman sum lower bound \(\sum_{n}(\log n)^{2}/\sqrt{n}\) which
diverges. The computation contains two transcription errors. The
Stirling asymptotic gives \((2n)!^{1/(2n)}\sim 2n/e\), not
\(\sqrt{2n}\): the power grows linearly in \(n\), not as
\(n^{1/2}\). The factor \((\log n)^{4n}\) in the upper bound
on \(\mu_{2n}\) becomes \((\log n)^{+2}\) after taking the
\(1/(2n)\)-th power of \(\mu_{2n}\), hence \((\log n)^{-2}\) in
\(\mu_{2n}^{-1/(2n)}\): the sign of the logarithmic exponent
reverses under inversion. Both errors together replace the claimed
divergent sum \(\sum(\log n)^{2}/\sqrt{n}\) with the convergent
Bertrand series \(\sum 1/(n(\log n)^{2})\). The corrected computation
is carried out in \Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:carleman-BL-explicit}.

\section{Bombieri--Lagarias Bounds \(\lambda_{n}\), Not \(\mu_{2n}\)}
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:BL-lambda-not-mu}

Bombieri--Lagarias~1999
(\Cref{v4-arith:vb:thm:BL-bound}) bounds the Li coefficient
\(\lambda_{n}=\sum_{\rho}[1-(1-1/\rho)^{n}]\) by
\(\lambda_{n}\leq n(c_{0}+\log n)/2\). This is a bound on a highly
oscillatory unit-modulus phase sum: under RH, \(|1-1/\rho|=1\) for
every non-trivial zero \(\rho\), so \(\lambda_{n}\) grows
sub-polynomially via phase cancellation. The moment sum
\(\mu_{2n}=\sum_{\rho}(1/4+\gamma_{\rho}^{2})^{n}\) is a monotone
positive sum whose growth is governed by the density of zeros high
in the critical strip via Riemann--von Mangoldt, with no
cancellation. These two quantities are related only through
\(\mathrm{H\text{-}P}^{\mathrm{Ar}}\)-conditional spectral-trace
identities (\Cref{v4-arith:hpar-det:cycle-5}), not through
elementary norm bounds; a direct transfer of the Bombieri--Lagarias
bound to \(\mu_{2n}\) requires a phase-cancellation-to-magnitude
comparison that is not available. The upper bound on
\(\mu_{2n}\) in \Cref{v4-arith:hpar-det:prop:BL-carleman-partial}
comes from Riemann--von Mangoldt zero counting combined with
RH-conditional saddle-point evaluation, not from the
Bombieri--Lagarias Li-coefficient bound. Closing the
\((\log n)^{4n}\to 1\) gap requires density-of-zeros improvements
on the critical line beyond Conrey's 40\%, via the
Selberg--Levinson--Conrey--Feng programme.

\section{Position of \(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\) in the Equivalence Chain}
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:chain-position}

Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:HPAr-vbconverse}
(\Cref{v4-arith:hpvbconv:thm:VBstar-converse-spectral-regularity})
establishes the four-way equivalence
\[
(\mathrm{VB^{*}c})\;\Leftrightarrow\;(\mathrm{Det})\;\Leftrightarrow\;
(\mathrm{HB+})\;\Leftrightarrow\;(\mathrm{BD+})
\]
under P2, P3, and \(\mathrm{H\text{-}P}^{\mathrm{Ar}}\), where
\((\mathrm{VB^{*}c})\) is the \emph{converse} ``F-RH
\(\Rightarrow\) \(\mathrm{VB}^{*}\)'', not the main direction
\(\mathrm{VB}^{*}\Rightarrow\) F-RH. Determinacy is equivalent to
the VB\(^{*}\) \emph{converse}. Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:HPAr-vbconverse}
further records
(\Cref{v4-arith:hpvbconv:rem:strict-content},
\Cref{v4-arith:hpvbconv:cor:three-strictly-finer}) that all four
conditions are strictly finer than RH: in particular,
\(\mathrm{RH}\not\Rightarrow\mathrm{Det}\) without an additional
arithmetic input, and \(\mathrm{Det}\Rightarrow\mathrm{RH}\) requires
\(\mathrm{H\text{-}P}^{\mathrm{Ar}}\) plus the VB\(^{*}\) main direction
(\Cref{v4-arith:vb:thm:three-equivalences}). Under P2, P3,
\(\mathrm{H\text{-}P}^{\mathrm{Ar}}\), one has
\(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\Rightarrow\mathrm{Det}
\Rightarrow(\mathrm{VB^{*}c})\), placing
\(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\) as an input to the converse
direction, not to the main direction and not to RH directly.
Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:hpar-det}'s characterisation of
\(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\) as not RH-equivalent in the
implication direction is therefore correct, but the reason is not
that the statement is classical: it is that determinacy sits
strictly above RH in the implication hierarchy, as established by
Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:HPAr-vbconverse}.

\section{Carleman Sum from the Bombieri--Lagarias Upper Bound: Corrected Computation}
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:carleman-BL-explicit}

\subsection{Upper Bound on \(\mu_{2n}\) via Riemann--von Mangoldt}
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:upper-bound}

Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:hpar-det:prop:BL-carleman-partial} proves,
via Riemann--von Mangoldt zero-counting and saddle-point evaluation,
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:eq:upper-bound}
\mu_{2n}\;\leq\;\frac{(2n)!}{(2\pi)^{4n}}\cdot(\log n)^{4n}\cdot(1+o(1)).
\end{equation}
The saddle-point derivation is standard and is not disputed here.

\subsection{Corrected Stirling Evaluation}
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:corrected-stirling}

\begin{lemma}[Stirling for \((2n)!^{1/(2n)}\)]
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:lem:stirling}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere. By Stirling's formula
\(n!=\sqrt{2\pi n}\,(n/e)^{n}(1+O(1/n))\),
\begin{equation}
(2n)!^{1/(2n)}\;=\;\frac{2n}{e}\cdot(4\pi n)^{1/(4n)}\cdot(1+O(1/n)).
\end{equation}
Since \((4\pi n)^{1/(4n)}\to 1\) as \(n\to\infty\), one has
\((2n)!^{1/(2n)}\sim 2n/e\), i.e., the \((2n)!^{1/(2n)}\) power
grows \emph{linearly} in \(n\), not as \(\sqrt{n}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\((2n)!=\sqrt{4\pi n}\,(2n/e)^{2n}(1+O(1/n))\) gives
\begin{align*}
(2n)!^{1/(2n)}
&=\bigl[\sqrt{4\pi n}\,(2n/e)^{2n}\bigr]^{1/(2n)}\cdot(1+O(1/n))^{1/(2n)}\\
&=(4\pi n)^{1/(4n)}\cdot(2n/e)\cdot\bigl(1+O(1/n^{2})\bigr).
\end{align*}
Since \((4\pi n)^{1/(4n)}=\exp(\tfrac{1}{4n}\log(4\pi n))
=1+\tfrac{\log(4\pi n)}{4n}+O(n^{-2}(\log n)^{2})\to 1\), the claim
follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[corrected Carleman-sum lower bound from BL upper bound]
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:prop:BL-carleman-corrected}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere. From
\Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:eq:upper-bound},
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:eq:mu-power}
\mu_{2n}^{1/(2n)}\;\leq\;
\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\cdot(2n)!^{1/(2n)}\cdot(\log n)^{2}\cdot(1+o(1))
\;\sim\;\frac{2n}{e(2\pi)^{2}}\cdot(\log n)^{2}.
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:eq:mu-inverse-lower}
\mu_{2n}^{-1/(2n)}\;\geq\;
\frac{e(2\pi)^{2}}{2n(\log n)^{2}}\cdot(1-o(1))
\;=\;\frac{c_{\mathrm{BL}}}{n(\log n)^{2}}
\end{equation}
for a constant \(c_{\mathrm{BL}}>0\). Summing,
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:eq:carleman-bound}
\sum_{n\geq 2}\mu_{2n}^{-1/(2n)}\;\geq\;
c_{\mathrm{BL}}\cdot\sum_{n\geq 2}\frac{1}{n(\log n)^{2}}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Direct substitution of \Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:lem:stirling} into
the \(1/(2n)\)-th power of the upper bound
\Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:eq:upper-bound}. The factor \((\log n)^{4n}\)
inside the bound becomes \((\log n)^{+2}\) after the
\(1/(2n)\)-th-root, and when inverted for \(\mu_{2n}^{-1/(2n)}\) becomes
\((\log n)^{-2}\). The factor \((2\pi)^{-4n}\) becomes \((2\pi)^{-2}\)
and when inverted becomes \((2\pi)^{+2}\). The factor
\((2n)!^{1/(2n)}\sim 2n/e\) in the numerator of \(\mu_{2n}^{1/(2n)}\)
becomes \(e/(2n)\) in \(\mu_{2n}^{-1/(2n)}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[the BL Carleman route is \emph{inconclusive}, not divergent]
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:cor:inconclusive}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere. The lower bound on the Carleman sum from
\Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:prop:BL-carleman-corrected} is
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:eq:bertrand}
\sum_{n\geq 2}\frac{1}{n(\log n)^{2}}\;<\;+\infty,
\end{equation}
a classical Bertrand series which \emph{converges}. Hence the
lower bound on \(\sum_{n}\mu_{2n}^{-1/(2n)}\) produced by the BL
upper bound on \(\mu_{2n}\) is finite. This does \emph{not} prove
the Carleman sum diverges; it is strictly inconclusive.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The Bertrand series \(\sum_{n\geq 2}1/(n(\log n)^{\alpha})\)
converges iff \(\alpha>1\), by the integral comparison
\(\int_{2}^{\infty}dx/(x(\log x)^{\alpha})
=(\alpha-1)^{-1}(\log 2)^{-(\alpha-1)}\) for \(\alpha>1\). For
\(\alpha=2\) this integral is finite, confirming convergence.

A lower bound on \(\sum \mu_{2n}^{-1/(2n)}\) which is itself finite
does not establish divergence of \(\sum \mu_{2n}^{-1/(2n)}\). The
Carleman sum may still diverge or converge; the BL upper bound on
\(\mu_{2n}\) with the \((\log n)^{4n}\) log-factor is simply too
weak to decide.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Source of the Stirling error in \Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:claim-3}]
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:rem:delta-error}
\Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:claim-3} asserts
\(\sum \mu_{2n}^{-1/(2n)}\geq c'\sum 2\pi(\log n)/\sqrt{2n}\). This
expression has \(\sqrt{n}\) in the denominator instead of \(n\), and
\((\log n)^{+1}\) in the numerator instead of \((\log n)^{-2}\). The
resulting sum \(\sum (\log n)/\sqrt{n}\) diverges, but the
transcription from \(\mu_{2n}^{-1/(2n)}\) via Stirling should
produce \(1/(n(\log n)^{2})\) per \Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:lem:stirling}
and power-rearrangement of \((\log n)^{4n}\), giving a convergent
Bertrand series. The source is the approximation
\((2n)!^{1/(2n)}\sim\sqrt{2n}\) instead of \(\sim 2n/e\): a
confusion between Stirling's formula and the incorrect
root-of-factorial approximation \(n!^{1/n}\sim\sqrt{n}\); the
correct asymptotic is \(n!^{1/n}\sim n/e\).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Carleman divergence under the RH-heuristic asymptotic \(\mu_{2n}\asymp C^{n}(n!)^{2}\)]
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:rem:heuristic-saves}
If one substitutes the RH-heuristic two-sided asymptotic
\(\mu_{2n}\asymp C^{n}(n!)^{2}\) (no \((\log n)^{4n}\) correction) of
\Cref{v4-arith:hpvbconv:prop:carleman-insufficient}, then
\(\mu_{2n}^{1/(2n)}\asymp C^{1/2}\cdot n/e\) giving
\(\mu_{2n}^{-1/(2n)}\asymp e/(C^{1/2}n)\) and the Carleman sum
\emph{does} diverge at the harmonic rate \(\sum 1/n\). But this
heuristic is \emph{not} established unconditionally; the
BL-plus-Conrey machinery has the \((\log n)^{4n}\) gap
(\Cref{v4-arith:hpar-det:cor:unconditional-gap}) which puts the
true sum into the log-convergent regime of
\Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:cor:inconclusive}. Hence Carleman's
criterion from BL-plus-Conrey alone is inconclusive; the
classical-heuristic form is what requires unconditional
confirmation.
\end{remark}

\section{Sharpening the \((\log n)^{4n}\) Gap Requires Zero-Density Hypotheses}
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:gap-analysis}

\subsection{The Bombieri--Lagarias Polylog Bound Does Not Sharpen to \(\mu_{2n}\asymp n!^{2}\) by Classical Analysis}
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:can-BL-sharpen}

\begin{proposition}[Bombieri--Lagarias bounds \(\lambda_{n}\); \(\mu_{2n}\) is governed by Riemann--von Mangoldt]
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:prop:BL-vs-mu}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere. Bombieri--Lagarias Theorem~2
(\Cref{v4-arith:vb:thm:BL-bound}) bounds
\(\lambda_{n}=\sum_{\rho}[1-(1-1/\rho)^{n}]\), a heavily
oscillatory unit-modulus-phase sum in \(\rho\). Under RH, \(|1-1/\rho|=1\)
for every non-trivial zero, so the Bombieri--Lagarias bound is a
statement about phase-cancellation. The moment sum
\(\mu_{2n}=\sum_{\rho}(1/4+\gamma_{\rho}^{2})^{n}\) is a monotone
positive sum (under RH) whose growth is governed by the density of
zeros high in the critical line via Riemann--von Mangoldt. The two
quantities are related through
\(\mathrm{H\text{-}P}^{\mathrm{Ar}}\)-conditional spectral-trace
identities, not through elementary norm bounds.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Bombieri--Lagarias bound gives
\(\lambda_{n}\geq-(n/2)\log n-O(n)\)
(\Cref{v4-arith:vb:thm:BL-bound}): polylog growth. A polylog bound
on \(\lambda_{n}\) carries no information about \(\mu_{2n}\):
\(\lambda_{n}\) grows via phase cancellation while \(\mu_{2n}\) is a
monotone positive sum with no cancellation. The identity
\(\mathrm{tr}(T_{n})=n\lambda_{n}\) holds only at the trace level
under \(\mathrm{H\text{-}P}^{\mathrm{Ar}}\)
(\Cref{v4-arith:hpar-det:cycle-5}).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[sharpening the unconditional \(\mu_{2n}\) upper bound from
\((\log n)^{4n}\) to \((\log n)^{o(n)}\) requires zero-density improvements]
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:prop:closing-gap-is-density-RH}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere. The \((\log n)^{4n}\) factor in
\Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:eq:upper-bound} comes from the
Conrey-style fluctuation envelope in the RvM zero-counting:
\(N(T)\sim (T/2\pi)\log(T/2\pi)\) with second-order error
\(O(\log T)\), so that the integral representation of \(\mu_{2n}\)
picks up a \((\log T)^{\cdot}\) factor at the saddle cutoff
\(T\sim\sqrt{n}\log n\). Removing this factor to obtain
\(\mu_{2n}\leq C^{n}n!^{2}\) \emph{with no logarithmic inflation}
requires improving the zero-counting asymptotic from the
unconditional \(N(T)=(T/2\pi)\log(T/2\pi)-T/2\pi+O(\log T)\) to a
form where the error term does not amplify by a factor of \(\log T\)
at each moment-integral saddle. This is a zero-density-hypothesis
improvement, a classical analytic-number-theory programme that is
currently open and is not known to follow from RH alone.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The RvM estimate \(N(T)=(T/2\pi)\log(T/2\pi)-T/2\pi+O(\log T)\)
(Edwards~1974 Thm.~6.7) has error \(O(\log T)\). In the saddle-point
evaluation \(\mu_{2n}\approx \int_{0}^{\infty}t^{2n}(dN/dt)dt\), the
saddle is at \(t\sim\sqrt{n}\log n\). The error term \(O(\log T)\)
evaluated at the saddle contributes a factor of
\(O(\log(\sqrt{n}\log n))\sim O(\log n)\) at the saddle, and the
\(n\)-fold application (from the \(n\)-th moment) amplifies this to
\(O((\log n)^{4n})\) in the final bound (the factor of 4 comes from
the \(\mu_{2n}\) being the \(2n\)-th moment, with two such
amplifications in the saddle-point evaluation and two from the
\(t^{2}\) factor).

The relevant fluctuation is
\(S(T)=\tfrac{1}{\pi}\arg\zeta(\tfrac12+iT)\), the oscillating part of
\(N(T)=\tfrac{T}{2\pi}\log\tfrac{T}{2\pi e}+\tfrac78+S(T)+O(1/T)\);
unconditionally \(S(T)=O(\log T)\) (von Mangoldt--Backlund), and this is
precisely the error that the \(n\)-fold saddle amplifies. Reducing the
amplified contribution to \((\log n)^{o(n)}\) cannot be achieved by
sharpening this pointwise bound: \(S(T)\) is provably unbounded
(Selberg~1946), so no \(O(1)\) error term for \(N(T)\) exists. Removing
the inflation instead requires zero-density control on the zeros high
in the critical strip (a density-hypothesis-type input in the sense
of Bombieri~1965) or a cancellation in the regularised moment
integral that the crude magnitude bound discards. Either is a major
open analytic-number-theory problem, adjacent to RH in depth.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[classical-analytic form, RH-adjacent difficulty]
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:rem:form-vs-difficulty}
\Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:prop:closing-gap-is-density-RH} establishes
the key distinction: the \emph{form} of
\(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\) is classical-analytic (a moment
asymptotic), but its \emph{difficulty} is RH-adjacent. The gap
between BL-Conrey's polylog-inflated bound and the target
\(C^{n}n!^{2}\) bound is precisely the density-of-zeros hypothesis for
\(\zeta\), which is a major open problem on the same level as RH.
Density-of-zeros is strictly weaker than RH (density-1-hypothesis
does not imply all zeros are on the line, only that exceptional
zeros are sparse), so \(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\not\Rightarrow\)RH
is consistent, but \(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\) is not known
unconditionally either.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Three Independent Axes: Form, Implication, Difficulty}
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:axis-distinction}

\Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:claim-paraphrase}'s conflation of classical
form with classical difficulty is resolved by separating three axes.

\begin{description}
\item[Form.] The statement of \(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\) is
the moment asymptotic
\(C_{-}^{n}n!^{2}\leq\mu_{2n}\leq C_{+}^{n}n!^{2}\): an object
in classical analysis, a moment sequence controlled by
saddle-point asymptotics.
\item[Implication direction.] \(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}
\not\Rightarrow\mathrm{RH}\): off-line zeros with subleading
\(2n\)-th moment contribution are consistent with
\(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\). The converse
\(\mathrm{RH}\Rightarrow\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\) holds
partially under regularisation: Riemann--von Mangoldt plus
saddle-point gives \(\mu_{2n}\asymp (2\pi)^{-4n}(2n)!\) up to
logarithmic corrections, which by Stirling is \(C^{n}n!^{2}\) up
to the density-hypothesis refinements required to remove the
\((\log n)^{4n}\) factor.
\item[Difficulty.] The remaining gap
(\Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:prop:closing-gap-is-density-RH}) is the
density-of-zeros hypothesis for \(\zeta\), a major open programme
of analytic number theory at the level of the density-1 hypothesis,
strictly weaker than RH in its conclusion but adjacent to RH in depth.
\end{description}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{|l|l|l|l|}
\hline
 & \textbf{Form} & \textbf{Implication to RH} & \textbf{Difficulty} \\
\hline
\(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\) & Classical-analytic & Does not imply RH & RH-adjacent \\
 & (moment asymptotic) & (weaker than RH in & (density-of-zeros \\
 & & one direction) & open programme) \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:claim-paraphrase} infers from the classical
form that the difficulty is classical; this conflates the first and
third axes. The implication axis (\Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:claim-2})
is correct: \(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\) is strictly weaker
than RH in the implication direction. The difficulty axis is
RH-adjacent.

\section{Determinacy, \(\mathrm{VB}^{*}\), and RH: The Correct Equivalence Chain}
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:chain-audit}

The two propositions below establish the correct form of the two
equivalences in the chain
\(\mathrm{Det}\Leftrightarrow\mathrm{VB}^{*}\text{ main direction}\Leftrightarrow\mathrm{RH}\).

\subsection{Step 1: Determinacy \(\Leftrightarrow\) VB\(^{*}\) (Which Direction?)}
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:step-1}

\begin{proposition}[Determinacy is equivalent to the \(\mathrm{VB}^{*}\) converse]
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:prop:det-converse-not-main}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere.
\Cref{v4-arith:hpvbconv:thm:VBstar-converse-spectral-regularity}
asserts
\[
(\mathrm{VB^{*}c})\;\Leftrightarrow\;(\mathrm{Det})\;\Leftrightarrow\;
(\mathrm{HB+})\;\Leftrightarrow\;(\mathrm{BD+})
\]
under P2, P3, \(\mathrm{H\text{-}P}^{\mathrm{Ar}}\), where
\((\mathrm{VB^{*}c})\) is ``F-RH \(\Rightarrow\) \(\mathrm{VB}^{*}\)''.
The main direction \(\mathrm{VB}^{*}\Rightarrow\) F-RH is the content
of \Cref{v4-arith:vb:thm:three-equivalences}, a separate conditional
implication not equivalent to determinacy.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{v4-arith:hpvbconv:rem:converse-does-not-help} records:
``The forward direction \(\mathrm{VB}^{*}\Rightarrow\mathrm{F\text{-}RH}\)
is the separate content of \Cref{v4-arith:rh:thm:VB-implies-F-RH},
which is a conditional theorem under P2, P3,
\(\mathrm{H\text{-}P}^{\mathrm{Ar}}\), and a positive Bost--Connes
regularised trace. The four-way equivalence of the converse does not
close the forward implication.'' Determinacy is equivalent to the
converse direction.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Step 2: Determinacy vs RH}
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:step-2}

\begin{proposition}[Determinacy is strictly finer than RH]
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:prop:det-strictly-finer}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere.
\Cref{v4-arith:hpvbconv:cor:three-strictly-finer} asserts that each
of \(\mathrm{VB}^{*}\)-converse, determinacy, and
Ba\'ez-Duarte sharp decay is strictly finer than RH. Determinacy is
strictly stronger than RH: \(\mathrm{Det}\Rightarrow\mathrm{RH}\)
holds only when combined with \(\mathrm{H\text{-}P}^{\mathrm{Ar}}\)
(\Cref{v4-arith:hpvbconv:thm:HPAr-implies-reduction}), and
\(\mathrm{RH}\Rightarrow\mathrm{Det}\) is not proved from RH alone.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{v4-arith:hpvbconv:rem:strict-content}: ``No structural
property of the zero distribution of \(\xi\) independent of RH is
known to force determinacy. Hence the \(\mathrm{VB}^{*}\) converse
requires an additional arithmetic input beyond RH.'' Hence
\(\mathrm{RH}\not\Rightarrow\mathrm{Det}\). The reverse implication
\(\mathrm{Det}\Rightarrow\mathrm{RH}\) requires
\(\mathrm{H\text{-}P}^{\mathrm{Ar}}\) plus the \(\mathrm{VB}^{*}\)
main direction (\Cref{v4-arith:vb:thm:three-equivalences}); the
two conditions are logically independent without auxiliary
hypotheses.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Correct Implication Diagram}
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:corrected-chain}

\begin{remark}[Position of \(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\) in the implication hierarchy]
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:diag:chain}
Under P2, P3, \(\mathrm{H\text{-}P}^{\mathrm{Ar}}\):
\begin{center}
\(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\;\Longrightarrow\;\text{(Carleman criterion)}\;\Longrightarrow\;\mathrm{Det}\;\Longleftrightarrow\;(\mathrm{VB}^{*}\text{ converse})\)
\end{center}
Independently, as a conditional theorem
(\Cref{v4-arith:vb:thm:three-equivalences}):
\begin{center}
\(\mathrm{VB}^{*}\text{ main direction}\;\Longrightarrow\;\mathrm{RH}.\)
\end{center}
Combined (\Cref{v4-arith:hpvbconv:thm:HPAr-implies-reduction}):
\begin{center}
\(\mathrm{H\text{-}P}^{\mathrm{Ar}}+\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}+\mathrm{VB}^{*}\text{ main direction}\;\Longrightarrow\;\mathrm{VB}^{*}\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\mathrm{RH}+\mathrm{Det}.\)
\end{center}
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[\(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\) closes the converse, not the forward]
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:rem:closes-converse}
In the corrected diagram, \(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\) contributes
to the VB\(^{*}\) \emph{converse} direction (RH\(\Rightarrow\)VB\(^{*}\)),
by forcing Hamburger determinacy which together with H-P\(^{\mathrm{Ar}}\)
and RH closes VB\(^{*}\). \(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\) does not
by itself close the VB\(^{*}\) \emph{main} direction nor RH. The
main-direction closure requires VB\(^{*}\) proper (as hypothesis) or
some alternative route to the Li positivity, which is not supplied
by \(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\).
\end{remark}

\section{Verdict}
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:verdict}

\begin{theorem}[Assessment of the four claims of
Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:hpar-det} on \(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\)]
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:thm:verdict}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere. The four claims
\Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:claim-1}--\Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:claim-paraphrase}
of Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:hpar-det} have the following status.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textbf{(1)}] \emph{Form}
(\Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:claim-1}): \(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\)
has classical-analytic form, a two-sided moment asymptotic. This
claim is accurate.
\item[\textbf{(2)}] \emph{Strict weakness}
(\Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:claim-2}):
\(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\not\Rightarrow\mathrm{RH}\). This
claim is accurate; the converse
\(\mathrm{RH}\Rightarrow\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\) also
requires density-of-zeros refinements beyond RH alone.
\item[\textbf{(3)}] \emph{Unconditional partial Carleman divergence}
(\Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:claim-3}): the Bombieri--Lagarias--Conrey
route gives a Carleman sum lower bound
\(\sum(\log n)/\sqrt{n}\), which diverges. This claim contains a
Stirling error.
\Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:prop:BL-carleman-corrected} shows the
correct lower bound is \(\sum 1/(n(\log n)^{2})\), a Bertrand series
that converges, hence the Bombieri--Lagarias route is inconclusive,
not divergent. The corrected statement is
\Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:cor:inconclusive}.
\item[\textbf{(4)}] \emph{Summary paraphrase}
(\Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:claim-paraphrase}): ``classical-analytic,
not RH-equivalent.'' The implication-direction component (not
RH-equivalent) is accurate. The difficulty component
(``classical-analytic'') conflates form with difficulty; the actual
difficulty, closing \((\log n)^{4n}\to 1\) via density-of-zeros
improvements, is RH-adjacent, not classical-elementary.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(1) is \Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:axis-distinction}: the statement is a
moment asymptotic.

(2) is \Cref{v4-arith:hpar-det:rem:residual-vs-RH} combined with
the off-line-zeros robustness argument.

(3) is \Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:prop:BL-carleman-corrected} and
\Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:cor:inconclusive}: the Stirling identity
\((2n)!^{1/(2n)}\sim 2n/e\) (not \(\sqrt{2n}\)) changes the lower
bound from the divergent series \(\sum(\log n)/\sqrt{n}\) to the
convergent Bertrand series \(\sum 1/(n(\log n)^{2})\).

(4) is \Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:rem:form-vs-difficulty} and
\Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:prop:closing-gap-is-density-RH}: closing the
logarithmic gap requires density-of-zeros improvements which,
while strictly weaker than RH, constitute a major open programme of
analytic number theory.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[\(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\) is genuinely weaker than RH]
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:cor:hypothesis-also-wrong}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere. The correct equivalences in the chain
\(\mathrm{Det}\Leftrightarrow\mathrm{VB}^{*}\text{ main direction}
\Leftrightarrow\mathrm{RH}\) are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Determinacy is equivalent to the \(\mathrm{VB}^{*}\)
\emph{converse}, not the main direction
(\Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:prop:det-converse-not-main}).
\item Determinacy is strictly finer than RH; the two conditions are
logically independent without auxiliary hypotheses
(\Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:prop:det-strictly-finer}).
\end{enumerate}
Hence \(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\) is genuinely weaker than RH
at the implication level.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{v4-arith:vb:thm:three-equivalences} and
\Cref{v4-arith:hpvbconv:thm:VBstar-converse-spectral-regularity}
are unambiguous on both the direction of the equivalence and the
strict finer-ness of determinacy above RH.
\end{proof}

\section{The Sharp Reduction of \(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\)}
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:honest-reduction}

\begin{theorem}[Sharp reduction of \(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\)]
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:thm:honest-reduction}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere. The sharp moment asymptotic
\(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}:\mu_{2n}\asymp C^{n}n!^{2}\)
unconditionally has the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Form}: classical-analytic; \(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\)
is a two-sided moment asymptotic.
\item \emph{Position in the implication hierarchy}: under
P2, P3, \(\mathrm{H\text{-}P}^{\mathrm{Ar}}\),
\(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\Rightarrow\mathrm{Det}
\Leftrightarrow\mathrm{VB}^{*}\) converse; does not imply RH
directly.
\item \emph{Implication to RH}:
\(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\not\Rightarrow\mathrm{RH}\);
strictly weaker in the implication direction.
\item \emph{Difficulty}: closing the unconditional
\((\log n)^{4n}\) gap requires density-of-zeros hypotheses for
\(\zeta\), an open analytic-number-theory programme of RH-adjacent
difficulty, technically strictly weaker than RH.
\item \emph{Unconditional Carleman route via
Bombieri--Lagarias and Conrey}: inconclusive; the correct
Bertrand-series lower bound \(\sum 1/(n(\log n)^{2})\) converges
(\Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:cor:inconclusive}).
\end{enumerate}
\(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\) is classical in form, strictly
weaker than RH in the implication direction, and RH-adjacent in
difficulty. The summary of Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:hpar-det}
(``classical-analytic, not RH-equivalent'') is accurate on the form
and implication axes and inaccurate on the difficulty axis.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(1) is \Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:axis-distinction}.
(2) is \Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:diag:chain}.
(3) is \Cref{v4-arith:hpar-det:rem:residual-vs-RH}.
(4) is \Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:prop:closing-gap-is-density-RH}.
(5) is \Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:cor:inconclusive}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Precise statement of the residual]
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:rem:what-delta-should-say}
\(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\) is an unconditional two-sided
moment asymptotic at the \(n!^{2}\) rate. Its form is
classical-analytic. Its implication relative to RH is strictly
weaker in one direction. Its difficulty is RH-adjacent: closing the
unconditional Bombieri--Lagarias--Conrey gap requires
zero-density-hypothesis-level improvements. Carleman's criterion
via the Bombieri--Lagarias--Conrey bounds alone is inconclusive; a
genuine Carleman divergence requires either density-of-zeros
improvements or the unconditional two-sided asymptotic
\(\mu_{2n}\asymp C^{n}n!^{2}\) without logarithmic inflation. The
remaining work is zero-density analytic number theory.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the correction to
Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:hpar-det}]
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:rem:not-downgrade}
The three-residual decomposition \(\mathrm{W}+\mathrm{H}+
\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\) of
\Cref{v4-arith:hpar-det:cor:wave3-structure} survives intact. Two
items are corrected: the Stirling error in
\Cref{v4-arith:hpar-det:prop:BL-carleman-partial}, which does not
affect the validity of the reduction but does affect the
unconditional-Carleman-divergence claim; and the difficulty
framing of \(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\) as
``classical-analytic'', which is accurate for the form and the
implication direction but inaccurate for the difficulty. The weight
selection (W) and Weil formula at Gaussian test functions (H)
components remain intact. The single correction is the difficulty
assessment of \(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\).
\end{remark}

\section{Conclusions}
\label{v4-arith:r-audit:summary}

\begin{enumerate}
\item The characterisation of \(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\) as
``classical-analytic, not RH-equivalent''
(\Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:thm:verdict}) is accurate on the form axis
and the implication axis and inaccurate on the difficulty axis.
The form is classical-analytic; the implication is strictly weaker
than RH; but the difficulty is RH-adjacent via the density-of-zeros
programme.
\item The unconditional Carleman-divergence argument via
Bombieri--Lagarias and Conrey contains a Stirling error
(\Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:cor:inconclusive}). The correct lower bound
on the Carleman sum is the Bertrand series \(\sum 1/(n(\log n)^{2})\),
which converges. The Bombieri--Lagarias route is inconclusive.
\item The correct form of the chain
\(\mathrm{Det}\Leftrightarrow\mathrm{VB}^{*}\text{ main direction}
\Leftrightarrow\mathrm{RH}\) is given by
\Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:cor:hypothesis-also-wrong}: determinacy is
equivalent to the \(\mathrm{VB}^{*}\) converse
(\Cref{v4-arith:hpvbconv:thm:VBstar-converse-spectral-regularity}),
and determinacy is strictly finer than RH.
\item \Cref{v4-arith:r-audit:thm:honest-reduction} separates three
axes: form (classical), implication direction (strictly weaker than
RH), and difficulty (RH-adjacent via density-of-zeros).
\(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\) is the single analytic residual of
the minimal path; its closure is a genuine open problem of analytic
number theory at the zero-density level.
\item The three-residual decomposition
\(\mathrm{W}+\mathrm{H}+\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\) of
\Cref{v4-arith:hpar-det:cor:wave3-structure} remains the tightest
reduction of the Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:rh-reformulation} completion path. The
Stirling error in the Carleman argument and the difficulty framing
are corrected; the structural decomposition stands.
\item Closing \(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\) requires either
(a) a density-of-zeros improvement pushing the Conrey lower bound
from 40\% of zeros on the line to the density-1 hypothesis with
sub-logarithmic error, or (b) an alternative positivity or
measure-regularisation route producing
\(\mu_{2n}\asymp C^{n}n!^{2}\) directly. Either route is a
substantive analytic-number-theory programme. The Vol~IV minimal
path to RH rests on three open problems of comparable difficulty
(W, H, \(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\)).
\end{enumerate}

The correction of Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:hpar-det}'s reduction
tightens but does not overturn it. The residual identification is
correct; the difficulty classification and one arithmetic step are
corrected. All three residuals are genuine open problems of
substantive analytic-number-theory difficulty, and
\(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\) specifically is a density-of-zeros
statement strictly weaker than RH but adjacent to it in difficulty.
