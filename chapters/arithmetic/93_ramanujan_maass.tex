\chapter[Ramanujan for Maass Forms: Sharpening \(7/64\) and a Sub-RH Conditional][Ramanujan for Maass forms]{Ramanujan for Maass Forms: Sharpening \(7/64\) and a Sub-RH Conditional}
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:chapter}

\section{Domain and Target}
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:domain}

Fix the target. For \(\mathrm{GL}_{2}/\mathbb{Q}\) with trivial central
character, a Maass cuspidal Hecke eigenform \(\phi\) at level~\(1\) has
local Satake parameters \((\alpha_{p}(\phi),\beta_{p}(\phi))\) with
\(\alpha_{p}\beta_{p}=1\) and
\(\theta_{p}(\phi):=\max(\log|\alpha_{p}|,\log|\beta_{p}|)/\log p\geq 0\).
The Ramanujan conjecture asserts \(\theta_{p}(\phi)=0\) for all \(p\) and
all cuspidal \(\phi\). The current best unconditional bound is
Kim--Sarnak 2003 Appendix~2 to \emph{J.~Amer.~Math.~Soc.}~16,
\(\sup_{p}\theta_{p}(\phi)\leq 7/64=0.109375\), established via the
symmetric-fourth-power lift \(\mathrm{sym}^{4}\phi\) on
\(\mathrm{GL}_{5}/\mathbb{Q}\) of Kim 2003 \emph{J.~Amer.~Math.~Soc.}~16
combined with a Luo--Rudnick--Sarnak 1995 \emph{Geom.~Funct.~Anal.}~5
\(L\)-function convexity argument.

\textbf{Target.} Compare the attempted sub-\(7/64\)
unconditional construction through higher symmetric-power functoriality
(Newton--Thorne 2021 \emph{Publ.~Math.~IHES}~134; Clozel--Harris--Taylor
2008 \emph{Publ.~Math.~IHES}~108 as prerequisite automorphy-of-lifts).
The unconditional output remains Kim--Sarnak \(7/64\); the sharper
bound is conditional on Maass symmetric-power functoriality, and full
Ramanujan is conditional on an explicit automorphic Ramanujan input.

\textbf{Domain restriction.} The chapter treats level~\(1\) Maass forms
on \(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H}\) with trivial
nebentypus; extension to arbitrary level and nebentypus follows the same argument
and is not used later. All
stated local Satake bounds are uniform in the archimedean parameter
of \(\phi\), in the sense of Iwaniec 2002 \S14.4.

\section{Construction~A: Kim--Shahidi Symmetric-Cube and the \(\mathbf{1/9}\) Baseline}
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:construction-A}

\subsection{The Symmetric-Cube Lift}
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:construction-A:sym3}

\begin{theorem}[Kim--Shahidi: \(\theta\leq1/9\)]
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:thm:KS-1-9}
For the level-one Maass form \(\phi\) fixed above,
\(\sup_p\theta_p(\phi)\leq1/9\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write \(\pi\) for the unitary cuspidal automorphic representation
attached to \(\phi\). Kim--Shahidi,
\emph{Functorial products for \(\mathrm{GL}_2\times\mathrm{GL}_3\)
and the symmetric cube for \(\mathrm{GL}_2\)},
Ann.~Math.~155 (2002), Theorem~6.1, constructs
\(\operatorname{Sym}^3\pi\) on \(\mathrm{GL}_4\).
This lift is cuspidal outside the dihedral and tetrahedral cases.
If it is cuspidal, Kim--Shahidi,
\emph{Cuspidality of symmetric powers with applications},
Duke Math.~J.~112 (2002), Proposition~4.8, gives
\[
|\alpha_p|,|\beta_p|<p^{1/9}
\]
at every unramified prime. All finite primes are unramified in the
level-one domain. Taking logarithms gives the stated bound.
The dihedral and tetrahedral cases are tempered, so their
exponents are zero. In the dihedral case this follows from
unitary automorphic induction of a character. In the tetrahedral
case it follows from the unitary Weil parameter described in
Proposition~3.3.8 of the same Duke paper.

Proposition~4.8 is the analytic input for this argument.
Its proof uses the symmetric-ninth \(L\)-function in
Proposition~4.7 and Shahidi's local estimate
(1988, Lemma~5.8). These analytic properties do not assert
cuspidal automorphy of \(\operatorname{Sym}^9\pi\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{General and Refined Symmetric-Power Bounds}
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:construction-A:delay}

For a unitary cuspidal representation \(\Pi\) of \(\mathrm{GL}_n/\mathbb Q\),
the Luo--Rudnick--Sarnak estimate bounds its unramified Satake
exponents by \(1/2-1/(n^2+1)\).
Jacquet--Shalika's local generic-unitary estimate instead gives
exponents strictly below \(1/2\).
The symmetric-power eigenvalues of a two-dimensional Satake
parameter are
\[
\alpha_p^m,\ \alpha_p^{m-1}\beta_p,\ \ldots,\ \beta_p^m.
\]
Since \(\alpha_p\beta_p=1\), their largest absolute logarithmic
exponent is \(m\theta_p(\phi)\).
When \(\operatorname{Sym}^3\pi\) is cuspidal, the general estimate gives
\[
\theta_p(\phi)\leq\frac{1/2-1/17}{3}=\frac5{34}.
\]
When \(\operatorname{Sym}^4\pi\) is cuspidal, it gives
\[
\theta_p(\phi)\leq\frac{1/2-1/26}{4}=\frac3{26}.
\]
The latter upper bound is weaker than \(1/9\), since
\(3/26-1/9=1/234>0\).
The \(1/9\) and \(7/64\) estimates therefore require the more
specific analytic inputs, beyond this general rank bound.

\section{Construction~B: Kim's Symmetric Fourth and the \(\mathbf{7/64}\) Bound}
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:construction-B}

\begin{theorem}[Kim--Sarnak: \(\theta\leq7/64\)]
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:thm:KS-7-64}
For the level-one Maass form \(\phi\) fixed above,
\(\sup_p\theta_p(\phi)\leq7/64=0.109375\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Kim's symmetric-fourth transfer constructs
\(\Pi=\operatorname{Sym}^4\pi\) on \(\mathrm{GL}_5\).
It is cuspidal outside the dihedral, tetrahedral, and octahedral
cases by Kim--Shahidi, Duke Math.~J.~112 (2002), Theorem~3.3.7.
In the cuspidal case, the Kim--Sarnak refinement in Appendix~2
to Kim, J.~Amer.~Math.~Soc.~16 (2003), gives
\[
\max_j\bigl|\log_p|\alpha_j(\Pi_p)|\bigr|
\leq\frac12-\frac1{1+5\cdot6/2}=\frac7{16}.
\]
This is the degree-five case of the refined estimate recorded in
Sarnak, \emph{Notes on the generalized Ramanujan conjectures},
equations~(21)--(24), pp.~666--667.
Its symmetric-square \(L\)-function argument has a smaller analytic
conductor than the general Rankin--Selberg argument.
The extremal symmetric-fourth Satake eigenvalue has exponent
\(4\theta_p(\phi)\). Division by four gives \(7/64\).
The three exceptional types are tempered, and hence satisfy the
same estimate with exponent zero.

The exterior-square construction involved in the transfer has
rank six. Let \(V=\mathbb C^2\) be the standard algebraic
representation of \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb C)\). Then
\[
\Lambda^2\operatorname{Sym}^3V
\cong(\operatorname{Sym}^4V\otimes\det V)\oplus(\det V)^3.
\]
For diagonal eigenvalues \(a,b\), the six weights on the left are
\(a^5b,a^4b^2,a^3b^3,a^3b^3,a^2b^4,ab^5\).
These are exactly the five weights of
\(\operatorname{Sym}^4V\otimes\det V\) and the weight of
\((\det V)^3\). Complete reducibility over \(\mathbb C\) gives
the displayed representation identity.
With trivial central character, this is the identity of unramified
Satake representations underlying
\(\Lambda^2(\operatorname{Sym}^3\pi)
=\operatorname{Sym}^4\pi\boxplus\mathbf1\).
The global transfer is the separate automorphy theorem used above.
The exterior square of \(\operatorname{Sym}^2V\) has rank three
and is a different representation.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[analytic estimates for the symmetric fourth]
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:rem:sym4-sharpness}
An analytic refinement through
\(L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{4}\phi)\) must specify the varying
parameters and an estimate that implies a stronger local Satake bound.
For a fixed unitary cuspidal representation \(\Pi\) of
\(\mathrm{GL}_{5}/\mathbb Q\), Nelson's
\emph{Bounds for standard \(L\)-functions}, Theorem~1.1, gives
\[
|L(\Pi,1/2+it)|\ll_{\Pi,\delta}
(1+|t|)^{5(1-\delta)/4},\qquad 0<\delta<1/4050.
\]
Here \(L\) is the finite standard \(L\)-function, with archimedean factors excluded.
This applies when \(\Pi=\operatorname{sym}^{4}\phi\) is cuspidal.
Its implicit constant may depend on \(\phi\).
An improvement of \(7/64\) by this route therefore requires
additional uniformity and a proved implication from the analytic
estimate to the local exponents. Cancellation beyond the estimate
is another possible input. Construction~C instead assumes
cuspidal automorphy of a higher Maass symmetric power.
\end{remark}

\section{Construction~C: Newton--Thorne Symmetric-Power Functoriality and the Sharpening}
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:construction-C}

\subsection{The Newton--Thorne Theorem}
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:construction-C:NT}

\begin{theorem}[Newton--Thorne 2021: unconditional holomorphic \(\mathrm{sym}^{n}\) functoriality]
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:thm:newton-thorne}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere (Newton--Thorne 2021 \emph{Publ.~Math.~IHES}~134,
Main Theorem; extended in Newton--Thorne 2023
\emph{Publ.~Math.~IHES}~137 to the non-CM case with full generality).
Let \(\phi\) be a holomorphic cuspidal Hecke newform of weight \(k\geq 2\)
on \(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\) that is non-CM. Then for every
\(n\geq 1\), the symmetric-\(n\)th-power lift \(\mathrm{sym}^{n}\phi\)
exists as a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\(\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}/\mathbb{Q}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Newton--Thorne 2021 (holomorphic, \(k\geq 2\)). The argument proceeds
by automorphy-lifting through Galois deformation rings of the
\(\ell\)-adic representation associated to \(\phi\), combined with
\(R=T\) theorems in the tame Taylor--Wiles patching framework
(Taylor--Wiles 1995 \emph{Ann.~Math.}~141, extended by
Clozel--Harris--Taylor 2008 \emph{Publ.~Math.~IHES}~108 to the
\(\mathrm{GL}_{n}\) setting).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Newton--Thorne does not cover Maass directly]
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:rem:NT-domain}
The Newton--Thorne theorem is stated for \emph{holomorphic} cuspidal
newforms, where the attached \(\ell\)-adic Galois representation
(Deligne 1971 \emph{S\'em.~Bourbaki}~355 for \(k\geq 2\); Deligne--Serre
1974 \emph{Ann.~Sci.~ENS.}~7 for \(k=1\)) is the input to the
automorphy-lifting theorem. Maass cusp forms lie outside the hypothesis of Newton--Thorne 2021:
the automorphy-lifting theorem requires an \(\ell\)-adic Galois representation
of algebraic Hodge weight \(\geq 1\), and the conjectural Langlands-attached
representation of a Maass form has weight~\(0\) in the Hodge sense.
The holomorphic companion construction gives a diagnostic
Rankin--Selberg convolution \(L(s,\phi\times\mathrm{sym}^{n}F)\), but it
does not transfer temperedness of \(\mathrm{sym}^{n}F\) into a
sub-\(7/64\) bound for \(\theta_{p}(\phi)\).
\end{remark}

\subsection{The Auxiliary Holomorphic Form and the Transfer}
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:construction-C:aux}

The attempted sharpening of \(7/64\) through a holomorphic companion is
only a test of source separation. It supplies no division of the Maass
Satake exponent by \(n\). A genuine sharper bound requires the
symmetric power of the Maass form itself.

\begin{definition}[Maass--holomorphic Rankin--Selberg pairing]
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:def:MH-RS}
\ClaimStatusDefinitional. Let \(\phi\) be a Maass cusp form of
\(\mathrm{GL}_{2}/\mathbb{Q}\) and \(F\) a holomorphic cuspidal newform of
weight \(k\geq 2\). The \emph{Maass--holomorphic Rankin--Selberg
\(L\)-function} is
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:eq:MH-RS}
L(s,\phi\times F)
\;:=\;\prod_{p}L_{p}(s,\phi\times F)
\;=\;\prod_{p}\prod_{i,j=1,2}\bigl(1-\alpha_{p,i}(\phi)\alpha_{p,j}(F)p^{-s}\bigr)^{-1},
\end{equation}
with \(\alpha_{p,i}(\phi)\in\{\alpha_{p}(\phi),\beta_{p}(\phi)\}\) and
analogously for \(F\). The completed \(L\)-function
\(\Lambda(s,\phi\times F)\) satisfies the Jacquet--Piatetski-Shapiro--Shalika
1983 functional equation.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[RS convolution for a Maass form with a tempered companion]
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:prop:sym-transfer}
Let \(\phi\) be a Maass cusp form and \(F\) a
holomorphic cuspidal newform of weight \(k\geq 2\), non-CM. Fix
\(n\geq 4\). The symmetric-\(n\)th-power lift \(\mathrm{sym}^{n}F\)
(Newton--Thorne 2021) is a cuspidal tempered automorphic representation
of \(\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}/\mathbb{Q}\). The Rankin--Selberg \(L\)-function
\(L(s,\phi\times\mathrm{sym}^{n}F)\), as a \(\mathrm{GL}_{2}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}\)
\(L\)-function, admits a functional equation, meromorphic continuation
to \(\mathbb{C}\), and satisfies: \(L(s,\phi\times\mathrm{sym}^{n}F)\neq 0\)
for \(\mathrm{Re}(s)\geq 1\) (Jacquet--Shalika 1981).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Newton--Thorne 2021 (Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w10-c:thm:newton-thorne}) gives
\(\mathrm{sym}^{n}F\) as a cuspidal \(\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}/\mathbb{Q}\)
representation. Deligne 1974 (Weil conjectures) gives temperedness.
Jacquet--Piatetski-Shapiro--Shalika (JPSS) 1983
\emph{Amer.~J.~Math.}~105 establishes the Rankin--Selberg \(L\)-function
theory for \(\mathrm{GL}_{2}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}\), including the
functional equation and meromorphic continuation. Jacquet--Shalika
1981 non-vanishing on \(\mathrm{Re}(s)=1\) applies to the cuspidal
\(\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}\)-factor \(\mathrm{sym}^{n}F\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Sharpened Bound: Construction~C Main Theorem}
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:construction-C:main}

\begin{remark}[why the companion trick is conditional]
Jacquet--Shalika bounds the two automorphic factors separately. It does
not divide the \(\mathrm{GL}_{10}\) exponent of \(\mathrm{sym}^{9}F\) by
nine to bound the Maass form \(\phi\). A sharper Maass bound therefore
requires functoriality for \(\mathrm{sym}^{9}\phi\) itself.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[sharpened Ramanujan bound: conditional on Maass symmetric-power functoriality]
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:thm:sharpened}
\emph{(conditional on the Maass symmetric-ninth-power
conjecture: that \(\mathrm{sym}^{9}\phi\) exists as a cuspidal automorphic
representation of \(\mathrm{GL}_{10}/\mathbb{Q}\) for the Maass form \(\phi\)
itself)}. Let \(\phi\) be a cuspidal Maass Hecke eigenform of
\(\mathrm{GL}_{2}/\mathbb{Q}\). Assuming \(\mathrm{sym}^{9}\phi\) is
cuspidal on \(\mathrm{GL}_{10}/\mathbb{Q}\), the Jacquet--Shalika 1981
bound applied to the \(\mathrm{GL}_{10}\) form \(\mathrm{sym}^{9}\phi\)
gives
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:eq:sharpened-final}
\sup_{p}\theta_{p}(\phi)
\;\leq\;\frac{1/2-1/(10^{2}+1)}{9}
\;=\;\frac{1/2-1/101}{9}
\;\approx\;0.05446,
\end{equation}
strictly below Kim--Sarnak's \(7/64\approx 0.1094\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Conditional on \(\mathrm{sym}^{9}\phi\) cuspidal automorphic on
\(\mathrm{GL}_{10}/\mathbb{Q}\) (the Maass symmetric-ninth-power conjecture),
the Jacquet--Shalika 1981 \emph{Amer.~J.~Math.}~103 non-vanishing of
\(L(s,\mathrm{sym}^{9}\phi\otimes(\mathrm{sym}^{9}\phi)^{\vee})\) on
\(\mathrm{Re}(s)=1\) forces the Satake parameters of \(\mathrm{sym}^{9}\phi\)
on \(\mathrm{GL}_{10}\) to satisfy
\(\theta_{p}(\mathrm{sym}^{9}\phi)\leq 1/2-1/101\).
The extremal Satake parameter of \(\mathrm{sym}^{9}\phi\) at \(p\) is
\(p^{9\theta_{p}(\phi)}\), so \(9\theta_{p}(\phi)\leq 1/2-1/101\), giving
the stated bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[form of Maass symmetric-power functoriality]
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:rem:construction-C-quality}
Newton--Thorne 2021 establishes \(\mathrm{sym}^{n}\phi\) cuspidal on
\(\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}/\mathbb{Q}\) for \emph{holomorphic} newforms \(\phi\).
The analogous statement for Maass forms is the content of the Langlands
global functoriality conjecture for \(\mathrm{GL}_{2}\to\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}\)
at non-algebraic archimedean type; it is open for all \(n\geq 5\).
The holomorphic companion trick -- forming \(L(s,\phi\times\mathrm{sym}^{n}F)\)
for a holomorphic form \(F\) -- does not produce a bound on
\(\theta_{p}(\phi)\) sharper than the direct \(\mathrm{GL}_{2}\)
Jacquet--Shalika bound \(\theta_{p}(\phi)\leq 2/5\): the
Rankin--Selberg non-vanishing constrains the \(\mathrm{GL}_{10}\) factor
\(\mathrm{sym}^{n}F\), not the \(\mathrm{GL}_{2}\) factor \(\phi\).
The bound \((1/2-1/101)/9\) in Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w10-c:thm:sharpened} is the
correct conditional bound on the Maass symmetric-power conjecture,
not an unconditional sharpening.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[local pairing errors require comparison estimates]
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:cor:Maass-residual-sharpened}
A Satake exponent bound \(\theta_p\leq\vartheta\) does not by
itself give a norm estimate between independently defined pairings.
An estimate of the form
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:eq:maass-residual-sharpened}
\bigl|\langle v_p,w_p\rangle^{\mathrm{Pet}}_p
-\langle v_p,\sigma_p(w_p)\rangle^{\mathrm{Koszul}}_p\bigr|
\leq C_p p^{-(1/2-\vartheta)}
\end{equation}
requires bounds for the actual arithmetic comparison maps, their
normalizations and the chosen local vectors. The exponent
\(\vartheta=7/64\), or the conditional symmetric-power improvement,
controls Satake parameters only. Moreover the indicated prime
majorants are not summable without additional decay.
\end{remark}

\section{Construction~D: Arthur Endoscopic Classification on Classical Groups}
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:construction-D}

The automorphic Ramanujan conjecture for a Maass form
\(\phi\) of \(\mathrm{GL}_{2}/\mathbb{Q}\) is detected by the tensor square
\(\phi\boxtimes\phi^{\vee}\). This is a \(\mathrm{GL}_{4}\)-object, usually
isobaric rather than cuspidal. Arthur's classification gives packet
normalisation for the classical-group transfers; it does not prove the
Ramanujan conjecture. The missing input is exactly temperedness of the
relevant \(\mathrm{GL}_{4}\) isobaric representation.

\subsection{Arthur's Endoscopic Classification}
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:construction-D:arthur}

\begin{theorem}[Arthur 2013: endoscopic classification for classical groups]
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:thm:arthur-2013}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere (Arthur 2013
\emph{Colloq.~Publ.}~61, \emph{The Endoscopic Classification of
Representations}, Thm.~1.5.2). For quasi-split classical groups over
a number field, the discrete automorphic spectrum is organised by
global Arthur parameters. The associated standard transfer to general
linear groups is automorphic, isobaric, and compatible with the local
Langlands parameters at unramified places. Temperedness is present
precisely for tempered Arthur parameters; it is not a formal consequence
of the classification.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Arthur 2013 \emph{The Endoscopic Classification} Thm.~1.5.2 and
\S4.3 give the parametrisation and the standard transfer. The
stabilisation input is the Langlands--Shelstad--Ng\^o--Waldspurger
endoscopic trace formula. M\oe glin--Waldspurger controls the
local discrete-series packets. None of these statements replaces the
generalised Ramanujan conjecture.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[stabilisation is not Ramanujan]
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:rem:arthur-SCF-form}
For \(F=\mathbb{Q}\) the required stabilisation rests on the fundamental
lemma and the endoscopic transfer identities. This is a theorem of the
trace formula. It identifies packets and transfers. It does not bound
Satake parameters.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The Ramakrishnan Functorial Chain}
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:construction-D:ramakrishnan}

\begin{proposition}[Ramakrishnan 2000: \(\mathrm{GL}_{2}\boxtimes\mathrm{GL}_{2}\to\mathrm{GL}_{4}\)]
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:prop:ramakrishnan}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere (Ramakrishnan 2000
\emph{Ann.~Math.}~152 \emph{Modularity of the Rankin--Selberg
\(L\)-series, and multiplicity one for \(\mathrm{SL}_{2}\)},
Thm.~M). Let \(\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\) be cuspidal automorphic
representations of \(\mathrm{GL}_{2}/\mathbb{Q}\). Then their
Langlands tensor product \(\phi_{1}\boxtimes\phi_{2}\) is an
automorphic representation of \(\mathrm{GL}_{4}/\mathbb{Q}\); it is
cuspidal unless \(\phi_{1}\) and \(\phi_{2}\) are twists of a common
dihedral form.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Ramakrishnan 2000 Thm.~M. The proof constructs the tensor product
via the converse theorem of Cogdell--Piatetski-Shapiro 1999
\emph{Publ.~Math.~IHES}~88, using Bump--Friedberg 1990
\emph{Duke~Math.~J.}~59 for the Rankin--Selberg \(L\)-function.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Automorphic Conditional}
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:construction-D:sub-RH}

\begin{hypothesis}[Arthur--Ramakrishnan--GRC hypothesis, \(\mathrm{AR}\text{-}\mathrm{GRC}\)]
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:hyp:AR-sub}
Assume:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(AR1)] Arthur's endoscopic classification
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w10-c:thm:arthur-2013} is available for the relevant
classical groups, with standard transfer compatible with unramified
local parameters.
\item[(AR2)] The Ramakrishnan tensor product
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:w10-c:prop:ramakrishnan}
is used for cuspidal automorphic representations attached to Maass
forms. For \(\phi_{2}=\phi_{1}^{\vee}\) the product is interpreted
isobarically.
\item[(AR3)] The isobaric representation
\(\phi\boxtimes\phi^{\vee}\) satisfies the generalised Ramanujan
conjecture at every finite place.
\end{enumerate}
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{theorem}[AR-GRC implies full Ramanujan for Maass forms]
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:thm:AR-implies-Ramanujan}
\emph{(conditional on
Hypothesis~\ref{v4-arith:w10-c:hyp:AR-sub})}. Under (AR1)+(AR2)+(AR3), every
cuspidal Maass Hecke eigenform \(\phi\) of
\(\mathrm{GL}_{2}/\mathbb{Q}\) satisfies the Ramanujan bound
\(\sup_{p}\theta_{p}(\phi)=0\), i.e.\ is tempered at every finite prime.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At an unramified finite prime write the unitary Satake parameters of
\(\phi\) as \(\alpha_{p},\beta_{p}\), with
\(|\alpha_{p}\beta_{p}|=1\). The local parameter of
\(\phi\boxtimes\phi^{\vee}\) has eigenvalues
\[
1,\quad 1,\quad \alpha_{p}/\beta_{p},\quad \beta_{p}/\alpha_{p}.
\]
By (AR3) these have modulus \(1\). Hence
\(|\alpha_{p}|=|\beta_{p}|\), and the central-character normalisation gives
\(|\alpha_{p}|=|\beta_{p}|=1\). Thus \(\phi_{p}\) is tempered.
The ramified finite primes are controlled by the same boundedness
condition on the Weil--Deligne parameter. Arthur's classification is
used only to keep the transfer normalisation fixed.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[AR-GRC is separate from classical RH in this argument]
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:prop:sub-RH-strict}
Hypothesis AR-GRC
(Hypothesis~\ref{v4-arith:w10-c:hyp:AR-sub}) is an automorphic input separate
from the classical Riemann hypothesis here. The proof below
uses neither RH nor any zero-placement statement for \(\zeta(s)\).
AR-GRC is supplied by GRC for the relevant \(\mathrm{GL}_{4}\) isobaric
tensor square together with the stated transfer inputs.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
GRC for the \(\mathrm{GL}_{4}\) isobaric tensor square gives bounded
local parameters. The proof of
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w10-c:thm:AR-implies-Ramanujan} then reads off the
\(\mathrm{GL}_{2}\) Satake radii. No statement about zeros of
\(\zeta(s)\) enters this implication. Conversely, the argument makes
no deduction from RH to AR-GRC.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the automorphic residual]
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:rem:sub-RH-meaning}
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w10-c:thm:AR-implies-Ramanujan} places the Maass-sector
temperedness requirement of Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w5-c:thm:main} on AR-GRC,
while leaving its arithmetic pairing maps as separate hypotheses,
which is the generalised Ramanujan conjecture for the
\(\mathrm{GL}_{4}\) isobaric tensor square, together with the stated
transfer normalisations. GRC for this object is open; it is an
automorphic temperedness statement, not a consequence of classical RH.
The Vol~IV P3 identity on the Maass sector holds without further
hypothesis conditional on GRC for \(\mathrm{GL}_{4}\).
\end{remark}

\section{Construction~E: Synthesis}
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:construction-E}

\begin{theorem}[Maass-sector P3 identity: local controls and exact conditional form]
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:thm:main}
For the displayed local pairing bounds assume the corresponding
norm estimates in Remark~\ref{v4-arith:w10-c:cor:Maass-residual-sharpened}.
For exact global equality assume the arithmetic placement and pairing
hypotheses of Hypothesis~\ref{v4-arith:kms-gns:hyp:arithmetic-pairing},
the stated Maass temperedness domain, and the adelic extension.
\emph{(local pairing bounds require comparison-norm estimates; conditional on the Maass symmetric-ninth-power
conjecture for the sharper local control; conditional on GRC for
\(\mathrm{GL}_{4}/\mathbb{Q}\) for the exact equality)}.
Here \(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}\) denotes the primitive automorphic
Hochschild trace class of the identity on the global arithmetic
Iwahori--Hecke category of \(\mathrm{GL}_{2}\), as defined in
Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:w5-c:full-vprim-P3}, and
\(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}_{\mathrm{cusp,Maass}}\subset
\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}\) its Maass cuspidal sector.
For local vectors \(v_{p},w_{p}\) in the \(p\)-component and global
vectors \(v,w\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}_{\mathrm{cusp,Maass}}\), the
available statements are:
The first inequality assumes the local norm estimate; the second also
assumes the Maass symmetric-ninth-power input. The final identity
assumes the comparison maps and GRC for \(\mathrm{GL}_4/\mathbb Q\).
\begin{align}
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:eq:main-KS}
\bigl|\langle v_{p},w_{p}\rangle^{\mathrm{Pet}}_{p}
-\langle v_{p},\sigma^{\mathrm{BC}}_{p}(w_{p})\rangle^{\mathrm{Koszul}}_{p}\bigr|
&\;\leq\;C_{p}p^{-25/64},
\\
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:eq:main-error}
\bigl|\langle v_{p},w_{p}\rangle^{\mathrm{Pet}}_{p}
-\langle v_{p},\sigma^{\mathrm{BC}}_{p}(w_{p})\rangle^{\mathrm{Koszul}}_{p}\bigr|
&\;\leq\;C_{p}p^{-(1/2-(1/2-1/101)/9)},
\\
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:eq:main-exact}
\langle v,w\rangle^{\mathrm{Pet}}
&\;=\;\langle v,\sigma^{\mathrm{BC}}(w)\rangle^{\mathrm{Koszul}}.
\end{align}
The first two lines do not assemble to an adelic correction term without
extra summability; the exact global identity is the third line.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The local bounds are exactly the assumed comparison-norm estimates
with the indicated Satake exponents. The exponents alone do not
prove those estimates.
Exact-equality form (\ref{v4-arith:w10-c:eq:main-exact}):
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w10-c:thm:AR-implies-Ramanujan} gives temperedness of
\(\phi\) under AR-GRC; Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w5-c:thm:tempered-recap} then
applies, with its arithmetic placement, pairing and adelic
hypotheses, to \(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}_{\mathrm{cusp,Maass}}\subset
\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}_{\mathrm{cusp,temp}}\). AR-GRC is
GRC for the relevant \(\mathrm{GL}_{4}\) isobaric tensor square by
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:w10-c:prop:sub-RH-strict}.
\end{proof}

\section{Satake Bounds and Their Hypotheses}
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:seven-obstruction}

The local exponent \(\theta_p\geq0\) uses Satake parameters normalized
by \(\alpha_p\beta_p=1\). Kim's symmetric-fourth transfer and the
Kim--Sarnak estimate give the unconditional bound \(\theta_p\leq7/64\).
The Rankin--Selberg theory of Jacquet--Piatetski-Shapiro--Shalika
(1983) and the Euler-product theory of Jacquet--Shalika (1981)
supply the associated \(L\)-functions and edge non-vanishing.
Non-vanishing on \(\operatorname{Re}(s)=1\) is distinct from an
estimate on \(\operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2\).
The proposed analytic refinement through
\(L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{4}\phi)\) requires a specified aspect,
analytic conductor, saving, and uniformity in the Maass form
\(\phi\). It also requires an argument converting that estimate
into a bound below \(7/64\) for the local Satake exponents.
These additional data are not specified by the edge non-vanishing theorem.

For Construction~C, assume that \(\operatorname{sym}^{9}\phi\)
is cuspidal automorphic on \(\mathrm{GL}_{10}/\mathbb Q\).
Under this Maass symmetric-power hypothesis,
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w10-c:thm:sharpened} gives
\[
\theta_p\leq\frac{1/2-1/101}{9}\approx0.0545.
\]
Newton--Thorne functoriality applies to the holomorphic companion
\(F\) of algebraic weight at least two. It does not supply the
symmetric-ninth-power hypothesis for \(\phi\).
The separation of the lowest-weight component uses the tensor
decomposition in Construction~C.

Construction~D uses the automorphic isobaric tensor square on
\(\mathrm{GL}_{4}/\mathbb Q\). Ramakrishnan's tensor product is
interpreted isobarically in the contragredient case.
Assuming the generalized Ramanujan conjecture for this tensor
square, Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w10-c:thm:AR-implies-Ramanujan} gives
\(\theta_p=0\). Arthur transfer supplies the packet normalization.
Its endoscopic inputs are Arthur (2013), Langlands--Shelstad (1987),
Ng\^o (2010), Waldspurger (2008), and Chaudouard--Laumon (2012).
The tensor-product and automorphy inputs are Ramakrishnan (2000)
and Cogdell--Piatetski-Shapiro (1999).
The dihedral case uses Deligne--Serre and Labesse--Langlands (1979).
Thus the \(7/64\) estimate, the conditional symmetric-ninth estimate,
and the conditional temperedness conclusion use different hypotheses.

\section{Residual Ramanujan Input}
\label{v4-arith:w10-c:open}

\paragraph{GRC for \(\mathrm{GL}_{4}/\mathbb{Q}\).}
The generalised Ramanujan conjecture for the relevant isobaric
\(\mathrm{GL}_{4}/\mathbb{Q}\) tensor square is open; the Construction~D
closure is conditional on it.
The current best unconditional bound is from Kim--Shahidi 2002
extensions to higher-rank groups:
\(\theta_{\mathrm{GL}_{4}}\leq 5/14\approx 0.357\).

\paragraph{Maass symmetric-power functoriality (Construction~C conditional).}
Construction~C's sharpened bound \((1/2-1/((n+1)^{2}+1))/n\) is conditional
on the Maass symmetric-\(n\)th-power conjecture: the existence of
\(\mathrm{sym}^{n}\phi\) as a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\(\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}/\mathbb{Q}\) for the Maass form \(\phi\) itself.
This is the Langlands global functoriality conjecture for
\(\mathrm{GL}_{2}\to\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}\) at non-algebraic archimedean
type; it is open for all \(n\geq 5\). Newton--Thorne 2021/2023 covers
only holomorphic forms (\(\ell\)-adic algebraic weight). The bound
\(\theta^{(n)}=(1/2-1/((n+1)^{2}+1))/n\to 0\) as \(n\to\infty\) at
rate \(\sim 1/(2n)\); closing to \(\theta=0\) via this mechanism would
require a proof of the Maass symmetric-power conjecture for all \(n\),
which would imply the Ramanujan conjecture by the Jacquet--Shalika
bound. It is stronger than the numerical estimate alone.

\paragraph{The dihedral edge case.}
Dihedral Maass forms lift from Hecke characters of quadratic fields
and are tempered by class field theory (Labesse--Langlands 1979,
Deligne--Serre 1974); Construction~D applies without the AR2 hypothesis.
