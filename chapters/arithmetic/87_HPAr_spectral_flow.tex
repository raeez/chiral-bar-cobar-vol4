\chapter{Modular Generators, Zero Divisors, and Operator Domains}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:hpar-spectral-flow}

The critical Bost--Connes generator, the occupation Hamiltonian,
and the idele scaling operator have different spectral measures.
The arithmetic realization problem asks for a self-adjoint operator
with the completed zeta divisor and the full signed Weil trace.
An intertwiner preserves eigenvalues on every source eigenvector
in its domain, while a unitary multiplication chart preserves
continuous spectral type. These elementary restrictions determine
which proposed comparisons can represent that divisor.
The explicit formula and the graph-domain calculations below give
separate distributional and operator statements.

\section{Setup: \(N_{\mathrm{BC}}\) and \(\Theta_{\xi}\) as Target Operators}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:setup}

The proposed operator comparison starts with the critical BC
generator and asks for a zero-divisor target. Their distinct
Hilbert carriers and spectra determine its domain requirements.

\subsection{Source: The Bost--Connes Log-Prime Operator}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:setup:source}

\begin{definition}[Critical generator and occupation restriction]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:def:bc-data}
On the critical GNS space \(\mathcal H_1\), use the basis of
\Cref{v4-arith:w5-b:lem:gns-restricted-tensor}:
\[
 \mathcal H_1=\ell^2(\mathbb Q_{>0})\widehat\otimes\ell^2(\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z),
 \quad N_{\mathrm{BC}}v_{q,r}=\tfrac12\log q\,v_{q,r},
 \quad\operatorname{Dom}N_{\mathrm{BC}}
 =\{c:\sum_{q,r}|\log q|^2|c_{q,r}|^2<\infty\}.
\]
Its eigenvalues have infinite multiplicity and their closure is
\(\mathbb R\). Let
\(\mathcal H_{\mathrm{BC}}:=K_2=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{v_{m^2,0}:m\ge1\}\)
when discussing occupation coordinates. This reducing subspace
carries \(N_{\mathrm{occ}}=N_{\mathrm{BC}}|_{K_2}\), whose simple eigenvalues
are \(\log m\). The full and restricted operators remain distinct.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Occupation coordinates]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:rem:bc-structure}
Let \(e_m\) be the coordinate vector of norm \(m^{-1/2}\) in
\(L_w=\ell^2(\mathbb N^\times,dm/m)\).
The map \(v_{m^2,0}\mapsto\sqrt m\,e_m\) is unitary.
Prime factorization identifies the orthonormal occupation basis
with the restricted tensor product of the bases indexed by
\(v_p(m)\), with vacuum index zero at every prime.
In these coordinates \(N_{\mathrm{occ}}\) is the closure of
\(\sum_p v_p(m)\log p\) on finite-support vectors.
This Hilbert tensor decomposition is not a map from the full
critical generator to the continuous idele scaling representation.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Target: The Hypothetical Zeta-Zero Divisor Operator}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:setup:target}

\begin{definition}[target Hilbert space and zero-divisor operator]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:def:target-data}
\ClaimStatusDefinitional Let \(\mathcal{Z}\) denote the multiset of
non-trivial zeros \(\rho=\beta_{\rho}+it_{\rho}\) of
\(\xi_{\mathbb{R}}\), with each occurrence indexed once, and write
\(\gamma_{\rho}:=t_{\rho}\) for the ordinate. The
\emph{formal target Hilbert space} is
\[
\mathcal{H}_{\xi}^{\mathrm{formal}}\;:=\;\bigoplus_{\rho\in\mathcal{Z}}\mathbb{C},
\]
with inner product making the one-dimensional summands
orthonormal. The \emph{formal ordinate multiplier} is the
diagonal symmetric operator
\[
H_{\mathrm{ord}}^{\mathrm{formal}}\;:\;(\xi_{\rho})_{\rho}\;\mapsto\;(t_{\rho}\xi_{\rho})_{\rho}
\]
on the maximal domain
\[
 \left\{c\in\ell^2(\mathcal Z):
             \sum_{\rho\in\mathcal Z}t_\rho^2|c_\rho|^2<\infty\right\}.
\]
This real diagonal operator is self-adjoint; its adjoint domain is
exactly the displayed square-summability domain. Coordinate
truncations show that finite-support vectors form a graph core.
Its point spectrum is the ordinate set, with one coordinate per
zero occurrence. Distinct zeros with the same ordinate add their
multiplicities. The \emph{critical-line
projection operator} is
\[
\Theta_{\xi}^{\mathrm{proj}}=\tfrac12\mathrm{id}+iH_{\mathrm{ord}}^{\mathrm{formal}}.
\]
Its divisor is the projection of the zero divisor to the critical
line; it is the actual zero divisor of \(\xi_{\mathbb R}\) exactly
under RH.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[why ``formal'']
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:rem:why-formal}
The triple \((\mathcal{H}_{\xi}^{\mathrm{formal}},H_{\mathrm{ord}}^{\mathrm{formal}},\Theta_{\xi}^{\mathrm{proj}})\)
exists trivially as a diagonal construction. It carries no
intrinsic arithmetic structure: the enumeration of
\(\{t_{\rho}\}\) is read from the zero set itself, and the diagonal
operator realises its spectrum by fiat. If one replaces
\(\beta_{\rho}\) by \(1/2\), one has already projected the divisor to
the critical line. The content of the Hilbert--P\'olya problem is
not the existence of \(\mathcal{H}_{\xi}^{\mathrm{formal}}\); it is
the existence of a Hilbert space \emph{with independent structure}
whose spectral data force the original zero divisor to equal this
projected divisor.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[non-formal target]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:def:non-formal-target}
\ClaimStatusDefinitional A \emph{non-formal target} is a pair
\((\mathcal{H}_{\xi},H_{\mathrm{HP}})\) where \(\mathcal{H}_{\xi}\) is a
Hilbert space carrying structure inherited from the Bost--Connes
framework (restricted-tensor adelic factorisation, Petersson inner
product on cuspidal cores, GNS modular data, or another
independently specified structure) and \(H_{\mathrm{HP}}\) is a
self-adjoint operator on \(\mathcal{H}_{\xi}\) whose spectral
resolution is canonical with respect to the inherited structure and
whose regularised determinant
\[
\det\nolimits_{\infty}\bigl(s\mathrm{id}-(\tfrac12\mathrm{id}+iH_{\mathrm{HP}})\bigr)
\]
has the non-trivial zero divisor of \(\xi_{\mathbb R}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[domain of ``canonical'']
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:rem:canonical-domain}
``Canonical'' means: the spectral resolution of \(H_{\mathrm{HP}}\) is
determined by the inherited structure up to a unitary equivalence
whose parameters are themselves determined by that structure. A
diagonal construction with eigenvalues labelled by an arbitrary
enumeration is not canonical; a diagonal construction whose
eigenvectors are explicit functions of arithmetic data (adelic
characters, automorphic forms, spectral projectors of a globally
defined operator) is.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Spectral Flow as Intertwining Operator}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:setup:flow}

\begin{definition}[An operator comparison]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:def:spectral-flow}
Choose explicitly either \(N=N_{\mathrm{BC}}\) on \(\mathcal H_1\)
or \(N=N_{\mathrm{occ}}\) on \(K_2\). An intertwining datum consists
of a self-adjoint \(H\) on a complex Hilbert space \(\mathcal L\),
a dense \(N\)-invariant subspace \(\mathcal D\subset\operatorname{Dom}N\),
and an injective linear map \(A:\mathcal D\to\operatorname{Dom}H\)
with dense range, satisfying
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:eq:intertwine}
 HA u=ANu\qquad(u\in\mathcal D).
\end{equation}
A zero-divisor realization additionally requires a complete
orthonormal eigenbasis of \(H\) whose eigenvalue multiset obeys
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:eq:spec-id}
 \{\tfrac12+i\lambda:\lambda\text{ an eigenvalue of }H\}
 =\{\rho:\xi_{\mathbb R}(\rho)=0\},
\end{equation}
with multiplicities, and a separately normalized determinant.
The existence of such a target is not an assertion that the
intertwining datum exists for either source.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Eigenvectors under an intertwiner]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:rem:unitarity-of-A}
If \(Nv=\lambda v\), \(v\in\mathcal D\), and \(A\) is an
intertwining datum, then \(Av\ne0\) and \(HAv=\lambda Av\).
No such datum containing a nonzero zero-energy source vector
can have the zero-divisor target above. In particular, an injective
intertwiner on the eigenvector core of either source is impossible.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Injectivity gives \(Av\ne0\), and the intertwining identity gives
the eigenvalue equation. Both source cores contain \(v_{1,0}\)
of energy zero, whereas \(\xi_{\mathbb R}(1/2)\ne0\).
Indeed, the alternating series
\(\eta(1/2)=\sum_{n\ge1}(-1)^{n-1}n^{-1/2}\) converges and is
positive: grouping consecutive pairs gives positive terms and
the first pair is positive. The alternating-series continuation
\(\eta(s)=(1-2^{1-s})\zeta(s)\) on \(\Re s>0\) then gives
\(\zeta(1/2)=\eta(1/2)/(1-\sqrt2)\ne0\).
The other completed factors at \(1/2\) are finite and nonzero.
Unboundedness of \(A\) does not alter this eigenvector argument.
\end{proof}

\section{Prime-Zero Mangoldt-Hadamard Duality Recap}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:mangoldt-hadamard}

The Riemann--von Mangoldt explicit formula and the Hadamard product
relate prime data to the zero divisor. In the Fourier formulation below,
the prime terms act on compact smooth time tests and the zero
functional acts on their entire Fourier transforms. This identity
does not by itself identify Hilbert-space operators.

\subsection{Hadamard Factorisation of \(\xi_{\mathbb{R}}\)}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:mh:hadamard}

\begin{proposition}[Hadamard factorisation]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:prop:hadamard}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere \emph{(Hadamard 1893;
\Cref{v4-arith:w5-b:eq:hadamard})}. The completed Riemann zeta
\(\xi_{\mathbb{R}}(s)=\tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\) is
entire of order \(1\), of genus \(1\), and admits the factorisation
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:eq:hadamard-factor}
\xi_{\mathbb{R}}(s)\;=\;\xi_{\mathbb{R}}(0)\,e^{Bs}\,\prod_{\rho}\Bigl(1-\frac{s}{\rho}\Bigr)e^{s/\rho},
\end{equation}
with \(\rho\) ranging over non-trivial zeros with multiplicity and
\[
 B=\frac{\xi_{\mathbb R}'(0)}{\xi_{\mathbb R}(0)}
   =-1-\frac\gamma2+\frac12\log(4\pi),
\]
where \(\gamma\) is Euler's constant and \(\xi_{\mathbb R}(0)=1/2\).
See Edwards 1974, \S2.7, and the normalization in
\Cref{v4-arith:pm:constant}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The function here is exactly the completed function of
\Cref{v4-arith:pm:constant}. Its identity
\(\xi_{\mathbb R}(s)=(s-1)\Gamma(1+s/2)\pi^{-s/2}\zeta(s)\)
gives
\[
 \frac{\xi_{\mathbb R}'(0)}{\xi_{\mathbb R}(0)}
 =-1-\frac\gamma2-\frac12\log\pi+\log(2\pi),
\]
using \(\Gamma'(1)=-\gamma\), \(\zeta(0)=-1/2\), and
\(\zeta'(0)=-\tfrac12\log(2\pi)\), as evaluated there.
Each genus-one factor has logarithmic derivative zero at the origin,
so this logarithmic derivative is the constant \(B\) in
\eqref{v4-arith:w9-r4:eq:hadamard-factor}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Riemann-von Mangoldt Explicit Formula}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:mh:rvm}

\begin{proposition}[Riemann-von Mangoldt explicit formula]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:prop:rvm}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere \emph{(von Mangoldt 1895; Edwards 1974 \S3)}.
For \(x>1\) non-integer,
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:eq:rvm}
\sum_{n\leq x}\Lambda(n)\;=\;x\;-\;\sum_{\rho}\frac{x^{\rho}}{\rho}\;-\;\frac{\zeta'(0)}{\zeta(0)}\;-\;\tfrac12\log(1-x^{-2}),
\end{equation}
where \(\Lambda(n)=\log p\) if \(n=p^k\) and \(0\) otherwise.
The zero sum means
\(\lim_{T\to\infty}\sum_{|\operatorname{Im}\rho|\leq T}x^\rho/\rho\),
with multiplicities and symmetric height truncation.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Mellin-Dual Measures}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:mh:mellin-dual}

\begin{definition}[prime and zero counting measures]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:def:counting-measures}
\ClaimStatusDefinitional The \emph{prime-counting distribution} is
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:eq:mu-prime}
\mu_{\mathrm{prime}}\;:=\;\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\Lambda(m)\,\delta_{\log m}\;=\;\sum_{p\in\mathbb{P}}\sum_{k\geq 1}(\log p)\,\delta_{k\log p}
\end{equation}
on \(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\). The \emph{ordinate shadow} of the
zero-counting distribution is
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:eq:mu-zero}
\mu_{\mathrm{ord}}\;:=\;\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal{Z}}\delta_{\gamma_{\rho}}
\end{equation}
on \(\mathbb{R}\). The actual Weil zero functional acts on
\(\mathscr P=\mathcal F_+C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)\), with
\((\mathcal F_+f)(z)=\int f(u)e^{izu}du\):
\[
\mathfrak Z_{\xi}(h):=\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal Z}h(z_{\rho}),
\qquad
z_{\rho}:=-i(\rho-\tfrac12).
\]
The equality \(\mathfrak Z_{\xi}(h)=\int_{\mathbb R}h\,d\mu_{\mathrm{ord}}\)
for every \(h\in\mathscr P\) is equivalent to RH.
Indeed, equality on Fourier images of convolution squares makes
the Weil form nonnegative; apply the Weil criterion.
Conversely, RH gives \(z_\rho=\gamma_\rho\) term by term.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Mellin-dual form of the explicit formula]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:prop:mellin-dual}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere \emph{(Weil 1952; Patterson 1988 Ch.~3)}.
Let \(\mathscr A=C_c^\infty(\mathbb R;\mathbb C)\), with its usual
inductive-limit topology, and let \(\mathscr P=\mathcal F_+\mathscr A\)
have the transported topology. For \(\varphi\in\mathscr A\), set
\[
 h(z)=\mathcal F_+\varphi(z)=\int_{\mathbb R}\varphi(t)e^{izt}\,dt,
 \qquad
 \varphi(t)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}h(u)e^{-iut}\,du.
\]
Write \(\psi_\Gamma=\Gamma'/\Gamma\) and define
\begin{align*}
 k(u)&=\log\pi-\Re\psi_\Gamma(\tfrac14+iu/2),\\
 P(\varphi)&=h(i/2)+h(-i/2),\qquad
 W_\infty(\varphi)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}h(u)k(u)\,du,\\
 W_{\mathrm{fin}}(\varphi)&=
 \sum_{m\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(m)}{\sqrt m}
             \bigl(\varphi(\log m)+\varphi(-\log m)\bigr).
\end{align*}
Then, with zeros counted with multiplicity,
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:eq:mellin-dual}
 \mathfrak Z_\xi(h)=\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal Z}h(z_\rho)
 =P(\varphi)-W_\infty(\varphi)-W_{\mathrm{fin}}(\varphi).
\end{equation}
The prime sum is finite and the zero sum converges absolutely.
For even \(\varphi\), the finite term is
\(2\mu_{1/2}(\varphi)\), where
\(\mu_{1/2}=\sum_{m\ge2}\Lambda(m)m^{-1/2}\delta_{\log m}\).
Under RH, \(z_\rho=\gamma_\rho\) and
\(\mathfrak Z_\xi(h)=\int_{\mathbb R}h\,d\mu_{\mathrm{ord}}\).
Without RH, replacing \(z_\rho\) by \(\gamma_\rho\) discards the
real-part information and does not give the explicit formula.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The Fourier convention and test carrier are those of
\Cref{v4-arith:w5-a:def:PW}. Apply
\Cref{v4-arith:pm:weil} to \(f=\varphi\).
Its polar terms are \(h(\pm i/2)\); its real-line density is
\(\Re\psi_\Gamma(1/4+iu/2)-\log\pi=-k(u)\).
This gives \eqref{v4-arith:w9-r4:eq:mellin-dual}, with the sign
convention of \Cref{v4-arith:w5-a:thm:weil-explicit}.
Compact support makes the prime sum finite. Repeated integration
by parts gives rapid decay of \(h\) in the strip
\(|\Im z|\le1/2\); the zero count in
\Cref{v4-arith:pm:zero-count} gives absolute convergence of the zero
sum. Evenness identifies the two prime evaluations. Finally,
\(z_\rho=\gamma_\rho-i(\Re\rho-1/2)\), proving the stated
specialization under RH.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the distributional identity and Hilbert realizations]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:rem:duality-distributional}
The Mellin--Hadamard identity in
\Cref{v4-arith:w9-r4:prop:mellin-dual} relates the continuous zero
functional on \(\mathscr P\) to the full polar, signed gamma and
prime functionals on \(\mathscr A\). The locally finite measures
\(\mu_{\mathrm{prime}}=\mu_0\) and \(\mu_{1/2}\) define distributions
on compact smooth tests. Neither is tempered, by
\Cref{v4-arith:pm:weights}; their Fourier transforms cannot be taken
in \(\mathscr S'(\mathbb R)\) by this construction.

There is nevertheless a signed Hilbert-space realization:
\Cref{v4-arith:sc:zero-representation} constructs the continuous
map \(E:\mathscr A\to K_Z=\ell^2(Z)\) and the self-adjoint involution
\(J\), with \((Ef)_\rho=(\mathcal F_+f)(z_\rho)\), such that
\[
 \langle Ef,JEg\rangle
 =P(f*g^*)-W_\infty(f*g^*)-W_{\mathrm{fin}}(f*g^*),
 \qquad g^*(t)=\overline{g(-t)}.
\]
Here \(Z\) indexes occurrences of zeros, including multiplicity.
This signed pairing does not supply a positive arithmetic-sector
unitary comparison. Realizing the prime and zero expressions as
spectral data of a single self-adjoint operator requires that
additional comparison, including the operator domains and the
spectral measures; the distributional identity alone supplies none
of these identifications.
\end{remark}

\section{Formal Spectral Flow \(A\) as Mellin-Inversion Symbol}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:formal-flow}

Mellin transformation followed by residue extraction defines a
sequence-valued map on compact smooth multiplicative tests.
Square summability and compatibility with a source operator require
separate proofs.

\subsection{The Mellin-Hadamard Inversion Symbol}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:formal-flow:symbol}

\begin{definition}[formal Mellin-Hadamard inversion]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:def:formal-MH}
\ClaimStatusDefinitional The \emph{formal Mellin-Hadamard inversion}
is the composite symbol
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:eq:formal-MH}
A^{\mathrm{sym}}\;:=\;\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Had}}\circ\mathcal{M}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal{M}\) is the Mellin transform
\((\mathcal{M}f)(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)x^{s-1}dx\) on a suitable
function class and \(\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Had}}\) is the
Hadamard-inversion formal symbol
\[
(\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Had}}F)(\gamma_{\rho})\;:=\;\operatorname*{Res}_{s=\rho}\frac{F(s)}{\xi_{\mathbb{R}}(s)}
\]
reading the residue of \(F/\xi_{\mathbb{R}}\) at each non-trivial zero
and writing it into the \(\rho\)-component of a formal sequence
indexed by \(\mathcal{Z}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Domains for a residue map]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:rem:Asym-not-operator}
The Mellin-residue prescription gives coordinates indexed by zeros.
To obtain a Hilbert-space operator, one must specify a source
Hilbert space, a dense domain on which the residue sequence is
square summable, and a proof of closability. The occupation space
is not a subspace of multiplicative Haar \(L^2\) through the
atomic prescription. A comparison with its generator requires
an additional map and a domain intertwining identity.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Formal Mellin--Hadamard Obstruction}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:formal-flow:formal-intertwine}

\begin{lemma}[formal Mellin--Hadamard obstruction]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:lem:formal-intertwine}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere Suppose \(f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})\)
and let \(Lf(x)=-\log x\,f(x)\). Suppose \(f\)
has Mellin transform \(F(s)=(\mathcal{M}f)(s)\) entire, and suppose
\(\rho\in\mathcal{Z}\) is a simple zero of \(\xi_{\mathbb R}\). Then
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:eq:formal-intertwine}
A^{\mathrm{sym}}\bigl(Lf\bigr)(\gamma_{\rho})
\;=\;
-\,\frac{F'(\rho)}{\xi_{\mathbb R}'(\rho)},
\qquad
A^{\mathrm{sym}}(f)(\gamma_{\rho})
\;=\;
\frac{F(\rho)}{\xi_{\mathbb R}'(\rho)}.
\end{equation}
Consequently the bare residue intertwining relation
\[
A^{\mathrm{sym}}L
\stackrel{?}{=}
M_{\gamma}A^{\mathrm{sym}}
\]
holds at \(\rho\) only under the additional interpolation condition
\[
F'(\rho)=-\gamma_{\rho}F(\rho).
\]
This interpolation condition concerns the test function and is not
a consequence of RH. No map identifying \(L\) with either BC
generator has been specified.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Define \((Lf)(x)=-\log x\,f(x)\) on \(C_c^\infty(\mathbb R_{>0})\). Mellin
transform gives
\((\mathcal{M}(Lf))(s)=-F'(s)\), where \(F'\) is the
derivative in the spectral variable. Since \(\rho\) is a simple zero,
\[
\operatorname*{Res}_{s=\rho}\frac{F(s)}{\xi_{\mathbb R}(s)}
=\frac{F(\rho)}{\xi_{\mathbb R}'(\rho)},
\qquad
\operatorname*{Res}_{s=\rho}\frac{-F'(s)}{\xi_{\mathbb R}(s)}
=-\frac{F'(\rho)}{\xi_{\mathbb R}'(\rho)}.
\]
Multiplication by the zero ordinate would instead give
\(\gamma_{\rho}F(\rho)/\xi_{\mathbb R}'(\rho)\). Equality is exactly
the displayed first-order interpolation condition.
This is not an identity on compact tests: take
\(f(x)=x^{1-\rho}\psi(x)\), where \(\psi\) is nonnegative, nonzero,
and compactly supported in an interval on which
\(\log x+\gamma_\rho\) has one strict sign. Then
\(F'(\rho)+\gamma_\rho F(\rho)
=\int(\log x+\gamma_\rho)\psi(x)dx\ne0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[RH is not enough for formal intertwining]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:rem:formal-assumes-RH}
\Cref{v4-arith:w9-r4:lem:formal-intertwine} shows that the continuous multiplier
\(L=-\log x\) differentiates the Mellin numerator before Hadamard residue
extraction. RH would place \(\rho\) on the critical line, but it would
not imply \(F'(\rho)=-\gamma_{\rho}F(\rho)\) for the Mellin transforms
in the source class. Thus the bare symbol
\(A^{\mathrm{sym}}\) is not a spectral-flow operator. A genuine
operator construction must supply a new domain theorem or boundary
condition that converts derivative residue data into zero-ordinate
multiplication without inserting that conclusion by definition.
\end{remark}

\section{Obstacle 1: Hilbert-Space Completions on Both Sides}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:obstacle-hilbert}

A sequence-valued residue prescription needs a source completion
and estimates in the chosen target norm. Abstract Hilbert
isomorphisms do not settle these domain or spectral requirements.

\subsection{Source-Side Completion}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:obstacle-hilbert:source}

\begin{definition}[Occupation source completion]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:def:source-completion}
Use \(L_w=\ell^2(\mathbb N^\times,dm/m)\) for occupation
coordinates, with \(N_{\mathrm{occ}}e_m=(\log m)e_m\).
Its maximal domain is
\(\{c:\sum_m(\log m)^2|c_m|^2/m<\infty\}\), and finite-support
vectors form a core. This is the restriction in
\Cref{v4-arith:w5-b:lem:zero-mode-embeds-gns}.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The atomic Mellin prescription]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:lem:no-mellin-on-HBC}
Sending \(e_m\) to the additive Dirac distribution \(\delta_{x=m}\)
does not embed \(L_w\) in \(L^2(\mathbb R_{>0},dx/x)\).
Its ordinary distributional Mellin transform is
\(m^{s-1}\), which is not square integrable on any vertical line.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A point mass is singular relative to \(dx/x\), so it has no
\(L^2\) density. Pairing the additive Dirac distribution with
\(x^{s-1}\) gives \(m^{s-1}\). On \(\Re s=c\) its modulus is
the positive constant \(m^{c-1}\), proving the second assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Atomic and continuous carriers]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:rem:lattice-ill-suited}
The atomic prescription fails on each basis vector before any
infinite Dirichlet-series convergence question arises.
Abstract Hilbert-space isometries still exist between the separable
infinite-dimensional spaces. An isometry preserving their stated
generators cannot exist, because the occupation generator has
an eigenvector basis and continuous scaling has no eigenvectors.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Target-Side Completion}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:obstacle-hilbert:target}

\begin{definition}[Distinct target constructions]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:def:target-completion}
The idele carrier is \(\mathcal K=L^2(C_{\mathbb Q},d^\times x)\)
of \Cref{v4-arith:w7-j:def:H-idele}.
The weighted adele-class Hilbert completion in
\Cref{v4-arith:w7-j:def:connes-ac-space} and its quotient
representation are different spaces.
A Petersson completion, when supplied by an automorphic
construction, is a third carrier. Neither these names nor
abstract Hilbert isomorphisms specify a generator-domain map
between any two of them.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Mellin transform on the idele norm]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:lem:mellin-on-idele}
The transform
\[
 (\mathcal Mg)(\tau)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}
 \int_0^\infty g(x)x^{-i\tau}\,dx/x
\]
is unitary from \(L^2(\mathbb R_{>0},dx/x)\) to
\(L^2(\mathbb R,d\tau)\). Tensoring with the identity on
\(L^2(C_{\mathbb Q}^1)\) gives the idele transform. It carries
\(-i\partial_{\log x}\), with logarithmic \(H^1\) domain, to
\(M_\tau\), with its maximal domain.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Substitute \(u=\log x\) and apply Fourier Plancherel and
\Cref{v4-arith:w7-j:lem:mellin-decomp}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Representations and completions]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:rem:completion-mismatch}
The Mellin transform diagonalizes the continuous scaling
representation, not the occupation or critical BC representation.
An injective map on their eigenvector cores cannot intertwine
these generators. A different completion or a distributional map
must state which operators, domains, and pairings it preserves.
\end{remark}

\section{Obstacle 2: Kernel of \(A\) as Bounded/Unbounded Operator}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:obstacle-kernel}

A residue prescription must define an operator on a stated domain.
An integral kernel is one possible construction; closability
requires an independent estimate.

\subsection{The Hadamard Inversion Kernel}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:obstacle-kernel:had}

\begin{definition}[Residue integrals and a line-kernel candidate]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:def:formal-had-kernel}
For a distinct zero \(\rho\), take a positively oriented small
circle \(C_\rho\) containing no other zero. The actual residue is
\[
 r_\rho(F)=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{C_\rho}\frac{F(s)}{\xi_{\mathbb R}(s)}ds.
\]
A different proposed line kernel is
\(K_{\mathrm{Had}}(\rho,s)=(2\pi i)^{-1}
[\xi_{\mathbb R}(s)(s-\rho)]^{-1}\).
It does not give the preceding residue by the same circle integral:
the additional factor changes the Laurent coefficient extracted.
Its integrability on the critical line can nevertheless be tested.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[the Hadamard kernel is not a Hilbert-Schmidt kernel]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:lem:had-not-HS}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere For the critical-line Mellin completion
compatible with the spectral-flow condition, the Hadamard kernel
\(K_{\mathrm{Had}}\) is not Hilbert--Schmidt. Its
Hilbert-Schmidt norm
\[
 \|K_{\mathrm{Had}}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 =\frac1{4\pi^2}\sum_\rho\int_{\mathbb R}
 \frac{d\tau}{|\xi_{\mathbb R}(1/2+i\tau)|^2
              |1/2+i\tau-\rho|^2}
\]
diverges at every zero \(\rho\) lying on the critical line.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(m_\rho\geq1\) be the order of vanishing at \(\rho\).
If \(\rho\) lies on the critical line, then
\(|\xi_{\mathbb{R}}(s)|^{-2}\sim C_{\rho}|s-\rho|^{-2m_{\rho}}\) along
the contour near \(s=\rho\). The integrand is then
\(|s-\rho|^{-2m_{\rho}-2}\), non-integrable on a one-dimensional
contour for \(m_{\rho}\geq 1\). Hardy's theorem gives infinitely many
zeros on the critical line. Hence the critical-line Hadamard residue
kernel is not Hilbert--Schmidt. Off-line zeros do not give this local
contour singularity; they are not needed for the conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Limits of the Hilbert--Schmidt obstruction]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:rem:unboundedness-intrinsic}
\Cref{v4-arith:w9-r4:lem:had-not-HS} rules out a Hilbert--Schmidt
Hadamard-residue kernel in the critical-line completion. A bounded
non-Hilbert--Schmidt operator is not thereby excluded. The unbounded
spectral-flow problem is the stronger demand imposed by the pole
residue prescription together with the zero-divisor determinant and
trace identities; it requires a closed densely defined operator and a
specified domain.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The Domain Problem}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:obstacle-kernel:domain}

\begin{proposition}[Dirichlet-series and residue domains]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:prop:candidate-domains}
Finite-support sequences and sequences satisfying
\(|c_m|=O_N(m^{-N})\) for every \(N\) are dense subspaces of
\(L_w\). For the latter sequences,
\(F_c(s)=\sum_m c_m m^{s-1}\) is entire, with derivatives
converging normally on compact sets. This does not imply vertical
rapid decrease. The sequence-valued residue map on this class
lands in \(\ell^2\) exactly on the subspace
\[
 \mathcal D_R=\{c:\sum_\rho|r_\rho(F_c)|^2<\infty\},
\]
where one coordinate is used per distinct zero.
Density and closability of this restricted map require further
estimates.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Finite sequences lie in both source classes and are dense in
\(L_w\) by coordinate truncation. On a compact set with
\(\Re s\leq B\), each derivative is bounded termwise by
\(|c_m|m^{B-1}(\log m)^k\); choosing \(N>B+k+2\) proves normal
convergence. The sequence \(c_1=1,c_m=0\) for \(m>1\) gives
\(F_c=1\), refuting vertical rapid decrease. The final criterion
is the definition of the \(\ell^2\) norm. At multiple zeros,
residue extraction uses a finite Taylor jet; it does not itself
supply one independent coordinate per zero multiplicity.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Additional domain requirements]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:rem:no-domain-gives-SA}
A different Mellin growth space, including a Beurling-type space,
requires an explicitly defined norm and comparison map before it
can replace \(\mathcal D_R\). Coefficient decay alone supplies
neither the residue estimates nor a generator intertwiner.
For any closed densely defined operator \(A\), \(A^*A\) is
self-adjoint and the polar decomposition exists. These general
facts do not imply that its partial isometry preserves the source
and target generators or has the required arithmetic range.
\end{remark}

\section{Adele-Class and Virtual Trace Constructions}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:connes-1999}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:connes-1999:construction}

\begin{definition}[Weighted adele-class comparison]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:def:connes-data}
For \(\delta>1\), use the map
\(E\phi(x)=|x|^{1/2}\sum_{q\ne0}\phi(qx)\) and Hilbert seminorm
of \Cref{v4-arith:w7-j:def:connes-ac-space}.
Its completion embeds isometrically in
\(L^2(C_{\mathbb Q},(1+\log^2|x|)^{\delta/2}d^\times x)\).
The cokernel of this closed image carries the quotient of the
left regular idele-class representation.
The norm-direction action is polynomially bounded; this weighted
representation is not asserted to be unitary.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Critical-zero trace]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:thm:connes-trace-eq}
Connes's \S III, Theorem~1 and Corollary~2, pp.~13--14,
identify the quotient spectrum and integrated trace with the
critical zeros, with the \(\delta\)-dependent multiplicities
stated in \Cref{v4-arith:w7-j:prop:connes-absorption}.
This is a statement about that quotient representation.
It does not identify the full critical BC generator with its
scaling generator.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Global trace comparison}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:connes-1999:limitation}
\begin{definition}[A full arithmetic trace identity]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:thm:connes-absorption}
For a specified trace construction, let
\(\mathcal T:\mathscr A\to\mathbb C\) be its distribution on
compact smooth logarithmic tests. The full comparison requirement is
\[
 \mathcal T(f)=P(f)-W_\infty(f)-W_{\mathrm{fin}}(f)
 \qquad(f\in\mathscr A).
\]
If a cutoff or regularized trace defines \(\mathcal T\), its
subtraction, convergence, and normalization are part of the
construction. The positivity requirement is separately
\(\mathcal T(f*f^*)\geq0\) for every \(f\in\mathscr A\).
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[The source map]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:rem:connes-not-uncond}
The identity map is not a map between the BC GNS space and
Connes's quotient Hilbert space. To compare their dynamics one
must specify a map and its domain. The eigenvector obstruction
excludes an injective exact intertwiner on the BC eigenvector
core with a zero-divisor target. Connes's global cutoff comparison
in \S VIII is distinct from the critical-zero quotient theorem.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Trace comparison and positivity]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:prop:connes-eq-HPAr}
Under the full trace identity above, positivity of \(\mathcal T\)
on convolution squares is equivalent to Weil positivity, and
hence to RH. This implication does not construct an intertwiner.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply the identity to \(f*f^*\) and use
\Cref{v4-arith:w5-a:cor:weil-positivity-RH}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Weighted and unweighted spaces]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:rem:adele-transfers}
Connes's completion uses a Schwartz summation seminorm and a
cokernel. It is not quotient Haar \(L^2\) on a non-Hausdorff space.
A comparison with the unweighted idele space must account for
this weight, quotient, and the action on the quotient.
\end{remark}

\section{Meyer's Virtual Character}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:meyer-2005}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:meyer-2005:construction}
\begin{definition}[Virtual representations]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:def:meyer-data}
A virtual representation \(\pi_+\ominus\pi_-\) has character
\(\operatorname{Tr}\pi_+(a)-\operatorname{Tr}\pi_-(a)\)
on test functions for which both integrated operators have a trace.
In Meyer's construction the carriers are smooth locally convex
representations with the summability structure of his \S5.
They are not defined as a single self-adjoint perturbation pair
on \(L^2(C_{\mathbb Q})\).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The virtual Weil character]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:thm:meyer-intertwiner}
Meyer's Theorems~5.8 and~5.11 identify the character of his smooth
summable virtual representation with the raw Weil distribution.
Its spectral multiplicities are the orders of poles minus the
orders of zeros of the relevant \(L\)-functions.
\end{theorem}
The source is \href{https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0311468}{Meyer,
pp.~43 and~47}. His Proposition~4.4, p.~26, instead concerns
Schwartz restriction and Fourier maps.

\subsection{Positive and signed traces}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:meyer-2005:limitation}
\begin{remark}[A character is not an operator intertwiner]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:rem:meyer-not-A}
The virtual character theorem supplies no unitary
\(\mathcal H_1\to L^2(\mathbb R)\) intertwining the BC generator
with a zero-divisor operator. Its signed trace also supplies no
positivity theorem for the difference. The central twist and
sign converting its raw character to \(W\) must be specified
before using the normalized Weil criterion.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[A positive trace comparison]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:thm:meyer-eq-HPAr}
Let \(H\) be self-adjoint. Suppose that, for every
\(f\in\mathscr A\), \(|h_f|^2(H)\) is trace class and
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(|h_f|^2(H))=W(f*f^*).
\]
Then Weil positivity holds. The hypothesis includes the full
arithmetic trace identity; it is not supplied by a virtual
character formula.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The spectral theorem makes \(|h_f|^2(H)\) positive.
A positive trace-class operator has nonnegative trace.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Trace ideals and summability]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:rem:meyer-schwartz-only}
Smooth summability on locally convex representation spaces and
trace-class differences on a Hilbert space require different
operator estimates. Neither condition changes point spectrum
into continuous spectrum under an exact intertwiner.
\end{remark}

\section{Obstructions to the Proposed Operator Comparisons}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:residual}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:residual:statement}
\begin{theorem}[Specified comparison obstructions]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:thm:irreducible-residual}
An injective intertwiner on the full BC or occupation eigenvector
core cannot produce the zero-divisor target.
The common tensor construction
\(M_\tau\otimes1+1\otimes N_{\mathrm{BC}}\) has no full ordinary wave
operators relative to \(M_\tau\otimes1\).
The literal entire-Mellin class \(\mathrm{PW}^{\mathrm{TM}}\) is zero.
These statements restrict the displayed constructions, not all
possible arithmetic realizations.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The first assertion is the eigenvector proposition.
The other two are proved in
\Cref{v4-arith:w7-j:prop:tensor-wave-obstruction} and
\Cref{v4-arith:w7-j:thm:ATP-TM-closure}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The complete Weil form]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:rem:residual-at-infty}
The finite-prime functional is explicit, but its explicit
calculation is not an absorption into a positive trace.
The condition remains
\(P(f*f^*)-W_\infty(f*f^*)-W_{\mathrm{fin}}(f*f^*)\geq0\)
for every compact smooth complex test. A local functional equation
or an idele scaling phase does not imply this inequality.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Maps and domains}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:residual:table}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:residual:why-irreducible}
\begin{proposition}[Limits of the obstructions]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:thm:irreducibility}
The eigenvector obstruction applies whenever the deciding
zero-energy vector is in the domain of an injective intertwiner.
It does not exclude a densely defined map whose domain omits that
vector, a noninjective map, or a comparison preserving a
specified distribution rather than the generator.
Each alternative requires its own domain and trace theorem.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The obstruction uses \(HAv=ANv=0\) and \(Av\ne0\).
Removing either hypothesis removes that inference. No
existence assertion follows from removing it.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}[The arithmetic realization question]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:rem:beilinson-closure}
A new construction must state its carrier and preserved operation.
The distributional explicit formula remains valid independently
of these failed generator transports. A general impossibility
claim for other arithmetic trace constructions does not follow.
\end{remark}

\section{Comparison with Cohomological and Scattering Models}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:placement}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:placement:deninger}
\begin{proposition}[Transport of a specified cohomological operator]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:prop:spectral-flow-eq-deninger}
Suppose a cohomological realization supplies a Hilbert space
\(H_D\), a self-adjoint \(D\), and a unitary \(U:H_D\to H'\).
Then \(UDU^{-1}\), on \(U\operatorname{Dom}D\), has the same
spectral multiplicities and trace functional as \(D\).
Any spectral determinant construction invariant under unitary
conjugation has the same normalization after this transport.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Functional calculus gives \(h(UDU^{-1})=Uh(D)U^{-1}\).
It identifies spectral projections and preserves traces of
trace-class operators. Apply the assumed determinant prescription
to the identical spectral data.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}[Cohomological comparisons]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:rem:deninger-renames}
A Deninger-type cohomological construction must supply the space,
operator, and comparison maps in addition to its determinant
identity. The proposition does not construct those data or
identify its generator with the full BC generator.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Scattering and signed traces}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:placement:atp}
\begin{proposition}[Scattering does not force Weil positivity]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:prop:spectral-flow-eq-atp}
Complete unitary scattering does not imply positivity of a trace
difference on Fourier squares. An ordinary trace-class spectral
shift giving a positive trace difference on every such square
must vanish.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The pair \(M_\lambda\oplus0,M_\lambda\oplus1\) has scattering
operator one, but trace difference \(h(1)-h(0)\).
For \(f(u)=\phi(u)+\phi(u-\pi)\), \(\int\phi=1\), and
\(h=|h_f|^2\), this is \(-4\).
The general vanishing statement is
\Cref{v4-arith:w7-j:thm:ATP-iff-S-unitary}.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}[The arithmetic test identity]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:rem:scattering-transfers}
Since \(\mathrm{PW}^{\mathrm{TM}}=\{0\}\), it gives no nonzero
class from which Weil positivity can be extended.
The proposed arithmetic trace must be constructed on all of
\(\mathscr A\), retaining the polar, archimedean, and finite-prime
terms. Its operators, domains, and regularization are additional
data, as in \Cref{v4-arith:w7-j:thm:obstruction-located}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Bulk observables and parameter derivatives}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:placement:acs}
\begin{proposition}[A scalar partition function does not fix its generator]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:prop:spectral-flow-eq-acs}
Equality of scalar partition functions does not by itself
identify the operator derivative in a family on a fixed Hilbert
space. Even finite-dimensional unitarily conjugate families can
have identical partition functions and different derivatives.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let \(D=\operatorname{diag}(0,1)\),
\(K=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\),
and \(D(s)=e^{isK}De^{-isK}\).
For every real \(s\) and \(\beta>0\),
\(\operatorname{Tr}e^{-\beta D(s)}=1+e^{-\beta}\),
whereas \(D'(0)=i[K,D]\ne0\). The constant family \(D\)
has the same traces and derivative zero.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}[The required bulk comparison]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:rem:acs-wilson}
An arithmetic Chern--Simons bulk construction must identify its
state space, observable, and domain before comparing a parameter
derivative with an ordinate operator. A partition identity alone
supplies none of these maps. The Hochschild trace class
\(\mathcal V^{\mathrm{prim}}\) is not, by itself, a vertex algebra or
a Hilbert-space generator.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Positive spectral realization}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:placement:unified}
\begin{theorem}[Positive trace implies the Weil inequality]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:thm:unified-residual}
A self-adjoint operator with the trace identity of
\Cref{v4-arith:w9-r4:thm:meyer-eq-HPAr} gives Weil positivity
and therefore RH. In an ordinary trace-class difference model,
the same positive nonzero Weil identity is impossible.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The positive trace proposition and the Weil criterion prove the
first assertion. The second is
\Cref{v4-arith:w7-j:thm:weil-as-shift}.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}[Separate realization requirements]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:rem:honest-summary}
A virtual character, a scattering determinant, a positive trace,
and a normalized spectral determinant are different data.
A comparison must specify its maps, domains, and test identities.
For a regularized trace, positivity must be proved separately.
The Dirac double below concerns closure of a specified operator;
it does not construct those arithmetic comparisons.
\end{remark}

\section{Polyakov--Dirac Normal Form for the Residual}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:polyakov-dirac-normal-form}

A scalar normalization does not determine an operator domain or
a spectral measure. The Dirac double tests essential self-adjointness
of an independently specified symmetric operator. Its determinant
and arithmetic trace remain separate conditions.

\subsection{Polyakov Rigidity of the Rank Residue}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:pdnf:polyakov}

\begin{proposition}[Polyakov rank-residue rigidity]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:prop:polyakov-scalar-rigidity}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere In the primary Heisenberg domain of
Chapters~\ref{v4-arith:polyakov} and
\ref{v4-arith:w9-r3:chapter}, any spectral-flow closure compatible
with
\begin{enumerate}
\item the rank-\(2\) per-prime Sugawara Heisenberg stress tensor,
\item the global Fock partition
\(Z(s)=\prod_{p}\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-p^{-sn})^{-2}\), and
\item the archimedean Polyakov anomaly of the rank-\(2\) free-field
completion,
\end{enumerate}
has determinant rank residue
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:eq:polyakov-rigid-c}
r_{\zeta}\;=\;2.
\end{equation}
This scalar identity supplies no Hilbert carrier, generator-domain
map, or zero-divisor determinant.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{v4-arith:w9-r3:thm:main} proves that the unrenormalised conformal scalar
\(\sum_{p}c^{(p)}\) diverges, that the negative global modes do not act
on the restricted vacuum, and that the global Fock-partition residue
gives \(r_{\zeta}=2\). The same chapter proves the Polyakov
rank comparison
\Cref{v4-arith:w9-r3:thm:polyakov-identity}, identifying this
finite-prime residue with the rank-\(2\) archimedean conformal
anomaly. These inputs fix the rank residue. They do not fix an
entire exponential factor in a spectral determinant:
multiplication by \(e^{a+bs}\) changes its logarithmic derivative
by \(b\), whose residue is zero. The normalization of a spectral
determinant remains separate from this rank comparison.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[what Polyakov does and does not buy]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:rem:polyakov-no-rh}
Polyakov regularisation is decisive for the determinant rank,
not for RH. It supplies the finite scalar \(r_{\zeta}=2\) and
rules out the divergent prime-zeta prescription; it does not
produce the Hilbert space on which the zero ordinates are a
self-adjoint spectrum.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Dirac Closure of the Remaining Operator}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:pdnf:dirac}

\begin{definition}[A specified symmetric core]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:def:residual-ordinate-core}
Choose a complex Hilbert space \(\mathcal H_{\mathrm{res}}\), a dense
linear subspace \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{res}}\), and a symmetric operator
\(H_{\mathrm{res}}^{\mathrm{sym}}\) with exactly that domain.
Inner products are linear in the first variable.
These are independently specified operator data. A multiplication
chart or a residue map is additional structure, with its own domain
requirements. No comparison with BC or with a zero-divisor operator
is part of this definition.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Dirac double of the residual]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:def:dirac-double}
\ClaimStatusDefinitional The \emph{Dirac double} of the residual
ordinate core is the odd symmetric operator
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:eq:dirac-double}
\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{res}}^{0}
\;:=\;
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & H_{\mathrm{res}}^{\mathrm{sym}}\\
H_{\mathrm{res}}^{\mathrm{sym}} & 0
\end{pmatrix}
\quad\text{on}\quad
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{res}}\oplus\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{res}},
\end{equation}
with grading
\(\Gamma=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\). A \emph{Dirac closure} is a
self-adjoint closure
\(\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{res}}=\overline{\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{res}}^{0}}\)
whose spectral measure satisfies the regularised determinant
identity
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:eq:dirac-det}
\det\nolimits_{\infty}\bigl(s\cdot\mathrm{id}
-({\tfrac12}\mathrm{id}+iH_{\mathrm{res}})\bigr)
\;=\;\xi_{\mathbb{R}}(s),
\end{equation}
where \(H_{\mathrm{res}}\) is the off-diagonal component recovered
from \(\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{res}}\) by the grading.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Dirac double detects self-adjointness]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:lem:dirac-double-detects-sa}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere Let \(H\) be a densely defined symmetric
operator on a Hilbert space \(\mathcal{H}\) and
\(\mathscr{D}_{H}^{0}=
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&H\\H&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\)
on \(\mathcal{D}(H)\oplus\mathcal{D}(H)\). Then
\(\mathscr{D}_{H}^{0}\) is essentially self-adjoint if and only if
\(H\) is essentially self-adjoint. When this holds,
\[
\overline{\mathscr{D}_{H}^{0}}
\;=\;
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & \overline{H}\\
\overline{H} & 0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\overline{\mathscr{D}_{H}^{0}}^{\,2}
\;=\;
\begin{pmatrix}
\overline{H}^{\,2} & 0\\
0 & \overline{H}^{\,2}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a symmetric \(H\),
\((\mathscr{D}_{H}^{0})^{*}=
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&H^{*}\\H^{*}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) on
\(\mathcal{D}(H^{*})\oplus\mathcal{D}(H^{*})\). Hence the closure of
\(\mathscr{D}_{H}^{0}\) is self-adjoint exactly when the closure of
\(H\) has adjoint domain equal to its own domain, i.e.\ exactly when
\(H\) is essentially self-adjoint. The displayed square is the usual
block-matrix product on
\(\operatorname{Dom}(\overline H^{\,2})
 \oplus\operatorname{Dom}(\overline H^{\,2})\), justified after
closure by the spectral theorem for the self-adjoint operator
\(\overline{H}\) (Reed--Simon I, Ch.~VIII).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Dirac double of a specified operator]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:thm:polyakov-dirac-normal-form}
For the specified \(H_{\mathrm{res}}^{\mathrm{sym}}\), essential
self-adjointness is equivalent to essential self-adjointness of
its Dirac double. If these conditions hold, requiring the closure
to have the normalized determinant
\eqref{v4-arith:w9-r4:eq:dirac-det} is exactly the additional
determinant condition in the definition of Dirac closure.
This equivalence concerns this operator and this domain.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply \Cref{v4-arith:w9-r4:lem:dirac-double-detects-sa}.
The off-diagonal component of the closed double is the closure
of the original symmetric operator. Substituting that component
in the determinant definition proves the remaining assertion.
Existence of a different Hilbert--P\'olya operator supplies no
core or unitary comparison with this specified one.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Closure and determinant]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:rem:single-estimate-left}
The squared graph norm \(\|u\|^2+\|H_{\mathrm{res}}^{\mathrm{sym}}u\|^2\)
defines the graph completion. Essential self-adjointness asks
whether the closure equals its adjoint. A determinant identity
requires an admissible spectral determinant and its normalization;
it is not a consequence of graph completeness.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[closed Tate-polar operator quotient]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:def:tate-polar-operator-quotient}
\ClaimStatusConditional A \emph{closed Tate-polar operator quotient}
for \(H_{0}=H_{\mathrm{res}}^{\mathrm{sym}}\) is additional data of a
Hilbert quotient
\[
\pi\colon \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{res}}\longrightarrow
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{res}}^{\circ}
\]
with closed finite-rank kernel \(P_{\mathrm{Tate}}\), an induced
dense domain
\[
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{res}}^{\circ}:=\pi(\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{res}}),
\]
and a symmetric operator \(H_{0}^{\circ}\) on
\(\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{res}}^{\circ}\) satisfying
\[
H_{0}^{\circ}\pi u=\pi H_{0}u,\qquad u\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{res}}.
\]
The quotient is required to be compatible with closure and adjoint
domains:
\[
\mathcal{D}(\overline{H_{0}^{\circ}})
=\pi\mathcal{D}(\overline{H_{0}}),
\qquad
\mathcal{D}((H_{0}^{\circ})^{*})
=\pi\mathcal{D}(H_{0}^{*}),
\]
after completing both sides in their graph norms. It is also required
to admit a separately specified unitary
\(U_{\mathrm{res}}:\mathcal H_{\mathrm{res}}^\circ\to L^2(\mathbb R)\)
that represents the quotient operator by multiplication on its
image domain, in the sense of
\Cref{v4-arith:w9-r4:def:meyer-coordinate-core}.
No pre-quotient unitary is presupposed.

The existence of this quotient and compatibility data is a
separate hypothesis.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[post-quotient notation]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:rem:post-tate-notation}
\ClaimStatusDefinitional In the boundary and deficiency discussion
below,
\[
T_{0}:=H_{0}^{\circ}\colon
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{res}}^{\circ}\subset
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{res}}^{\circ}\longrightarrow
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{res}}^{\circ}
\]
denotes the post-Tate residual operator supplied by
\Cref{v4-arith:w9-r4:def:tate-polar-operator-quotient}. The
pre-quotient operator \(H_{0}=H_{\mathrm{res}}^{\mathrm{sym}}\) is only
the source operator. Thus every adjoint, deficiency kernel, boundary
quotient, and multiplication-coordinate graph statement below is a statement
about \(T_{0}\). Without the closed Tate-polar quotient, these formulas
are well-formed as conditional operator theory but not as an
unconditional construction.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Boundary Form and Deficiency Kernels}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:pdnf:what-remains}

The boundary quotient tests essential self-adjointness of the
specified operator. Rank-residue normalization does not determine
that quotient, the spectral determinant, or the arithmetic trace.

\begin{definition}[residual boundary form]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:def:residual-boundary-form}
\ClaimStatusDefinitional Under the post-quotient notation of
\Cref{v4-arith:w9-r4:rem:post-tate-notation}, the adjoint-domain
boundary form is the Hermitian symplectic form
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:eq:boundary-form}
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{res}}(u,v)
\;:=\;
\langle T_{0}^{*}u,v\rangle-\langle u,T_{0}^{*}v\rangle,
\qquad
u,v\in\mathcal{D}(T_{0}^{*}).
\end{equation}
The \emph{residual boundary space} is the quotient
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:eq:boundary-space}
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{res}}
\;:=\;
\mathcal{D}(T_{0}^{*})/\mathcal{D}(\overline{T_{0}}),
\end{equation}
with the induced form still denoted \(\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{res}}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Boundary quotient and essential self-adjointness]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:prop:polyakov-boundary-target}
For the specified densely defined symmetric \(T_0\),
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:eq:polyakov-boundary-target}
 \mathcal Q_{\mathrm{res}}=0
\end{equation}
is equivalent to essential self-adjointness.
Vanishing of the boundary form on the original domain alone
asserts only symmetry. Identifying this operator boundary space
with a BV--BFV boundary space requires a separate map preserving
the forms and domains.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The closure is self-adjoint exactly when
\(\operatorname{Dom}\overline T_0=\operatorname{Dom}T_0^*\),
which is the zero-quotient condition. Symmetry already makes the
boundary form vanish on \(\operatorname{Dom}T_0\), regardless
of whether its adjoint domain is larger. Neither assertion
includes a determinant or arithmetic trace identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[deficiency spaces of the residual]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:def:deficiency-spaces}
\ClaimStatusDefinitional The residual deficiency spaces are
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:eq:deficiency-spaces}
\mathcal{N}_{+}:=\ker(T_{0}^{*}-i),
\qquad
\mathcal{N}_{-}:=\ker(T_{0}^{*}+i).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Dirac two-test form of the residual]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:thm:dirac-two-test}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere For this specified \(T_0\), a self-adjoint closure with the
stated determinant exists exactly when both conditions hold:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Deficiency vanishing:}
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:eq:deficiency-vanishing}
\mathcal{N}_{+}=0=\mathcal{N}_{-}.
\end{equation}
\item \emph{Determinant identity for the closure:}
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:eq:closure-det}
\det\nolimits_{\infty}
\bigl(s\cdot\mathrm{id}
-({\tfrac12}\mathrm{id}+i\overline{T_{0}})\bigr)
\;=\;
\xi_{\mathbb{R}}(s).
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
Define the post-quotient double
\[
 \mathscr D_{T_0}^{0}
 =\begin{pmatrix}0&T_0\\T_0&0\end{pmatrix}
 \quad\text{on }\mathcal C_{\mathrm{res}}^\circ
                  \oplus\mathcal C_{\mathrm{res}}^\circ.
\]
Equivalently, this Dirac double
\(\mathscr D_{T_0}^{0}\) has no deficiency states and its
closed off-diagonal component has determinant \(\xi_{\mathbb R}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By von Neumann's deficiency-index criterion, a densely defined
symmetric operator \(T_{0}\) is essentially self-adjoint if and only
if
\(\ker(T_{0}^{*}-i)=\ker(T_{0}^{*}+i)=0\). Thus
\eqref{v4-arith:w9-r4:eq:deficiency-vanishing} is exactly the
essential self-adjointness part of
\Cref{v4-arith:w9-r4:def:dirac-double}. By
\Cref{v4-arith:w9-r4:lem:dirac-double-detects-sa}, the same
condition is equivalent to essential self-adjointness of the Dirac
double \(\mathscr D_{T_0}^{0}\).

The regularized determinant is an additional datum for this
closure. Equation
\eqref{v4-arith:w9-r4:eq:closure-det} is imposed on
\(\overline T_0\), the off-diagonal component of the closed
post-quotient double. Combining the deficiency criterion with this
separate determinant identity proves the stated equivalence.
The pre-quotient double is a different operator. No existence theorem for another
operator identifies its domain with this one.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of deficiency vanishing]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:rem:smartest-next-calculation}
Vanishing of both deficiency kernels proves essential
self-adjointness of \(T_0\). The determinant condition remains
separate. In a unitary multiplication chart, deficiency vanishing
has the stronger spectral consequence proved below: the closure
is continuous multiplication and cannot realize a discrete
zero-eigenvalue divisor.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The Deficiency Calculation}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:pdnf:deficiency-calculation}

In multiplication coordinates the deficiency kernels are the orthogonal
complements of the ranges of \((M_t \pm i)\) on the residual core,
with the sign convention matching the conjugate-linear second slot in
the inner product. The computation applies to a specified residual
multiplication chart and the closed Tate-polar operator quotient of
\Cref{v4-arith:w9-r4:def:tate-polar-operator-quotient}; without those
it is conditional operator theory. The obstruction is a graph-core
question for the residual Hadamard domain, not an Euler-product
calculation.

\begin{definition}[multiplication-coordinate residual chart]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:def:meyer-coordinate-core}
\ClaimStatusDefinitional Let
\[
M_{t}\colon \mathcal{D}(M_{t})\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dt)\to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dt),\qquad (M_{t}f)(t)=t f(t),
\]
be the self-adjoint multiplication operator. A \emph{multiplication-coordinate
residual chart} is a unitary map, denoted \(U_{\mathrm{res}}\) by
abuse of notation,
\[
U_{\mathrm{res}}\colon\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{res}}^{\circ}
\longrightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dt),
\]
from the residual quotient Hilbert space of
\Cref{v4-arith:w9-r4:def:tate-polar-operator-quotient} such that the
multiplication-coordinate residual core is
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:eq:meyer-coordinate-core}
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{res}}^{M}
\;:=\;
U_{\mathrm{res}}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{res}}^{\circ})
\;\subset\;
\mathcal{D}(M_{t}),
\end{equation}
and
\[
T_{0}=H_{0}^{\circ}
\quad\text{is represented by}\quad
M_{t}|_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{res}}^{M}} .
\]
This chart is additional data. The virtual character theorem
\Cref{v4-arith:w9-r4:thm:meyer-intertwiner} does not supply it.
Once specified, it determines the graph and spectral-type
consequences below.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[deficiency kernels are range defects]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:thm:deficiency-range-defect}
\ClaimStatusProvedHereConditional Assume a multiplication-coordinate residual chart in
the sense of \Cref{v4-arith:w9-r4:def:meyer-coordinate-core}. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:eq:deficiency-range-defect-plus}
U_{\mathrm{res}}\mathcal{N}_{+}
\;=\;
\bigl((M_{t}+i)\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{res}}^{M}\bigr)^{\perp},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:eq:deficiency-range-defect-minus}
U_{\mathrm{res}}\mathcal{N}_{-}
\;=\;
\bigl((M_{t}-i)\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{res}}^{M}\bigr)^{\perp}.
\end{equation}
Consequently \(\mathcal{N}_{+}=0=\mathcal{N}_{-}\) if and only if
both ranges
\[
(M_{t}-i)\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{res}}^{M},
\qquad
(M_{t}+i)\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{res}}^{M}
\]
are dense in \(L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dt)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We prove the \(+\) identity; the \(-\) identity is identical with
\(i\) replaced by \(-i\). The inner product is linear in the first
slot and conjugate-linear in the second. By definition,
\(u\in\mathcal{N}_{+}\) means \(u\in\mathcal{D}(T_{0}^{*})\) and
\((T_{0}^{*}-i)u=0\). Equivalently, for every
\(f\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{res}}^{\circ}\),
\[
\langle T_{0}f,u\rangle
\;=\;
\langle f,iu\rangle .
\]
Since the second slot is conjugate-linear,
\(\langle f,iu\rangle=-i\langle f,u\rangle\).
Transport by \(U_{\mathrm{res}}\). Write
\(\tilde u=U_{\mathrm{res}}u\) and
\(\tilde f=U_{\mathrm{res}}f\). Since \(T_{0}\) is represented by
\(M_{t}\) on \(\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{res}}^{M}\), the adjoint equation
becomes
\[
\langle M_{t}\tilde f,\tilde u\rangle
\;=\;
\langle \tilde f,i\tilde u\rangle ,
\qquad
\tilde f\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{res}}^{M}.
\]
Hence
\[
\langle (M_{t}+i)\tilde f,\tilde u\rangle=0
\qquad
\text{for all }\tilde f\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{res}}^{M},
\]
which says precisely that
\(\tilde u\in((M_{t}+i)\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{res}}^{M})^{\perp}\).
The converse is the same implication read backwards, so
\eqref{v4-arith:w9-r4:eq:deficiency-range-defect-plus} follows.
The density criterion follows because a subspace has zero orthogonal
complement exactly when its closure is the full Hilbert space.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[range density is graph density for \(M_t\)]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:cor:bulk-deficiency-graph-core}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere For a subspace
\(\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{D}(M_t)\), the following are equivalent:
\[
\overline{\mathcal{C}}^{\,\|\cdot\|_{M_t}}=\mathcal{D}(M_t),
\qquad
\overline{(M_t-i)\mathcal{C}}^{\,L^2}=L^2(\mathbb R,dt),
\qquad
\overline{(M_t+i)\mathcal{C}}^{\,L^2}=L^2(\mathbb R,dt).
\]
Consequently, under the residual multiplication chart hypothesis,
\(\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{res}}^{M}\) is a graph core for \(M_t\) if and
only if
\[
\mathcal{N}_{+}=0=\mathcal{N}_{-}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For \(\operatorname{Im}z\neq0\), the map
\[
M_{t}-z\colon \mathcal{D}(M_{t})_{\mathrm{graph}}\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dt)
\]
is boundedly invertible: its inverse is multiplication by
\((t-z)^{-1}\), which maps \(L^{2}\) into \(\mathcal{D}(M_{t})\) with
bounded graph norm. Hence a subspace is dense in the graph norm if
and only if its image under \(M_t-z\) is dense in \(L^2\). Apply this
with \(z=i\) and \(z=-i\), then use
\Cref{v4-arith:w9-r4:thm:deficiency-range-defect}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[rank-one graph-boundary model]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:lem:rank-one-graph-boundary}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere Let
\(\psi(t)=(1+|t|)^{-1}\). Then
\(\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dt)\) but
\(\psi\notin\mathcal{D}(M_t)\). Define
\[
\mathcal{C}_{\psi}
:=
\{\,f\in\mathcal{D}(M_t)\mid
\langle (M_t-i)f,\psi\rangle=0\,\}.
\]
Then \(\mathcal{C}_{\psi}\) is \(L^{2}\)-dense and graph-closed of
codimension one, but it is not graph-dense. Its range defects are
\[
\bigl((M_t-i)\mathcal{C}_{\psi}\bigr)^{\perp}
=\mathbb C\psi,
\qquad
\bigl((M_t+i)\mathcal{C}_{\psi}\bigr)^{\perp}
=
\mathbb C\,\frac{t+i}{t-i}\psi .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The function \(\psi\) is square-integrable, while
\(t\psi(t)\) is not square-integrable. Since
\[
M_t-i\colon\mathcal{D}(M_t)_{\mathrm{graph}}\to L^2(\mathbb R,dt)
\]
is a bounded isomorphism, \(\mathcal{C}_{\psi}\) is the inverse image
of the closed hyperplane \(\psi^\perp\); hence it is graph-closed of
codimension one and its first range defect is \(\mathbb C\psi\).
The defining functional is not continuous for the \(L^2\)-topology on
\(\mathcal{D}(M_t)\), because such continuity would represent it by
an \(L^2\)-vector, equivalently would put \(\psi\) in
\(\mathcal{D}(M_t)\). Therefore its kernel is \(L^2\)-dense.

For the second range, write
\[
(M_t+i)(M_t-i)^{-1}
\]
as multiplication by \((t+i)/(t-i)\), a unitary multiplier on \(L^2\).
The orthogonal complement of its image of \(\psi^\perp\) is the span
of \((t+i)(t-i)^{-1}\psi\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[point conditions are graph-invisible]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:lem:point-conditions-graph-invisible}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere Let \(\Gamma\subset\mathbb R\) be locally
finite. Then \(C_c^\infty(\mathbb R\setminus\Gamma)\) is graph-dense
in \(\mathcal{D}(M_t)\). In particular, a boundary condition that is
only pointwise at the zero ordinates cannot obstruct graph density for
the ordinary \(M_t\)-graph norm.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The graph norm of \(M_t\) is the \(L^2\)-norm for the measure
\((1+t^2)\,dt\). Locally finite sets have zero measure for this
weight. Given \(f\in\mathcal{D}(M_t)\), truncate \(f\) to a compact
interval, remove intervals of arbitrarily small weighted measure
around the finitely many points of \(\Gamma\) in that interval, and
then mollify. The approximants lie in
\(C_c^\infty(\mathbb R\setminus\Gamma)\) and converge in the graph
norm.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Hadamard-supported graph functional]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:def:hadamard-supported-graph-functional}
\ClaimStatusDefinitional Let
\[
\Gamma_{\xi}^{\mathrm{crit}}:=\{\,\gamma\in\mathbb R\mid
\tfrac12+i\gamma\text{ is a non-trivial zero of }\zeta\,\}
\]
with multiplicities forgotten. This is the real critical-line
ordinate support relevant to the Hilbert--P\'olya target; it is not an
assertion that the full Hadamard divisor has already been realised on
\(\mathbb R\). A continuous functional
\[
\beta\colon \mathcal{D}(M_t)_{\mathrm{graph}}\longrightarrow\mathbb C
\]
is \emph{Hadamard-supported on the zero ordinates} if
\[
\beta(\phi)=0
\qquad
\text{for every }
\phi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R\setminus\Gamma_{\xi}^{\mathrm{crit}}).
\]
This is the graph-norm formulation of the idea that the boundary
condition sees only point or finite-jet Hadamard residue data at the
zero ordinates.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[no Hadamard residue functional in the ordinary graph dual]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:thm:no-hadamard-graph-dual}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere The ordinary \(M_t\)-graph dual contains no
non-zero Hadamard-supported functional. Equivalently,
\[
\beta\in(\mathcal{D}(M_t)_{\mathrm{graph}})^{*},
\qquad
\operatorname{supp}\beta\subset\Gamma_{\xi}^{\mathrm{crit}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\beta=0
\]
in the sense of
\Cref{v4-arith:w9-r4:def:hadamard-supported-graph-functional}.
Thus there is no non-zero
\[
\beta_{\mathrm{Had}}\colon \mathcal{D}(M_t)_{\mathrm{graph}}\to\mathbb C
\]
whose graph-closed kernel is exactly a Hadamard-residue boundary
condition supported only at the zero ordinates. The statement also
rules out finite-jet residue data at those ordinates, if such data are
asked to be continuous for the ordinary \(M_t\)-graph topology.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The non-trivial zeros of \(\zeta\) form a discrete subset of the
critical strip, and only finitely many have ordinate in a bounded
interval. Hence \(\Gamma_{\xi}^{\mathrm{crit}}\) is locally finite as a subset of
\(\mathbb R\). By
\Cref{v4-arith:w9-r4:lem:point-conditions-graph-invisible},
\[
C_c^\infty(\mathbb R\setminus\Gamma_{\xi}^{\mathrm{crit}})
\]
is dense in \(\mathcal{D}(M_t)\) for the graph norm. If
\(\beta\) is continuous for the graph norm and vanishes on this dense
subspace, it vanishes on all of \(\mathcal{D}(M_t)\).

Equivalently, the graph norm is the \(L^2\)-norm for the weighted
measure \((1+t^2)\,dt\), so the graph dual is represented by weighted
\(L^2\)-functions, or, after the isometric resolvent chart
\[
M_t-i\colon \mathcal{D}(M_t)_{\mathrm{graph}}\xrightarrow{\sim}
L^2(\mathbb R,dt),
\]
by \(L^2\)-vectors. No non-zero \(L^2\)-vector is supported on the
locally finite set \(\Gamma_{\xi}^{\mathrm{crit}}\), which has Lebesgue measure zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Hadamard residue data is graph-invisible]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:cor:beta-had-no-go}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere In the ordinary multiplication graph topology,
\(\beta_{\mathrm{Had}}\) does not exist as a non-zero graph-dual
boundary functional. Therefore a boundary condition whose only data are
point or finite-jet Hadamard residues at the locally finite real
ordinate set \(\Gamma_{\xi}^{\mathrm{crit}}\) is graph-invisible: it
cannot by itself obstruct graph density of
\(\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{res}}^{M}\) in \(\mathcal{D}(M_t)\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[surviving branches after the graph-dual no-go]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:rem:beta-had-surviving-branches}
The preceding corollary is not an exhaustive boundary-state
classification. It eliminates only the ordinary point-supported graph-dual path. Assuming
the closed Tate-polar quotient and residual multiplication chart have been
constructed, a surviving operator obstruction must take a different form.
The known nonexclusive presentations are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the exact Hadamard residue or finite-jet trace lives in a stronger
rigged, Paley--Wiener, de Branges, or reproducing-kernel topology
\(\Phi_{\xi}\subset\mathcal{D}(M_t)_{\mathrm{graph}}\), where point
evaluation is continuous;
\item the ordinary graph obstruction is nonlocal, represented under the
resolvent chart by an \(L^2\)-vector or closed \(L^2\)-subspace as in
\Cref{v4-arith:w9-r4:lem:rank-one-graph-boundary}, and is therefore
not point-residue data;
\item a physical Ward/BV--BFV argument supplies extra boundary phase
space not yet identified with
\(\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{res}}\); this is a physics residual, not an
operator-theoretic graph-dual boundary functional.
\end{enumerate}
These are different possible constructions. No equivalence between
them, or with Weil positivity, follows from the graph-dual theorem. The
stronger-topology approach would require, at minimum, a continuous
Hadamard trace-and-jet map
\(\Phi_{\xi}\to\ell^{2}(\Gamma_{\xi}^{\mathrm{crit}},w)\), compatibility
with the post-Tate quotient and adjoint domains, and a proof that the
resulting closed condition is not a tautological insertion of the zero
divisor.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Graph density fixes the closure]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:rem:deficiency-calculation-answer}
In a specified unitary multiplication chart, graph density gives
\(\overline T_0\) unitarily equivalent to \(M_t\).
Its spectral measures are then absolutely continuous.
Point-supported residue functionals are absent from the ordinary
graph dual; nonlocal graph conditions can have nonzero deficiency,
as the rank-one graph-boundary example shows. Neither outcome
constructs a zero-divisor operator on this chart.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Graph Closure and Spectral Type}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:pdnf:execution-attempt}

The ordinary multiplication closure has continuous spectrum.
The diagonal zero-space construction instead uses zeros as input.
The following comparison makes their different spectral types explicit.

\begin{lemma}[classical multiplication-line graph closure]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:lem:meyer-line-graph-closure}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere Let
\[
M_{t}^{0}:C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dt)
\longrightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dt),
\qquad
(M_{t}^{0}f)(t)=t f(t).
\]
Then \(M_{t}^{0}\) is essentially self-adjoint. Its graph-norm
closure is multiplication by \(t\) on
\[
\mathcal{D}(M_{t})
\;=\;
\{\,f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dt)\mid t f(t)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dt)\,\}.
\]
The spectrum is the absolutely continuous spectrum
\(\mathrm{Spec}(M_{t})=\mathbb{R}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The operator \(M_{t}\) with the displayed maximal domain is
self-adjoint by the spectral theorem for multiplication operators.
The core \(C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\) is dense in the graph norm:
truncate \(f\) by compactly supported cutoffs and then mollify; both
\(f\) and \(t f\) converge in \(L^{2}\). Hence the closure of
\(M_{t}^{0}\) is \(M_{t}\). Since \(M_{t}\) is multiplication by the
coordinate on \(L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dt)\), its spectrum is the essential
range of \(t\), namely \(\mathbb{R}\), with absolutely continuous
spectral measure.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[why the classical closure is insufficient]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:prop:classical-closure-insufficient}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere The closure of
\Cref{v4-arith:w9-r4:lem:meyer-line-graph-closure} does not solve
the spectral-flow problem. Its graph norm is complete and its
operator is self-adjoint, but its spectrum is continuous
\(\mathbb{R}\), not a divisor with zero multiplicities of
\(\xi_{\mathbb R}\).
Consequently it cannot satisfy
\[
\det\nolimits_{\infty}
\bigl(s\cdot\mathrm{id}-({\tfrac12}\mathrm{id}+iM_{t})\bigr)
=\xi_{\mathbb{R}}(s)
\]
as a zero-eigenvalue determinant.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The determinant identity in
\Cref{v4-arith:w9-r4:eq:closure-det} has divisor
\(\mathrm{div}\,\xi_{\mathbb R}\). The multiplication operator
\(M_{t}\) on \(L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dt)\) has Lebesgue spectral measure on
the line, not this determinant divisor. An eigenvector would have to be supported on a singleton, which
has Lebesgue measure zero. This excludes a determinant whose zeros
are precisely finite-multiplicity eigenvalues of this operator.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[formal zero-space closure is tautological]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:prop:formal-zero-closure-tautological}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere On the formal zero Hilbert space
\(\mathcal{H}_{\xi}^{\mathrm{formal}}\) of
\Cref{v4-arith:w9-r4:def:target-data}, the diagonal ordinate
operator has graph-norm closure. Its projected operator
\(\Theta_{\xi}^{\mathrm{proj}}\) satisfies the regularised
determinant identity for \(\xi_{\mathbb R}\) only if the original
zero divisor already lies on the critical line. This proves no
non-formal spectral-flow closure.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The finite-support diagonal operator on
\(\bigoplus_{\rho\in\mathcal Z}\mathbb{C}\) closes in the graph norm to
the maximal diagonal multiplication operator by the real numbers
\(t_{\rho}=\mathrm{Im}(\rho)\); its closure is self-adjoint. The
operator \(\Theta_{\xi}^{\mathrm{proj}}=\tfrac12+iH_{\mathrm{ord}}^{\mathrm{formal}}\)
has spectral divisor
\(\{\tfrac12+it_{\rho}\}\). Hadamard factorisation identifies its
regularised determinant with \(\xi_{\mathbb R}\) only when
\(\beta_{\rho}=1/2\) for every \(\rho\). Otherwise it is the
determinant of the projected divisor, not of \(\xi_{\mathbb R}\).
The construction has therefore used the zero divisor and then
discarded its real-part defect. It supplies no Bost--Connes, Meyer,
Connes, Deninger, or chiral-homological source for the Hilbert space
or the Weil--Connes trace; it is the formal target explicitly excluded
by \Cref{v4-arith:w9-r4:rem:why-formal}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Spectral type forced by a multiplication chart]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:thm:execution-result}
Let \(T_0\) be densely defined and symmetric. Suppose a unitary
\(U:\mathcal H\to L^2(\mathbb R,dt)\) satisfies
\(UT_0U^{-1}=M_t|_{\mathcal C}\), with
\(\mathcal C\subset\operatorname{Dom}M_t\).
If \(T_0\) is essentially self-adjoint, then
\[
 U\overline T_0U^{-1}=M_t.
\]
Consequently the closure has purely absolutely continuous
spectrum and no eigenvalues. It cannot have the nonempty
zero-eigenvalue determinant divisor \(\operatorname{div}\xi_{\mathbb R}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The closure of \(M_t|_{\mathcal C}\) is a restriction of \(M_t\).
Essential self-adjointness makes it self-adjoint. A self-adjoint
operator has no proper self-adjoint extension: taking adjoints
reverses the inclusion and gives the reverse inclusion. Thus the
closure equals \(M_t\). Its scalar spectral measures have densities
\(|g(t)|^2dt\). An eigenvector of eigenvalue \(\lambda\) would
be supported on the null set \(\{\lambda\}\), hence is zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[A different spectral carrier]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r4:rem:failed-execution-record}
Within the stipulated unitary chart, closing the symmetric
operator cannot create a zero-eigenvalue divisor.
A stronger topology can make residue evaluation continuous,
but its Hilbert completion, operator domain, and arithmetic trace
must then be constructed. The preceding theorem concerns every
essentially self-adjoint restriction in the ordinary multiplication
chart, not only the compact smooth core.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Comparison of the constructions.}
Modular theory supplies the critical generator on its GNS space.
Mellin analysis diagonalizes idele norm scaling. Residue calculus
gives meromorphic evaluation maps, while Birman--Krein theory
relates a specified trace-class pair to its spectral shift.
Meyer's virtual representation supplies a signed character.
Their comparison requires explicit maps, compatible domains,
and preservation of the complete normalized Weil functional.
