\section{Chains on Little Intervals and Rectification}
\label{v4-arith:ks:E1-automatic}

The operad of little intervals records ordered associative
composition. Its chains can be compared with the associative operad
over any coefficient ring. This statement concerns the operad;
formality of an individual algebra is a different question.

\begin{theorem}[Little-interval chains]
\label{v4-arith:thm:E1-formality-automatic}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere. For any commutative ring \(R\), the map
\[
 C_*(E_1;R)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Ass}_R
\]
that sends each configuration to its ordering of the input intervals
is an aritywise quasi-isomorphism of operads. Here singular chains
use the shuffle map for products, and
\(\mathrm{Ass}_R(n)=R[S_n]\) lies in degree zero.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At arity \(n\), the little-interval space has one component for each
ordering of the intervals. In one such component, their left and
right endpoints satisfy the ordered linear inequalities for
nonoverlapping intervals of positive length. This region is convex
and nonempty. Straight-line contraction to a fixed configuration
shows that its singular chains have homology \(R\) in degree zero
and zero otherwise, over any \(R\). Substitution of intervals
substitutes their orderings, so the chain augmentation is operadic.
\end{proof}

When the chosen algebra model categories admit rectification along
this operad map, it replaces an \(E_1\)-algebra by an equivalent
associative differential graded algebra. It does not replace that
algebra by its cohomology with zero differential.

\begin{corollary}[Higher operations can survive rectification]
\label{v4-arith:cor:ordered-E1-survives}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere. Torsion-freeness of
\(H_*(E_1;\mathbb Z)=\mathrm{Ass}_{\mathbb Z}\) does not imply
that an associative differential graded algebra is formal or that
its minimal model has vanishing higher products.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For \(A=k[x]/(x^3)\), the strict endomorphism algebra of its
augmentation resolution has
\(H^*E=\Lambda(u)\otimes k[v]\), but
\(\langle u,u,u\rangle=\{-v\}\ne\{0\}\), with zero
indeterminacy; see \eqref{v4-prologue:eq:cubic-primitives} and
\eqref{v4-prologue:eq:massey}. Its cohomology algebra with zero
differential has zero triple product. Quasi-isomorphisms preserve
this single-valued Massey product, so the strict algebra is not
formal. The comparison equations of
Theorem~\ref{v4-thm:recognition-one-step} retain precisely the
higher components needed to transport such data.
\end{proof}

\section{\(E_{2}\)-Formality Fails Integrally}
\label{v4-arith:ks:E2-fails}

\begin{theorem}[Salvatore--Wahl; Cohen LNM~533; Fresse--Willwacher arXiv:1503.08699]
\label{v4-arith:thm:E2-fails-integrally}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere. Algebras over
\(C_{\bullet}(E_{2};\mathbb{F}_{p})\) carry non-trivial
Dyer--Lashof power operations not encoded by the Gerstenhaber
homology operad; these operations obstruct integral formality. Over
\(\mathbb{Q}\), Tamarkin's
theorem (arXiv:math/9803025) establishes
\(C_{\bullet}(E_{2};\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{\sim}H_{\bullet}(E_{2};\mathbb{Q})=\mathrm{Ger}_{\mathbb{Q}}\)
(where \(\mathrm{Ger}_{\mathbb{Q}}\) is the Gerstenhaber operad over \(\mathbb{Q}\)),
a torsor for the Grothendieck--Teichm\"{u}ller group
\(\mathrm{GRT}_{1}(\mathbb{Q})\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[no integral replacement by the Gerstenhaber operad]
\label{v4-arith:cor:no-integral-E2}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere. An integral \(E_{2}\)-algebra structure is not
forbidden. What fails is the replacement
\[
C_{\bullet}(E_{2};\mathbb Z)\simeq \mathrm{Ger}_{\mathbb Z}.
\]
Consequently an \(E_{2}\)-chiral-centre enhancement of
\(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}\) over \(\mathbb Z\), if present, must
retain the chain-level \(E_{2}\)-operad and its power operations. After
tensoring with \(\mathbb Q\), Tamarkin formality identifies the
rational structure with a \(\mathrm{GRT}_{1}(\mathbb Q)\)-torsor.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Kaledin Japanese cocycle]
Kaledin isolates the arithmetic obstruction: for
an associative \(\mathbb{Z}\)-algebra \(A\), the reduction
\(A\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}\) may carry a non-trivial Hochschild
\(A_{\infty}\)-deformation class, the ``Japanese cocycle,'' in
\(\mathrm{HH}^{2}(A\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p})/\mathrm{HH}^{2}(A)\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}\).
Such classes are obstructions to naive integral strictification. They
do not, by themselves, prove nonexistence of an integral
\(E_{2}\)-algebra.
\end{remark}

\section{A Specified Strict Associative Model}
\label{v4-arith:ks:higher-products-vanish}

A strict model is an actual differential graded algebra with an
actual comparison to the proposed arithmetic operation system.
The quasi-isomorphism of coefficient operads supplies neither the
arithmetic algebra nor its geometric collision maps.

\begin{theorem}
\label{v4-arith:thm:mk-vanish}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere. For a differential graded associative
\(R\)-algebra \(A\), its displayed \(A_\infty\)-presentation has
only its differential and binary multiplication. A
quasi-isomorphic minimal presentation can have nonzero higher
operations; they must be retained by the comparison maps.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The differential graded identities are the \(A_\infty\)
equations for \(m_1=d_A\), \(m_2=\mu_A\) and
\(m_n=0\) for \(n\geq3\). The cubic example in the preceding
corollary proves the second assertion. Its nonzero triple Massey
product cannot disappear under a quasi-isomorphism.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Integral associative presentations]
\label{v4-arith:cor:strict-associativity-Z}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere. A supplied associative differential graded
\(\mathbb Z\)-algebra gives an integral \(A_\infty\)-algebra
by the preceding construction. Identifying it with an arithmetic
field system additionally requires the maps of
\eqref{v4-eq:recognition-comparison}, including their unit,
collision and higher-substitution conditions.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
No division occurs in the differential graded or suspended bar
identities. The second assertion specifies the additional
structure of that comparison: the coefficient algebra by itself
has no maps to the independently given geometric operations.
\end{proof}

\section{The Chiral Bracket via Adelic Residue}
\label{v4-arith:ks:chiral-bracket-adelic}

\begin{definition}[archimedean \(\lambda\)-bracket on the cover]
\label{v4-arith:def:arith-lambda-bracket}
Let \(\overline{C}\) denote the Arakelov compactification of the
arithmetic curve, obtained by adjoining the archimedean place
\(|\cdot|_{\infty}\) to \(\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbb{Z}\).
The Beilinson--Drinfeld \(\lambda\)-bracket is defined only on the
archimedean Heisenberg cover, where a holomorphic diagonal and a
complex residue exist:
\begin{equation}
\{a,b\}_{\infty}\;=\;\mathrm{Res}_{z=w}\,\omega_{a,b}(z,w).
\end{equation}
No finite prime contributes a vertex-algebra residue on
\(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}\). A finite-place bracket would require
a separate prismatic construction and is not part of the Hochschild
trace class.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[finite-place residue obstruction]
\label{v4-arith:thm:chiral-bracket-adelic}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere. The archimedean bracket above does not descend
to a Beilinson--Drinfeld bracket on
\(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}\) over \(\overline{C}\). Finite places are
totally disconnected in the factorization topology used here, and the
global object is an \(E_{1}\) Hochschild trace class rather than a
vertex algebra.
\end{theorem}

\section{The arithmetic Hall comparison}
\label{v4-arith:ks:CoHA-conjectural}

A cohomological Hall comparison starts with an enhanced arithmetic
category, its extension correspondences and its coefficient theory.
A Hochschild trace class does not specify these data.
Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:hall:chapter} constructs the finitary and geometric
Hall operations and separates their arithmetic comparison problems.

\begin{theorem}[Kontsevich--Soibelman]
\label{v4-arith:thm:KS-CoHA}
For a finite quiver over $\mathbb C$ with zero potential, equivariant
cohomology of its representation stacks has the extension product
of Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:hall:thm:coha-shuffle}.
For a potential, the critical product uses normalized monodromic
vanishing cycles, as defined in
Section~\ref{v4-arith:hall:sec:critical}. The dimension shifts,
coefficient category and chosen semistable or Serre loci are part
of that construction.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Davison--Meinhardt]
\label{v4-arith:thm:davison-integrality}
Assume the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:hall:thm:critical-pbw},
including its stability and coefficient data. The critical Hall algebra
has a PBW isomorphism of underlying graded objects with symmetric powers of
$H(B\mathbb C^\times)_{\mathrm{vir}}\otimes\mathcal B$.
Its perverse associated graded is free commutative in the specified
symmetry, or free supercommutative after the stated sign modification.
An algebra isomorphism with a free supercommutative algebra holds
for the finite symmetric, zero-potential quiver of
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:hall:thm:vertex-envelope}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{question}[Arithmetic CoHA comparison]
\label{v4-arith:thm:arithmetic-CoHA-conjectural}
Construct an arithmetic quiver with potential, or another independently
defined Hall category, and the enhanced arithmetic object to compare
with its BPS object. Specify the coefficient theory, stability and
completion, and construct a comparison preserving the extension
operations and their primitive maps. If places label vertices and
Hecke correspondences supply arrows, construct the actual incidence
spaces and their compatible finite-place assembly.
\end{question}

The Bost--Connes system has its own semigroup and time evolution.
These data do not specify a quiver representation stack or a
cohomological Hall product. Even a single isolated vertex with zero
potential has the exterior algebra on infinitely many odd charge-one
generators. One loop gives infinitely many even generators over
$\mathbb Q$ (Example~\ref{v4-arith:hall:ex:zero-one-loop}).
A polynomial generator for each prime therefore does not describe
these CoHAs. An arithmetic BPS identification requires the functor
in Question~\ref{v4-arith:hall:qn:arithmetic}.

\section{Euler series and cyclic classes}
\label{v4-arith:ks:logL-not-cyclic}

\begin{observation}
\label{v4-arith:obs:logL-not-cyclic}
On $\operatorname{Re}s>1$ the absolutely convergent formulas are
\[
 \log\zeta(s)=\sum_p\sum_{k\geq1}\frac{p^{-ks}}k,\qquad
 -\frac{\zeta'(s)}{\zeta(s)}
       =\sum_p\sum_{k\geq1}(\log p)p^{-ks}.
\]
The logarithms occur in the logarithmic derivative. An algebraic
quiver potential is instead a cyclic class in a path algebra.
Passing from either series to a potential requires a specified
coefficient ring, any necessary completion, and an actual map
compatible with traces.
\end{observation}

Kontsevich--Soibelman's exponential Hodge theory provides a recipient
for potential-dependent Hall invariants. Applying it to an arithmetic
Euler series first requires the potential and its comparison map.
A scalar identity does not supply a cyclic-homology class or that map.

\section{Arithmetic Formality Stratification}
\label{v4-arith:ks:stratification}

The little-interval chain operad has the integral associative
comparison of Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:thm:E1-formality-automatic}. Individual
algebras can nevertheless retain the nonzero Massey products of
Corollary~\ref{v4-arith:cor:ordered-E1-survives}. A supplied strict
integral algebra gives the presentation of
Corollary~\ref{v4-arith:cor:strict-associativity-Z}; neither a geometric
field realization nor a residue bracket follows from the operad
comparison. The \(E_2\) question and the arithmetic Hall
comparison require their own operation data. Question~\ref{v4-arith:thm:arithmetic-CoHA-conjectural} asks for the
arithmetic quiver, potential, coefficient theory and BPS comparison.
These constructions are required before identifying a trace class
with an enhanced arithmetic object.
