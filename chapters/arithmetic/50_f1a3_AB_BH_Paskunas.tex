\chapter{F1.a.3 (A+B): Block-Additive Hochschild and Paskunas
Ext\({}^{1}\)-Vanishing}
\label{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB}

A block decomposition separates two arithmetic comparison problems:
identifying each block with its spectral category, and transporting
extension classes through that identification. Hochschild additivity
uses the specified orthogonal idempotents. The per-block functors
require their own coefficient, central-action and trace compatibility.

For finite modules over a Noetherian domain, localization at the
fraction field kills positive extension groups. An arithmetic generic
fibre has this property only after the corresponding category equivalence
and localization comparison are supplied. At wild primes, a quotient
of a regular local ring may have a nonreduced generic fibre with
nonzero self-extensions. The reducedness hypothesis and explicit
counterexample below isolate this obstruction. Ordinary-block deformation
and the very-special multiplicity comparison retain their separate
arithmetic hypotheses.

\section{Block Decomposition Recall (Bernstein and BH)}
\label{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:block-recall}

\subsection{Characteristic-Zero Bernstein Decomposition}

\begin{theorem}[Bernstein 1984; Bernstein--Deligne 1984]
\label{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:thm:bernstein}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere Let \(G\) be a connected reductive
\(p\)-adic group and fix an algebraically closed coefficient
field \(k\) of characteristic zero. The category
\(\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{sm}}_{G}\) of smooth \(G\)-representations with
coefficients in \(k\) decomposes as a direct product over a
canonical set of blocks:
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:eq:bernstein-decomposition}
\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{sm}}_{G}\;=\;\prod_{\mathfrak{s}\in\mathfrak{B}(G)}
\mathrm{Block}_{\mathfrak{s}},
\end{equation}
where \(\mathfrak{B}(G)\) is the set of inertial equivalence classes
of supercuspidal supports. The decomposition is realised by a
central idempotent decomposition of the Bernstein centre
\(Z(\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{sm}}_{G})=\prod_{\mathfrak{s}}Z_{\mathfrak{s}}\)
into a product of integral domains.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Source]
Bernstein 1984, ``Le centre de Bernstein'', in Deligne's notes;
Bernstein--Deligne 1984 (collected in \emph{Travaux de Bernstein}).
For the block decomposition and torsor of unramified twists modulo
self-twists, see also Helm, arXiv:1201.1874, Theorems~3.4--3.5,
pp.~7--8.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[central-character fibres of Bernstein blocks]
\label{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:prop:block-chi-compat}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
Let \(k\) be algebraically closed of characteristic
zero, and let \(\chi:Z(G)\to k^\times\) be a smooth
central character. For a Bernstein block
\(\mathcal B_{\mathfrak s}\), put
\[
 \mathcal B_{\mathfrak s}^{\chi}
 =\{M\in\mathcal B_{\mathfrak s}:
        z|_M=\chi(z)\,\mathrm{id}_M
        \text{ for every }z\in Z(G)\}.
\]
Then
\[
 \operatorname{Rep}^{\mathrm{sm},\chi}_k(G)
   \simeq \prod_{\mathfrak s}
                     \mathcal B_{\mathfrak s}^{\chi},
\]
with zero factors omitted.

Let \(\delta_z\in Z_{\mathfrak s}\) denote the
central operation supplied by \(z\in Z(G)\), and
let
\[
 I_{\mathfrak s,\chi}
   =(\delta_z-\chi(z):z\in Z(G))
       \subseteq Z_{\mathfrak s}.
\]
The ring \(Z_{\mathfrak s}/I_{\mathfrak s,\chi}\)
acts on \(\mathcal B_{\mathfrak s}^{\chi}\).
This is a fibre condition inside the block.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each Bernstein projector commutes with the action
of \(Z(G)\). Thus every component of an object
with central character \(\chi\) again has that
central character. Conversely, a family of block objects reconstructs a smooth
representation by its direct sum, and the sum satisfies the same
scalar equations. This proves the
categorical decomposition.

The generators of \(I_{\mathfrak s,\chi}\)
act as zero on the indicated subcategory, so
the central action factors through the quotient.
No claim that taking the categorical centre
commutes with this quotient is needed.
\end{proof}

For $G=GL_2(F)$, fix a uniformizer $\varpi$ and normalized
parabolic induction. In the unramified principal-series block write
$a=\eta_1(\varpi)$, $b=\eta_2(\varpi)$ for its two unramified
inducing characters, so $t=a+b$ and $d=ab$. The modulus character
in normalized induction is trivial on scalar matrices. Consequently
the central operation of $u\in\mathcal O_F^\times$ is $1$, and that
of $\varpi$ is $d$, in the ring $k[t,d,d^{-1}]$. These equalities
hold as central operations: their values on all supercuspidal supports
are respectively $1$ and $ab$, and the block ring is reduced.
If $\chi$ is unramified, put $c=\chi(\varpi)\in k^\times$.
Then $I_{\mathfrak s,\chi}=(d-c)$ and the fibre has the quotient
scalar action $k[t,d,d^{-1}]/(d-c)$. It is nonzero, for instance on
any irreducible constituent of the normalized induction of the
unramified characters with values $1,c$ at $\varpi$.
If $\chi$ is ramified, choose $u\in\mathcal O_F^\times$ with
$\chi(u)\ne1$. The relation $1-\chi(u)$ is an invertible scalar,
so the quotient and the fixed-character fibre category are zero.
Thus fixing central character neither selects a whole Bernstein
block nor gives a nonzero fibre in every block.

\subsection{BH Block Specialisation at \(G=GL_{2}\)}

\begin{proposition}[types and fixed central character]
\label{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:prop:BH-specialisation}
\ClaimStatusProvedHereConditional
Suppose a type \(\tau\) realizes a
characteristic-zero Bernstein block
\(\mathcal B_{\mathfrak s}\) of \(GL_2(F)\)
through a Morita equivalence with its Hecke
algebra \(H_\tau\). Then
\(\mathcal B_{\mathfrak s}^{\chi}\) corresponds
to the category of modules over
\[
 H_\tau/
   (\delta_z-\chi(z):z\in F^\times).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Morita equivalence identifies central
natural operations. The equations defining
central character \(\chi\) therefore become
the displayed relations on \(H_\tau\)-modules.
\end{proof}

The chosen-type hypothesis is the type-to-block comparison relevant to
Bushnell--Henniart, \emph{The Local Langlands Conjecture for $GL(2)$},
Chapters~11--16. It does not assert uniqueness of a type. The small-prime
questions in \Cref{v4-arith:f1a3-BH:prop:BH-types-p2} and
\Cref{v4-arith:f1a3-BH:prop:BH-types-p3} require their own coefficient
regime and the specified type realization.

\begin{remark}[coefficient field regimes]
\label{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:rem:coeff-regime}
Characteristic-zero Bernstein theory, integral or mod-$\ell$ theory
with $\ell\ne p$, and mod-$p$ or $p$-adic block theory are distinct
coefficient regimes. The characteristic-zero result above does not
assert its integral-domain description for arbitrary modular
coefficients. The Pa{\v s}k{\=u}nas block structure in
\Cref{v4-arith:deg-f1a:thm:colmez-paskunas-blocks} concerns its
specified mod-$p$ category. It is not automatically a refinement
of a characteristic-zero Bernstein decomposition.
The small-prime residuals of Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:f1a3-BH}
retain their residual-characteristic assumptions. Comparing these
regimes requires coefficient-change functors and a proof of their
compatibility with the selected blocks and central actions.
\end{remark}

\section{F1.a.3.A: Block-Additive BH Packaging}
\label{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:A-packaging}

\subsection{Block-Additivity of Hochschild}

\begin{lemma}[Cyclic chains of orthogonal dg blocks]
\label{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:lem:HH-block-additive}
Let \(k\) be a commutative ring and
\(\{\mathcal A_i\}_{i\in I}\) a set-indexed family of small
\(k\)-linear dg categories with \(K\)-flat morphism complexes over
\(k\), for example chosen cofibrant dg models. Form the orthogonal
dg union \(\mathcal A\): its objects are the disjoint union of the
objects of the \(\mathcal A_i\), its morphism complexes within a
block are unchanged, and all morphism complexes between distinct
blocks are zero. Put \(\mathcal C=\operatorname{Perf}(\mathcal A)\)
and \(\mathcal C_i=\operatorname{Perf}(\mathcal A_i)\).
Use the derived Hochschild trace, computed by the cyclic bar complex
of these dg Morita models. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:eq:HH-block-additive}
 \mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}(\mathcal C)
 \simeq\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}(\mathcal C_i)
\end{equation}
as \(S^1\)-equivariant complexes over \(k\).
The objects of \(\mathcal C\) have finite block support. The same
formula applies to a compactly generated \(k\)-linear presentable
category only when its compact category is specified by this
finite-support dg model and its trace is that of the compact model.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In simplicial degree \(n\), the cyclic bar construction is the direct sum
\[
 \bigoplus_{X_0,\ldots,X_n}
 \mathcal A(X_n,X_0)\otimes_k\mathcal A(X_{n-1},X_n)
 \otimes_k\cdots\otimes_k\mathcal A(X_0,X_1),
\]
with the degree-zero case given by endomorphism complexes. If the
objects in a cyclic word lie in more than one block, some consecutive
pair, including the closing pair, lies in distinct blocks. Its morphism
complex is zero, so that summand vanishes. Every surviving cyclic
word lies in exactly one \(\mathcal A_i\). The internal differential,
composition faces, unit degeneracies and cyclic rotation all preserve
that block. Thus the cyclic object itself is the direct sum of the
block cyclic objects. The \(K\)-flat hypothesis makes these tensor
products compute the derived cyclic bar construction. Realization
commutes with direct sums, and the cyclic structure induces the
stated \(S^1\)-equivariant equivalence; equivalently the Hochschild
differential and Connes operator preserve this decomposition.

The representable \(\mathcal A\)-modules each have support in one
block. A perfect module is obtained from finitely many representables
by finite sums, shifts, cones and retracts, so it has finite support
and is perfect in each supported block. Conversely a finite-support
family of perfect block modules is perfect over \(\mathcal A\).
This identifies \(\operatorname{Perf}(\mathcal A)\) with the
finite-support sum of the \(\operatorname{Perf}(\mathcal A_i)\).
Passing to perfect modules leaves the chosen dg Morita trace unchanged,
so the cyclic computation gives the displayed formula for \(\mathcal C\).
The presentable version uses precisely this specified compact model;
it makes no assertion that a symmetric monoidal invariant preserves
arbitrary products.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Multiplication and arithmetic realization]
\label{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:rem:HH-block-additive-what}
The lemma supplies an \(S^1\)-equivariant complex, not an
\(E_1\)-algebra structure. A multiplicative comparison additionally
requires specified \(E_2\)-monoidal enhancements inducing
\(E_1\) trace products, and comparison maps preserving those operations
and units. For infinitely many nonzero unital
blocks, the direct sum with coordinatewise multiplication has no
unit: an element has finite support and cannot act as the identity
on an omitted nonzero block. Arithmetic use of the lemma requires
actual \(k\)-linear dg models, the stated compact or finite-support
identification, and compatible block-index and coefficient maps.
A characteristic-zero Bernstein decomposition alone does not supply
these data for a residual-characteristic spectral category.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Block-Wise Comparison}

The index and coefficient category must be specified before applying
a block comparison. In characteristic zero put
\[
 \mathfrak B(G)_\chi
   =\{\mathfrak s\in\mathfrak B(G):
                     \mathcal B_{\mathfrak s}^{\chi}\ne0\},
\]
where $\mathcal B_{\mathfrak s}^{\chi}$ is the fibre category defined
above. It is not the set of entire blocks on which a single central
character holds. For an integral, mod-$p$ or $p$-adic application,
choose instead a coefficient ring $\Lambda$, a smooth character
$\chi:Z(G)\to\Lambda^\times$, and the intended representation
category $\mathcal R_{p,\chi}^{\Lambda}$. Assume a specified
orthogonal decomposition into nonzero block categories
$\mathcal B_i^{\Lambda,\chi}$, indexed by $I_{p,\chi}^{\Lambda}$.
Write $\mathrm{Block}_i=\mathcal B_i^{\Lambda,\chi}$ in this chosen regime.
No identification of this index with $\mathfrak B(G)_\chi$ is implicit.

In the following conditional comparisons,
$\mathrm D^{\mathrm{sph\text{-}fin}}([G])_{\chi,i}$ denotes the
chosen stable realization of $\mathcal B_i^{\Lambda,\chi}$ with its
stated spherical-finiteness condition. This notation requires the
realization and its block projectors as data. The spectral category
must be defined over the same coefficient ring, or connected to it
by a specified coefficient-change functor. A comparison across
coefficient regimes requires that functor, a map of block indices
and compatibility with $z=\chi(z)$, fixed determinant, and the
central actions. Characteristic-zero Bernstein decomposition does
not provide these data for the wild-EG category.

\begin{theorem}[block-wise BH type-block comparison]
\label{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:thm:block-wise-comparison}
\ClaimStatusProvedHereConditional Under
Conjecture~\ref{v4-arith:f1a3-BH:conj:BH-packaging} applied block-by-block and
Conjecture~\ref{v4-arith:f1a3-BH:conj:wild-compact-generation}, the following
equivalence of $S^1$-equivariant Hochschild complexes holds for each
$i\in I_{p,\chi}^{\Lambda}$, provided the single-block and arithmetic
Hochschild comparisons below are supplied in this same coefficient
category, with the stated central-character and fixed-determinant
compatibilities and the dg Morita trace models of
Lemma~\ref{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:lem:HH-block-additive}:
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:eq:block-wise-comparison}
\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}\bigl(\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{sph\text{-}fin}}([GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})])_{\chi,i}\bigr)
\;\simeq\;
\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}\bigl(\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{EG,wild},p,\chi,i}_{GL_{2}})\bigr),
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{EG,wild},p,\chi,i}_{GL_{2}}\)
is the component of the wild-enhanced Emerton--Gee stack
assigned to the chosen coefficient block $i$ by the specified
type-to-component map of
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:f1a3-BH:prop:type-to-component-p2} (at \(p=2\)) or
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:f1a3-BH:prop:type-to-component-p3} (at \(p=3\)).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Use, as coefficient-compatible comparison hypotheses, the single-block equivalence
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:f1a3-BH:thm:single-block-exhaustion} and the
Ben-Zvi--Nadler arithmetic extension
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:bzn:thm:HH-IndCoh-arith}. The automorphic block decomposition is the stipulated decomposition
of $\mathcal R_{p,\chi}^{\Lambda}$; in characteristic zero its fibre
version follows from Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:prop:block-chi-compat}.
The corresponding spectral decomposition and its index map are
additional comparison data, with the fixed-determinant condition.
The component description in
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:deg-f1a:prop:spectral-blocks} must be realized in these
same coefficients. Both sides restrict
to the block \(i\) inside the specified dg model. Functoriality of
its cyclic bar construction gives the trace comparison. An
\(E_1\)-algebra comparison requires separately supplied compatible
monoidal structures and preservation of their operations.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[F1.a.3.A assembly]
\label{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:cor:F1a3-A-assembly}
\ClaimStatusProvedHereConditional Under the block-by-block inputs
of Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:thm:block-wise-comparison}, and the
specified finite-support compact models of
Lemma~\ref{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:lem:HH-block-additive} on both sides,

\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}\bigl(\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{sph\text{-}fin}}([GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})])_{\chi}\bigr)
\;\simeq\;
\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}\bigl(\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{EG,wild},p,\chi}_{GL_{2}})\bigr)
\end{equation}
holds as an \(S^1\)-equivariant equivalence of complexes, by

A multiplicative or unital enhancement is an additional comparison
requirement, especially for an infinite block index.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}

Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:thm:block-wise-comparison} over
\(i\in I_{p,\chi}^{\Lambda}\) combined with
Lemma~\ref{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:lem:HH-block-additive} on both sides.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Paskunas Block Centraliser Structure}

\begin{theorem}[Paskunas 2013 block centraliser structure]
\label{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:thm:Paskunas-centraliser}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere Let \(p\geq 5\) and fix a residual
representation
\(\rho\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\to GL_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})\)
and central character \(\chi\). The Bernstein block
\(\mathrm{Block}_{\rho}\) of
\(\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{sm},\chi}_{GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}\) is
Morita equivalent to the category of modules over the
\emph{block centraliser algebra}
\(A_{\rho}=\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{Block}_{\rho}}(P_{\rho})\), where
\(P_{\rho}\) is the projective cover of the unique simple quotient
\(\pi_{\rho}\) determined by local Langlands correspondence. The
algebra \(A_{\rho}\) is a complete Noetherian local ring
satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Generic case (irreducible \(\rho\) or generic reducible
\(\rho\)):} \(A_{\rho}\) is a regular local ring of dimension \(2\) or
\(3\) depending on the residual type, with explicit generators given
by pseudo-character deformations.
\item \emph{Non-generic case (special reducible \(\rho\),
``ordinary block''):} \(A_{\rho}\) is still complete Noetherian
local but no longer regular; it is a quotient of a regular local
ring by an explicit ideal captured by the Paskunas ``ordinary
block'' theorem.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Source]
Paskunas 2013, ``The image of Colmez's Montreal functor'',
Publ.~IHES~118:1--191, Theorems~1.4, 1.5, and 1.6.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[location of the two cases in Paskunas 2013]
\label{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:rem:Paskunas-centraliser-location}
The regular-local dimension for the generic case is computed in
Paskunas 2013 \S 6, with explicit pseudo-character-deformation
generators. The non-generic (ordinary block) case is in \S 10.
Updated extent of the ordinary block is in Paskunas 2015
(Duke Math.\ J.\ 164 (2015), 297--359; arXiv:1306.2691), ``On
the Breuil--M\'ezard conjecture''.
\end{remark}

\subsection{\(\mathrm{Ext}^{1}\)-Vanishing in Generic Blocks}

\begin{proposition}[extension vanishing over the fraction field]
\label{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:prop:Ext1-generic-p5}
Let \(R\) be a commutative Noetherian domain, let
\(K=\operatorname{Frac}(R)\), and let \(M,N\) be finite \(R\)-modules.
Then
\[
 \operatorname{Ext}^1_R(M,N)\otimes_R K
 \simeq\operatorname{Ext}^1_K(M\otimes_R K,N\otimes_R K)=0.
\]
In particular, the statement applies to a regular local domain.
It asserts vanishing at the generic point, not at every regular point.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Noetherianity gives a free resolution of \(M\) with finitely generated
terms: take a finite set of generators for each successive kernel.
Localization is exact, and homomorphisms from each finite free term
commute with localization. Localizing the resulting Hom complex
therefore computes the displayed extension group over \(K\).
Every short exact sequence of vector spaces over \(K\) splits.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[arithmetic transport on a specified generic fibre]
\label{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:cor:Ext1-zero-generic-locus}
Suppose a specified arithmetic block category is exactly equivalent
to finite modules over a commutative Noetherian domain \(R\), and
its specified generic-fibre category corresponds under this equivalence
to finite \(K=\operatorname{Frac}(R)\)-modules.
Then generic-fibre extensions vanish. If a spectral locus is also
identified with this localization by compatible functors, the same
vanishing holds in its corresponding category.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
An exact equivalence identifies extension classes. Apply
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:prop:Ext1-generic-p5}
and the stated localization comparison. Neither regularity alone
nor flatness alone constructs the arithmetic or spectral equivalence.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Non-Generic Block: Controlled Deformation}

\begin{proposition}[Local self-extensions and conditional arithmetic transport]
\label{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:prop:Ext1-ordinary-controlled}
For a commutative Noetherian local ring \((A,\mathfrak m,k)\),
there is a natural isomorphism
\[
 \operatorname{Ext}^1_A(k,k)\cong
 \operatorname{Hom}_k(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2,k).
\]
For distinct simples \(S,S'\) in an arithmetic ordinary block, suppose
an explicit \(k\)-linear class comparison identifies
\(\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathrm{Block}_\rho}(S,S')\) with a specified
subspace \(D_\rho\) of this dual cotangent space for \(A=A_\rho\).
Then its dimension is \(\dim_k D_\rho\). If in addition
\(\dim_k\mathfrak m_\rho/\mathfrak m_\rho^2\leq3\) is established,
this gives the bound \(\dim_k D_\rho\leq3\).
Breuil--Mezard multiplicities alone supply neither this class
comparison nor the bound on the embedding dimension.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply \(\operatorname{Hom}_A(-,k)\) to
\(0\to\mathfrak m\to A\to k\to0\).
The restriction \(\operatorname{Hom}_A(A,k)\to
\operatorname{Hom}_A(\mathfrak m,k)\) is zero, since
\(\mathfrak m k=0\), and \(\operatorname{Ext}^1_A(A,k)=0\).
Thus the connecting map identifies \(\operatorname{Hom}_A(\mathfrak m,k)\)
with \(\operatorname{Ext}^1_A(k,k)\). Every such homomorphism kills
\(\mathfrak m^2\), giving the displayed dual cotangent space.
Concretely, a functional gives the extension obtained by pushing out
\(0\to\mathfrak m\to A\to k\to0\) along \(\mathfrak m\to k\).
The arithmetic conclusion follows from the separately assumed class
comparison. There is no subtraction by a number of defining relations:
for \(A=k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)\), the embedding dimension is one
and \(\operatorname{Ext}^1_A(k,k)=k\). This space is annihilated by
\(\mathfrak m\), and is not a nonzero free \(A/\mathfrak m^2\)-module.
Every simple module over a commutative local ring is its residue field;
comparison with distinct arithmetic simples therefore requires the
specified additional map.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[conditional content is narrow]
\label{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:rem:ordinary-narrow}
The ``ordinary block'' covered by Paskunas 2013
Theorem~1.6 is the \emph{only} non-generic block at \(p\geq 5\). All
other blocks are generic, and
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:prop:Ext1-generic-p5} applies
after the exact generic-fibre comparisons of
Corollary~\ref{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:cor:Ext1-zero-generic-locus}. The
ordinary block itself splits further into a ``very special'' sub-case
(where Breuil--Mezard multiplicities are conjectural but
individually explicit) and a ``non-very-special'' sub-case. The
arithmetic application of
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:prop:Ext1-ordinary-controlled}
still requires the explicit class comparison, independently of whether
the multiplicity values are known. The ``very special'' sub-case is one additional named residual, strictly
smaller than the whole ordinary block.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The \(p\in\{2,3\}\) Extension}

\begin{conjecture}[block centraliser at wild primes]
\label{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:prop:centraliser-wild}
Let \(p\in\{2,3\}\) and let
\(\tau\in\mathbf{T}^{\mathrm{BH}}_{p,\chi}\) be a BH type in the
principal block (the Ext\({}^{1}\)-carrying block of
Remark~\ref{v4-arith:f1a3-BH:rem:ext1-type-p2} at \(p=2\), or one of the
three types in Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:f1a3-BH:prop:A2-quiver-p3} at
\(p=3\)). The block centraliser algebra
\(A_{\tau}=\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{Block}^{\mathrm{BH}}_{\tau}}(P_{\tau})\)
is a complete Noetherian local ring of dimension \(2\) (at \(p=2\)) or
dimension \(\leq 3\) (at \(p=3\)), and is a quotient of a regular
local ring by an ideal to be specified in the arithmetic block.
Question~\ref{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:prop:R4-Ext1-p3} asks for
its relation to a finite quiver model; it does not determine this
ideal or the regularity of the complete centraliser.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[gap in the wild-prime block centraliser argument]
\label{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:rem:centraliser-wild-conditional}
Conjecture~\ref{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:prop:centraliser-wild} is the
\(p\in\{2,3\}\) analogue of
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:thm:Paskunas-centraliser}. Paskunas'
original paper explicitly restricts to \(p\geq 5\) on account of the
Colmez Montreal-functor hypothesis; the restriction is technical
but has no published lift in the cited range. The expected dimensions are separate arithmetic hypotheses.
The proposed local degree-one dimensions in
Questions~\ref{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:prop:R4-Ext1-p2}
and~\ref{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:prop:R4-Ext1-p3} determine neither
Krull dimension nor regularity of the block centraliser.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[conditional generic-fibre extension vanishing]
\label{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:cor:Ext1-wild-conditional}
Let \(R=A_\tau\) be a reduced commutative Noetherian ring, and let
\(Q(R)\) be its total quotient ring. Assume the specified arithmetic
generic-fibre category is equivalent, as an exact category, to
\(Q(R)\)-modules. Then all its short exact sequences split, and
its \(\operatorname{Ext}^1\) groups vanish.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
A reduced Noetherian ring has finitely many minimal primes
\(\mathfrak p_i\), and
\(Q(R)\simeq\prod_i\operatorname{Frac}(R/\mathfrak p_i)\).
Indeed, localization at all non-zero-divisors leaves a reduced
Noetherian zero-dimensional ring, hence a finite product of fields.
Modules over each field are vector spaces, so every short exact
sequence splits. The finite product decomposition and the assumed
exact equivalence preserve these splittings.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[a nonreduced generic fibre]
A quotient of a regular ring need not satisfy this conclusion.
Take \(R=k[[t,u,\epsilon]]/(\epsilon^2)\).
It is complete Noetherian local of dimension two. With
\(K=\operatorname{Frac}(k[[t,u]])\), localization at its minimal
prime \((\epsilon)\) gives \(R_{(\epsilon)}=K[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)\).
The periodic free resolution with differential multiplication by
\(\epsilon\) gives \(\operatorname{Ext}^1_{R_{(\epsilon)}}(K,K)=K\).
Thus the quotient presentation in
Conjecture~\ref{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:prop:centraliser-wild}
does not imply generic semisimplicity. Reducedness and the arithmetic
localization or Morita comparison are separate requirements.
Even for a regular ring, closed-point extensions need not vanish:
\(\operatorname{Ext}^1_{k[[t]]}(k,k)=k\).
\end{example}

\section{Coefficient Categories and Comparison Conventions}
\label{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:source-comparison}

The block comparisons involve characteristic-zero smooth representations,
residual-characteristic block algebras and spectral categories.
Their coefficient fields, module conventions, central characters and
comparison functors must be specified separately. The following
conventions retain these distinctions.

\subsection{Block-Additivity: References and Conventions}
\label{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:comparison-block-additivity}

\paragraph{Block and categorical references.}
Bernstein (1984) and Bernstein--Deligne (1984) describe the Bernstein
centre and characteristic-zero block decomposition of
$\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{sm}}_G$; Bushnell--Henniart (2006) concerns
$GL_2$ types. Pa{\v s}k{\=u}nas (2013, 2015) treats the specified
mod-$p$ block-centraliser setting. Ben-Zvi--Nadler,
arXiv:0904.1247, concerns categorical character theory, and
Dotto--Emerton--Gee, arXiv:2603.26887, supplies its stated
fixed-central-character functor. Comparing these categories requires
the coefficient-compatible block and functor data above; the list of
references does not identify their carriers.

\paragraph{Sign and convention.} Bernstein--Deligne use the left-regular
representation of the Bernstein centre; Paskunas uses the right-regular;
both yield equivalent block-projection functors under the
opposite-algebra convention. Bushnell--Henniart uses
Langlands-normalised central characters; the \(\chi\) convention here
matches. The Hochschild homology orientation follows the ``co-Hochschild''
convention from Lurie HA~5.5.3, consistent with Ben-Zvi--Nadler and
Costello--Gwilliam.

\paragraph{Ambient category.} The block decomposition of
\(\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{sm},\chi}_{GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}\) lives in the
abelian category of smooth \(GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\)-representations;
the spectral side is \(\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}\) on a formal
algebraic stack. Both admit compatible derived enhancements in
presentable stable \(\infty\)-categories.

\paragraph{Trace carrier.} Lemma~\ref{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:lem:HH-block-additive}
uses orthogonal \(k\)-linear dg models with derived cyclic chains and
finite-support perfect modules. Realizing these models and their
coefficient comparisons on an arithmetic category remains a hypothesis.

\paragraph{Functoriality.} Block-decomposition is functorial under
the specified block projectors. The cyclic-chain construction is
functorial on the chosen dg models, giving an additive trace
comparison when the coefficient and block maps have been supplied.

\subsection{Per-Block Residual Isolation: References and Conventions}
\label{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:comparison-per-block}

\paragraph{Projective covers and the Montreal functor.}
Pa{\v s}k{\=u}nas (2013) supplies the blockwise projective-cover
setting, and Colmez (2010), Ast\'erisque~330, concerns the Montreal
functor between its automorphic and Galois categories. The
Bernstein--Deligne and Ben-Zvi--Nadler constructions enter through
the separate block and categorical comparisons. Their use together
requires the specified coefficient and endomorphism-algebra maps.

\paragraph{Convention alignment.} Paskunas' projective cover
\(P_{\rho}\) uses the Montreal functor normalisation; this matches the
\(P_{\tau}\) of Conjecture~\ref{v4-arith:f1a3-BH:conj:BH-packaging} under the
BH-to-residual correspondence
\(\tau\mapsto\rho_{\tau}\) of
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:f1a3-BH:prop:type-to-component-p2}.

\paragraph{Hypothesis.} The per-block hypothesis is named explicitly as
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:prop:A-residual-taxonomy}(2); no further
hypothesis is required for the structural factorisation. The residual
(R.A) is the named open content.

\subsection{Generic-Block \(\mathrm{Ext}^{1}\)-Vanishing: References and Conventions}
\label{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:comparison-ext1-generic}

For a Noetherian domain \(R\), a free resolution with finitely generated terms of a finite
module computes its extension groups. Localizing that resolution at
\(K=\operatorname{Frac}(R)\) commutes with the Hom complex and its
cohomology. The resulting vector-space extensions split. This is the
argument of Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:prop:Ext1-generic-p5}.

The arithmetic application requires an exact block equivalence and
a compatible generic-fibre localization. A spectral application also
requires the specified functor identifying the spectral locus with
that localization. These are the hypotheses of
Corollary~\ref{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:cor:Ext1-zero-generic-locus}.
Regularity at a closed point does not imply extension vanishing there.

\subsection{Wild-Prime Block Centraliser: References and Conventions}
\label{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:comparison-wild-prime}

\paragraph{Wild types and block algebras.} The wild-prime argument uses
Bushnell--Henniart 2006 \S~12--15 (wild types at \(p=2,3\)) and
Auslander--Reiten--Smal\o{} 1995 (linear-quiver block algebra theory).
Paskunas 2013 does not cover \(p\in\{2,3\}\) directly; this is the
source of the gap named as (R.B.2).

\paragraph{Convention and hypothesis.}
The explicit finite model in Example~\ref{v4-arith:wild:three-vertex}
uses right modules and arrows \(L_0\to L_1\to L_2\).
Its adjacent Yoneda product is nonzero in degree two. An arithmetic
block comparison must specify its relations and preserve these
products; the finite model gives no regularity theorem for a
complete local centraliser. The quotient presentation and dimension assumptions
in Conjecture~\ref{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:prop:centraliser-wild}
do not imply generic semisimplicity. The vanishing assertion uses
the reducedness and category equivalence of
Corollary~\ref{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:cor:Ext1-wild-conditional}.

\paragraph{Ordinary-block controlled deformation.} The ordinary-block
argument uses Paskunas 2013 \S~10 and Paskunas 2015 (Breuil--Mezard
multiplicity formula). The very-special Breuil--Mezard multiplicity
is the named residual (R.B.1). Its computation does not construct
the class comparison required by
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:prop:Ext1-ordinary-controlled}.
