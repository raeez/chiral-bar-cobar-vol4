\chapter{Adelic Scaling, Scattering, and the Weil Trace}
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:ATP-adelic-scattering}

The Weil pairing is positive precisely when the nontrivial zeta zeros
lie on the critical line. A scattering operator instead compares two
self-adjoint dynamics on their absolutely continuous subspaces.
The question is whether arithmetic dynamics can produce the full signed
Weil trace. The distinction between these two constructions already
appears in a positive rank-one perturbation.

\section{A Rank-One Trace Difference}
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:domain}

Use the complex test algebra
\(\mathscr A=C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)\), with additive convolution and
\(f^*(u)=\overline{f(-u)}\). Put
\[
 h_f(z)=\int_{\mathbb R}f(u)e^{izu}\,du,\qquad
 \mathscr P=\{h_f:f\in\mathscr A\},\qquad
 h^\sharp(z)=\overline{h(\bar z)}.
\]
Then \(h_{f*f^*}=h_fh_f^\sharp\), whose restriction to the real
line is \(|h_f|^2\). The signed Weil distribution is
\(W=P-W_\infty-W_{\mathrm{fin}}\), with all three terms as in
\Cref{v4-arith:w5-a:thm:weil-explicit}.
Its positivity condition is \(W(f*f^*)\geq0\) for every
\(f\in\mathscr A\). No evenness restriction is imposed.

\begin{proposition}[Complete scattering with a signed trace]
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:prop:rank-one-sign}
On \(L^2(\mathbb R,d\lambda)\oplus\mathbb C\), let
\[
 A_0=M_\lambda\oplus0,\qquad A_1=M_\lambda\oplus a,
 \qquad a>0,
\]
with common domain \(\operatorname{Dom}M_\lambda\oplus\mathbb C\),
where \(\operatorname{Dom}M_\lambda=\{g:\lambda g\in L^2\}\).
The difference is positive and has rank one. The wave operators are
complete and the scattering operator is the identity. For every
\(h\in\mathscr S(\mathbb R)\), however,
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(h(A_1)-h(A_0))=h(a)-h(0)
 =\int_0^a h'(\lambda)\,d\lambda.
\]
The corresponding spectral shift is
\(\xi=\mathbf1_{(0,a)}\), and \(-d\xi=\delta_a-\delta_0\)
is not positive, even on the tests \(|h_f|^2\), \(f\in\mathscr A\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The absolutely continuous subspace of each operator is the first
summand. On that summand
\(e^{itA_1}e^{-itA_0}=1\), so both wave operators have exactly
that range. Functional calculus gives the displayed trace difference
on the one-dimensional second summand. The integral identity fixes
its spectral-shift sign.
Choose \(\phi\in\mathscr A\) with \(\int\phi=1\), and set
\(f(u)=\phi(u)+\phi(u-\pi/a)\).
Then \(h_f(z)=(1+e^{i\pi z/a})h_\phi(z)\), so
\(|h_f(a)|^2-|h_f(0)|^2=-4\).
Also \(e^{-2\pi i\xi}=1\) almost everywhere, despite the negative
atom of \(-d\xi\).
\end{proof}

The example separates a unitary scattering phase from a positive
trace distribution. To test an arithmetic comparison, the Hilbert
spaces, domains, and perturbation must first be specified.

\section{The Idele Scaling Operator}
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:framework}
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:framework:hilbert}

\begin{definition}[Idele carrier and measure]
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:def:H-idele}
Let \(\mathbb A\) be the adele ring of \(\mathbb Q\), let
\(C_{\mathbb Q}=\mathbb Q^\times\backslash\mathbb A^\times\), and put
\(C_{\mathbb Q}^1=\ker(|\cdot|:C_{\mathbb Q}\to\mathbb R_{>0})\).
The section represented by the idele \((x,1,1,\ldots)\), \(x>0\),
gives \(C_{\mathbb Q}\cong\mathbb R_{>0}\times C_{\mathbb Q}^1\).
Give the compact group \(C_{\mathbb Q}^1\) Haar probability measure
\(d\mu_1\), and use \(dx/x\) on the first factor. Define
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:eq:H-idele}
 \mathcal K=L^2(C_{\mathbb Q},d^\times x)
 =L^2(\mathbb R,du;E),\qquad
 E=L^2(C_{\mathbb Q}^1,d\mu_1),\quad u=\log x.
\end{equation}
Here \(d^\times x\) is the product measure, of infinite total mass.
Let \(P_0\) be the rank-one orthogonal projection of \(E\) onto
its constant functions.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Unitary Mellin transform and domain]
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:lem:mellin-decomp}
The transform
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:eq:mellin-decomp}
 (\mathcal Mg)(\tau)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}
 \int_0^\infty g(x)x^{-i\tau}\,\frac{dx}{x}
\end{equation}
extends to a unitary from \(\mathcal K\) to
\(L^2(\mathbb R,d\tau;E)\). On the logarithmic side put
\(D_0=-i\partial_u\), with domain \(H^1(\mathbb R;E)\).
It is self-adjoint, has purely absolutely continuous spectrum
\(\mathbb R\), and transforms to \(M_\tau\otimes1_E\), with domain
\(\{F:\int\tau^2\|F(\tau)\|_E^2d\tau<\infty\}\).
A core is \(\mathscr S(\mathbb R)\otimes_{\mathrm{alg}}E\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The substitution \(x=e^u\) preserves the stated measures.
The ordinary Fourier Plancherel theorem, applied in each coordinate
of an orthonormal basis of \(E\), proves unitarity.
Integration by parts on the Schwartz core gives
\(\mathcal M(-i\partial_u g)=\tau\mathcal Mg\).
Real multiplication on its maximal domain is self-adjoint:
its adjoint has the same domain, as testing against compactly
supported functions shows. Truncate in \(\tau\) and in finitely many
\(E\)-coordinates, then approximate the resulting functions by smooth
compactly supported functions. This gives graph-norm density of the
stated core. Each spectral measure has density
\(\|F(\tau)\|_E^2\) with respect to Lebesgue measure. Every real
interval has nonzero spectral projection, proving the spectrum claim.
\end{proof}

The spherical space \(L^2(\mathbb R)\otimes\operatorname{im}P_0\)
is a reducing subspace. Its Schwartz functions are not a dense
operator core for all of \(\mathcal K\).
The transform above uses the unitary character \(x^{-i\tau}\).
A formula with exponent \(1/2+i\tau\) requires an additional
weight in the source measure or a specified multiplication by
\(x^{1/2}\).

\section{The Critical Modular Generator on a Common Carrier}
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:framework:generator}

The critical Bost--Connes generator acts on a different Hilbert space.
By \Cref{v4-arith:w5-b:def:N-BC}, its orthonormal basis and domain are
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 N_{\mathrm{BC}}v_{q,r}=\nu_qv_{q,r},\qquad
 \nu_q=\tfrac12\log q,\qquad
 (q,r)\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\times\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z,\\
 \operatorname{Dom}N_{\mathrm{BC}}
 =\left\{\sum c_{q,r}v_{q,r}:
               \sum\nu_q^2|c_{q,r}|^2<\infty\right\}.
 \end{gathered}
\]
Every eigenvalue has infinite multiplicity. The simple occupation
energies \(\log m\) instead occur in the reducing space
\(K_2=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{v_{m^2,0}:m\geq1\}\),
as in \Cref{v4-arith:w5-b:lem:zero-mode-embeds-gns}.

\begin{definition}[Tensor comparison operators]
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:def:scattering-space}
Define
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:eq:H-scatter}
 \widetilde{\mathcal K}
 =L^2(\mathbb R,d\tau)\widehat\otimes\mathcal H_1
 =L^2\bigl(\mathbb R;\ell^2(\mathbb Q_{>0}\times\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z)\bigr).
\end{equation}
The joint multiplication operators are
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:eq:D-BC}
 (\widetilde D_0F)_{q,r}(\tau)=\tau F_{q,r}(\tau),\qquad
 (\widetilde D_{\mathrm{BC}}F)_{q,r}(\tau)
   =(\tau+\nu_q)F_{q,r}(\tau).
\end{equation}
Each has its maximal multiplication domain. In particular,
\[
 \operatorname{Dom}\widetilde D_{\mathrm{BC}}
 =\left\{F:\int_{\mathbb R}\sum_{q,r}
 |\tau+\nu_q|^2|F_{q,r}(\tau)|^2\,d\tau<\infty\right\}.
\]
This operator is the closure of
\(M_\tau\otimes1+1\otimes N_{\mathrm{BC}}\) on finite channel Schwartz
functions. The common tensor construction does not identify
\(\widetilde{\mathcal K}\) with an arithmetic quotient of \(\mathcal K\).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Tensor wave obstruction]
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:prop:tensor-wave-obstruction}
Both tensor operators are self-adjoint and purely absolutely
continuous. The strong limits of
\(e^{it\widetilde D_{\mathrm{BC}}}e^{-it\widetilde D_0}\), as
\(t\to+\infty\) or \(t\to-\infty\), exist on precisely the
closed subspace supported on \(q=1\). They equal the identity there.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The maximal multiplication argument in the preceding lemma applies
on each channel. Finite channel truncations followed by smooth
compact approximations converge in the graph norm of either
operator, proving the assertion about closures. The spectral measure
of \(F\) for \(\widetilde D_{\mathrm{BC}}\) is the sum of the translated
measures \(|F_{q,r}(\lambda-\nu_q)|^2d\lambda\), hence is absolutely
continuous. The free case is identical without translation.
On each channel the wave family is multiplication by
\(e^{it\nu_q}\). If this family converges on a vector, its projection
onto any nonzero channel also converges. For \(\nu_q\ne0\), choose
\(s\) with \(e^{is\nu_q}\ne1\). The norm of the difference at times
\(t+s\) and \(t\) is the nonzero constant
\(|e^{is\nu_q}-1|\|F_{q,r}\|\), which contradicts convergence.
Thus all such channels vanish. Since \(\nu_q=0\) exactly when
\(q=1\), the remaining assertion follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Transport and the zero divisor]
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:rem:relation-to-HHP}
An isometry intertwining \(N_{\mathrm{BC}}\) with a bounded operator
cannot exist: the unit vectors \(v_{q,r}\) have unbounded eigenvalues.
An isometry intertwining it with \(D_0\) cannot exist either,
since \(D_0\) has no eigenvectors. Abstract isomorphism of separable
Hilbert spaces asserts neither intertwining property.
The hypothetical zero-divisor operator of
\Cref{v4-arith:w5-b:def:twist} requires its separately stated
operator, domain, and determinant comparison. The tensor
construction supplies none of those zero-divisor data.
\end{remark}

\section{Trace-Class Scattering}
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:framework:S-matrix}
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:reformulation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:reformulation:birman-krein}

\begin{definition}[Wave operators]
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:def:wave-operators}
For self-adjoint operators \(A_0,A_1\) on one complex Hilbert space,
write \(P_{\mathrm{ac}}(A_j)\) for the absolutely continuous spectral
projection. When the strong limits exist, define
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:eq:wave-operators}
 W_\pm(A_1,A_0)=\mathop{\mathrm{s}\!\!-
 lim}_{t\to\pm\infty}e^{itA_1}e^{-itA_0}P_{\mathrm{ac}}(A_0).
\end{equation}
Completeness means that both ranges equal
\(P_{\mathrm{ac}}(A_1)\mathcal H\). In that case
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:eq:S-operator}
 S=W_+^*W_-
\end{equation}
is unitary on \(P_{\mathrm{ac}}(A_0)\mathcal H\).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Trace-class scattering and spectral shift]
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:prop:birman-krein}
Let \(A_0\) be self-adjoint and let \(V=V^*\) be trace class.
Set \(A_1=A_0+V\) on \(\operatorname{Dom}A_0\).
The wave operators exist and are complete. There is a real
\(\xi\in L^1(\mathbb R)\), with
\(\|\xi\|_1\leq\|V\|_1\), such that for \(h\in\mathscr S(\mathbb R)\),
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:eq:birman-krein}
 \operatorname{Tr}(h(A_1)-h(A_0))
 =\int_{\mathbb R}h'(\lambda)\xi(\lambda)\,d\lambda.
\end{equation}
In a direct-integral spectral representation of \(A_0\) on its
absolutely continuous subspace, \(S\) has unitary fibres
\(S(\lambda)\), with \(S(\lambda)-1\) trace class almost everywhere,
and
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:eq:BK-det}
 \det S(\lambda)=e^{-2\pi i\xi(\lambda)}\quad\text{almost everywhere}.
\end{equation}
The normalization of \(\xi\) is specified by
\[
 \Delta_V(z)=\det(1+V(A_0-z)^{-1}),\qquad
 \xi(\lambda)=\pi^{-1}\operatorname{Im}\log\Delta_V(\lambda+i0),
\]
where the analytic logarithm in the upper half-plane tends to zero
as \(\operatorname{Im}z\to+\infty\).
\end{theorem}

These are the Kato--Rosenblum, Krein trace, and Birman--Krein
scattering theorems with the sign convention of
\Cref{v4-arith:w7-j:prop:rank-one-sign}.
The operator and logarithm hypotheses are recorded in
\href{https://arxiv.org/pdf/0712.3120}{Behrndt--Malamud--Neidhardt},
pp.~2--3, equations~(1.1)--(1.5); their Theorem~2.7, p.~15,
also treats finite-deficiency self-adjoint extension pairs.
The determinant relation by itself determines \(\xi\) only modulo
integer-valued functions. It does not replace the normalized
perturbation determinant.

\begin{proposition}[A specified rank-one pair on the idele carrier]
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:prop:wave-existence}
On the Mellin realization of \(\mathcal K\), choose
\(g\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)\) with \(\|g\|_2=1\), and \(a\in\mathbb R\).
Let \(Q_gF=g\int\overline{g(\lambda)}F(\lambda)\,d\lambda\), and set
\[
 V_a=aQ_g\otimes P_0,\qquad D_a=D_0+V_a,
 \qquad\operatorname{Dom}D_a=\operatorname{Dom}D_0.
\]
This pair has complete wave operators. Its perturbation determinant is
\[
 \Delta_a(z)=1+a\int_{\mathbb R}
 \frac{|g(\lambda)|^2}{\lambda-z}\,d\lambda.
\]
Almost everywhere, the scattering matrix is the identity on
\((1-P_0)E\) and is multiplication on \(P_0E\) by
\[
 s_a(\lambda)=
 \frac{\overline{\Delta_a(\lambda+i0)}}{\Delta_a(\lambda+i0)}.
\]
The spectral shift is the boundary argument of \(\Delta_a\) with
exactly the preceding logarithm normalization.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The operator \(Q_g\otimes P_0\) is an orthogonal projection of
rank one. Hence \(V_a\) is bounded self-adjoint and trace class,
with trace norm \(|a|\). The general theorem applies.
The rank-one determinant formula \(\det(1+u\ell)=1+\ell(u)\)
gives the displayed determinant. On the complementary compact
channels both dynamics are identical. The remaining channel has
spectral multiplicity one for \(D_0\), so its scattering fibre
is its determinant. The Birman--Krein formula and
\(\xi_a=\pi^{-1}\arg\Delta_a(\lambda+i0)\) give the stated quotient.
The boundary values and the quotient are understood almost
everywhere, as in the general theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Finite truncations and trace ideals]
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:rem:trace-class-domain}
Any specified finite-rank self-adjoint perturbation of \(D_0\)
satisfies the same theorem. A limit requires its own trace-ideal
estimate. For example, on orthonormal vectors \(u_j\), the diagonal
operator \(\sum_j b_j|u_j\rangle\langle u_j|\) is trace class exactly
when \(\sum_j|b_j|<\infty\). Square summability gives only the
Hilbert--Schmidt ideal. Neither a formal cutoff nor nuclearity
between different Schwartz spaces proves this Hilbert trace-class
condition. The functions \(g\) and coefficients \(a\) above have
not been identified with the Bost--Connes generator.
\end{remark}

\section{Positivity of a Spectral-Shift Distribution}
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:reformulation:ATP-unitarity}

\begin{theorem}[The ordinary trace-class positivity obstruction]
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:thm:ATP-iff-S-unitary}
Let \(\xi\in L^1(\mathbb R)\) be real, and define
\(T(h)=\int h'\xi\) on \(\mathscr S(\mathbb R)\).
The following conditions are equivalent:
\[
 T(|h_f|^2)\geq0\quad(f\in\mathscr A),
 \qquad -d\xi\geq0,
 \qquad \xi=0\text{ almost everywhere}.
\]
In particular, a nonzero positive trace distribution cannot arise
as the ordinary trace difference of a trace-class perturbation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The map \(T\) is Schwartz-continuous, since
\(|T(h)|\leq\|\xi\|_1\|h'\|_\infty\).
Compactly supported smooth functions are dense in Schwartz space:
for a Schwartz function \(f\), multiplication by
\(\chi(u/R)\), where \(\chi=1\) near zero, converges to \(f\)
in every Schwartz seminorm. Fourier transformation is a Schwartz
isomorphism. Thus the first inequality implies
\(T(|g|^2)\geq0\) for every \(g\in\mathscr S\).

Let \(\varphi\in C_c^\infty\) be nonnegative. Choose a nonnegative
compact smooth function \(\eta\) equal to one on a neighborhood
of \(\operatorname{supp}\varphi\). The function
\(g_\varepsilon=(\varphi+\varepsilon\eta^2)^{1/2}\) is smooth:
on a neighborhood of the support of \(\varphi\) its argument is
positive, and outside that neighborhood it equals
\(\sqrt\varepsilon\eta\). It has compact support. Consequently
\(T(\varphi)+\varepsilon T(\eta^2)\geq0\).
Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) proves that \(-d\xi\) is a
positive distribution.

For a nonnegative smooth approximate identity \(\rho_\varepsilon\),
\(\xi*\rho_\varepsilon\) is a real, nonincreasing, integrable
smooth function. Such a function is zero: a positive value forces
an infinite integral on its left, and a negative value forces one
on its right. Hence \(\xi*\rho_\varepsilon=0\) for every
\(\varepsilon\), and convergence in \(L^1\) gives \(\xi=0\).
The reverse implications follow directly.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The phase and its derivative]
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:rem:honest-caveat}
For locally integrable \(\xi\), its distributional derivative is
defined on smooth compact tests by
\(\langle-d\xi,h\rangle=\int\xi h'\).
It need not initially be a signed measure. Positivity makes it
a positive measure and forces \(\xi\) to have a nonincreasing
representative. The additional global \(L^1\) condition in the
theorem forces that measure to vanish. A regularized trace outside
this class requires a separate theorem, including its test space
and normalization.
\end{remark}

\section{Mellin Divisibility and Multiplication Phases}
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:tate-mellin-closure}
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:sub-class}

An ordinary Mellin integral near zero records the Taylor
coefficients of an entire function as poles. This constrains a
proposed divisibility by the entire completed xi-function before
any zero-location argument is available.

\begin{definition}[The entire-Mellin test class]
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:def:TM-subclass}
Let \(\mathrm{PW}_2\) denote the entire functions of exponential
type whose real restrictions belong to \(L^2(\mathbb R)\).
Define \(\mathrm{PW}^{\mathrm{TM}}\) to consist of its real even
functions \(f\) for which the ordinary Mellin integral continues as
\[
 Mf(s)=\xi_{\mathbb R}(s)b(s),\qquad
 b(s)=\overline{b(1-\bar s)},
\]
where \(b\) is entire of exponential type and decreases faster than
every inverse power on each closed vertical strip. Here
\(\xi_{\mathbb R}(s)=\tfrac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\)
with its entire continuation. This Mellin integral uses \(f(t)\)
on \(t>0\); it is distinct from the Fourier transform of the
logarithmic compact test algebra \(\mathscr A\).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Entire continuation forces the zero function]
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:thm:ATP-TM-closure}
If \(f\) is entire, \(f|_{\mathbb R}\in L^2\), and its ordinary
Mellin transform is entire, then \(f=0\).
In particular, \(\mathrm{PW}^{\mathrm{TM}}=\{0\}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The Mellin integral converges absolutely on \(0<\Re s<1/2\).
Near zero this follows from boundedness of \(f\). At infinity,
Cauchy--Schwarz applies because
\(\int_1^\infty t^{2\Re s-2}dt<\infty\).
Both estimates are locally uniform on smaller strips, including
logarithmic factors from differentiation, so the integral is
holomorphic there. Write \(a_n=f^{(n)}(0)/n!\). For each \(N\geq0\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 Mf(s)={}&\int_1^\infty f(t)t^{s-1}dt
 +\int_0^1\left(f(t)-\sum_{n=0}^Na_nt^n\right)t^{s-1}dt
 +\sum_{n=0}^N\frac{a_n}{s+n}.
 \end{split}
\]
The first integral is holomorphic for \(\Re s<1/2\).
The Taylor remainder makes the second holomorphic for
\(\Re s>-N-1\). The residue at \(s=-n\) is therefore \(a_n\).
Entire continuation forces every coefficient to vanish.
The identity theorem gives \(f=0\). The divisibility condition
makes \(Mf\) entire, proving the final assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Zeros and the test topology]
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:rem:T2-restriction}
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:rem:TM-density}
The completed xi-function vanishes at all nontrivial zeros, with
multiplicity. It does not vanish at the trivial zeros of \(\zeta\),
where gamma poles cancel those zeros.
The zero-class theorem uses neither this divisor nor RH.
It implies nondensity of \(\mathrm{PW}^{\mathrm{TM}}\) in every
nonzero normed \(\mathrm{PW}_2\) space. A measure-zero condition
on values of a transform, by itself, would not imply nondensity
in an \(L^2\) topology.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Functional equations and wave limits}
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:classical-mellin}

\begin{proposition}[The completed functional-equation ratio]
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:prop:TM-S-classical}
The meromorphic ratio
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:eq:S-TM}
 \frac{\xi_{\mathbb R}(1/2+iz)}{\xi_{\mathbb R}(1/2-iz)}
\end{equation}
has the removable continuation \(1\) on \(\mathbb C\).
For an entire function of real type, \(b(\bar s)=\overline{b(s)}\),
the ratio \(b(1/2+it)/b(1/2-it)\) has modulus one wherever defined.
The distinct symmetry \(b(s)=\overline{b(1-\bar s)}\) alone does
not imply this modulus identity.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The functional equation \(\xi_{\mathbb R}(s)=\xi_{\mathbb R}(1-s)\)
identifies numerator and denominator as entire functions, including
their zero orders. The real-type assertion follows by complex
conjugation. For the last assertion take
\(b(s)=1+i(s-1/2)\). It has the stated reflection symmetry, whereas
the ratio on the critical line is \((1-t)/(1+t)\).
This example tests the algebraic implication of that symmetry;
it is not an element of the entire-Mellin test class.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Multiplication perturbations]
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:prop:multiplication-waves}
Let \(v:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) be measurable and finite almost
everywhere. On \(L^2(\mathbb R)\), give \(M_\lambda\) and
\(M_{\lambda+v(\lambda)}\) their maximal domains.
Their ordinary wave operators exist on the full free space if
and only if \(v=0\) almost everywhere.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The wave family is \(M_{e^{itv}}\).
If it converges strongly, then for every real \(s\) and every
\(g\in L^2\),
\[
 \|(e^{isv}-1)g\|
 =\|e^{i(t+s)v}g-e^{itv}g\|\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus \(e^{isv}=1\) almost everywhere for every rational \(s\).
The intersection of these countably many full-measure sets forces
\(v=0\). The converse follows because the two operators coincide.
\end{proof}

A functional-equation ratio therefore needs a separately constructed
scattering realization. Replacing \(D_0\) by multiplication by
\(\tau+\tfrac12\arg\xi_{\mathbb R}(1/2+i\tau)\) does not supply
one unless that perturbation vanishes almost everywhere.

\section{Arithmetic Trace Carriers}
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:nc-residual}
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:connes-reduction}
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:sources}

\begin{definition}[The adele quotient and a Hilbert seminorm]
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:def:connes-ac-space}
The adele class space is the quotient
\(X_{\mathbb Q}=\mathbb A/\mathbb Q^\times\).
Let \(\mathscr S(\mathbb A)\) be the Bruhat--Schwartz space for the
additive adeles, with self-dual additive Haar measure. Put
\[
 \mathscr S(\mathbb A)_0
 =\{\phi:\phi(0)=0,\ \int_{\mathbb A}\phi(x)dx=0\},\qquad
 (E\phi)(x)=|x|^{1/2}\sum_{q\in\mathbb Q^\times}\phi(qx).
\]
For \(\delta>1\), give this space the seminorm
\[
 \|\phi\|_\delta^2
 =\int_{C_{\mathbb Q}}|E\phi(x)|^2
 (1+\log^2|x|)^{\delta/2}\,d^\times x.
\]
Quotient by its nullspace and complete. This is the Hilbert
construction in Connes, \emph{Trace formula in noncommutative
geometry and the zeros of the Riemann zeta function},
\href{https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9811068}{\S III, (6)--(19), pp.~10--12}.
Write \(L^2_\delta(C_{\mathbb Q})\) for the weighted target space
with measure \((1+\log^2|x|)^{\delta/2}d^\times x\).
The completed map \(E\) is isometric and has closed image.
Its cokernel \(H_\delta=L^2_\delta(C_{\mathbb Q})/\operatorname{im}E\)
carries the quotient of \(V(a)\eta(x)=\eta(a^{-1}x)\).
The identity \(EU(a)=|a|^{1/2}V(a)E\), for
\(U(a)\phi(x)=\phi(a^{-1}x)\), makes the image invariant.
The weighted norm-direction representation is polynomially bounded,
not asserted to be unitary. The quotient generator in Connes's
convention is the derivative of \(V(e^t)\); its critical-zero
spectral values are imaginary. This is distinct from the
self-adjoint free generator on unweighted \(L^2(C_{\mathbb Q})\).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Scope of the critical-zero realization]
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:prop:connes-absorption}
In the quotient representation on \(H_\delta\), Connes's \S III, Theorem~1, p.~13, realizes the zeros
on the critical line in each norm-one character sector.
For \(\delta>1\), the multiplicity of a critical zero of
multiplicity \(m\) is the largest integer \(n\) satisfying
\(n<(1+\delta)/2\) and \(n\leq m\).
Its Corollary~2 gives the trace of integrated Schwartz operators
with these multiplicities. This theorem is a critical-zero
realization, not a self-adjoint realization of every nontrivial zero.
\end{proposition}

Meyer's \emph{On a representation of the idele class group related
to primes and zeros of \(L\)-functions},
\href{https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0311468}{Theorems~5.8 and~5.11,
pp.~43 and~47}, uses a different carrier: a smooth summable
virtual representation \(\pi_+\ominus\pi_-\).
Its character is the raw Weil distribution, with poles and zeros
having opposite signed multiplicities. His \S3.2, p.~17, descends
suitable equivariant operators to weighted idele-class spaces.
His Proposition~4.4, p.~26, gives Schwartz restriction and Fourier
maps. These statements specify neither a bounded transport of
\(N_{\mathrm{BC}}\) nor the perturbation of an idele scaling operator.
Passing between the raw character and \(W=P-W_\infty-W_{\mathrm{fin}}\)
requires the central twist, sign, and test-function normalization.

\section{The Full Signed Trace Comparison}
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:reformulation:identification}
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:comparison}

\begin{theorem}[Transfer on the specified arithmetic test algebra]
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:thm:weil-as-shift}
Suppose independently specified self-adjoint operators
\(A_0,A_1=A_0+V\), with \(V=V^*\) trace class, satisfy
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:eq:weil-as-shift}
 W(f)=\operatorname{Tr}(h_f(A_1)-h_f(A_0))
 \qquad(f\in\mathscr A).
\end{equation}
Then, for their normalized spectral shift \(\xi\),
\[
 W(f*f^*)=\int_{\mathbb R}(|h_f|^2)'(\lambda)\xi(\lambda)d\lambda.
\]
If this form is nonnegative on all \(\mathscr A\), the trace
functional vanishes on every Schwartz spectral test. In particular,
a nonzero positive Weil functional cannot have this ordinary
trace-class realization.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply the stipulated identity to \(f*f^*\), use
\(h_{f*f^*}=h_fh_f^\sharp\), and apply the trace formula.
The positivity obstruction forces \(\xi=0\), which makes the
trace formula zero for every Schwartz function. Under Weil
positivity, RH holds by \Cref{v4-arith:w5-a:cor:weil-positivity-RH}.
The zero-side formula then is a sum of \(|h_f(\gamma_\rho)|^2\).
It is nonzero for some \(f\): choose a nontrivial zero ordinate
\(\gamma_0\), a compact smooth \(\phi\) of integral one, and
\(f(u)=e^{-i\gamma_0u}\phi(u)\). The term at \(\gamma_0\) is
positive, and the other terms are nonnegative. Existence of
nontrivial zeros is part of \Cref{v4-arith:pm:zero-count}.
This contradicts vanishing and proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Required arithmetic data]
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:rem:identification-buys}
The trace identity in the theorem is additional arithmetic data.
Trace-class estimates, wave completeness, and a functional equation
do not imply it. A proposed regularized replacement must specify
its carrier, scaling action, dense test domain, subtraction terms,
and trace functional before imposing equality with \(W\).
If it uses Bost--Connes data, it must also construct the map from
\(\mathcal H_1\), or its stated occupation restriction, and prove
which domains and operations that map preserves. Nuclear characters
on locally convex spaces and Hilbert trace differences are distinct
constructions.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Independent conditions on an arithmetic trace]
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:thm:obstruction-located}
Let \(\mathcal T:\mathscr A\to\mathbb C\) be a linear distribution
obtained from a specified arithmetic trace construction.
If \(\mathcal T(f)=W(f)\) for every \(f\in\mathscr A\), then
\(\mathcal T(f*f^*)\geq0\) for every \(f\) is equivalent to A-TP,
and hence to RH. Existence of \(\mathcal T\), the comparison
identity, and this positivity condition are separate requirements.
The tensor and multiplication constructions above fail the wave
requirement; the rank-one construction satisfies it but supplies
no arithmetic trace identity.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Substitute \(f*f^*\) in the stated identity and apply the Weil
positivity criterion on \(\mathscr A\). The final assertions are
the tensor wave obstruction, the multiplication proposition, and
the explicit rank-one construction, respectively.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Comparison with the other analytic formulations]
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:rem:three-reductions}
The bounded-interval digamma inequality and the restricted
automorphic comparison concern the same full signed distribution
only when their stated test maps and residual terms agree with
\(P-W_\infty-W_{\mathrm{fin}}\).
A scattering comparison additionally needs the trace identity above.
Connes's cutoff trace and Meyer's virtual character supply precise
alternative trace settings; neither is identified here with the
critical BC generator by a unitary map. The non-Hausdorff quotient
alone proves no impossibility theorem for other Hilbert realizations.
\end{remark}

\section{Consequences for the Arithmetic Realization Problem}
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:form}

\begin{theorem}[Scaling and scattering constructions]
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:thm:summary}
The idele scaling operator has the self-adjoint domain and Mellin
realization specified above. Coupling it by tensor addition to the
full critical BC generator gives self-adjoint dynamics but no full
ordinary wave operators. Specified finite-rank perturbations do
have complete scattering; their unitary matrices do not imply
positivity of the trace difference. The ordinary entire-Mellin
class \(\mathrm{PW}^{\mathrm{TM}}\) is zero. A nonzero positive
Weil trace therefore requires a construction beyond the ordinary
trace-class comparison stated here, with its arithmetic test
identity and positivity proved on all of \(\mathscr A\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply the Mellin-domain lemma, the tensor wave obstruction, the
rank-one pair and signed-trace propositions, the entire-Mellin
vanishing theorem, and the trace-comparison theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Arithmetic comparison problem]
\label{v4-arith:w7-j:rem:beilinson}
An arithmetic BC comparison must realize
\(W=P-W_\infty-W_{\mathrm{fin}}\) on \(\mathscr A\).
Positivity of this trace is the full Weil condition.
\end{remark}
