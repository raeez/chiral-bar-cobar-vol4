\chapter{BZN\({}^{\mathrm{Ar}}\): Loop-Space Convention and Wild-Prime Summands}
\label{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion}

The arithmetic trace comparison has two separate requirements. First,
the internal mapping stack
\(\underline{\mathrm{Map}}(B\widehat{\mathbb Z},\mathcal X)\)
must be compared with finite-quotient and ind-completed loop models
under explicit formal-convergence hypotheses. Second, local arithmetic
categories at the primes two and three must be constructed with their
coefficient rings, extension groups and trace maps.

The finite-algebra calculations below show why the second requirement
cannot be replaced by a Loewy-length argument. A radical-square-zero
algebra can have nonzero Ext in arbitrarily high degrees. Even when
two local trace complexes are concentrated in degrees zero and one,
their tensor over a common field can have a nonzero degree-two term.
An integral distinct-primary model behaves differently, but needs
actual integral carriers and flatness hypotheses. These calculations
constrain the proposed arithmetic comparison; they do not construct
its categories, trace maps or completed global assembly.

\section{The Two Loop-Convention and Wild-Prime Conditions}
\label{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:context}

\subsection{Origin of the Two Conditions}

The structural comparison of Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:comparison} at
\S\ref{v4-arith:sw:wave2} contains four conditions in the F3.BZN\({}^{\mathrm{Ar}}\)
reduction. Three of these (the formal-Noetherian
extension of Gaitsgory--Rozenblyum; the cotangent-complex check for
the Emerton--Gee stack; and the R1 relabeling) were absorbed into
the R1 split \(\mathrm{R1.fin\text{-}type}+\mathrm{R1.formal}\)
stated in Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:comparison}. The remaining two,
not absorbed into the existing reduction, are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{R3.} Ambiguity in the loop-space convention.
Three candidate definitions of
\(L^{\mathrm{Ar}}\mathcal{X}=\mathrm{Map}(B\widehat{\mathbb{Z}},\mathcal{X})\)
are in play:
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbf{(a)}] \emph{Cofiltered-limit convention.}
\(L^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathrm{cofilt}}\mathcal{X}:=\varprojlim_{n}\mathrm{Map}(B(\mathbb{Z}/n),\mathcal{X})\),
the limit over finite quotients of \(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\).
\item[\textbf{(b)}] \emph{Internal-Hom convention.}
\(L^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathrm{intHom}}\mathcal{X}:=\underline{\mathrm{Map}}_{\mathrm{dSt}^{\wedge}_{\mathrm{Noeth}}}(B\widehat{\mathbb{Z}},\mathcal{X})\),
the internal mapping stack in the \(\infty\)-category of formal
Noetherian derived stacks.
\item[\textbf{(c)}] \emph{Ind-construction convention.}
\(L^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathrm{ind}}\mathcal{X}:=\varinjlim_{n}\mathrm{Ind}\bigl(\mathrm{Map}(B(\mathbb{Z}/n),\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{perf}})\bigr)\),
the ind-completion over finite quotients, applied to the perfect
locus and re-ind-extended.
\end{itemize}
These agree for \(\mathcal{X}\) finite-type smooth, but the formal
Noetherian setting is sensitive to the order of limits and
ind-completions.

\item \textbf{R4.} Local arithmetic extension and trace data.
For the proposed exceptional contributions at \(p=2,3\), specify
the coefficient categories, compute their extensions and construct
maps to their trace complexes. A positive-degree trace contribution
is not a formal consequence of non-semisimplicity. Its global effect
also depends on the common coefficient ring and tensor product.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Logical Structure}

R3 is a convention plus a convergence hypothesis: the internal Hom is
the chosen definition, and comparison with the other two definitions
requires compactness and formal-colimit compatibility. R4 includes
the local categories, trace comparisons and tensor assembly. The
explicit algebra calculations in Section~\ref{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:R4}
show the additional hypotheses needed for a degree-one-only answer.

\section{R3: Arithmetic Loop-Space Convention}
\label{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:R3}

\subsection{The Three Candidate Definitions}

Throughout this section \(\mathcal{X}\in\mathrm{dSt}^{\wedge}_{\mathrm{Noeth}}\)
is a formal Noetherian derived algebraic stack over \(\mathbb{Z}\)
(or over \(\mathrm{Spf}\,\mathbb{Z}_{p}\) when working locally at a
prime). We demand:
\begin{itemize}
\item \(\mathcal{X}\) is locally presented as the formal completion
of a derived Noetherian affine scheme
\(\mathcal{X}_{0}=\mathrm{Spec}\,A_{0}^{\bullet}\) along an ideal
\(I\subset\pi_{0}(A_{0}^{\bullet})\);
\item the cotangent complex \(\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{X}}\) has finite
Tor-amplitude \([a,b]\) over the structure sheaf;
\item the Postnikov tower \(\pi_{\leq n}\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}\)
stabilises in each tor-layer (equivalently, \(\mathcal{X}\) is
\(t\)-bounded in the sense of Gaitsgory--Rozenblyum Vol.~I~\S2).
\end{itemize}
For the arithmetic target these conditions must be checked
for the Selmer-derived stack
\(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{Sel}}_{\bar\rho,\det,\mathcal{L}}\); the local
Emerton--Gee theorem supplies only the local formal inputs.

\begin{definition}[the three candidate arithmetic free loop spaces]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:def:three-candidates}
For \(\mathcal{X}\in\mathrm{dSt}^{\wedge}_{\mathrm{Noeth}}\), define
\begin{align}
L^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathrm{cofilt}}\mathcal{X}
&\;:=\;\varprojlim_{n\geq 1}\mathrm{Map}\bigl(B(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}),\mathcal{X}\bigr),\\
L^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathrm{intHom}}\mathcal{X}
&\;:=\;\underline{\mathrm{Map}}_{\mathrm{dSt}^{\wedge}_{\mathrm{Noeth}}}\bigl(B\widehat{\mathbb{Z}},\mathcal{X}\bigr),\\
L^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathrm{ind}}\mathcal{X}
&\;:=\;\varinjlim_{n\geq 1}\mathrm{Ind}\bigl(\mathrm{Map}(B(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}),\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{perf}})\bigr),
\end{align}
where:
\begin{itemize}
\item in (a) the limit is along the cofiltered system
\(\{\mathbb{Z}/n\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\) under divisibility;
\item in (b) the internal mapping stack is computed in the
\(\infty\)-category \(\mathrm{dSt}^{\wedge}_{\mathrm{Noeth}}\) of
formal Noetherian derived pro-\'etale stacks;
\item in (c) \(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{perf}}\) denotes the perfect
locus (dualisable objects in the formal quasi-coherent
derived category), and \(\mathrm{Ind}\) is the ind-completion in
stable presentable \(\infty\)-categories.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\subsection{Explicit Computation of the Three Candidate Definitions}

\begin{proposition}[three candidate loop-space formulas]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:prop:R3-three-computations}
\emph{Conjectural statement.} Let \(\mathcal{X}\in\mathrm{dSt}^{\wedge}_{\mathrm{Noeth}}\)
be formal Noetherian derived algebraic with cotangent complex
\(\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{X}}\) of finite Tor-amplitude. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Cofiltered-limit formula.}
\begin{equation}
L^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathrm{cofilt}}\mathcal{X}
\;=\;\varprojlim_{n}\Bigl(\mathcal{X}\times_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{X}_{(n)}\Bigr),
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal{X}_{(n)}\subset\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}\) is the
\(n\)-th formal neighbourhood of the diagonal.
\item \emph{Internal-Hom formula.}
\begin{equation}
L^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathrm{intHom}}\mathcal{X}
\;=\;\mathcal{X}\times_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}}^{\mathrm{d},\wedge}\mathcal{X},
\end{equation}
the derived formal self-intersection of \(\mathcal{X}\) along its
diagonal, computed in \(\mathrm{dSt}^{\wedge}_{\mathrm{Noeth}}\)
with the derived tensor product taken over the formal diagonal.
\item \emph{Ind-construction formula.}
\begin{equation}
L^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathrm{ind}}\mathcal{X}
\;=\;\mathrm{Ind}\Bigl(\varinjlim_{n}\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{perf}}\times_{\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{perf}}\times\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{perf}}}\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{(n)}\Bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Evidence]
(1) The mapping stack from \(B(\mathbb{Z}/n)\) to \(\mathcal{X}\) is
the fixed-point locus of the order-\(n\) automorphism action, which
is the derived fibre product along the \(n\)-th iterate of the
diagonal. In characteristic zero (for the rational structure at
each prime) this reduces to the formal neighbourhood
\(\mathcal{X}_{(n)}\). Taking the limit over \(n\) under divisibility
restricts to the formal self-intersection in the limit.

(2) Internal Hom in \(\mathrm{dSt}^{\wedge}_{\mathrm{Noeth}}\) is
computed by the derived loop formula
\(\mathcal{X}\times^{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{X}\)
(Gaitsgory--Rozenblyum's AMS DAG monographs Vol.~I Ch.~8
Thm.~8.1.1.9); the formal-Noetherian decoration adds a
completion along the diagonal, yielding the indicated
\(\times^{\mathrm{d},\wedge}\).

(3) Perfect locus: dualisable objects in
\(\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathcal{X})\) form \(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{perf}}\),
a compactly generated full subcategory. The mapping stack
restricts to \(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{perf}}\) in each factor; the
cofiltered colimit over \(n\) and the subsequent ind-completion
together recover the dualisable content. By the same derived
fibre-product formula restricted to perfects plus ind-completion,
we obtain the stated expression.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[separation of the three limits]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:rem:three-formulas}
The three formulas differ in the order of \((\varinjlim/\varprojlim)\)
operations and in whether they apply to \(\mathcal{X}\) or to
\(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{perf}}\). Equality of the three models
requires convergence of the Postnikov tower, generation by perfect
objects under ind-completion, and compatibility of formal completion
with the profinite limit.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Conventions for the Three Loop Models}

\paragraph{Conventions.} Derived mapping stack
convention follows Gaitsgory--Rozenblyum Vol.~I Ch.~8; profinite
classifying-stack convention follows Deligne 1990 \S 5; the two are
compatible via
\(B\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}=\varprojlim_{n}B(\mathbb{Z}/n)\).

\paragraph{Ambient category.} All three candidate
constructions take values in the \(\infty\)-category
\(\mathrm{dSt}^{\wedge}_{\mathrm{Noeth}}\) of formal Noetherian
derived pro-\'etale stacks. Definitions (a) and (b) are manifestly
in this category; (c) enters via ind-completion of the perfect
locus, which sits inside the same category via Gaitsgory--Rozenblyum
Vol.~I Ch.~5.

\paragraph{Hypotheses.} Finite Tor-amplitude of
\(\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{X}}\) and \(t\)-boundedness of the Postnikov
tower are explicit hypotheses of
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:prop:R3-three-computations}.

\subsection{Equivalence of the Three Definitions on Formal Noetherian Derived Stacks}

\begin{theorem}[conditional comparison of the three arithmetic loop spaces]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:thm:R3-three-agree}
\emph{Conditional statement.} Let \(\mathcal{X}\in\mathrm{dSt}^{\wedge}_{\mathrm{Noeth}}\)
be formal Noetherian derived algebraic with cotangent complex
\(\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{X}}\) of finite Tor-amplitude and
\(t\)-bounded. Assume:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(B\widehat{\mathbb Z}\)-mapping stacks are computed by the
profinite limit of the finite cyclic mapping stacks on \(\mathcal X\);
\item formal completion along the diagonal commutes with the relevant
derived tensor products;
\item the perfect locus generates the same loop object after
ind-completion.
\end{enumerate}
Then there are canonical equivalences of formal
Noetherian derived stacks
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:eq:three-agree}
L^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathrm{cofilt}}\mathcal{X}
\;\xleftarrow{\;\sim\;}\;L^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathrm{intHom}}\mathcal{X}
\;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\;L^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathrm{ind}}\mathcal{X}.
\end{equation}
All three express the derived formal self-intersection of
\(\mathcal{X}\) along its diagonal.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Under the first two hypotheses,
\(L^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathrm{cofilt}}\mathcal X\) and
\(L^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathrm{intHom}}\mathcal X\) compute the same
completed derived self-intersection of the diagonal. Under the third
hypothesis, the ind-construction gives the ind-completion of the same
self-intersection on the perfect locus. The maps are the canonical
comparison maps induced by the universal property of internal Hom,
formal completion, and ind-completion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[what fails without the hypotheses]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:rem:hyp-failures}
Noetherianness, \(t\)-boundedness, and finite Tor-amplitude do not by
themselves identify all three loop models. They are the natural
ambient hypotheses under which the three comparison maps can be
asked to be equivalences.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[application to the Selmer-derived target]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:cor:R3-for-EG}
\emph{Comparison hypothesis.} If the Selmer-derived target
\(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{Sel}}_{\bar\rho,\det,\mathcal{L}}\) satisfies
the formal-Noetherian and bounded cotangent hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:thm:R3-three-agree}, then the three
candidate definitions of
\(L^{\mathrm{Ar}}\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{Sel}}_{\bar\rho,\det,\mathcal{L}}\)
all compute the derived formal self-intersection along the diagonal
and agree canonically.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Hypotheses of the Loop Comparison}

\paragraph{Conventions.} Postnikov-tower and formal-completion

\paragraph{Ambient category.} All three constructions sit in
\(\mathrm{dSt}^{\wedge}_{\mathrm{Noeth}}\); their equivalence is

\paragraph{Hypotheses.} Finite Tor-amplitude, \(t\)-boundedness,
Noetherianness are stated in the hypothesis set of

\paragraph{Functoriality.} The zig-zag
(\ref{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:eq:three-agree})

\(\mathrm{dSt}^{\wedge}_{\mathrm{Noeth}}\to\mathrm{dSt}^{\wedge}_{\mathrm{Noeth}}\).

\paragraph{Named theorems.} Gaitsgory--Rozenblyum supplies the

\subsection{Canonical Choice and Consistency with Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:arith-bzn}}

\begin{definition}[canonical arithmetic free loop space]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:def:canonical-L-Ar}
For \(\mathcal{X}\in\mathrm{dSt}^{\wedge}_{\mathrm{Noeth}}\) formal
Noetherian derived algebraic with the hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:thm:R3-three-agree}, the
\emph{canonical arithmetic free loop space of \(\mathcal{X}\)} is
\begin{equation}
L^{\mathrm{Ar}}\mathcal{X}\;:=\;L^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathrm{intHom}}\mathcal{X}
\;=\;\underline{\mathrm{Map}}_{\mathrm{dSt}^{\wedge}_{\mathrm{Noeth}}}\bigl(B\widehat{\mathbb{Z}},\mathcal{X}\bigr).
\end{equation}
Under
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:thm:R3-three-agree},
\(L^{\mathrm{Ar}}\mathcal{X}\) is canonically equivalent to both
\(L^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathrm{cofilt}}\mathcal{X}\) and
\(L^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathrm{ind}}\mathcal{X}\); we adopt the internal
Hom as definitional.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[why internal Hom is canonical]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:rem:why-intHom}
The internal Hom construction is canonical in two senses:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Functoriality in the source.} \(\underline{\mathrm{Map}}(B\widehat{\mathbb{Z}},-)\)
is manifestly functorial in \(\mathcal{X}\) without choosing a
cofiltered presentation of \(B\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\) or an
ind-completion presentation of \(\mathcal{X}\).
\item \emph{Universal property.} By the standard closed-monoidal
structure on \(\mathrm{dSt}^{\wedge}_{\mathrm{Noeth}}\) (Lurie HTT
\S 7.3, transported via Gaitsgory--Rozenblyum Vol.~I), the
internal Hom is the right adjoint to the product functor
\((-\times B\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})\). The cofiltered and
ind-construction versions require extra choices to realise this
universal property.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\section{Wild-Prime Extension and Trace Comparisons}
\label{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:R4}

A Yoneda extension is computed in a specified abelian category; a
Hochschild class belongs to a specified trace complex. Relating the two
requires a map, not just equality of dimensions. Two elementary
calculations also constrain the possible degrees of a global trace:
a radical-square-zero algebra can have higher Ext, and tensoring two
complexes supported in degrees zero and one can produce degree two.
We first make these calculations over explicit coefficient rings.
Their arithmetic realization remains a separate comparison problem.

\subsection{Finite Algebras and Higher Extensions}

\begin{lemma}[degreewise finiteness of extensions]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:lem:EO-ext}
Let \(A\) be a finite-dimensional algebra over a field \(k\).
There are finitely many isomorphism classes of simple right
\(A\)-modules, and \(\operatorname{Ext}^n_A(M,N)\) is finite-dimensional
for finite-dimensional \(M,N\) and each \(n\ge0\).
The total graded Ext space need not be finite-dimensional.
No identification with Hochschild homology is included in this statement.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every simple module is a quotient of the regular module, whose finite
composition series contains only finitely many simple factors.
A finite-dimensional module admits a surjection from a finite sum of
copies of \(A\). Its kernel is finite-dimensional; repeating constructs
a resolution with a finite-dimensional projective term in each degree.
Applying \(\operatorname{Hom}_A(-,N)\) gives a finite-dimensional space
in each degree, hence finite-dimensional cohomology. The following
example proves the final assertion about total dimension.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[a periodic resolution]
\label{v4-arith:wild:dual-numbers}
For \(A=k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)\) and \(S=A/(\epsilon)\),
\[
 \operatorname{Ext}^n_A(S,S)=k\qquad(n\ge0).
\]
Thus Loewy length two does not imply projective dimension one.
In characteristic two, \(A\cong k[C_2]\), so the finite tensor category
of finite-dimensional \(k[C_2]\)-modules also has infinite-dimensional
total Yoneda algebra.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Multiplication by \(\epsilon\) has kernel and image both equal to
\(k\epsilon\). The augmentation kernel is the same subspace. Hence
\[
 \cdots\xrightarrow{\epsilon}A\xrightarrow{\epsilon}A
 \xrightarrow{\epsilon}A\longrightarrow S\longrightarrow0
\]
is an exact free resolution. Applying \(\operatorname{Hom}_A(-,S)\)
gives one copy of \(k\) in every nonnegative degree, with zero
differential because \(\epsilon\) acts trivially on \(S\).
If \(\operatorname{char}k=2\), writing \(\epsilon=g-1\) for the
generator of \(C_2\) gives \(\epsilon^2=g^2-2g+1=0\).
The two-dimensional algebras are therefore isomorphic.
The group-algebra coproduct gives its usual tensor category.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[paths and radical-square-zero Ext]
\label{v4-arith:wild:path-count}
Let \(Q\) be a finite quiver, \(J\) its arrow ideal, and
\(B=kQ/J^2\). Use right modules and concatenate paths from left to
right. For the simple \(S_i=e_iB/e_iJ\),
\[
 \dim_k\operatorname{Ext}^n_B(S_i,S_j)
 =\#\{\text{oriented paths of length }n\text{ from }i\text{ to }j\}.
\]
Consequently all \(\operatorname{Ext}^{n\ge2}\) between simples vanish
if and only if \(Q\) has no composable pair of arrows.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The projective cover is \(P_i=e_iB\), and its radical has one copy
of \(S_j\) for each arrow \(i\to j\), because every product of two
arrows is zero. Thus
\(\Omega S_i=\bigoplus_{i\to j}S_j\).
Iteration gives one copy of \(S_j\) in \(\Omega^nS_i\) for each
length-\(n\) path. The resulting minimal projective resolution has
its differentials in the radicals. Every map from a projective to a
simple kills that radical, so applying \(\operatorname{Hom}_B(-,S_j)\)
gives zero differentials. This proves the formula, including \(n=0\).
A composable pair gives a nonzero degree-two group. If no such pair
exists, there are no paths of any length at least two.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[a three-vertex block and its Yoneda product]
\label{v4-arith:wild:three-vertex}
Take \(Q=(0\xrightarrow{a}1\xrightarrow{b}2)\) and \(B=kQ/(ab)\).
The right projectives have bases
\(P_0=\langle e_0,a\rangle\), \(P_1=\langle e_1,b\rangle\),
and \(P_2=\langle e_2\rangle\). There is an exact resolution
\[
 0\longrightarrow P_2\xrightarrow{e_2\mapsto b}P_1
 \xrightarrow{e_1\mapsto a}P_0\longrightarrow S_0\longrightarrow0.
\]
Indeed the middle map kills \(b\), since \(ab=0\); its kernel is
\(kb\), and its image is the augmentation kernel \(ka\).
It follows that
\[
 \operatorname{Ext}^1_B(S_0,S_1)=k,\qquad
 \operatorname{Ext}^1_B(S_1,S_2)=k,\qquad
 \operatorname{Ext}^2_B(S_0,S_2)=k.
\]
The product of the two adjacent degree-one classes is the nonzero
degree-two class: the short exact sequence
\(0\to S_1\to P_0\to S_0\to0\) gives the dimension-shifting
isomorphism
\(\operatorname{Ext}^1_B(S_1,S_2)\cong\operatorname{Ext}^2_B(S_0,S_2)\),
whose connecting map is Yoneda composition.
Thus the relation \(ab=0\) in \(B\) does not make the corresponding
Yoneda product zero. Reversing all arrows reverses the ordered pairs
in the Ext groups; it does not eliminate degree two.

For comparison, the unquotiented path algebra \(kQ\) has
\(\operatorname{rad}P_0\cong P_1\) and
\(\operatorname{rad}P_1\cong P_2\), hence length-one projective
resolutions of its simples. This is a different algebra; its
\(P_0\) has Loewy length three. An arithmetic identification with
one of these algebras must specify which relations it preserves.
\end{example}

\subsection{Regular Hochschild Homology of the Finite Models}

The same two algebras permit complete trace calculations. They show
that extensions between simples and regular Hochschild homology
can behave differently even in these small examples. Throughout
this subsection \(k\) is any field, the algebras are ordinary and
ungraded, and all tensors are algebraic. For an algebra \(R\), put
\(R^e=R\otimes_k R^{\mathrm{op}}\) and
\(HH_n(R)=\operatorname{Tor}^{R^e}_n(R,R)\), with regular
coefficients. This is Hochschild homology, not the cohomology
of derivations used for the Bost--Connes algebra.

\begin{proposition}[the regular trace of the dual numbers]
\label{v4-arith:wild:HH-dual-numbers}
For \(A=k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)\),
\begin{align*}
 HH_0(A)&=A,\\
 HH_{2r+1}(A)&=A/(2\epsilon A) &&(r\ge0),\\
 HH_{2r}(A)&=\operatorname{ann}_A(2\epsilon) &&(r\ge1).
\end{align*}
If \(\operatorname{char}k\ne2\), each positive-degree group has
dimension one; odd groups are \(A/(\epsilon)\) and positive even
groups are \(\epsilon A\). If \(\operatorname{char}k=2\), then
\(HH_n(A)=A\) for every \(n\ge0\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Identify \(A^e=k[x,y]/(x^2,y^2)\), with \(x\) acting on the left
and \(y\) on the right. Set \(u=x-y\), \(v=x+y\). Consider
\[
 \cdots\xrightarrow{u}A^e\xrightarrow{v}A^e
 \xrightarrow{u}A^e\xrightarrow{\mu}A\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad \mu(x)=\mu(y)=\epsilon.
\]
There is one free term in each nonnegative degree; the differential
from an odd degree is multiplication by \(u\), and from a positive
even degree multiplication by \(v\). The composites vanish because
\(uv=vu=0\). For \(r=\alpha+\beta x+\gamma y+\eta xy\),
\[
 ur=\alpha(x-y)+(\gamma-\beta)xy,\qquad
 vr=\alpha(x+y)+(\beta+\gamma)xy.
\]
Hence \(\ker u=\langle x+y,xy\rangle=\operatorname{im}v\),
\(\ker v=\langle x-y,xy\rangle=\operatorname{im}u\), and
\(\ker\mu=\langle x-y,xy\rangle\). Both images have dimension
two, including in characteristic two. This is an exact free
bimodule resolution.

Tensoring with the regular \(A\) makes \(u\) zero and \(v\)
multiplication by \(2\epsilon\). Thus the odd differentials are
zero and the positive even differentials are \(2\epsilon\), giving
the asserted quotients and annihilators. If two is invertible,
\(\operatorname{ann}_A(2\epsilon)=\epsilon A\); if two is zero,
all differentials vanish.

The normalized Hochschild complex checks the signs directly.
Since \(A/k1=k\epsilon\), its degree-\(n\) term is
\(A\otimes(k\epsilon)^{\otimes n}\). Interior products vanish,
and the first and cyclic last faces give
\[
 b_n(c\otimes\epsilon^{\otimes n})
  =(1+(-1)^n)c\epsilon\otimes\epsilon^{\otimes(n-1)}.
\]
This is the same alternating zero and \(2\epsilon\) differential.
\end{proof}

The simple-module calculation in
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:wild:dual-numbers} gives
\(\operatorname{Ext}^n_A(S,S)=k\) in every degree and characteristic.
In characteristic two its dimension is one, whereas the regular
Hochschild group has dimension two. The two calculations use
different resolutions and different coefficient carriers.

\begin{proposition}[the regular trace of the directed three-vertex model]
\label{v4-arith:wild:HH-three-vertex}
For \(B=k(0\xrightarrow a1\xrightarrow b2)/(ab)\),
\[
 HH_0(B)=ke_0\oplus ke_1\oplus ke_2,\qquad
 HH_n(B)=0\quad(n\ge1).
\]
Thus the nonzero Ext groups and Yoneda product of
Example~\ref{v4-arith:wild:three-vertex} do not force nonzero
positive-degree regular Hochschild homology.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let \(S=ke_0\oplus ke_1\oplus ke_2\), \(J=ka\oplus kb\).
Then \(B=S\oplus J\), \(J^2=0\), and
\(J\otimes_SJ=k(a\otimes b)\); there are no composable triples.
The relative normalized bimodule bar terms are
\(P_n=B\otimes_SJ^{\otimes_Sn}\otimes_SB\), with
\begin{align*}
 P_0&=\bigoplus_{i=0}^2 Be_i\otimes_k e_iB,\\
 P_1&=(Be_0\otimes_k e_1B)\oplus(Be_1\otimes_k e_2B),\\
 P_2&=Be_0\otimes_k e_2B.
\end{align*}
Each is a projective bimodule: each summand is an idempotent direct
summand of the free bimodule \(B\otimes_kB\).
The differentials, retaining arrows in the bar notation, are
\begin{align*}
 d_1(l\otimes j\otimes r)&=lj\otimes r-l\otimes jr,\\
 d_2(l\otimes a\otimes b\otimes r)
 &=la\otimes b\otimes r+l\otimes a\otimes br.
\end{align*}
The missing middle term is \(-l\otimes ab\otimes r=0\).

Here is a direct exactness check. The dimensions of
\(P_0,P_1,P_2\) are \(8,4,1\), and a basis of \(P_1\) is
\begin{align*}
 q_1&=e_0\otimes a\otimes e_1,&
 q_2&=e_0\otimes a\otimes b,\\
 q_3&=e_1\otimes b\otimes e_2,&
 q_4&=a\otimes b\otimes e_2.
\end{align*}
Their \(d_1\)-images are respectively
\[
 a\otimes e_1-e_0\otimes a,\quad a\otimes b,\quad
 b\otimes e_2-e_1\otimes b,\quad -a\otimes b.
\]
The first three are independent, so \(\ker d_1=k(q_2+q_4)\).
The generator \(e_0\otimes a\otimes b\otimes e_2\) of \(P_2\)
maps to \(q_2+q_4\); hence \(d_2\) is injective and exactness
holds at \(P_1\). Multiplication \(P_0\to B\) is surjective,
annihilates \(\operatorname{im}d_1\), and has kernel of dimension
\(8-5=3\). This proves exactness also at \(P_0\), in every
characteristic. Thus \(0\to P_2\to P_1\to P_0\to B\to0\)
is a projective bimodule resolution.

The regular trace of a summand is
\[
 B\otimes_{B^e}(Be_i\otimes_k e_jB)\cong e_jBe_i.
\]
The map sends the class of \(c\otimes(l\otimes r)\) to \(rcl\);
its inverse sends \(t\in e_jBe_i\) to the class of
\(t\otimes(e_i\otimes e_j)\). Balancing verifies that these
maps are well defined and inverse. A path from \(i\) to \(j\)
can therefore contribute only if there is a return path from
\(j\) to \(i\). Here
\(e_1Be_0=e_2Be_1=e_2Be_0=0\) and \(e_iBe_i=ke_i\).
The traced complex is \(k^3\) in degree zero and zero in every
positive degree, as asserted.
\end{proof}

In this example the relation \(ab=0\) contributes a genuine
bimodule-resolution term in degree two, but regular trace kills
it because its endpoints differ. Extension groups between simples
retain the directed paths; Hochschild homology requires cyclic
endpoint matching. Neither calculation constructs the arithmetic
Ext-to-trace comparison in the following questions.

\subsection{The Local Arithmetic Comparisons}

\begin{remark}[non-semisimplicity and the coefficient category]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:prop:non-ss-wild}
The example \(k[C_2]\) in characteristic two is non-semisimple:
\(0\to S\to A\to S\to0\) is not split. Transferring such a result
to an arithmetic Iwahori block requires that block, its coefficient
ring and its relation to the finite algebra to be specified.
The primes \(2,3\) alone do not determine a coefficient category.
The fields \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\) and
\(\overline{\mathbb F}_\ell\) have characteristics zero and \(\ell\),
respectively, and require different specialization arguments.
\end{remark}

\begin{question}[the proposed contribution at two]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:prop:R4-Ext1-p2}
Construct the arithmetic block and its trace complex
\(C_2^{\mathrm{wild}}\), with a comparison realizing the proposed
one-dimensional degree-one contribution
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:eq:Ext1-p2}
 H_1(C_2^{\mathrm{wild}})\cong W_2,\qquad W_2\cong k.
\end{equation}
The coefficient field and the map from extension data to this trace
homology are part of the construction. In characteristic two, the
trivial and sign characters of \(C_2\) are the same character,
since \(-1=1\). They cannot serve as two distinct simples in an
off-diagonal Ext calculation. The dual-number model does give a
one-dimensional self-Ext in degree one, but it also gives nonzero
Ext in every higher degree. A claim of local amplitude at most one
therefore needs an actual resolution or a trace calculation that
accounts for the higher extension classes.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[the proposed contribution at three]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:prop:R4-Ext1-p3}
Construct the arithmetic block, coefficient field and comparison
maps that would realize
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:eq:Ext1-p3}
 H_1(C_3^{\mathrm{wild}})\cong W_3,\qquad W_3\cong k\oplus k.
\end{equation}
In the explicit three-vertex algebra of
Example~\ref{v4-arith:wild:three-vertex}, the two classes are in
\(\operatorname{Ext}^1(S_0,S_1)\) and
\(\operatorname{Ext}^1(S_1,S_2)\), with the fixed right-module
convention, and they have a nonzero Yoneda product in degree two.
An arithmetic comparison must preserve these directions and account
for that product. In characteristic three,
\(X^3-1=(X-1)^3\), so there is no primitive third root of unity.
A primitive-cube-root specialization in characteristic zero is a
different model, requiring an explicit comparison with a modular
coefficient category. Neither a choice of root nor a
Calabi--Yau or Frobenius structure by itself constructs the map
from Yoneda extensions to Hochschild homology.
\end{question}

\begin{hypothesis}[the large-prime trace datum]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:thm:bez-ss-large-p}
For the chosen common coefficient ring \(R\), suppose that the
arithmetic local trace complexes \(C_p\) have been constructed and
that, for every \(p\ge5\), there is a specified equivalence
\(C_p\simeq B_p[0]\) with an \(R\)-module \(B_p\).
This is a hypothesis on the actual trace complexes, including their
arithmetic comparison; it is not inferred solely from an assertion
about a Yoneda algebra or a numerical prime threshold.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{corollary}[conditional large-prime vanishing]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:cor:vanishing-at-large-p}
Under Hypothesis~\ref{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:thm:bez-ss-large-p},
\(H_i(C_p)=0\) for every \(i>0\) and \(p\ge5\).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The specified equivalence identifies the homology with that of a
module placed in degree zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[conditional vanishing of the large-prime wild part]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:thm:R4-correction-vanishes-p-large}
If a wild summand is specified inside such a \(C_p\), its
positive-degree homology vanishes for \(p\ge5\).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Homology preserves a direct-sum decomposition, so the summand's
positive-degree homology is a direct summand of zero.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Four Terms in a Derived Tensor Product}

Complexes are now homologically graded: the differential lowers
degree, and \(M[i]\) places a module \(M\) in degree \(i\).
All tensors in this subsection are algebraic derived tensors over
the displayed common ring. A completed tensor requires its own
continuity and exactness assertions.

\begin{lemma}[two split local complexes]
\label{v4-arith:wild:four-term-tensor}
For a commutative ring \(R\), suppose
\(C_2\simeq B_2\oplus W_2[1]\) and
\(C_3\simeq B_3\oplus W_3[1]\) in \(D(R)\), with specified splittings.
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:wild:eq:four-term-tensor}
\begin{aligned}
 C_2\otimes_R^{\mathrm L}C_3\simeq{}&
 (B_2\otimes_R^{\mathrm L}B_3)\\
 &\oplus\bigl((W_2\otimes_R^{\mathrm L}B_3)
        \oplus(B_2\otimes_R^{\mathrm L}W_3)\bigr)[1]\\
 &\oplus(W_2\otimes_R^{\mathrm L}W_3)[2].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Over a field, with \(B_2=B_3=k\), \(W_2=k\), and \(W_3=k^2\), this is
\(k\oplus k^3[1]\oplus k^2[2]\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose projective resolutions of the four modules. Distributing
the tensor of the two finite direct sums gives the four displayed
terms and their shifts. Over a field the modules are already flat,
so the zero-differential representatives compute the derived tensor.
The total differential is
\(d(x\otimes y)=dx\otimes y+(-1)^{|x|}x\otimes dy=0\).
For a basis vector \(x\) of \(W_2\) and a basis \(y,z\) of \(W_3\),
\(x\otimes y\) and \(x\otimes z\) are independent degree-two
cycles and cannot be boundaries. More generally, the tensor of two
nonzero vector spaces over a field is nonzero: linear functionals
nonzero on chosen vectors detect a nonzero elementary tensor.
Graded commutativity does not cancel products between different factors.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[distinct-primary integral coefficients]
\label{v4-arith:wild:distinct-primary}
If \(M_2\) is a \(2\)-primary torsion abelian group and \(M_3\)
is a \(3\)-primary torsion abelian group, then
\(M_2\otimes_{\mathbb Z}^{\mathrm L}M_3=0\).
If \(B_2,B_3\) are flat abelian groups in degree zero, it follows that
\((B_2\oplus M_2[1])\otimes_{\mathbb Z}^{\mathrm L}
 (B_3\oplus M_3[1])\) is concentrated in degrees zero and one.
For \(B_2=B_3=\mathbb Z\), \(M_2=\mathbb Z/2\), and
\(M_3=(\mathbb Z/3)^2\), it is
\(\mathbb Z\oplus(\mathbb Z/2\oplus(\mathbb Z/3)^2)[1]\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Resolve \(\mathbb Z/2^a\) by
\([\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{2^a}\mathbb Z]\).
After tensoring with \(\mathbb Z/3^b\), its differential is an
isomorphism, so the complex is acyclic. Finite primary groups
are finite direct sums of cyclic primary groups. Arbitrary primary
torsion groups are filtered unions of finite subgroups; tensor and
Tor commute with these filtered colimits. There is no higher Tor
beyond degree one over \(\mathbb Z\), since the kernel of a
surjection from a free abelian group is free.
This proves the first assertion. Flatness of \(B_2,B_3\) removes
higher Tor from the other three terms in
\eqref{v4-arith:wild:eq:four-term-tensor}, proving the remaining claims.
Without flat degree-zero factors, those terms can carry additional
homology. Completion is not part of this algebraic argument.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Arithmetic Assembly Problem}

\begin{question}[the full-target arithmetic trace comparison]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:thm:F3-BZN-Ar-corrected}
Construct compatible local arithmetic categories and trace complexes,
a common coefficient ring, their completed restricted-tensor assembly,
and a comparison with the full Weil/\((\varphi,\Gamma)\) target, of the form
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:eq:F3-BZN-Ar-corrected}
 \mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}(\mathrm{Hk}^{\mathrm{Iw}}_{GL_2,\mathrm{glob}})
 \simeq \mathcal O(\mathrm{LocSys}^{W,\varphi\Gamma}_{GL_2})^{\mathrm{flat}}
 \oplus\mathcal W.
\end{equation}
Here \(\mathcal W\), its coefficient carrier and its degrees must be
computed from that construction. The procyclic \(B\widehat{\mathbb Z}\)
model records only the inertia comparison, not the full target.
The formal-loop hypotheses, F1, F2, and local degree-zero comparisons
remain necessary parts of the proposed construction; their names do
not supply a completed K\"unneth map.

If actual local traces over a common field split as
\(k\oplus k[1]\) and \(k\oplus k^2[1]\), the full two-prime tensor
has the degree-two term of Lemma~\ref{v4-arith:wild:four-term-tensor}.
Its additive degree-one part is
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:eq:wild-summand}
 \mathcal W_{\le1}=k[1]\oplus(k\oplus k)[1].
\end{equation}
This can describe the positive part of a specified degree-at-most-one
truncation, or a quotient killing the mixed product. It is not the full
wild tensor contribution. To realize such a truncation or quotient
arithmetically, its map and compatibility with the operations must
also be constructed.
\end{question}

\begin{remark}[remaining degree-zero factors]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:rem:domain}
If the restricted tensor of the other primes is a degree-zero module
\(D\), every term of \eqref{v4-arith:wild:eq:four-term-tensor} must
also be tensored with \(D\). Thus even the degree-one terms carry
all complementary degree-zero factors. Taking units at almost every
prime permits non-unit entries at both two and three; it does not
remove their mixed term. A common-field calculation cannot be
replaced by the distinct-primary integral calculation without actual
integral models and their comparison maps.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[conditions for degree-one assembly]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:cor:F3-corrected-closes}
Suppose actual local trace complexes over \(R\) have the two specified
splittings, all remaining factors assemble into a flat degree-zero
module \(D\), and a proved assembly map identifies the global trace
with \(D\otimes_R^{\mathrm L}C_2\otimes_R^{\mathrm L}C_3\).
If \(B_2,B_3\) are flat and
\(W_2\otimes_R^{\mathrm L}W_3=0\), its homology is concentrated in
degrees zero and one, with underlying split complex
\[
 D\otimes_R B_2\otimes_R B_3
 \ \oplus\
 D\otimes_R\bigl((W_2\otimes_R B_3)
          \oplus(B_2\otimes_R W_3)\bigr)[1].
\]
Any completed or arithmetic sheaf interpretation additionally requires
that the realization and completion preserve these identifications.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{v4-arith:wild:four-term-tensor}.
The mixed term vanishes by hypothesis, and flatness removes higher
Tor from the remaining terms and from tensoring with \(D\).
The specified assembly map then transports this decomposition.
\end{proof}

An extension of the comparison in Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:arith-bzn}
must preserve the local trace complexes and the chosen tensor
assembly. A degree-zero comparison does not determine the
positive-degree part, and a list of local Ext dimensions does not
supply a trace comparison.

\begin{proposition}[the tensor terms required by a local comparison]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:prop:minimal-amendment}
Suppose an arithmetic comparison identifies its two exceptional
local traces over a field with \(k\oplus k[1]\) and
\(k\oplus k^2[1]\), and identifies their assembly with their full
derived tensor. Then that assembly is
\(k\oplus k^3[1]\oplus k^2[2]\).
If the remaining factors assemble into a degree-zero vector space
\(D\), all three terms must also be tensored with \(D\).
The additive expression in
\eqref{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:eq:wild-summand} describes only the

requires extra vanishing hypotheses, as in
Corollary~\ref{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:cor:F3-corrected-closes},
or a specified truncation or quotient.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{v4-arith:wild:four-term-tensor} to the specified
local equivalences. Tensoring with \(D\) is exact over a field.
The two independent degree-two classes persist whenever \(D\ne0\).
\end{proof}

\section{Loop-Convention Boundary}
\label{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:summary}

\begin{enumerate}

a completed arithmetic realization also needs a comparison preserving

\item \textbf{The arithmetic target.}
The full Weil/\((\varphi,\Gamma)\) trace comparison in
Question~\ref{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:thm:F3-BZN-Ar-corrected}
