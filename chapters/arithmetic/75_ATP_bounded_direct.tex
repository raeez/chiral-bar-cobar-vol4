\chapter{Archimedean Tate Kernel Positivity: the Integral Energy Sub-Class}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:chapter}

The digamma auxiliary form has a negative spectral core and a positive
tail. An integral-energy estimate compares their masses and proves
nonnegativity on an explicit class of compact smooth tests.
Moment-preserving differential operators supply tests in this class
and tests outside both the integral and supremum sufficient classes.
The signed gamma form differs from this auxiliary form by a scalar
multiple of the $L^2$ pairing. The complete Weil inequality also
retains its prime and polar contributions.

\section{Kernel and Integral Energy}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:context}
The integral-energy estimate concerns an auxiliary digamma form.
Here $\psi_\Gamma=\Gamma'/\Gamma$, and $\widehat f=h_f$ in this chapter.
Use the angular Fourier transform
$h_f(u)=\int_{\mathbb R}f(x)e^{iux}\,dx$ and set
\[
 g(u)=\Re\psi_\Gamma(1/4+iu/2)+\log\pi,\qquad
 k(u)=2\log\pi-g(u),
\]
\[
 G_g(f,h)=\frac1{2\pi}\int h_f(u)\overline{h_h(u)}g(u)\,du.
\]
The signed gamma form of the explicit formula is therefore
\[
 W_\infty(f*h^*)=2\log\pi\langle f,h\rangle_2-G_g(f,h).
\]
This identity follows from Plancherel. It retains the angular
normalization and differs from the cyclic normalization used for
$Q_g$ in Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:w7-d:chapter}.

\subsection{Domain}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:domain}
Let $\mathscr A=C_c^\infty(\mathbb R;\mathbb C)$ with its
inductive-limit topology. Its real even subspace is denoted by $E$.
For $f\in E$ set
\[
 M_f(s)=\int f(x)e^{sx}\,dx,\qquad
 L(f)=\left(\int f,\int f\cosh x,\int xf(x)\sinh x\,dx\right).
\]
The class $\mathscr A_{13}=\ker L\subset E$ has precisely the
reality and double-Mellin-zero conditions at $0,1$.
Compact support makes $M_f$ entire and rapidly decreasing on every
closed vertical strip. No weight-dependent strip restriction remains.
These conditions impose no modularity on a coefficient multiplier.

\begin{lemma}[closed moment space and moment correction]
\label{v4-arith:consumer75:moment-projection}
The space $\mathscr A_{13}$ is closed of real codimension three in $E$.
There is a continuous real linear projection
$\Pi:E\to\mathscr A_{13}$ of the form $\Pi=1-RL$,
where $R:\mathbb R^3\to E$ has compactly supported image and $LR=1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Integration against each smooth weight is continuous on every
fixed-support test space, hence on the inductive limit.
Take normalized even mollifiers concentrated at $0,\{\pm1\}$,
and $\{\pm2\}$. Their moment columns tend to the columns obtained
by evaluating $1,\cosh x,x\sinh x$ at $0,1,2$.
The limiting determinant is
$2\sinh(1)(\cosh(1)-1)^2>0$.
For a sufficiently small fixed width the moment matrix $C$ is
invertible. If the three mollifiers are $b_0,b_1,b_2$, define
$Rv=\sum_{j=0}^2b_j(C^{-1}v)_j$.
Then $LR=1$, $\Pi^2=\Pi$, and $L\Pi=0$.
The image of $R$ has fixed compact support, so $R$ and $\Pi$ are
continuous. Surjectivity of $L$ gives the codimension.
\end{proof}

Write $t_*$ for the positive zero of $g$, and $M=-g(0)$.
For nonzero $f\in\mathscr A_{13}$ define the core and tail energies
\[
 A_g[f]=\int_{|u|<t_*}|h_f(u)|^2(-g(u))\,du,\qquad
 B_g[f]=\int_{|u|>t_*}|h_f(u)|^2g(u)\,du.
\]
Thus $G_g(f,f)=(B_g[f]-A_g[f])/(2\pi)$.
The supremum sufficient condition is
\[
 2t_*M\sup_{|u|<t_*}|h_f(u)|^2\le B_g[f].
\]
The integral-energy condition replaces this supremum by core mass.
Both conditions concern $G_g$. The full arithmetic form remains
\[
 W(f*f^*)=P(f*f^*)-2\log\pi\|f\|_2^2
                  +G_g(f,f)-W_{\mathrm{fin}}(f*f^*).
\]
In particular, neither moment correction nor positivity of $G_g$
proves the full Weil inequality.


For the energy comparison fix $\tau_0>t_*$ and a continuous
nondecreasing function $\ell:[\tau_0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)$ satisfying
$\ell(T)\le g(T)$ and $\ell(T)=O(1+\log(2+T))$.
The unconditional choice $\ell=g|_{[\tau_0,\infty)}$ is available.
Indeed, the Hurwitz series
\[
 g(u)=g(0)+\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{u^2/4}
 {(n+1/4)((n+1/4)^2+u^2/4)}
\]
is strictly increasing for $u>0$, and the digamma asymptotic gives
logarithmic growth. Since $g(0)<0$ and $g(u)\to\infty$, there is
exactly one $t_*>0$. Other choices of $\ell$ require the displayed
lower-bound property. The proposed Stirling expression is one
specific lower-bound question, separate from this energy theorem.

\section{A proposed Stirling specialization of the lower weight}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:stirling-lower-bound}

\subsection{Stirling Series for the Digamma and Sign Control}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:stirling}

\begin{definition}[the digamma remainder]
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:lem:stirling-series}
For $\Re z>0$ and $N\ge0$, define $R_N(z)$ by
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:eq:stirling-series}
 \psi(z)=\log z-\frac1{2z}
 -\sum_{j=1}^N\frac{B_{2j}}{2jz^{2j}}+R_N(z).
\end{equation}
Here $\log$ is the principal branch and $B_{2j}$ are Bernoulli numbers,
with $B_2=1/6$, $B_4=-1/30$ and $B_6=1/42$.
The proposed sectorial bound for this derivative remainder is
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:eq:remainder-bound}
 |R_N(z)|\le
 \frac{|B_{2N+2}|}{(2N+2)|z|^{2N+2}}
 \sec^{2N+2}(\arg z/2).
\end{equation}
Using this bound requires a proof for the digamma remainder with
these constants. A remainder estimate for $\log\Gamma$ cannot
simply be differentiated as an inequality.
\end{definition}
The asymptotic expansion and remainder problem are treated in
Whittaker--Watson~1927, \S12.33, and Olver,
\emph{Asymptotics and Special Functions}, Ch.~8.
The energy theorem below uses $\ell\le g$ and does not require
\eqref{v4-arith:w7-e:eq:remainder-bound}.

\begin{lemma}[real-part Stirling at \(z=1/4+it/2\)]
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:lem:re-psi}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere. For \(t\in\mathbb{R}\) set
\(z(t):=1/4+it/2\). Then
\begin{align}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:eq:re-log-z}
\mathrm{Re}\,\log z(t)&\;=\;\tfrac{1}{2}\log(1/16+t^{2}/4)\;=\;\tfrac{1}{2}\log\bigl((1+4t^{2})/16\bigr),\\
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:eq:re-reciprocal-z}
\mathrm{Re}\,\frac{-1}{2z(t)}&\;=\;-\frac{1}{8|z(t)|^{2}}\;=\;-\frac{2}{1+4t^{2}}.
\end{align}
The one-step truncation of \eqref{v4-arith:w7-e:eq:stirling-series}
(with \(N=0\), no Bernoulli terms) gives
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:eq:stirling-1}
\mathrm{Re}\,\psi(z(t))\;=\;\tfrac{1}{2}\log\bigl((1+4t^{2})/16\bigr)-\frac{2}{1+4t^{2}}+\mathrm{Re}\,R_{0}(z(t)).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\(|z(t)|^{2}=(1/4)^{2}+(t/2)^{2}=1/16+t^{2}/4\). Hence \(\log|z|=
(1/2)\log(1/16+t^{2}/4)\). For \(1/z\): \(\mathrm{Re}(1/z)=\mathrm{Re}\,z/|z|^{2}=(1/4)/|z|^{2}\),
so \(\mathrm{Re}(-1/(2z))=-(1/8)/|z|^{2}=-2/(1+4t^{2})\). The identity
follows by direct substitution in \eqref{v4-arith:w7-e:eq:stirling-series}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Lower Bound \(g_{L}\)}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:g-lower-bound}

\begin{proposition}[conditional Stirling lower weight]
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:thm:g-lower-bound}
Define
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:eq:g-L-def}
 g_L(t)=\tfrac12\log((1+4t^2)/16)+\log\pi-\frac2{1+4t^2},
\end{equation}
and let $t_L^*>0$ be its unique positive zero.
Suppose the fourth-order bound proposed in
\Cref{v4-arith:w7-a:def:g-lower}, \Cref{v4-arith:w7-a:prop:g-stirling-4}
holds on $|t|\ge t_L^*$ in the precise form
\[
 g(t)\ge g_L(t)+\frac{20u-72}{15u^2},\qquad u=1+4t^2.
\]
Then $g(t)>g_L(t)$ on that tail.
Consequently $\ell=g_L|_{[\tau_0,\infty)}$ is an admissible
positive lower weight for every $\tau_0>t_L^*$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
As a function of $u\ge1$, $g_L$ has derivative
$1/(2u)+2/u^2>0$, is negative at one and tends to infinity.
At $u=18/5$, use $\pi^2<10$ to obtain
$g_L<\log(3/2)-5/9<1/2-5/9<0$.
Hence the zero has $u>18/5$, and the assumed correction
$(20u-72)/(15u^2)$ is strictly positive on the stated tail.
Monotonicity and logarithmic growth give the properties of $\ell$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the quantitative remainder question]
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:rem:quantitative-gap}
The first Bernoulli correction is
\[
 -\frac1{12}\Re(z(t)^{-2})
 =\frac{4t^2-1}{192(1/16+t^2/4)^2}
 =\frac1{3t^2}+O(t^{-4}).
\]
A uniform estimate for $g-g_L$ on a finite interval requires a
remainder bound in addition to this leading term.
The conditional proposition states the exact lower estimate needed
from the fourth-order calculation. The unconditional choice $\ell=g$
on a positive tail avoids this separate remainder question.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[the lower-weight zero]
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:cor:g-L-zero}
The function $g_L$ has exactly one positive zero $t_L^*$, and is
nonnegative for $|t|\ge t_L^*$. Under the fourth-order hypothesis
of the preceding proposition, $t_*<t_L^*$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Existence and uniqueness follow from the derivative calculation.
Under that hypothesis $g(t_L^*)>g_L(t_L^*)=0$.
Strict monotonicity of $g$ on the positive axis gives $t_*<t_L^*$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Sign-domain comparison}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:sign-domain}
Under the fourth-order lower-bound hypothesis, $g<0$ on
$|t|<t_*$, while $g_L<0$ on $|t|<t_L^*$.
On $t_*\le|t|<t_L^*$, $g\ge0>g_L$.
On $|t|\ge t_L^*$, $g>g_L\ge0$.
These specialized comparisons are conditional on that bound.
For the energy argument, any positive nondecreasing lower weight
$\ell\le g$ on $[\tau_0,\infty)$ suffices.

\section{The integral-energy class}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:integral-energy}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:energy-fractions}
\begin{definition}[spectral fractions]
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:def:energy-fractions}
For any nonzero $f\in L^2(\mathbb R)$, let $h_f$ denote its angular Fourier transform in the Plancherel sense. For $T\ge\tau_0>t_*$, set
\begin{align}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:eq:alpha-core}
 \alpha(f)&=\frac{\int_{|u|<t_*}|h_f(u)|^2du}{\|h_f\|_2^2},\\
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:eq:beta-far}
 \beta(f,T)&=\frac{\int_{|u|>T}|h_f(u)|^2du}{\|h_f\|_2^2},\\
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:eq:gamma-mid}
 \gamma(f,T)&=1-\alpha(f)-\beta(f,T).
\end{align}
All three fractions are nonnegative and sum to one.
\end{definition}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:IE-definition}
\begin{definition}[integral and supremum classes]
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:def:IE-subclass}
Use the lower weight $\ell$ specified above. Put
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:eq:IE-condition}
 \mathrm{IE}(T)=\{f\in\mathscr A_{13}\setminus\{0\}:
                        M\alpha(f)\le\ell(T)\beta(f,T)\},\qquad T\ge \tau_0.
\end{equation}
Write $\mathrm{HFD}$ for the nonzero $f\in\mathscr A_{13}$ satisfying
\[
 2t_*M\sup_{|u|<t_*}|h_f(u)|^2\le B_g[f].
\]
The notations $\mathrm{PW}^{\mathrm{hol},\mathrm{IE}}_F(T)$ and
$\mathrm{PW}^{\mathrm{hol},\mathrm{HFD}}_F$ in the energy formulas
refer to these compact moment classes, without an automorphy assertion.
\end{definition}
\begin{remark}[dependence on the threshold]
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:rem:monotonicity-T1}
Increasing $T$ increases $\ell(T)$ and decreases $\beta(f,T)$.
Their product need not be monotone. The threshold is therefore part
of the sufficient condition.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Unconditional \(G_g\)-Positivity on \(\mathrm{PW}^{\mathrm{hol},\mathrm{IE}}_{F}\)}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:IE-closure}

\begin{theorem}[integral-energy bound]
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:thm:IE-ATP}
For a nonzero $f\in\mathscr A_{13}$, suppose $T_1\ge \tau_0$ and
$M\alpha(f)\le \ell(T_1)\beta(f,T_1)$.
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:eq:IE-closure}
 G_g(f,f)\ge0,\qquad
 W_\infty(f*f^*)\le2\log\pi\|f\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
The full Weil inequality holds exactly when
\[
 G_g(f,f)\ge2\log\pi\|f\|_2^2
             +W_{\mathrm{fin}}(f*f^*)-P(f*f^*).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
On the core, $-g\le M$, so
$A_g[f]\le M\alpha(f)\|h_f\|_2^2$.
On the middle band $g\ge0$, while on the far tail
$g(u)\ge \ell(|u|)\ge \ell(T_1)\ge0$.
Consequently $B_g[f]\ge \ell(T_1)\beta(f,T_1)\|h_f\|_2^2$.
The assumed inequality gives $B_g[f]\ge A_g[f]$.
The signed gamma identity proves the second bound.
Substitution into the complete explicit formula proves the last equivalence.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Strict Enlargement over the bounded-interval reduction HFD}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:strict-enlargement}

\begin{proposition}[strict extension of the supremum criterion]
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:prop:strict-inclusion}
On the nonzero tests in $\mathscr A_{13}$, the union of the
supremum class and the integral-energy classes strictly contains
the supremum class.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix a nonzero real even $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$.
Put $D=d/dx$, $\mathcal L=-D^2(D^2-1)^2$, and
$p(u)=u^2(1+u^2)^2$. Integration by parts gives
\[
 M_{\mathcal Lv}(s)=-s^2(s^2-1)^2M_v(s),\qquad
 h_{\mathcal Lv}(u)=p(u)h_v(u).
\]
Thus $\mathcal Lv\in\mathscr A_{13}$ for every real even compact test $v$.
For $A\ge1$ let $\varphi_A(x)=A^{-1/2}\varphi(x/A)$.
Choose $T_1>\tau_0$, $R>T_1$, and set
\[
 f_A=A^2\mathcal L\varphi_A
           +c\mathcal L(\cos(Rx)\varphi_A),\qquad c>0.
\]
Write $H=h_\varphi$, $C_0=\int|H(v)|^2dv$, and
$C_2=\int v^4|H(v)|^2dv>0$.
The three packets have centers $0,R,-R$ and width $A^{-1}$.
At the core center, with $v=Au$,
\[
 h_{A^2\mathcal L\varphi_A}(v/A)
 =A^{1/2}v^2(1+v^2/A^2)^2H(v).
\]
At each tail center the leading profile is
$cA^{1/2}p(R)H(v)/2$.
Schwartz decay and the substitution $v=A(u-u_0)$ give
\[
 \int_{|u|<t_*}|h_{f_A}|^2\to C_2,\qquad
 \int_{|u|>T_1}|h_{f_A}|^2\to D_R:=\frac{c^2p(R)^2}2C_0,
 \qquad B_g[f_A]\to g(R)D_R.
\]
For justification, each off-center factor has argument differing by
$AR$ or $2AR$. Arbitrarily high Schwartz bounds make every cross
integral tend to zero, also with the weight $1+\log(2+|u|)$.
The same bounds dominate each centered integral after substitution.
Choose $c$ so that $MC_2<\ell(T_1)D_R$.
The strict integral-energy inequality then holds for all large $A$.
Since $v^2H(v)$ is not identically zero, the core supremum grows
at least linearly in $A$. The quantity $B_g[f_A]$ stays bounded.
The supremum criterion therefore fails for all large $A$.
Every $f_A$ satisfies the moments exactly.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[which profile IE captures and HFD misses]
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:rem:IE-advantage}
The HFD class is constrained by pointwise control of \(\widehat{f}\)
over the core; the IE class is constrained by integrated \(L^{2}\)
control and by a lower bound on the weighted tail energy. A narrow core peak with distributed
tail may lie in IE and fail HFD. A tail concentrated near
\(t_{\ast}\) may lie in HFD and fail IE. The two conditions are
transverse sufficient criteria.
\end{remark}

\section{Complement of \(\mathrm{PW}^{\mathrm{hol},\mathrm{IE}}_{F}\)}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:residual}

\subsection{Complement Structure}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:complement}

\begin{theorem}[IE residual as concentration inequality]
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:thm:IE-residual}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere. \(f\in\mathscr A_{13}\setminus\{0\}\setminus\bigcup_{T_{1}}\mathrm{PW}^{\mathrm{hol},\mathrm{IE}}_{F}(T_{1})\)
if and only if for every \(T_{1}\geq \tau_0\),
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:eq:residual-concentration}
\ell(T_{1})\beta(f,T_{1})\;<\;M\alpha(f).
\end{equation}
In particular,
\[
\sup_{T_{1}\geq \tau_0}\ell(T_{1})\beta(f,T_{1})\leq M\alpha(f).
\]
Thus the residual is not the complement of high frequency; it is the
locus where the logarithmic weighted tail bound never exceeds the core term.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\eqref{v4-arith:w7-e:eq:residual-concentration} is
\eqref{v4-arith:w7-e:eq:IE-condition} reversed. For residual \(f\) the
reversed inequality holds at every \(T_{1}\); conversely the reversed
inequality at every \(T_{1}\) excludes membership in every IE class.
Taking the supremum gives the displayed necessary bound. No decay statement
stronger than this weighted-tail bound follows without an independent
regularity estimate on \(f\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[which test functions are in the residual]
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:rem:residual-characterization}
Residual test functions have too little logarithmically weighted
far-tail mass relative to their core mass. Low-frequency approximate
identities give model examples after the weighted tail is made
uniformly small. Individual Li approximants require a separate
spectral comparison; no membership assertion for them is made here.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Prolate-Spheroidal Characterization}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:prolate}

\begin{theorem}[two-scale prolate sufficient criterion]
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:thm:prolate-characterization}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere. Fix Paley--Wiener exponential type \(T\) for
\(h_f\), i.e., \(\mathrm{supp}(f)\subset[-T,T]\). Let
\(\{\lambda_{k}(T,\Omega)\}_{k=0}^{\infty}\) denote the
Slepian--Pollak prolate eigenvalues of the integral operator
\(K_{T,\Omega}\colon L^{2}([-T,T])\to L^{2}([-T,T])\)
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:eq:prolate-operator}
(K_{T,\Omega}f)(u)\;:=\;\int_{-T}^{T}\frac{\sin(\Omega(u-v))}{\pi(u-v)}\,f(v)\,dv,
\end{equation}
sorted \(\lambda_{0}\geq\lambda_{1}\geq\ldots>0\). Then for
\(f\in\mathscr A\setminus\{0\}\) with support in \([-T,T]\),
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:eq:prolate-alpha-bound}
\alpha(f)\;\leq\;\lambda_{0}(T,t_{\ast}).
\end{equation}
For every \(T_{1}\geq \tau_0\),
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:eq:prolate-beta-bound}
\beta(f,T_{1})\;\geq\;1-\lambda_{0}(T,T_{1}).
\end{equation}
Consequently all \(f\in\mathscr A_{13}\setminus\{0\}\) of type at most
\(T\) lie in \(\mathrm{PW}^{\mathrm{hol},\mathrm{IE}}_{F}(T_{1})\) if
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:eq:two-scale-prolate-condition}
M\lambda_{0}(T,t_{\ast})\;\leq\;
\ell(T_{1})\bigl(1-\lambda_{0}(T,T_{1})\bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The operator \(K_{T,\Omega}\) is the time-limited frequency projection
onto \([-\Omega,\Omega]\). Hence
\[
\frac{\int_{-\Omega}^{\Omega}|\widehat f(t)|^{2}\,dt}
{\int_{\mathbb R}|\widehat f(t)|^{2}\,dt}
=\frac{\langle K_{T,\Omega}f,f\rangle}{\langle f,f\rangle}
\leq \lambda_{0}(T,\Omega).
\]
Taking \(\Omega=t_{\ast}\) gives
\eqref{v4-arith:w7-e:eq:prolate-alpha-bound}. Taking
\(\Omega=T_{1}\) gives the mass bound inside \([-T_{1},T_{1}]\), hence
\(\beta(f,T_{1})\geq1-\lambda_{0}(T,T_{1})\). If
\eqref{v4-arith:w7-e:eq:two-scale-prolate-condition} holds, then
\[
M\alpha(f)\leq M\lambda_{0}(T,t_{\ast})
\leq \ell(T_{1})(1-\lambda_{0}(T,T_{1}))
\leq \ell(T_{1})\beta(f,T_{1}),
\]
so \(f\in\mathrm{PW}^{\mathrm{hol},\mathrm{IE}}_{F}(T_{1})\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[quantitative prolate bounds]
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:rem:prolate-quantitative}
For $c=T\Omega$, positivity of the concentration operator gives
$\lambda_0(T,\Omega)\le\min(1,2c/\pi)$: its trace is the integral
of its diagonal kernel, $2T\Omega/\pi$.
On rescaling to $[-1,1]$, its kernel is
$\sin(c(x-y))/(\pi(x-y))=c/\pi+O(c^3)$ uniformly for small $c$.
The operator norm of the remainder is $O(c^3)$, so
$\lambda_0=2c/\pi+O(c^3)$ by comparison with the constant rank-one kernel.
For large $c$, testing any fixed nonzero compact smooth function
and using Fourier tail decay gives $\lambda_0\to1$.
These single-scale facts control only the core fraction.
The far-tail bound at the second scale $T_1$ is still required
in \eqref{v4-arith:w7-e:eq:two-scale-prolate-condition}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Absolute Uniform Closure Sub-Class}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:uniform-sub-class}

A uniform closure from prolate theory requires a core scale and a
far-tail scale.

\begin{corollary}[uniform \(G_g\)-positivity under a two-scale prolate inequality]
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:cor:uniform-narrow-PW}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere. Fix \(T_{0}>0\) and \(T_{1}\geq \tau_0\).
If
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:eq:uniform-narrow-condition}
M\lambda_{0}(T_{0},t_{\ast})\;\leq\;
\ell(T_{1})\bigl(1-\lambda_{0}(T_{0},T_{1})\bigr),
\end{equation}
then \(G_g(f,f)\geq0\) for every
\(f\in\mathscr A_{13}\setminus\{0\}\) of exponential type \(T\leq T_{0}\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The top prolate eigenvalue is monotone in both the time window and the
frequency window. Hence for \(T\leq T_{0}\),
\[
\alpha(f)\leq\lambda_{0}(T_{0},t_{\ast}),\qquad
\beta(f,T_{1})\geq1-\lambda_{0}(T_{0},T_{1}).
\]
Hypothesis \eqref{v4-arith:w7-e:eq:uniform-narrow-condition} gives
\(M\alpha(f)\leq \ell(T_{1})\beta(f,T_{1})\). Thus
\(f\in\mathrm{PW}^{\mathrm{hol},\mathrm{IE}}_{F}(T_{1})\), and
\Cref{v4-arith:w7-e:thm:IE-ATP} gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[significance]
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:rem:narrow-PW-significance}
\Cref{v4-arith:w7-e:cor:uniform-narrow-PW} is a uniform
\(G_g\)-positivity result only after the two prolate eigenvalues in
\eqref{v4-arith:w7-e:eq:uniform-narrow-condition} are verified. The
single estimate \(\alpha(f)\leq\lambda_{0}(T_{0},t_{\ast})\) controls
the core but says nothing about how much mass has crossed the far-tail
threshold \(T_{1}\).
\end{remark}

\section{Quantification of the Archimedean Positivity Extension}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:quantification}

\begin{theorem}[properness of the two sufficient classes]
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:thm:extent}
The union of the supremum and integral-energy classes is a proper
subset of $\mathscr A_{13}\setminus\{0\}$.
Together with \Cref{v4-arith:w7-e:prop:strict-inclusion}, this gives
both strict inclusions in \eqref{v4-arith:w7-e:eq:extent-bound},
with ambient class $\mathscr A_{13}\setminus\{0\}$.
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:eq:extent-bound}
 \mathrm{HFD}\subsetneq\mathrm{HFD}\cup\bigcup_{T_1}\mathrm{IE}(T_1)
 \subsetneq\mathscr A_{13}\setminus\{0\}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the compact test $f_A=A^2\mathcal L\varphi_A$ from the preceding
construction, omitting both tail packets.
Its spectral mass converges to $C_2>0$ and concentrates at zero.
In particular, $\alpha(f_A)\to1$. Moreover,
\[
 \int_{|u|>\tau_0}(1+\log(2+|u|))|h_{f_A}(u)|^2du\longrightarrow0.
\]
Indeed, after $v=Au$, the integrand is bounded by a constant times
$(1+\log(2+|v|))(1+|v|)^{12}|H(v)|^2$ on
$|v|>A\tau_0$. This is integrable, and its tail tends to zero.
Monotonicity and logarithmic growth of $\ell$ give
\[
 \sup_{T_1\ge \tau_0}\ell(T_1)\beta(f_A,T_1)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus every integral-energy condition fails for large $A$.
The same weighted-tail estimate gives $B_g[f_A]\to0$, while
the core supremum grows linearly in $A$.
The supremum condition fails as well.
The Mellin identity for $\mathcal L$ proves all moment conditions,
without a further cutoff or a modularity assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[what the gap captures]
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:rem:gap-captures}
The gap
\((\mathscr A_{13}\setminus\{0\})\setminus\bigcup_{T_{1}}\mathrm{PW}^{\mathrm{hol},\mathrm{IE}}_{F}(T_{1})\)
is non-empty. Its model elements are low-frequency approximate
identities with negligible logarithmic far tail. No assertion about a
particular Li approximant follows without computing its weighted
spectral profile.
\end{remark}

\section{Compatibility with Chapters 61, 57, and the Bounded-Interval Reduction}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:compatibility}

\subsection{IE Class and Chapter 61 Bounded-Interval Reduction}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:compat-ch61}

\begin{proposition}[arithmetic inequality after the energy bound]
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:prop:compat-bounded}
For an integral-energy test $f\in\mathscr A_{13}$, one has
$G_g(f,f)\ge0$. Its full Weil inequality is equivalent to
\[
 G_g(f,f)\ge 2\log\pi\|f\|_2^2
                  +W_{\mathrm{fin}}(f*f^*)-P(f*f^*).
\]
The moments defining $\mathscr A_{13}$ do not remove $P(f*f^*)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The energy estimate proves the first assertion. The explicit formula
and the identity $k+g=2\log\pi$ prove the equivalence.
Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:w5-a:chapter} gives a compact test in
$\mathscr A_{13}$ with nonzero polar form.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Three Non-Elementary Constructions Revisited}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:three-constructions}

The zero-density, Gaussian and Hermite--Biehler comparisons of
\Cref{v4-arith:w6-a:rem:live-constructions} pose separate questions.
The integral-energy theorem supplies no implication between them.

\begin{remark}[Gaussian and compact test comparisons]
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:rem:construction-interactions}
The function $f_\sigma(x)=e^{-x^2/(2\sigma^2)}$ has angular transform
$h_{f_\sigma}(u)=\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}e^{-\sigma^2u^2/2}$.
It belongs to the exponential test space $\mathscr G$, and has no
compact support. For a fixed $T_1>\tau_0$, its core fraction tends
to zero and its far-tail fraction tends to one as $\sigma\downarrow0$.
Thus it satisfies the auxiliary integral-energy inequality for small
$\sigma$. As $\sigma\to\infty$, its core fraction tends to one and
its logarithmically weighted tail tends to zero.
These are statements about the Gaussian spectral profile.
The Gaussian does not satisfy the zero moments at $0,1$, since $M_{f_\sigma}(0)>0$.
The differential construction above instead supplies exact compact
moment tests with the required energy profiles.

The explicit formula extends from compact tests to $\mathscr G$
in its exponential topology, as proved in
\Cref{v4-arith:sc:gaussian-extension}.
That extension retains $P-W_\infty-W_{\mathrm{fin}}$.
A spectral-width estimate for $G_g$ supplies no isometry into an
arithmetic or automorphic Hilbert space.
An identification with a positive native pairing requires a linear
map $I$ on the specified tests and the equality
$\langle If,Ih\rangle=W(f*h^*)$.
Zero counts and prolate concentration estimates do not construct
this map or prove the pairing equality.
\end{remark}

\section{Consequences of the energy estimates}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:summary}
The lower bound $\ell$ gives the sufficient condition
$M\alpha(f)\le \ell(T_1)\beta(f,T_1)$ for $G_g(f,f)\ge0$.
The two-scale prolate inequality implies this condition uniformly
on a fixed compact support interval whenever its displayed bound holds.
The resulting signed gamma estimate is
$W_\infty(f*f^*)\le2\log\pi\|f\|_2^2$.

The compact differential constructions prove that the union of the
integral and supremum classes strictly enlarges the supremum class,
and that their union is a proper subset of the nonzero tests in
$\mathscr A_{13}$. No inclusion of the supremum class in the union
of integral-energy classes is needed.
The complementary class is exactly the locus where
$\ell(T_1)\beta(f,T_1)<M\alpha(f)$ for every $T_1\ge \tau_0$.

\begin{remark}[the remaining arithmetic inequality]
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:rem:beilinson-closing}
\label{v4-arith:w7-e:rem:quantification}
The energy comparison controls the auxiliary form.
For each admissible test, full Weil positivity still requires
\[
 G_g(f,f)+P(f*f^*)\ge
 2\log\pi\|f\|_2^2+W_{\mathrm{fin}}(f*f^*).
\]
A proof on the moment kernel addresses that kernel. Extending it to
all compact smooth tests also requires the mixed pairings and the
finite-dimensional complement of its moment projection.
\end{remark}
