\chapter{BZN\(^{\mathrm{Ar}}\): Formal Noetherian
Hochschild and Derived Structure on Emerton--Gee}
\label{v4-arith:bzn-sub}

\emph{The arithmetic Ben-Zvi--Nadler comparison factors through four
independent inputs. The finite-type theorem of Gaitsgory--Rozenblyum
applies to smooth finite-type derived stacks. A candidate global
spectral object is a Selmer deformation functor on one coefficient
category. A formal geometric representative must be constructed before
the formal comparison can be applied. That comparison requires a categorical Hochschild-colimit hypothesis,
an arithmetic loop-space hypothesis, and a flat-singular-support
hypothesis. The inertia shadow does not contain the full
Weil/\((\varphi,\Gamma)\) datum, and the primes \(2\) and \(3\) carry
non-semisimple Hecke summands.}

\section{Formal Boundary of the Comparison}
\label{v4-arith:bzn-sub:context}

\subsection{Structural Inputs}

Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:arith-bzn} isolates the desired arithmetic
Hochschild trace equivalence
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:bzn-sub:eq:F3-statement}
\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}\bigl(\mathrm{Hk}^{\mathrm{Iw}}_{GL_{2},\mathrm{glob}}\bigr)
\;\simeq\;
\mathcal{O}\bigl(\mathrm{LocSys}^{W,\varphi\Gamma}_{GL_{2}}\bigr)^{\mathrm{flat}}
\end{equation}
as an equivalence of \(E_{1}\)-algebras in stable presentable
\(\infty\)-categories. The finite-type BZN theorem supplies only one
factor of the argument. The arithmetic comparison requires the
following additional inputs:
\begin{description}
\item[\textbf{R1.fin-type.}] The Gaitsgory--Rozenblyum theorem
(AMS DAG Vol.~II Part~V Ch.~III) that
\(\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}(\mathrm{IndCoh}(\mathcal{X}))\simeq\mathcal{O}(L\mathcal{X})\)
holds for smooth finite-type derived stacks \(\mathcal{X}\).
\emph{Form: theorem} (Gaitsgory--Rozenblyum).
The Selmer-derived stack
\(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{Sel}}_{\bar\rho,\det,\mathcal{L}}\)
is initially a formal deformation functor. If its chosen geometric
representative is formal Noetherian rather than finite-type,
R1.formal below is the required extension.
\item[\textbf{R1.formal.}] The extension of the Gaitsgory--Rozenblyum
theorem to formal Noetherian derived stacks. For
\(\mathcal{X}\) a formal Noetherian derived algebraic stack and
\(\mathcal{C}=\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}(\mathcal{X})\),
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}\bigl(\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}(\mathcal{X})\bigr)
\;\simeq\;
\mathcal{O}\bigl(L^{\mathrm{Ar}}\mathcal{X}\bigr)^{\mathrm{flat}}.
\end{equation}
\emph{Form: conjectural; a genuinely open extension requiring new arguments beyond the finite-type case.}
\item[\textbf{R2.}] Construct the global Selmer deformation functor
on a fixed coefficient category, with an identified determinant and
actual maps from the local-condition functors. Its tangent is then
the corresponding Selmer complex. Comparison with local parameter
stacks, geometric representability, formal Noetherianity, and
nilpotent-support compatibility are further requirements on the
chosen local conditions.
\item[\textbf{R3.}] Comparison from the inertia shadow
\(\mathrm{Map}(B\widehat{\mathbb{Z}},-)\) to the full
Weil/\((\varphi,\Gamma)\) local-system target.
\emph{Form: conjectural}; the inertia-only identification via
\(\mathrm{Map}(B\widehat{\mathbb{Z}},-)\) constitutes a procyclic shadow of the full
Weil/\((\varphi,\Gamma)\) target.
\item[\textbf{R4.}] Non-semisimple summand at wild primes \(p=2,3\):
\(\mathrm{Ext}^{1}\) contributions to
\(\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}(\mathrm{Hk}^{\mathrm{Iw}}_{GL_{2},p})\)
invisible to semisimple templates. \emph{Form: conjectural}; carried
by the wild Hecke category.
\end{description}

\subsection{R2 and R1.formal}

The conditions R2 and R1.formal are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{R2} defines a global Selmer deformation functor
by a homotopy pullback. Its local functors must use the same residual
coefficient field and determinant convention as the global functor.
Local parameter stacks can be used only after their comparison maps
with these functors are constructed. Geometric representability and
derived Noetherianity remain requirements for the proposed spectral
stack. They are not consequences of a product-colimit presentation.
\item \textbf{R1.formal} follows from three hypotheses
(R1.formal.a, b, c). The first is a Hochschild trace theorem after
the categorical colimit presentation of
\(\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}\) is supplied. The second and third
are respectively the arithmetic loop-space comparison and the
flat-singular-support comparison.
\end{enumerate}

R1.fin-type is external. R3 is not definitional: it is the passage
from the procyclic inertia shadow to the full
Weil/\((\varphi,\Gamma)\) target. R4 is the non-semisimple wild-prime
summand.

\subsection{Derived Enhancement of the Emerton--Gee Stack}

Emerton--Gee arXiv:1908.07185 is written in the language of
Noetherian formal algebraic stacks in the sense of the
Stacks Project, with the smoothness and mass-formula structure at
the level of ordinary (non-derived) schemes. Gaitsgory--Rozenblyum
DAG requires a derived enhancement: the cotangent complex must be
computed as a perfect complex (or almost-perfect, in the formal
case) in the \(\infty\)-category of quasi-coherent sheaves, and the
ind-colimit presentation must preserve this structure at every
stage. The translation is not automatic. It asks for
cotangent-complex functoriality under the chosen formal presentation,
compatibility with formal thickening, and preservation under the
Selmer fibre product. These are hypotheses on the derived
enhancement, not consequences of the underived mass formula alone.

\subsection{Finite-Type Hypotheses in the Gaitsgory--Rozenblyum Argument}

The Gaitsgory--Rozenblyum proof of
\(\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}(\mathrm{IndCoh}(\mathcal{X}))\simeq\mathcal{O}(L\mathcal{X})\)
uses three ingredients that each rest on finite-type smoothness
and require new arguments in the formal Noetherian setting:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Atiyah duality for \(\mathrm{IndCoh}\): the self-duality
\(\mathrm{IndCoh}(\mathcal{X})^{\vee}\simeq\mathrm{IndCoh}(\mathcal{X})\)
under the Atiyah pairing, which requires \(\mathcal{X}\) smooth
finite-type.
\item Smooth-base-change for \(\mathrm{IndCoh}\), which also uses
finite-type.
\item Loop-space duality: the free loop space \(L\mathcal{X}\) of a
finite-type smooth stack is itself smooth (with dimension equal to
\(\dim\mathcal{X}\), by the standard calculation).
\end{enumerate}
For formal Noetherian \(\mathcal{X}\), each ingredient requires a formal
analogue: Atiyah duality must be extended to the formal setting (conjectural
in general), smooth base-change must be replaced by formal-smooth
base-change (subject of ongoing work, e.g., Mok 2025),
and the loop space \(L\mathcal{X}\) is in general not smooth finite-type.

The formal comparison therefore has the form of a criterion. Given
the categorical colimit presentation of
\(\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}\), Hochschild trace commutes with it;
the loop and flat-locus comparisons remain separate geometric
hypotheses.

\section{Logical Form of the Inputs}
\label{v4-arith:bzn-sub:table}

The input structure is as follows:

\begin{description}
\item[\textbf{R2 tangent complex.}]
The continuous deformation functor has tangent
\(C^\bullet(G,\mathrm{ad}\bar\rho)[1]\), retaining automorphisms
in degree \(-1\). A chosen identification of its determinant replaces
the coefficients by \(\mathrm{ad}^0\bar\rho\), including in residual
characteristic two. A comparison with a derived local parameter stack
must identify that stack's formal deformation functor first. See
\Cref{v4-arith:bzn-sub:prop:EG-tangent-local}.

\item[\textbf{R2 global Galois constraint.}]
Restriction of a global representation gives a morphism to the product
of full local deformation functors. The pointed local-condition
functors map to that same product, over the same category
\(\mathrm{Art}_{W(k)}\). Their homotopy pullback is
\eqref{v4-arith:fle-table:eq:selmer-stack}, and its tangent is the
mapping fiber of restriction minus the local tangent maps.
The trace-zero notation in
\eqref{v4-arith:fle-table:eq:selmer-tangent} uses a determinant
identification preserved by arrows.

\item[\textbf{R2 geometric enhancement.}]
A geometric representative must have the finiteness, cotangent, and
singular-support properties needed by the intended category of sheaves.
A smooth atlas is a smooth map to a stack. Its existence alone does
not make the stack smooth over the coefficient base. Pointwise tangent
bounds also do not prove perfectness of a family of cotangent complexes.
These are additional hypotheses in
\Cref{v4-arith:bzn-sub:thm:R2-main}.

\item[\textbf{R1.formal three inputs.}]
The Gaitsgory--Rozenblyum theorem, ported from finite-type to formal
Noetherian, requires three separate arguments: (i) HH-colim-continuity
(whether \(\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}\) commutes with the ind-colimit
presentation of the formal stack); (ii) arithmetic loop-space formal
computation (whether \(L^{\mathrm{Ar}}\mathcal{X}
\simeq\mathrm{Map}(B\widehat{\mathbb{Z}},\mathcal{X})\) admits a workable
formal description); (iii) flat-locus pullback (whether the
nilpotent-singular-support-flat locus on the loop space matches
the Emerton--Gee flat locus of \Cref{v4-arith:bzn:def:flat-locus}).
The first is a trace theorem once the categorical colimit exists; the
other two are geometric hypotheses.
(See \Cref{v4-arith:bzn-sub:thm:R1-formal-reduction}.)

\item[\textbf{R1.formal sub-reductions.}]
R1.formal.a is the Hochschild trace statement for a given filtered
colimit in the Morita \((\infty,2)\)-category.
R1.formal.b reduces to the existence of a cotangent-complex formal
loop-space identification (a precise statement in Lurie DAG and
Gaitsgory--Rozenblyum Vol.~II). R1.formal.c reduces to the
compatibility of Arinkin--Gaitsgory nilpotent singular support with
the \((\varphi,\Gamma)\)-module Fargues--Scholze flat locus.
(See \Cref{v4-arith:bzn-sub:thm:R1-formal-a-closes} and
\Cref{v4-arith:bzn-sub:thm:R1-formal-bc-reduction}.)

\item[\textbf{Consistency with the initial arithmetic comparison.}]
The arithmetic comparison of \Cref{v4-arith:bzn:thm:HH-IndCoh-arith}
uses a pro-\'etale extension of Ben-Zvi--Francis--Nadler integral
transforms. In the present notation this is the R1.formal.b
loop-space hypothesis together with the R1.formal.c flat-locus
hypothesis.
\end{description}

\section{R2: The Emerton--Gee Cotangent Complex}
\label{v4-arith:bzn-sub:R2}

\subsection{The Local Formal Deformation Problem}

\begin{proposition}[local tangent and the geometric comparison]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-sub:prop:EG-tangent-local}
Let \(k\) be a finite field of characteristic \(\ell\), and let
\(\bar\rho:G_{\mathbb Q_p}\to\mathrm{GL}_2(k)\) be continuous.
The unframed derived continuous deformation functor on
\(\mathrm{Art}_{W(k)}\) has tangent
\[
 T\mathrm{Def}_{G_{\mathbb Q_p}}
 \simeq C^\bullet(G_{\mathbb Q_p},\mathrm{ad}\bar\rho)[1].
\]
A derived enhancement of a local parameter stack has this tangent at
\(\bar\rho\) if its formal deformation functor is equivalent to
this one. The claimed equivalence must preserve the coefficient
category, residual identification, and allowed arrows.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:coefficients:prop:tangent} computes the
linearized representation equation and its infinitesimal conjugations,
and then its response to simplicial square-zero modules. An equivalence
of formal deformation functors identifies those responses, hence their
tangent complexes. It is the required extra hypothesis for the stack
comparison.
\end{proof}

The framed first-order tangent is \(Z^1\), whereas strict conjugacy
classes give \(H^1\). In the unframed stack, \(H^0\) remains in
tangent degree \(-1\). It is not removed from the tangent complex
by being an automorphism group. With a determinant identification,
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:coefficients:prop:determinant} gives
\(C^\bullet(G_{\mathbb Q_p},\mathrm{ad}^0\bar\rho)[1]\).
Fixing only the determinant value with all strict conjugations is a
different deformation problem.

\begin{remark}[pointwise bounds and perfectness]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-sub:rem:perfect-tor}
If the continuous cohomology groups are finite-dimensional and vanish
outside \(0,1,2\), the tangent at this field-valued point is perfect
of amplitude contained in \([-1,1]\). Indeed, over a field a bounded
complex with finite-dimensional cohomology is equivalent to a bounded
complex of finite-dimensional vector spaces. Its \(H^0\) produces
tangent degree \(-1\), and its \(H^2\) produces degree one.
Neither term disappears by changing the word used for the moduli
object. This pointwise statement does not prove perfectness over a
formal coefficient family. It also gives no uniform bound for real
places at coefficient characteristic two, by
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:coefficients:prop:real-two}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A Common Recipient for Cotangent Comparisons}

\begin{proposition}[the pointed cotangent pro-system]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-sub:prop:cotangent-ind-level}
Let \(X=\varinjlim_iX_i\) be an objectwise filtered colimit of
pointed functors on derived affine \(k\)-schemes, with compatible
\(k\)-points \(x_i\). Suppose \(X_i\) has cotangent complex
\(L_i\) at \(x_i\). For a square-zero coefficient module \(N\),
the space of lifts of the point to \(k\oplus N\) is
\[
 \operatorname{Der}_x(X,N)
 \simeq\varinjlim_i\operatorname{Map}_{D(k)}(L_i,N).
\]
The transition maps give a well-typed inverse system
\(L_j\to L_i\) in \(D(k)\). This assertion does not identify a
cotangent object in \(\mathrm{IndCoh}(X)\) without additional
representability and categorical comparison data.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By definition, the lift space is the homotopy fiber at \(x\) of
\(X(k\oplus N)\to X(k)\). Filtered colimits of spaces commute
with finite homotopy limits, so this fiber is the filtered colimit
of the corresponding fibers for \(X_i\). Each is represented by
\(L_i\) by the cotangent universal property. A transition
\(X_i\to X_j\) induces
\(\operatorname{Map}(L_i,N)\to\operatorname{Map}(L_j,N)\), hence
\(L_j\to L_i\), compatibly with composition.
\end{proof}

Closed immersions do not make the cotangent complexes of their source
and target equal by pullback. For the origin
\(i:\operatorname{Spec}k\hookrightarrow\mathbb A^1_k\),
\(i^*\mathbb L_{\mathbb A^1_k/k}=k\) in degree zero, whereas
\(\mathbb L_{k/k}=0\). The relative cotangent complex is \(k[1]\),
and the transitivity triangle is \(k\to0\to k[1]\).
Thus an ind-presentation must retain its actual cotangent maps.
There is no automatic inverse-limit formula obtained by moving objects
from different sheaf categories into \(\mathrm{IndCoh}(X)\).

\begin{corollary}[cotangents of a derived product]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-sub:cor:cotangent-product}
Let \(X,Y\) be derived stacks over a common base \(\operatorname{Spec}R\)
with cotangent complexes, and write \(\pi_X,\pi_Y\) for the
projections from their derived product. Then
\[
 \mathbb L_{X\times_R^{\mathbf R}Y/R}
 \simeq\pi_X^*\mathbb L_{X/R}\oplus\pi_Y^*\mathbb L_{Y/R}.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Fix a map from \(T\) to the derived product and a square-zero
extension \(T[N]\). Its lifts are exactly pairs of lifts to
\(X\) and \(Y\) over \(R\). Their spaces of derivations are
therefore a product. The direct sum on the right represents this product of
mapping spaces for every test \(T\) and coefficient module \(N\).
The cotangent universal property gives the stated equivalence.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Selmer Tangent and Its Geometric Requirements}

\begin{theorem}[Selmer deformation functor]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-sub:thm:R2-main}
Fix the common coefficient category, determinant identification,
residual representation, and pointed local functors of
Definition~\ref{v4-arith:fle-table:def:selmer-stack}. Their homotopy
pullback \(\mathcal X^{\mathrm{Sel}}_{\bar\rho,\delta,\mathcal L}\)
exists as a functor. If the local tangent maps are
\(L_v\to C^\bullet(G_{\mathbb Q_v},\mathrm{ad}^0\bar\rho)\),
its tangent is
\[
 T\mathcal X^{\mathrm{Sel}}_{\bar\rho,\delta,\mathcal L}
 \simeq C^\bullet_{\mathcal L}
        (G_{\mathbb Q,S},\mathrm{ad}^0\bar\rho)[1].
\]
To use this functor as the geometric spectral target, the following
remain additional requirements:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a geometric representative and comparisons with the chosen local
parameter stacks on the same coefficient category.
\item the Noetherian, perfectness, and cotangent-amplitude properties
needed for the intended sheaf theory.
\item a definition of the specified nilpotent singular support and its
compatibility with the Selmer cotangent maps.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The pullback is computed in functors to spaces. Its tangent is the
fiber of the global and local tangent maps, as proved in
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:coefficients:thm:selmer-tangent}.
The further geometric assertions are hypotheses, not conclusions of
this functor calculation. In particular, nonzero \(H^2_{\mathcal L}\)
is compatible with quasi-smoothness of a derived scheme, as shown in
Example~\ref{v4-arith:coefficients:ex:quasismooth}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[the inertia shadow does not determine the full target]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-sub:cor:R3-definitional}
\ClaimStatusNeedsVerification. Given
\Cref{v4-arith:bzn-sub:thm:R2-main}, R3 still requires
the full-target comparison:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Map}\bigl(B\widehat{\mathbb{Z}},\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{Sel}}_{\bar\rho,\det,\mathcal{L}}\bigr)
\;\longrightarrow\;
\mathrm{LocSys}^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{GL_{2}}\bigl(B\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\bigr),
\end{equation}
as a procyclic shadow, and then a comparison with
\(\mathrm{LocSys}^{W,\varphi\Gamma}_{GL_{2}}\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{v4-arith:bzn:def:L-ar} and
\Cref{v4-arith:bzn:cor:L-ar-BGL2-is-LocSys},
\(\mathrm{LocSys}^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{GL_{2}}(B\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})=L^{\mathrm{Ar}}BGL_{2}=\mathrm{Map}(B\widehat{\mathbb{Z}},BGL_{2})\).
The map from
\(\mathrm{Map}(B\widehat{\mathbb{Z}},\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{Sel}}_{\bar\rho,\det,\mathcal{L}})\)
is obtained only after choosing an inertia-shadow comparison morphism
\(\iota^{\mathrm{Sel}}\colon\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{Sel}}_{\bar\rho,\det,\mathcal{L}}\to\mathrm{LocSys}^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{GL_{2}}(B\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})\)
(\Cref{v4-arith:bzn:prop:EG-to-LocSys}). That inertia-only morphism retains only the procyclic structure,
discarding Frobenius and \((\varphi,\Gamma)\) data; the full comparison
\(\mathrm{Map}(B\widehat{\mathbb{Z}},\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{Sel}}_{\bar\rho,\det,\mathcal{L}})\simeq\mathrm{LocSys}^{W,\varphi\Gamma}_{GL_{2}}\)
requires a separate argument (R3).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Source and Convention Comparison for R2}
\label{v4-arith:bzn-sub:R2-obstruction-analysis}

\paragraph{The deformation functor.}
The construction uses continuous derived deformation functors on
\(\mathrm{Art}_{W(k)}\), with specified residual point and
determinant convention. The local and global maps must exist there
before their homotopy pullback is formed. A description by a local
parameter stack requires a comparison of formal functors, not only
an agreement of first-order dimensions.

\paragraph{Degrees and sheaf categories.}
The tangent of the unframed deformation functor is a shifted cochain
complex. Automorphisms remain in degree \(-1\).

A geometric cotangent complex lives initially in quasi-coherent
sheaves on its own base. An identification with an ind-coherent
object uses the relevant comparison functor and its hypotheses.
The pointwise inverse system in
\Cref{v4-arith:bzn-sub:prop:cotangent-ind-level} has the common
recipient \(D(k)\). It makes no global categorical identification.

\paragraph{Products and formal presentations.}
The product formula in
\Cref{v4-arith:bzn-sub:cor:cotangent-product} follows from pairs
of derivations over a common base. A closed-immersion presentation
has relative cotangent terms and must retain their transition maps.
Neither formula proves formal Noetherianity or the singular-support
compatibility needed by the arithmetic Hochschild comparison.

\begin{remark}[derived geometric enhancement]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-sub:rem:R2-novelty}
An equivalence between a chosen derived local parameter stack and its
continuous deformation functor would identify their tangents by
\Cref{v4-arith:bzn-sub:prop:EG-tangent-local}. It would still leave
the global representability, finiteness, and support requirements
of \Cref{v4-arith:bzn-sub:thm:R2-main}. First-order tangent data,
or an ordinary moduli presentation, do not supply that equivalence.
\end{remark}

\section{R1.formal: Formal Hochschild Criterion}
\label{v4-arith:bzn-sub:R1formal-reduction}

\subsection{The Three Inputs}

\begin{theorem}[formal BZN criterion]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-sub:thm:R1-formal-reduction}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere. Let \(\mathcal{X}\) be a derived formal
algebraic stack in the Gaitsgory--Rozenblyum sense, Noetherian,
presented as an ind-colimit \(\mathcal{X}=\varinjlim_{i}\mathcal{X}_{i}\)
of finite-type derived stacks under closed immersions (as in the
Emerton--Gee structure theorem). Let
\(\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{X})=\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}(\mathcal{X})\).
Assume the finite-type BZN comparison on each \(\mathcal{X}_{i}\), and
assume the three conditions below. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:bzn-sub:eq:R1-formal}
\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}\bigl(\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}(\mathcal{X})\bigr)\;\simeq\;\mathcal{O}\bigl(L^{\mathrm{Ar}}\mathcal{X}\bigr)^{\mathrm{flat}},
\end{equation}
namely R1.formal, holds.
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbf{(R1.formal.a)}] \emph{HH-colim continuity.} The
Hochschild trace preserves the ind-colimit presentation of
\(\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}(\mathcal{X})\):
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}\bigl(\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}(\mathcal{X})\bigr)\;\simeq\;\varinjlim_{i}\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}\bigl(\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}(\mathcal{X}_{i})\bigr),
\end{equation}
under the transition maps induced by closed immersions.
\textbf{Form: theorem after the categorical colimit presentation is
given} (trace continuity; proved in
\Cref{v4-arith:bzn-sub:thm:R1-formal-a-closes}).
\item[\textbf{(R1.formal.b)}] \emph{Arithmetic loop-space formal
computation.} For a formal Noetherian derived stack \(\mathcal{X}\)
as above, the free loop space admits a formal ind-colimit
description
\begin{equation}
L^{\mathrm{Ar}}\mathcal{X}\;\simeq\;\varinjlim_{i}L^{\mathrm{Ar}}\mathcal{X}_{i}
\end{equation}
compatible with the closed immersions; equivalently,
\(L^{\mathrm{Ar}}=\mathrm{Map}(B\widehat{\mathbb{Z}},-)\) commutes
with the ind-colimit presentation.
\textbf{Form: conjectural} (Lurie \(\infty\)-topos formalism in the
pro-\'etale formal setting; see
\Cref{v4-arith:bzn-sub:prop:R1-formal-b-domain}).
\item[\textbf{(R1.formal.c)}] \emph{Flat-locus pullback
compatibility.} The flat locus \(L^{\mathrm{Ar}}\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{flat}}\)
(cut out by the Arinkin--Gaitsgory nilpotent-singular-support
condition lifted to the loop space) is compatible with the
ind-colimit structure:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{O}\bigl(L^{\mathrm{Ar}}\mathcal{X}\bigr)^{\mathrm{flat}}\;\simeq\;\varinjlim_{i}\mathcal{O}\bigl(L^{\mathrm{Ar}}\mathcal{X}_{i}\bigr)^{\mathrm{flat}},
\end{equation}
with the flat loci on finite-type pieces matching the
\((\varphi,\Gamma)\)-module Fargues--Scholze flat locus of
\Cref{v4-arith:bzn:def:flat-locus}.
\textbf{Form: conjectural} (Arinkin--Gaitsgory singular support
extended to this formal Selmer setting; see
\Cref{v4-arith:bzn-sub:prop:R1-formal-c-domain}).
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Assume (R1.formal.a), (R1.formal.b), and (R1.formal.c). By
(R1.formal.a),
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}(\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}(\mathcal{X}))\;\simeq\;\varinjlim_{i}\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}(\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}(\mathcal{X}_{i})).
\end{equation}
By the Gaitsgory--Rozenblyum theorem applied term-wise,
\begin{equation}
\varinjlim_{i}\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}(\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}(\mathcal{X}_{i}))\;\simeq\;\varinjlim_{i}\mathcal{O}(L\mathcal{X}_{i})^{\mathrm{flat}}.
\end{equation}
By (R1.formal.b),
\(\varinjlim_{i}L\mathcal{X}_{i}\simeq L^{\mathrm{Ar}}\mathcal{X}\)
(noting \(L=L^{\mathrm{Ar}}\) on finite-type pieces via the
reduction \(S^{1}\to B\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\) in the pro-\'etale
setting; this is the content of
\Cref{v4-arith:bzn:thm:arith-fact-homology} on each finite-type
piece). By (R1.formal.c),
\begin{equation}
\varinjlim_{i}\mathcal{O}(L\mathcal{X}_{i})^{\mathrm{flat}}\;\simeq\;\mathcal{O}(L^{\mathrm{Ar}}\mathcal{X})^{\mathrm{flat}}.
\end{equation}
Composing gives R1.formal.
\end{proof}

\subsection{R1.formal.a: HH Colimit Continuity}

\begin{theorem}[Hochschild trace along a categorical colimit]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-sub:thm:R1-formal-a-closes}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere. Let \(\mathcal{X}=\varinjlim_{i}\mathcal{X}_{i}\)
be a formal presentation by derived stacks under closed immersions.
Assume that the canonical functor
\[
\varinjlim_i \mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}(\mathcal{X}_i)
\longrightarrow
\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}(\mathcal{X})
\]
is an equivalence in the \(E_2\)-monoidal Morita
\((\infty,2)\)-category of stable presentable categories. Then
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}\bigl(\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}(\mathcal{X})\bigr)\;\simeq\;\varinjlim_{i}\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}\bigl(\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}(\mathcal{X}_{i})\bigr)
\end{equation}
as \(E_{1}\)-algebras in stable presentable \(\infty\)-categories.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Hochschild homology is the trace of the identity endofunctor in the
Morita \((\infty,2)\)-category of stable presentable categories. The
trace functor is symmetric monoidal and preserves filtered colimits of
dualizable objects and dualizable morphisms; this is the form of
Lurie HA~5.5.3 used here, and it is also obtained from the cyclic bar
description of To\"en. Applying the trace to the assumed categorical
colimit gives the displayed equivalence. The \(E_1\)-algebra structure
is transported by Dunn additivity from the \(E_2\)-monoidal
convolution structure.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[where the geometry enters]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-sub:rem:R1-formal-a-unconditional}
The theorem is purely categorical. It does not prove that
\(\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}(\mathcal{X})\) is the filtered
colimit of the categories on the finite pieces. That assertion is a
geometric compatibility statement for formal completion, singular
support, and the closed-immersion transition functors.
\end{remark}

\subsection{R1.formal.b: Arithmetic Loop-Space Formal Computation (Conditional)}

\begin{proposition}[R1.formal.b domain]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-sub:prop:R1-formal-b-domain}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere. R1.formal.b reduces to:
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbf{(b.1)}] The internal mapping stack
\(\mathrm{Map}(B\widehat{\mathbb{Z}},-)\) commutes with filtered
colimits in derived pro-\'etale stacks under closed immersions.
\item[\textbf{(b.2)}] For a formal Noetherian derived stack
\(\mathcal{X}\) with \(\mathcal{X}=\varinjlim_{i}\mathcal{X}_{i}\),
the natural map
\(\varinjlim_{i}\mathrm{Map}(B\widehat{\mathbb{Z}},\mathcal{X}_{i})\to\mathrm{Map}(B\widehat{\mathbb{Z}},\mathcal{X})\)
is an equivalence in the pro-\'etale topology.
\end{itemize}
Both (b.1) and (b.2) are Conjectural.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of reduction]
R1.formal.b as stated is
\(L^{\mathrm{Ar}}\mathcal{X}\simeq\varinjlim_{i}L^{\mathrm{Ar}}\mathcal{X}_{i}\).
By \Cref{v4-arith:bzn:def:L-ar},
\(L^{\mathrm{Ar}}\mathcal{X}=\mathrm{Map}(B\widehat{\mathbb{Z}},\mathcal{X})\).
The ind-colimit compatibility of this mapping stack is precisely
(b.1) (the general statement about the mapping functor) plus
(b.2) (verification of the specific map for the ind-colimit
presentation of \(\mathcal{X}\)). Conversely, given (b.1) and (b.2), R1.formal.b is
immediate.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Compactness and the arithmetic mapping object]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-sub:rem:R1-formal-b-not-automatic}
An object \(M\) is compact when its space-valued mapping functor
\(\operatorname{Map}(M,-)\) preserves filtered colimits.
Preservation by an internal mapping object must instead be checked
after evaluation on the test objects of its ambient category.
Neither assertion follows from finite cardinality of a group.

For a prime \(p\), the space \(B(\mathbb Z/p)\) is not compact in spaces.
Let \(g\) generate \(\mathbb Z/p\).
The periodic group resolution has alternating differentials
\(1-g\) and \(1+g+\cdots+g^{p-1}\). Applying homomorphisms to the
trivial module \(\mathbb F_p\) makes both differentials zero, so its
cohomology is nonzero in every nonnegative degree.

A compact space is a retract, up to homotopy, of a finite complex,
and has bounded cohomology. Thus this classifying space is not compact.

The profinite cyclic group uses all finite cyclic quotients with
divisibility transition maps. The \(p\)-power tower describes only
\(\mathbb Z_p\). A presentation by classifying objects requires
the specified topology, and an inverse limit does not by itself
preserve compactness. Statements (b.1) and (b.2) require the actual
comparison maps for the proposed formal arithmetic targets.
The finite-module Artin--Rees theorem does not supply them, as the
limit exchange in \eqref{v4-arith:weil:eq:completion-counterexample}
illustrates.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[size of the loop-space hypothesis]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-sub:rem:R1-formal-b-domain-estimate}
The hypothesis has three parts: compactness of
\(B\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\) against the relevant formal targets,
commutation of \(\mathrm{Map}(B\widehat{\mathbb{Z}},-)\) with the
chosen formal-Noetherian ind-colimits, and compatibility with
\(\mathrm{IndCoh}\)-functoriality.  Each part is a statement in the
pro-\'etale topology. Finite-level mapping behavior and passage to the
profinite limit each require their own proof.
\end{remark}

\subsection{R1.formal.c: Flat-Locus Pullback Compatibility (Conditional)}

\begin{proposition}[R1.formal.c domain]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-sub:prop:R1-formal-c-domain}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere. R1.formal.c reduces to:
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbf{(c.1)}] \emph{Arinkin--Gaitsgory nilpotent singular
support extends to derived formal algebraic stacks.} The conic
subcategory \(\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}\subset\mathrm{IndCoh}\)
is defined for derived formal algebraic stacks in the GR DAG~Vol.~II
sense, preserving the Arinkin--Gaitsgory
functoriality (arXiv:1201.6343).
\item[\textbf{(c.2)}] \emph{Flat locus preserved under ind-colimit.}
For an ind-colimit \(\mathcal{X}=\varinjlim_{i}\mathcal{X}_{i}\),
the flat locus on the loop space commutes with the ind-colimit:
\(L^{\mathrm{Ar}}\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{flat}}\simeq\varinjlim_{i}L^{\mathrm{Ar}}\mathcal{X}_{i}^{\mathrm{flat}}\).
\item[\textbf{(c.3)}] \emph{Flat locus matches FS locus.} On each
finite-type piece \(\mathcal{X}_{i}\), the
Arinkin--Gaitsgory flat locus of the loop space coincides with the
\((\varphi,\Gamma)\)-module Fargues--Scholze flat locus of
\Cref{v4-arith:bzn:def:flat-locus}.
\end{itemize}
All three statements are hypotheses.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of reduction]
R1.formal.c as stated is
\(\mathcal{O}(L^{\mathrm{Ar}}\mathcal{X})^{\mathrm{flat}}\simeq\varinjlim_{i}\mathcal{O}(L^{\mathrm{Ar}}\mathcal{X}_{i})^{\mathrm{flat}}\),
with the flat loci on finite-type pieces matching
\Cref{v4-arith:bzn:def:flat-locus}. Unpacking: this requires the
flat locus to be \emph{defined} on each finite-type \(\mathcal{X}_{i}\)
(which is a statement about nilpotent singular support extending to
derived formal algebraic stacks; this is (c.1)), to \emph{commute
with ind-colimit} (which is (c.2)), and to \emph{match the FS flat
locus} on each finite-type piece (which is (c.3)). Conversely, (c.1)
\(+\) (c.2) \(+\) (c.3) imply R1.formal.c by pasting the three
statements together.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[flat support is a separate geometry]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-sub:rem:R1-formal-c-precise}
Arinkin--Gaitsgory singular support is defined by the cotangent
geometry of a quasi-smooth stack.  The Fargues--Scholze flat locus is
defined by \((\varphi,\Gamma)\)-module geometry.  Equality of the two
loci on the Emerton--Gee finite pieces is a comparison theorem, not a
definition.  Compatibility with the formal colimit is another theorem
condition.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Sufficient Criterion for F3.BZN\texorpdfstring{\(^{\mathrm{Ar}}\)}{Ar}}

\begin{theorem}[sufficient criterion for F3.BZN\(^{\mathrm{Ar}}\)]
\label{v4-arith:bzn-sub:thm:R1-formal-bc-reduction}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere. Suppose the structural splitting
\Cref{v4-arith:sw:thm:BZN-Ar-split} is imposed. The conjunction
\begin{itemize}
\item R1.fin-type (Gaitsgory--Rozenblyum: theorem on smooth
finite-type).
\item R1.formal.a (Hochschild trace along the given categorical
colimit).
\item R1.formal.b (arithmetic loop-space formal computation:
conjectural).
\item R1.formal.c (flat-locus pullback compatibility: conjectural).
\item R2 (Selmer-derived target, tangent complex, formal
Noetherianity, and nilpotent-support compatibility).
\item R3 (arithmetic free loop space identification: full-target
comparison still required,
\Cref{v4-arith:bzn-sub:cor:R3-definitional}).
\item R4 (non-semisimple summand at wild primes \(p=2,3\):
wild Hecke summands included).
\end{itemize}
implies F3.BZN\(^{\mathrm{Ar}}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{v4-arith:sw:thm:BZN-Ar-split} expresses
F3.BZN\(^{\mathrm{Ar}}\) as R1 \(+\) R2 \(+\) R3 \(+\) R4, where R1 further
splits into R1.fin-type \(+\) R1.formal
(\Cref{v4-arith:bzn-sub:context}). R1.fin-type is
Gaitsgory--Rozenblyum; R1.formal splits into R1.formal.a
(\Cref{v4-arith:bzn-sub:thm:R1-formal-a-closes}, theorem) plus
R1.formal.b (\Cref{v4-arith:bzn-sub:prop:R1-formal-b-domain},
conjectural) plus R1.formal.c
(\Cref{v4-arith:bzn-sub:prop:R1-formal-c-domain}, conjectural) by
\Cref{v4-arith:bzn-sub:thm:R1-formal-reduction}. R2 supplies the
Selmer target and tangent complex and includes the required finiteness
and singular-support hypotheses. R3 supplies the full
Weil/\((\varphi,\Gamma)\) comparison. R4 inserts the wild-prime
summands. These inputs are exactly the factors in the structural
splitting, hence their conjunction implies F3.BZN\(^{\mathrm{Ar}}\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Source and Convention Comparison for R1.formal}
\label{v4-arith:bzn-sub:R1formal-obstruction-analysis}

\paragraph{Source independence.} The argument for R1.formal draws on
four disjoint source lists: Lurie HA~5.5.3 / 4.8 / 7.4 (HH,
cotangent, colimits); Gaitsgory--Rozenblyum DAG Vol.~II Part~V
Ch.~III (HH on smooth finite-type); Arinkin--Gaitsgory
arXiv:1201.6343 (nilpotent singular support); Fargues--Scholze
arXiv:2102.13459 (local Langlands).

\paragraph{HH conventions.} Lurie HA places HH on symmetric monoidal
stable presentable \(\infty\)-categories in spectra; Gaitsgory--Rozenblyum
uses a derived-category trace on \(\mathrm{IndCoh}\); To\"en 2014
uses a direct simplicial-object computation. All three are equivalent:
HH of a stable \(\infty\)-category is the colimit over the cyclic
category of the self-tensor-product sequence (McCarthy--Schwede,
Blumberg--Mandell).

\paragraph{Ambient category.} All objects live in derived pro-\'etale
stacks with the \((\infty,2)\)-category of correspondences for
\(\mathrm{IndCoh}\)-valued functoriality.

\paragraph{Hypotheses.} The main hypothesis on \(\mathcal{X}\) is
Noetherianness together with derived-formal-algebraic structure.  For
the Selmer target this includes the formal Noetherian and
nilpotent-support compatibility conditions of
\Cref{v4-arith:bzn-sub:thm:R2-main}.

\paragraph{Functoriality.} HH-colim continuity is a functorial
statement on the \(\infty\)-category of stable presentable
\(\infty\)-categories; the criterion by three inputs is
itself functorial in \(\mathcal{X}\).

\paragraph{Source of the implication.} The criterion
\Cref{v4-arith:bzn-sub:thm:R1-formal-reduction} is one-way.  The
finite-type theorem, Hochschild trace continuity, loop-space
compatibility, and flat-locus compatibility imply the formal BZN
comparison.  The converse is not used.

\section{Boundary Conditions}
\label{v4-arith:bzn-sub:residuals}

\subsection{Form of the Inputs}

The seven inputs have different logical weights. R1.fin-type is the
Gaitsgory--Rozenblyum finite-type theorem. R1.formal.a is a trace
theorem after the categorical colimit presentation is known. R2 fixes
the Selmer target and tangent complex, but its use in formal BZN
requires formal Noetherianity and nilpotent-support compatibility for
the chosen local conditions. R1.formal.b, R1.formal.c, R3, and R4 are
geometric hypotheses.

\subsection{Logical Consequence}

The arithmetic BZN comparison is therefore not a consequence of a
product formula for local stacks. It is a statement about a Selmer
derived fibre product, a formal Hochschild trace, a pro-\'etale loop
object, a flat singular-support locus, and wild Hecke summands.  The
finite-type theorem and the trace theorem do not eliminate the loop,
flat-locus, full-target, or wild-prime hypotheses.

\subsection{Open Problems}

The reduction of \Cref{v4-arith:bzn-sub:thm:R1-formal-bc-reduction}
poses three precise open problems.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{R1.formal.b.} Does \(\mathrm{Map}(B\widehat{\mathbb{Z}},-)\)
commute with ind-colimits under closed immersions in derived pro-\'etale
stacks? The relevant tools are Lurie's \(\infty\)-topos formalism
(HTT~6, HA~4.4) combined with Bhatt--Scholze pro-\'etale (\S7).
A positive answer proves R1.formal.b; a counterexample restricts
\Cref{v4-arith:bzn:thm:arith-fact-homology} to the cofinal
finite-type subclass.
\item \textbf{R1.formal.c.} Does the Arinkin--Gaitsgory nilpotent-singular-support
flat locus coincide with the \((\varphi,\Gamma)\)-module Fargues--Scholze
flat locus on each finite-type piece of the Emerton--Gee ind-colimit?
The comparison between Fargues--Scholze arXiv:2102.13459 and
Arinkin--Gaitsgory arXiv:1201.6343 can be checked on unramified
crystalline representations with fixed Hodge--Tate weight.
\item \textbf{R4.} What are the \(\mathrm{Ext}^{1}\)-contributions to
\(\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}(\mathrm{Hk}^{\mathrm{Iw}}_{GL_{2},p})\) at
wild primes \(p=2,3\), and do they shift the arithmetic BZN
identification? The Etingof--Ostrik 2004 finite-tensor-category
framework applies; a model case is \(p=2\) with tame restriction
to the depth-1 local category.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Formal Noetherian Boundary}
\label{v4-arith:bzn-sub:summary}

The global spectral target for F3.BZN\(^{\mathrm{Ar}}\) is the
Selmer-derived stack
\(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{Sel}}_{\bar\rho,\det,\mathcal{L}}\) with tangent
complex \(C^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{L}}(G_{\mathbb{Q},S},\mathrm{ad}^{0}\rho)[1]\)
(\Cref{v4-arith:bzn-sub:thm:R2-main}); formal Noetherianity,
quasi-smoothness, and nilpotent-support compatibility for the
chosen local deformation problem remain hypotheses.
R3 is a full-target hypothesis under R2
(\Cref{v4-arith:bzn-sub:cor:R3-definitional}).

R1.formal follows from three conditions
(\Cref{v4-arith:bzn-sub:thm:R1-formal-reduction}).
R1.formal.a is Hochschild trace continuity along the given
categorical colimit (\Cref{v4-arith:bzn-sub:thm:R1-formal-a-closes}).
R1.formal.b is the arithmetic loop-space formal computation, and
R1.formal.c is flat-locus pullback compatibility
(\Cref{v4-arith:bzn-sub:prop:R1-formal-b-domain} and
\Cref{v4-arith:bzn-sub:prop:R1-formal-c-domain}).

Thus F3.BZN\(^{\mathrm{Ar}}\) is conditional on R1.formal.b,
R1.formal.c, the finiteness and support part of R2, R3, and R4
(\Cref{v4-arith:bzn-sub:cor:F3-BZN-closure-path}). The pro-\'etale
extension of Ben-Zvi--Francis--Nadler is not a formal theorem of the
finite-type theory; in this notation it is the loop-space hypothesis
together with the flat-locus comparison.
