\chapter{Test Maps, Heegner Heights, and the Weil Positivity Problem}
\label{v4-arith:w8-atp:chapter}

The full Weil inequality is defined on compact complex tests.
To compare it with a spectral statistic or an arithmetic height,
one needs a map from the source variables to those tests and
an identity of pairings. The Fourier-window construction below
gives such a test map. The Heegner construction gives a second
map and an exact height-plus-residual identity. Their images
need not be dense, and positivity of the residual remains an
additional condition. An explicit divisor example shows why
ordinate statistics alone do not determine the signed pairing.

\section{The Compact Test Algebra and the Weil Pairing}
\label{v4-arith:w8-atp:target}

Let $\mathscr A=C_c^\infty(\mathbb R;\mathbb C)$ have its usual
locally convex inductive-limit topology. On tests supported in a fixed
compact set, convergence means uniform convergence of every derivative.
Write
\[
 h_f(z)=\mathcal F_+f(z)=\int_{\mathbb R}f(u)e^{izu}\,du,
 \qquad f^*(u)=\overline{f(-u)},\qquad
 (f*g)(u)=\int_{\mathbb R}f(v)g(u-v)\,dv.
\]
Give $\mathscr P=\mathcal F_+\mathscr A$ the transported topology,
as in Definition~\ref{v4-arith:w5-a:def:PW}. Its elements are entire
and rapidly decreasing in every closed horizontal strip. The continuous
signed Weil functional is
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w8-atp:eq:weil-W}
 W(f)=P(f)-W_\infty(f)-W_{\mathrm{fin}}(f).
\end{equation}
The polar and real-place terms are those of
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w5-a:thm:weil-explicit}; the finite-place term is
\[
 W_{\mathrm{fin}}(f)=\sum_{p,k\geq1}\frac{\log p}{p^{k/2}}
                 \bigl(f(k\log p)+f(-k\log p)\bigr).
\]
This sum is finite for compact tests. Put
$q_W(f,g)=W(f*g^*)$, linear in $f$ and conjugate-linear in $g$.
The relation $W(f^*)=\overline{W(f)}$ makes this form Hermitian.
A-TP means $q_W(f,f)\geq0$ for every $f\in\mathscr A$;
Corollary~\ref{v4-arith:w5-a:cor:weil-positivity-RH} identifies it with RH.

For a complex vector space $S$ and a specified linear map
$J:S\to\mathscr A$, define the pulled-back Hermitian form
\[
 q_J(u,v)=W\bigl(Ju*(Jv)^*\bigr).
\]
The source variable $u$ and the compact test $Ju$ belong to
different spaces. The constructions below give explicit such maps.

The five source families below concern different input objects.
Each comparison requires a test map and an identity for the full
normalized pairing, or a proved equivalence with that pairing.

\section{Comparison of Pairings and Extension of Tests}
\label{v4-arith:w8-atp:convergence}

\begin{theorem}[Comparison and extension of the Weil form]
\label{v4-arith:w8-atp:thm:convergence}
Let $J:S\to\mathscr A$ be linear, and let $b$ be a Hermitian
form on $S$ satisfying
\[
 b(u,v)=W\bigl(Ju*(Jv)^*\bigr)\qquad(u,v\in S).
\]
Then $b\geq0$ is equivalent to Weil positivity on $J(S)$.
Suppose, in addition, that every $f\in\mathscr A$ admits
$f_n\in J(S)$ with all supports contained in one compact set
and $f_n\to f$ in every smooth seminorm. Then $b\geq0$
implies A-TP on all of $\mathscr A$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The first assertion follows by putting $u=v$ in the identity.
Under the approximation hypothesis,
$f_n*f_n^*\to f*f^*$ smoothly, with support in one compact
set. Continuity of the Weil distribution gives
$W(f*f^*)=\lim_n W(f_n*f_n^*)\geq0$.
\end{proof}

Translation gives another way to represent every convolution square,
without approximating every test.

\begin{lemma}[Transfer by translation]
\label{v4-arith:w8-atp:lem:translation-transfer}
For $a\in\mathbb R$, put $\tau_a f(t)=f(t-a)$.
Let
\[
 S_+=C_c^\infty((0,\infty);\mathbb C)
\]
Give $S_+$ its usual test-function topology, and let
$J_+:S_+\to\mathscr A$ be extension by zero.
For all $f,g\in\mathscr A$ and $a\in\mathbb R$,
\[
 (\tau_a f)*(\tau_a g)^*=f*g^*,
 \qquad q_W(\tau_a f,\tau_a g)=q_W(f,g).
\]
The image $J_+(S_+)$ is not dense in $\mathscr A$.
Nevertheless, positivity of the pulled-back form $q_{J_+}$
is equivalent to A-TP.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every element of $S_+$ has support compactly contained in
$(0,\infty)$, so its extension by zero is smooth.
The involution and convolution satisfy
\[
 (\tau_a g)^*=\tau_{-a}(g^*),\qquad
 (\tau_a f)*(\tau_{-a}g^*)=f*g^*.
\]
The second identity follows by substituting $w=v-a$ in
the convolution integral. Applying $W$ proves the pairing identity.

Evaluation at $-1$ is a continuous nonzero functional on
$\mathscr A$ which vanishes on $J_+(S_+)$, so this image is
not dense. For any $f\in\mathscr A$, compactness of its support
allows a sufficiently large $a$ such that
$\operatorname{supp}(\tau_a f)\subset(0,\infty)$.
Thus positivity on $J_+(S_+)$ gives
\[
 q_W(f,f)=q_W(\tau_a f,\tau_a f)\geq0.
\]
The converse follows by restriction.
\end{proof}

This argument uses equality of convolution squares, not density
of the source image. Translation does not shorten a support interval,
so it does not place every compact test in the fixed Fourier window.
Neither this lemma nor the approximation theorem proves positivity
on its source image.

\begin{definition}[The full positivity condition]
\label{v4-arith:w8-atp:defn:consolidated-residual}
Write $\mathsf R_{\mathrm{A\text{-}TP}}$ for
$W(f*f^*)\geq0$ for every $f\in\mathscr A$, with the polar,
finite-prime and archimedean terms all present. The digamma contribution
is one summand of this condition.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Comparison with the full functional]
\label{v4-arith:w8-atp:rem:fivefold}
A comparison must retain the full normalized functional, or prove an
explicit equivalence with it. The continuity argument above extends
positivity under its common-compact approximation hypothesis.
Lemma~\ref{v4-arith:w8-atp:lem:translation-transfer} gives a different
extension mechanism, even from a nondense image.
The theorem supplies no test map or pairing identity for an unnamed source.
\end{remark}

\section{Fourier Windows and Height Comparisons}
\label{v4-arith:w8-atp:new-closures}

\subsection{Compact Fourier windows and co-Poisson constraints}
\label{v4-arith:w8-atp:new-closures:RS}

For an integer degree $m\geq1$, the corresponding window is
$(-1/m,1/m)$. We first use the degree-one window $(-1,1)$.
Put $\mathscr A_1^{\mathrm{ev}}
=\{f\in C_c^\infty((-1,1);\mathbb C):f(-t)=f(t)\}$.
Specify a family $\mathcal B\subset\mathscr D'(\mathbb R)$
of distributions representing the desired co-Poisson constraints.
Using the translations $\tau_a$ defined above, set
\[
 \mathscr A_{1,\mathcal B}
 =\{f\in\mathscr A_1^{\mathrm{ev}}:
       B(\tau_a f)=0\text{ for every }B\in\mathcal B,
                                      \ a\in\mathbb R\}.
\]
No nonzero element is required by this definition. The associated
entire source class and its test map are
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w8-atp:eq:PW-CP-RS}
 \mathrm{PW}^{\mathrm{CP}}(\mathcal B)
 =\mathcal F_+\mathscr A_{1,\mathcal B},\qquad
 (J_{\mathrm{CP}}h)(u)
 =\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}h(t)e^{-itu}\,dt.
\end{equation}
Give $C_c^\infty((-1,1))$ its usual test-function inductive-limit
topology, and $\mathscr A_{1,\mathcal B}$ its subspace topology.
Give the entire source class the topology transported from this subspace. Fourier inversion makes
$J_{\mathrm{CP}}$ a continuous linear bijection onto that
compact-test space. A particular co-Poisson construction must
specify the distributions $\mathcal B$; the following result
holds for every such specified family.

\begin{theorem}[The window pairing and the correlation test]
\label{v4-arith:w8-atp:thm:Quant-A-TP-RS}
For $h,k\in\mathrm{PW}^{\mathrm{CP}}(\mathcal B)$,
\[
 q_{J_{\mathrm{CP}}}(h,k)
 =\sum_\rho h(z_\rho)\overline{k(\bar z_\rho)},
 \qquad z_\rho=-i(\rho-\tfrac12).
\]
The sum converges absolutely. Under RH this form is positive
semidefinite. It is also positive semidefinite when its source
space is zero, without any zero-location hypothesis.

For $f\in\mathscr A_1^{\mathrm{ev}}$, the function
\[
 (\mathcal R f)(x_1,x_2)
 =h_f\bigl(2\pi(x_1-x_2)\bigr)
\]
is a symmetric, translation-invariant pair-correlation test,
rapidly decreasing on $x_1+x_2=0$. With Fourier kernel
$e^{-2\pi i(x_1\xi_1+x_2\xi_2)}$, its Fourier distribution is
\[
 f(\xi_1)\delta(\xi_1+\xi_2),
\]
supported in $|\xi_1|+|\xi_2|<2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply the explicit formula to
$J_{\mathrm{CP}}h*(J_{\mathrm{CP}}k)^*$.
Its Fourier transform is $h(z)\overline{k(\bar z)}$.
Rapid decay in the critical strip and the zero-count estimate
give absolute convergence. Under RH each $z_\rho$ is real,
so the diagonal value is $\sum_\rho|h(z_\rho)|^2$.
On the zero space the only value is zero.

Evenness of $f$ gives symmetry of $\mathcal Rf$; its formula
gives translation invariance. Fourier decay proves the required
decrease on the difference line. Finally
\[
 \mathcal Rf(x_1,x_2)
 =\int_{\mathbb R}f(u)e^{2\pi i(x_1u-x_2u)}\,du,
\]
which proves the Fourier-distribution and support assertions.
\end{proof}

Rudnick--Sarnak, \emph{Zeros of principal $L$-functions and random
matrix theory}, \S1, TF1--TF3 and Theorems~1.1--1.2, pp.~3--5,
use Fourier support $\sum_j|\xi_j|<2/m$ for a cuspidal $GL_m$ input,
with $m\leq3$ or their coefficient hypothesis (1.7). For the pair test
above, support in $(-1/m,1/m)$ gives precisely that bound.
The window $(-1,1)$ is the degree-one case. Their smoothed theorem
permits complex zero parameters; their real-spacing formulation
adds RH. These correlation sums are different functionals from
$q_{J_{\mathrm{CP}}}$; the test construction above identifies no
equality between them.

\begin{remark}[Restricted tests and the full cone]
\label{v4-arith:w8-atp:rem:Quant-RS-narrow}
The image of $J_{\mathrm{CP}}$ lies in tests supported in
$[-1,1]$ and is not dense in $\mathscr A$: evaluation at any
point outside that interval vanishes on the image but not on
all compact tests. A nonzero annihilator and an extension beyond
this window are separate requirements. Different parameters
do not prove two test images disjoint. The literal entire-Mellin
class in Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w7-j:thm:ATP-TM-closure} is zero.
\end{remark}

\begin{example}[The annihilator depends on its defining distributions]
If $\mathcal B=\{\delta_0\}$, then
$\delta_0(\tau_a f)=f(-a)$ for every $a$, so
$\mathscr A_{1,\mathcal B}=0$.
If instead $\mathcal B$ consists of the distribution $f\mapsto\int f$,
choose a nonzero even bump $b\in C_c^\infty((-1,1))$ and set $f=b''$.
Then $f$ is even, $\int\tau_a f=0$ for every $a$, and $f\ne0$:
otherwise $b$ would be affine and compactly supported, hence zero.
These are explicit annihilator examples, not a construction of an
arithmetic co-Poisson family.
\end{example}

\subsection{Heegner Heights and Compact-Test Gram Matrices}
\label{v4-arith:w8-atp:new-closures:heeg}

Let $F=\sum_{n\geq1}a_n(F)e^{2\pi inz}$ be a normalized weight-$2$
newform on $\Gamma_0(N)$ with trivial character. Let $D<0$ be an odd
fundamental discriminant prime to $N$, set $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt D)$,
and assume $\chi_D(p)=+1$ for every $p\mid N$. These primes split in $K$.
Let $H$ be the Hilbert class field, $\mathcal G=\operatorname{Gal}(H/K)$,
$h_K=|\mathcal G|$, and $u_K=|\mathcal O_K^\times|/2$.
Choose a classical Heegner point $x\in X_0(N)(H)$ and put
$c=[(x)-(\infty)]\in J_0(N)(H)$.

Set $V=J_0(N)(H)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb C$, and let $\Theta$
be a symmetric theta-divisor class on $J_0(N)$.
The global $H$-height for $2\Theta$ gives the Hermitian pairing
$\langle\ ,\ \rangle_H$ on $V$, linear in the first variable.
Explicitly, if $B_H$ is its real symmetric pairing, then
$\langle a+ib,c+id\rangle_H
=B_H(a,c)+B_H(b,d)+iB_H(b,c)-iB_H(a,d)$.
It is positive definite on this finite-dimensional vector space;
torsion has disappeared in the tensor product.

For $(n,N)=1$, the good Hecke operators $T_n$ commute and are
self-adjoint for this pairing. Their correspondences are defined over
$\mathbb Q$ and are self-dual, so height functoriality gives
$\langle T_nv,w\rangle_H=\langle v,T_nw\rangle_H$; they also commute
with $\mathcal G$. Simultaneous diagonalization defines the orthogonal
projection $e_F$ onto the particular complex eigenspace
\[
 V_F=\{v\in V:T_nv=a_n(F)v\text{ for all }(n,N)=1\}.
\]
This is the eigenspace for $F$, not the sum of its Galois conjugates;
it may be zero. For a character $\chi:\mathcal G\to\mathbb C^\times$, put
\[
 c_{\chi,F}=e_F\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal G}\chi(\sigma)^{-1}\sigma c.
\]
These are the unaveraged character components. The factor $h_K^{-1}$
is not inserted.

For an ideal class $\mathfrak a$ of $K$, let $r_{\mathfrak a}(n)$ count
integral ideals of norm $n$ in that class. Identify characters of the
class group with characters of $\mathcal G$ by the Artin map. The
Rankin series in the following formula is
\[
 \begin{split}
 L_{\mathfrak a}(F,s)
 &=\left(\sum_{\substack{n\geq1\\(n,DN)=1}}\chi_D(n)n^{1-2s}\right)
       \sum_{n\geq1}a_n(F)r_{\mathfrak a}(n)n^{-s},\\
 L(F,\chi,s)&=\sum_{\mathfrak a}\chi(\mathfrak a)L_{\mathfrak a}(F,s),
 \end{split}
\]
initially in a right half-plane and then by analytic continuation.
Gross--Zagier, \emph{Heegner points and derivatives of $L$-series},
I\S\S2--6, especially (4.3), (5.1), (5.3)--(5.4) and Theorem~(6.3),
pp.~227--230, give the self-dual correspondences and the normalization
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w8-atp:eq:GZ-height}
 \|c_{\chi,F}\|_H^2=C_{F,K}L'(F,\chi,1),\qquad
 C_{F,K}=\frac{h_Ku_K^2\sqrt{|D|}}{8\pi^2(F,F)},\qquad
 (F,F)=\int_{\Gamma_0(N)\backslash\mathfrak H}|F(z)|^2dx\,dy.
\end{equation}
This is a Rankin derivative centered at $s=1$, not a formula for
$L'(F,1)$ alone. Changing the ground-field height, averaging the
character component or changing the polarization changes the constant.

\begin{lemma}[Character Gram matrix]
\label{v4-arith:w8-atp:lem:character-gram}
For $r\geq1$ and characters $\chi_1,\ldots,\chi_r$ of $\mathcal G$, let
$G_{ij}=\langle c_{\chi_i,F},c_{\chi_j,F}\rangle_H$ and define
$D_{F,K}u=\sum_j u_jc_{\chi_j,F}$ on $\mathbb C^r$.
Distinct characters give orthogonal vectors. Repeated characters give
\[
 G_{ij}=
 \begin{cases}C_{F,K}L'(F,\chi_i,1),&\chi_i=\chi_j,\\
 0,&\chi_i\ne\chi_j,
 \end{cases}
\]
and consequently
\[
 \|D_{F,K}u\|_H^2
 =C_{F,K}\sum_{\chi}
       \left|\sum_{j:\chi_j=\chi}u_j\right|^2L'(F,\chi,1).
\]
The sum runs over the distinct characters selected.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The global height is invariant under $\mathcal G$, since its places
are permuted by this group. The projection $e_F$ commutes with that
action, and reindexing the character sum gives
$\tau c_{\chi,F}=\chi(\tau)c_{\chi,F}$.
Thus invariance and the first-linear convention imply
\[
 G_{ij}=\chi_i(\tau)\overline{\chi_j(\tau)}G_{ij}
 \qquad(\tau\in\mathcal G).
\]
For distinct characters one can choose $\tau$ with the scalar different
from $1$, so $G_{ij}=0$. For equal characters the two vectors coincide;
Equation~\eqref{v4-arith:w8-atp:eq:GZ-height} gives their squared norm.
Expand the squared norm of $D_{F,K}u$ and group equal characters.
\end{proof}

Choose a nonzero $\phi\in C_c^\infty((-1/4,1/4);\mathbb C)$ and put
$\phi_j(t)=\phi(t-j)$ for $1\leq j\leq r$.
Their supports are disjoint. With $S=\mathbb C^r$, define
\[
 J_\Phi u=\sum_{j=1}^r u_j\phi_j\in\mathscr A,
 \qquad D_{F,K}u=\sum_{j=1}^r u_jc_{\chi_j,F}\in V.
\]
The map $J_\Phi$ is continuous and injective: restriction to the support
of each nonzero $\phi_j$ recovers $u_j$. Define
$\mathrm{PW}^{\mathrm{Heeg}}_F(K,\Phi)=\mathcal F_+J_\Phi(S)$
with the topology transported from $S$. Its compact-test map is Fourier
inversion followed by inclusion in $\mathscr A$.
The dictionary $\Phi$ is additional data, not determined by $(F,K)$.

Define Hermitian matrices
\[
 M_{ij}=W(\phi_i*\phi_j^*),\qquad R_{ij}=M_{ij}-G_{ij}.
\]
Each entry of $M$ is given by the polar term, gamma integral and finite
prime sum of the full explicit formula. For a matrix $A$, write
$A[u,v]=\sum_{i,j}u_i\overline{v_j}A_{ij}$, and $A\succeq0$ if
$A[u,u]\geq0$ for all $u$.

\begin{theorem}[Height comparison on a specified test image]
\label{v4-arith:w8-atp:thm:Arakelov-Heegner}
The matrix $G$ is positive semidefinite, and
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w8-atp:eq:Heegner-formula}
 W(J_\Phi u*(J_\Phi v)^*)
 =\langle D_{F,K}u,D_{F,K}v\rangle_H+R[u,v].
\end{equation}
Weil positivity on $J_\Phi(S)$ is equivalent to $R+G\succeq0$,
or $R\succeq-G$. The stronger condition $R\succeq0$ is sufficient.
If $D_{F,K}u=0$, positivity in that direction is exactly $R[u,u]\geq0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The height pairing gives $G[u,u]=\|D_{F,K}u\|_H^2\geq0$.
Linearity of $J_\Phi$, sesquilinearity of $q_W$ and the definition
$M=G+R$ prove the identity. Its diagonal gives the positivity assertions.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Repeated characters and test normalization]
For $r=2$ and $\chi_1=\chi_2$, the vector $u=(1,-1)$ satisfies
$D_{F,K}u=0$, whereas $J_\Phi u=\phi_1-\phi_2\ne0$.
The height contributes zero in this direction, leaving precisely
$R[u,u]=W((\phi_1-\phi_2)*(\phi_1-\phi_2)^*)$ to control.
For $r=1$, suppose $\|c_{\chi,F}\|_H^2=g>0$. Replacing $\phi_1$
by $\varepsilon\phi_1$ changes $M_{11}$ to
$\varepsilon^2M_{11}$ for real $\varepsilon>0$, while $G_{11}=g$
is unchanged. Thus $R_{11}=\varepsilon^2M_{11}-g<0$ for sufficiently
small $\varepsilon$. The stronger sufficient condition $R\succeq0$
is sensitive to the chosen dictionary and is not a consequence of
height positivity.
\end{example}

An arithmetic-intersection interpretation requires further data:
a regular proper arithmetic model of $X_0(N)$ over $\mathcal O_H$,
degree-zero representatives, vertical corrections orthogonal to the
fibre components, and archimedean Green metrics. Let $\widehat V_{\mathbb R}$
be the chosen real space of corrected degree-zero arithmetic divisor classes,
and put $\widehat V_{\mathbb C}=\widehat V_{\mathbb R}\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C$.
Let $I_{\mathbb R}$ be its real symmetric admissible intersection pairing
and $I_{\mathbb C}$ its complex-bilinear extension. The pairing needed
here is the Hermitian extension
\[
 I_H(A,B)=I_{\mathbb C}(A,\overline B),
\]
where conjugation acts on the complex coefficients of arithmetic
classes. Specify a complex-linear lift $\widehat D:S\to\widehat V_{\mathbb C}$
whose degree-zero divisor class is $D_{F,K}$, and a normalization
$\kappa>0$ for which one proves
\[
 -\kappa I_H(\widehat D(u),\widehat D(v))
       =\langle D_{F,K}u,D_{F,K}v\rangle_H.
\]
The model, metrics, lifts and numerical
normalization are comparison data; the displayed equality is required,
not inferred from the notation. It is this Hermitian form, rather than
the complex-bilinear intersection form, that can match $q_W$.

Faltings--Hriljac arithmetic Hodge theory and a proposed Petersson--
Arakelov comparison of Jorgenson--Kramer (2003) must be applied with
these specific conventions. A geometrically defined Bott--Chern and
vertical correction form $R^{\mathrm{geom}}$ gives the proposed Weil
comparison only after proving $R^{\mathrm{geom}}[u,v]=R[u,v]$ on $S$.
That identity and $R^{\mathrm{geom}}\succeq0$ imply positivity on
$J_\Phi(S)$ by the theorem. The definition $R=M-G$ proves neither
this geometric identification nor its sign.

\begin{remark}[Comparison with other test images]
\label{v4-arith:w8-atp:rem:heeg-distinct}
The Heegner construction uses degree-zero classes and a specified test
dictionary. The domain-restricted Koszul--Petersson comparison of
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:kp:thm:main-full} in
Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:kp-compat} has different input data.
A proposed Rankin--Selberg central-value comparison through $\Upsilon_F$
at $L(1/2,F\otimes\bar F)$ must exhibit the relevant cusp form,
establish its required modularity and normalization, define its
compact-test map, and prove the pairing identity on its stated domain.
The images of the test maps must then be compared before drawing a
conclusion about their intersection.
Different inputs prove neither disjointness nor density.
The finite-dimensional image $J_\Phi(S)$ is not dense in $\mathscr A$:
a finite-dimensional subspace of a Hausdorff locally convex space is
closed, and $\mathscr A$ is infinite-dimensional, as disjointly
supported bumps show.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The Iwasawa--Kato Comparison Question}
\label{v4-arith:w8-atp:negative:iwasawa}

\begin{remark}[Finite-prime data and the full Weil pairing]
\label{v4-arith:w8-atp:thm:iwasawa-auxiliary-structure}
The Iwasawa comparison question concerns the following proposed inputs:
Mazur--Wiles value formulas; Kato Euler systems and Bloch--Kato--Beilinson
height pairings; a proposed archimedean counterpart of Ferrero--Washington;
Perrin--Riou and Bloch--Kato exponentials; a proposed $p\to\infty$
Connes--Marcolli comparison; Kato reciprocity maps $\exp^*/\log$;
Kolyvagin and Coates--Wiles height constructions; Colmez--Fontaine
$p$-adic representations; Fukaya--Kato noncommutative methods and
Skinner--Urban; and a proposed Dirichlet $p$-adic transport.
The named reference family comprises Mazur--Wiles (1984), Kato (2004,
\emph{Ast\'{e}risque} 295), Perrin--Riou (1995), Fukaya--Kato (2006),
Colmez, Bloch--Kato (1990), Kolyvagin (1988), Coates--Wiles (1977),
Ferrero--Washington (1979), Connes--Marcolli and Skinner--Urban.

For each such route, the missing comparison is a specified map to
$\mathscr A$ and an identity with $q_W$, or an explicit equivalence,
including the polar and archimedean terms. The existence, coefficients
and domain of that map must be part of the assertion. No completeness
of this list, exclusion of all Iwasawa methods, or positive pairing
comparison is assumed here. Functional-equation symmetry alone does
not provide a sign for $q_W$; the abstract divisor example below already
separates these properties. The conditional arithmetic complementarity
statement in Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:thm:P1-resolution} retains its
arithmetic evaluation, central-defect, and detection hypotheses.
It supplies no test map in this chapter.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[An archimedean Iwasawa comparison]
\label{v4-arith:w8-atp:rem:deninger-archimedean}
Deninger's cohomological analogies (ICM 1998; \emph{Motives and
algebraic cycles}, 2002) motivate asking for an archimedean analogue
of an Iwasawa main-conjecture construction. To obtain a formulation
of A-TP by this route, one must construct its carrier, operators and
pairing and prove equivalence with the full normalized Weil condition.
No such construction or RH-equivalence follows merely from the analogy.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Ordinate statistics and the divisor pairing}
\label{v4-arith:w8-atp:negative:BGS}

\begin{theorem}[Ordinate data do not determine the divisor form]
\label{v4-arith:w8-atp:thm:BGS-consequent}
There are conjugation- and reflection-invariant divisors in
$0<\Re s<1$ with identical ordinate multisets whose forms
\[
 Q_{\mathcal D}(f)=
 \sum_{\rho\in\mathcal D}
 h_f(z_\rho)\overline{h_f(\bar z_\rho)},
 \qquad f\in\mathscr A,
\]
have different positivity properties. Their ordinate statistics
are identical, including every statistic formed from finite
tuples of ordinates.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $\Gamma\subset\mathbb R$ be a locally finite symmetric
multiset with polynomial counting growth, and let $0<a<1/2$.
Compare the multisets
\[
 \mathcal D_0=\{1/2+i\gamma:\gamma\in\Gamma\}
                 \sqcup\{1/2,1/2\},\qquad
 \mathcal D_a=\{1/2+i\gamma:\gamma\in\Gamma\}
                 \sqcup\{1/2-a,1/2+a\}.
\]
Both have ordinate multiset $\Gamma\sqcup\{0,0\}$ and
the stated symmetries. The first form is a sum of absolute
squares. Choose an even nonnegative nonzero
$\phi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$ and put
\[
 f_L(u)=\phi(u-L)-\phi(u+L),\qquad
 c=h_\phi(ia)=h_\phi(-ia)>0.
\]
For real $t$, $|h_{f_L}(t)|\leq2|h_\phi(t)|$.
The number $C=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}|h_\phi(\gamma)|^2$
is finite by rapid decay and polynomial counting growth.
At the two nonreal spectral parameters,
\[
 h_{f_L}(ia)=-2c\sinh(aL),\qquad
 h_{f_L}(-ia)=2c\sinh(aL).
\]
Consequently
\[
 Q_{\mathcal D_a}(f_L)
 \leq4C-8c^2\sinh^2(aL)<0
\]
for sufficiently large $L$. All sums converge absolutely.
Changing the real parts has left every ordinate statistic
unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the statistical comparison]
\label{v4-arith:w8-atp:rem:statistical-upside-unchanged}
The countermodel is a statement about abstract divisors and
the information retained by ordinate statistics. It is not
a zeta divisor and satisfies no stipulated prime explicit
formula; it proves no logical independence assertion about
RH for the fixed Riemann zeta function.
The dynamical implication in
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:tfe:thm:stat-upside} additionally assumes a
zero-divisor realization, semiclassical dynamics, and the
appropriate BGS universality statement. RH alone supplies
none of those dynamical hypotheses.
\end{remark}
\section{Extension to the Full Weil Cone}
\label{v4-arith:w8-atp:residual}

The next proposition extends positivity by common-compact approximation.
Positivity on separate images does not itself control cross terms on
their linear span. Translation supplies a separate extension when every
convolution square has a representative in the given image.

\begin{proposition}[Extension by approximation of compact tests]
\label{v4-arith:w8-atp:prop:consolidated}
Suppose a family of specified test maps $J_\alpha:S_\alpha\to
\mathscr A$ has positive pulled-back Weil forms. Then $q_W$
is positive on the union of their images. If every compact test
is a smooth limit of tests from that union with all supports
in a common compact set, A-TP follows. For the Heegner map,
the exact positivity condition is $R+G\succeq0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The comparison identities give positivity on each image.
The continuity argument in
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w8-atp:thm:convergence} gives the extension.
The Heegner condition is the Gram-matrix identity.
\end{proof}

\section{Relation to Scattering and Arithmetic Realizations}
\label{v4-arith:w8-atp:relations}

Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:w7-j:ATP-adelic-scattering} constructs specified
self-adjoint scattering pairs and proves obstructions to positivity
of their ordinary trace differences. Its literal entire-Mellin class
is zero. Neither a scattering phase nor an inequality for a single
summand supplies the full identity with $P-W_\infty-W_{\mathrm{fin}}$.

A-TP remains equivalent to RH on the full compact-test algebra.
Under their respective sign hypotheses, the specified comparisons
establish positivity on their stated images. Passing to all compact
tests requires a further argument, such as approximation or the
translation identity. The abstract
divisor countermodel concerns the information lost by taking
ordinates; it does not remove the arithmetic hypotheses of
a zeta realization. A dynamical route to GUE statistics retains
the separate assumptions of
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:tfe:thm:stat-upside}.

The arithmetic-gravity comparisons in
Chapters~\ref{v4-arith:3d-acs-E1bar}--\ref{v4-arith:tfe-gutzwiller}
retain their separate state-space, chiral-homology, spectral-flow and
semiclassical hypotheses. A proposed prismatic interpretation at the
archimedean place also requires its own carrier and comparison;
it is not constructed by the Fourier or height maps here.

\section{The Remaining Comparison Requirements}
\label{v4-arith:w8-atp:summary}

\begin{enumerate}
\item The comparison theorem transfers positivity through a
specified map and pairing identity. Common-compact approximation
gives one extension to all tests. Translation gives another, without
requiring a dense image.
\item The Fourier-window map identifies an actual restricted
test space. The Heegner map identifies a finite test image and
the residual matrix $R=M-G$. Their parameterizations do not
establish disjointness or density of their images.
\item Ordinate statistics are unchanged in the two explicit
divisor models, although one divisor form has a negative square.
This statement does not assert an arithmetic counterexample
to RH or a theorem that RH implies GUE statistics.
\item The full residual is
$W(f*f^*)\geq0$ for every $f\in\mathscr A$.
\end{enumerate}

A comparison with A-TP must preserve the full normalized functional,
or prove an explicit equivalence with it, together with an argument
transferring positivity to all compact tests.
