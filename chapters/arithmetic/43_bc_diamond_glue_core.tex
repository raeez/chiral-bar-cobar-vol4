\chapter{Two-Prime Restriction and Bernstein Actions}
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core}

A global Galois representation restricts to a representation at each
finite place. Reconstructing the global object from two restrictions
is a different problem. Even with fixed determinant and absolutely
irreducible residual representation, two restrictions can agree for
distinct global lifts. The resulting deformation ring map nevertheless
constructs a pairwise pullback module and its local central actions.
Comparing this module with a global prismatic sheaf requires an
isomorphism of the actual objects, including their extension data.

\section{The Deformation Problem}
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:statement}
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:table}

Fix distinct finite primes $p,q$ and a coefficient prime
$p_0\ne p,q$. Let $k$ be a finite field of characteristic $p_0$,
and put $W=W(k)$. Choose a finite set $S$ of places of $\mathbb Q$
containing $p,q,p_0,\infty$. Write $G_{\mathbb Q,S}$ for the Galois
group of the maximal extension unramified outside $S$.
Fix a continuous representation
$\bar\rho:G_{\mathbb Q,S}\to GL_2(k)$ which is absolutely
irreducible, and let $\bar D$ be its determinant polynomial law.
Here a determinant of dimension two is a multiplicative homogeneous
polynomial law of degree two on the group algebra, as in
Definition~\ref{v4-arith:w5-h:def:chenevier-det-trace}.
Its characteristic coefficients, rather than a trace alone, define
the object in all residual characteristics.

Choose decomposition-group maps
$G_{\mathbb Q_p},G_{\mathbb Q_q}\to G_{\mathbb Q,S}$.
Choose closed local and global deformation conditions preserved by
restriction. These may include a fixed determinant character lifting
$\det\bar\rho$. For a finite local $W$-algebra $A$ with residue
field $k$, let $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{glob}}(A)$ and
$\mathcal D_v(A)$ denote the corresponding sets of continuous
lifts of the residual determinant, for $v=p,q$.
The local residual restrictions need not be absolutely irreducible.

Chenevier's representability theorem gives complete Noetherian local
rings $R^{\mathrm{Ch}},R_p^{\mathrm{Ch}},R_q^{\mathrm{Ch}}$ for
these functors. The precise hypotheses and primary locators are in
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:w5-h:prop:chenevier-moduli-via-det-trace}:
Chenevier, arXiv:0809.0415, Proposition~3.3 and Example~3.4,
p.~42, and Example~3.6 and Proposition~3.7, p.~43.
Each closed condition must be represented by the quotient used here.
These are formal deformation rings over one coefficient ring $W$.
An identification with functions on a local or global spectral stack
is additional geometric data.

\subsection{A Pair of Indistinguishable Local Restrictions}

\begin{proposition}[nonuniqueness with absolutely irreducible residual representation]
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:prop:two-prime-nonuniqueness}
There are distinct continuous rank-two determinant deformations over
$\mathbb Z/8\mathbb Z$, with the same absolutely irreducible
residual representation and fixed determinant character, whose
restrictions at $3$ and $5$ coincide.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $K$ be the splitting field of $X^3-X-1$ over $\mathbb Q$.
The polynomial has no root modulo $2$, hence is irreducible over
$\mathbb Q$. Its discriminant is $-23$, so its Galois group is
$S_3$. An integral standard representation of this group is generated
by
\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 B=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Direct multiplication gives $A^3=B^2=1$ and $BAB=A^{-1}$.
Modulo $2$, the two eigenlines of $A$ over $\mathbb F_4$ are
interchanged by $B$. Thus this residual representation is absolutely
irreducible. Inflate the integral representation to
$\rho:G_{\mathbb Q}\to GL_2(\mathbb Z_2)$.

Let $\chi$ be the quadratic character of
$L=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{241})$.
For $v=3,5$, the polynomial $X^2-241$ has the simple root $1$
modulo $v$. A root $u_r$ modulo $v^r$ lifts uniquely in its residue
class modulo $v^r$: the correction $u_r+c v^r$ is determined by
an equation with invertible coefficient $2u_r$ modulo $v$.
Taking the limit gives a root in $\mathbb Z_v$.
Hence $\chi|_{G_{\mathbb Q_v}}=1$ for both places.
Set $\rho'=\chi\rho$. Then
\[
 \bar\rho'=\bar\rho,\qquad
 \det\rho'=\chi^2\det\rho=\det\rho.
\]
The restrictions of $\rho$ and $\rho'$ at $3$ and $5$ agree.
Both are unramified outside
$S=\{2,3,5,23,241,\infty\}$.

The unique quadratic subfield of $K$ is $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-23})$,
which differs from $L$. Consequently $K\cap L=\mathbb Q$, and
the composite extension has Galois group $S_3\times C_2$.
Choose $g$ whose image is $(1,-1)$. Then
\[
 \det(T-\rho(g))=T^2-2T+1,\qquad
 \det(T-\rho'(g))=T^2+2T+1.
\]
The traces are $2$ and $6$ modulo $8$.
Thus the determinant laws of the two reductions modulo $8$ are
distinct lifts of the same residual law, with the asserted common
local restrictions and fixed determinant character.
\end{proof}

The example lies in a single finite-ramification deformation problem.
A more restrictive arithmetic condition could exclude one lift.
Uniqueness on such a condition would require its own proof.

\section{Restriction, Closed Image, and Fibres}
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:prismatic}

The local product functor is represented by
\[
 B_{p,q}=R_p^{\mathrm{Ch}}\widehat\otimes_W R_q^{\mathrm{Ch}}
 =\varprojlim_r
 (R_p^{\mathrm{Ch}}/\mathfrak m_p^r)
       \otimes_W(R_q^{\mathrm{Ch}}/\mathfrak m_q^r).
\]
The completion is for the sum of the maximal ideals.
Restriction is a natural transformation
$\mathcal D_{\mathrm{glob}}\to\mathcal D_p\times\mathcal D_q$,
so its map on representing rings has the opposite direction.

\begin{proposition}[the formal restriction diagram]
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:thm:prismatic-nygaard-transition}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
Restriction induces a continuous local $W$-algebra homomorphism
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:eq:chenevier-restriction-map}
 r_{p,q}^{\sharp}:B_{p,q}\longrightarrow R^{\mathrm{Ch}},
 \qquad x\otimes y\longmapsto r_p^{\sharp}(x)r_q^{\sharp}(y).
\end{equation}
For the map in Equation~\eqref{v4-arith:bcglue-core:eq:chenevier-restriction-map},
put $J_{p,q}=\ker r_{p,q}^{\sharp}$ and
$C_{p,q}=B_{p,q}/J_{p,q}$. Then
\[
 B_{p,q}\twoheadrightarrow C_{p,q}\hookrightarrow R^{\mathrm{Ch}},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Spf}R^{\mathrm{Ch}}\longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Spf}C_{p,q}\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Spf}B_{p,q}.
\]
For a continuous local map $b:B_{p,q}\to A$, the fibre over the
prescribed local pair is represented over $A$ by
$R^{\mathrm{Ch}}\widehat\otimes_{B_{p,q},b}A$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Restricting the universal determinant gives maps
$r_v^{\sharp}:R_v^{\mathrm{Ch}}\to R^{\mathrm{Ch}}$.
Their images commute, and their $W$-actions agree.
The completed tensor-product property gives
$r_{p,q}^{\sharp}$ with the displayed formula.
The kernel is closed. The quotient is complete and injects into
$R^{\mathrm{Ch}}$. Since the rings are profinite, its image is
closed. The formal-spectrum diagram reverses these maps.
For a finite local $A$-algebra $A'$, a map from the completed
base change to $A'$ is precisely a map $R^{\mathrm{Ch}}\to A'$
whose restriction to $B_{p,q}$ is the prescribed map through $A$.
This proves the fibre assertion. It is the same construction as
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w5-h:thm:ch1-closure}.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[the closed image of two-prime restriction]
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:def:cross-prime-chenevier}
The formal scheme $\operatorname{Spf}C_{p,q}$ is the closed image
of restriction inside the local deformation product.
A proposed two-prime Selmer algebra $R_{p,q}^{\mathrm{Ch}}$
requires a specified $B_{p,q}$-algebra structure and comparison
$R_{p,q}^{\mathrm{Ch}}\to C_{p,q}$ before the two can be identified.
The quotient $C_{p,q}$ itself imposes the kernel relations only.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[a flat closed-image map with a nonliftable point]
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:ex:closed-image}
Let $B=k[[x]]$ and $R=k[[t]]$, with $x\mapsto t^2$.
Every series in $t$ is uniquely $f(t^2)+t g(t^2)$.
Thus $R$ is free of rank two over $B$, and the map is injective.
Its kernel quotient is $B$ itself.
Nevertheless, the local point
$B\to k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$, $x\mapsto\epsilon$, has no lift
to $R$. A local image of $t$ is $a\epsilon$, whose square is zero.
Flatness does not turn the closed image into the image functor
on these Artinian points.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[an ordinary fibre with nontrivial derived structure]
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:ex:derived-fibre}
For $B=k[[x]]\to R=k$, $x\mapsto0$, the fibre over the residue
point has ordinary coordinate ring $k$.
The free resolution $[B\xrightarrow{x}B]$, in cohomological
degrees $-1,0$, gives
\[
 k\otimes_B^{\mathbb L}k\simeq k\oplus k[1],\qquad
 H^{-1}(k\otimes_B^{\mathbb L}k)=k.
\]
Thus the derived fibre retains information lost by the ordinary
fibre. The same calculation proves that this map is not flat.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[the information retained by restriction]
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:rem:why-chenevier}
The kernel quotient records equations satisfied by local pairs.
The fibres record their global lifts.
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:bcglue-core:prop:two-prime-nonuniqueness}
shows that those lifts need not be unique, so
$r_{p,q}^{\sharp}$ need not be surjective.
In a derived deformation theory the fibre uses derived completed
tensor product. None of these constructions identifies a Nygaard
filtration or supplies a comparison with prismatic sheaves.
\end{remark}

\section{The Pairwise Pullback and Its Central Actions}
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:local}

\begin{definition}[completed external product and pullback]
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:def:diagonal-pullback}
Let $M_p,M_q$ be finite modules over the local deformation rings.
Their completed external product is the finite $B_{p,q}$-module
\[
 M_{p,q}=\varprojlim_r
 (M_p/\mathfrak m_p^rM_p)\otimes_W
 (M_q/\mathfrak m_q^rM_q).
\]
The module obtained by ordinary restriction pullback is
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:eq:diagonal-pullback}
 M_{p,q}^{\mathrm{res}}
 =R^{\mathrm{Ch}}\widehat\otimes_{B_{p,q}}M_{p,q}
 =\varprojlim_r
 \frac{R^{\mathrm{Ch}}\otimes_{B_{p,q}}M_{p,q}}
 {\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{glob}}^r
       (R^{\mathrm{Ch}}\otimes_{B_{p,q}}M_{p,q})}.
\end{equation}
The algebraic base change is finite over $R^{\mathrm{Ch}}$ and
therefore already complete. Equation~\eqref{v4-arith:bcglue-core:eq:diagonal-pullback}
also specifies its topology.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[the action on the formal pullback]
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:def:bernstein-product-action}
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:prop:formal-pullback-action}
Let $Z_p,Z_q$ be unital $W$-algebras acting
$R_p^{\mathrm{Ch}}$-linearly and $R_q^{\mathrm{Ch}}$-linearly on
$M_p,M_q$, respectively. Each acting endomorphism is continuous
for the maximal-ideal topology. Then $M_{p,q}^{\mathrm{res}}$ has
a canonical action of $Z_p\otimes_W Z_q$, given by
\[
 (z_p\otimes z_q)(a\otimes(m_p\otimes m_q))
   =a\otimes(z_pm_p\otimes z_qm_q).
\]
This action commutes with the $R^{\mathrm{Ch}}$-action.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Finite presentations of $M_p,M_q$ show that $M_{p,q}$ is finite
over $B_{p,q}$. Base change gives a finite module over
$R^{\mathrm{Ch}}$. Local linearity makes the displayed action
compatible with the balancing relations and with every quotient
in the inverse limits. It therefore defines an action on the
completed tensors. The two local actions affect separate factors,
so they commute. Multiplication on the first factor commutes with
both. No topology on the abstract algebras $Z_p,Z_q$ is needed.
\end{proof}

The associated coherent sheaf on $\operatorname{Spf}R^{\mathrm{Ch}}$
is the ordinary pullback of the completed external product.
The derived construction replaces the external product and pullback
by their derived completed versions. Its boundedness requires
additional Tor conditions. The ordinary pullback is exact only on
a domain where the required higher Tor groups vanish.

\begin{proposition}[transport of ordinary central actions]
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:thm:local-bernstein}
For a $W$-linear category $\mathcal C$, let
$Z(\mathcal C)=\operatorname{End}(\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal C})$
be its ordinary centre. A $W$-linear equivalence
$F:\mathcal C\to\mathcal D$ induces an isomorphism
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:eq:local-bernstein-equals-spectral}
 Z(\mathcal C)\xrightarrow{\sim}Z(\mathcal D).
\end{equation}
For a fully faithful functor, this statement applies to its essential
image. It does not identify the centre of a larger target category.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose a quasi-inverse $G$ and the unit and counit isomorphisms.
A natural endomorphism $z$ of $\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal C}$ defines
an endomorphism of $\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal D}$ by conjugating
$FzG$ with the counit. Naturality gives a central action, and
composition and addition are preserved. This defines the map in
Equation~\eqref{v4-arith:bcglue-core:eq:local-bernstein-equals-spectral}.
The construction with $G$
is its inverse. Restricting a fully faithful functor to its essential
image makes it an equivalence.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[local coefficients and Bernstein blocks]
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:rem:local-domain}
For smooth representations of $GL_2(\mathbb Q_v)$ in
characteristic zero with coefficient prime different from $v$,
the Bernstein decomposition has the form
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:eq:bernstein-decomp}
 \mathrm{Rep}(GL_2(\mathbb Q_v))
   =\prod_{\mathfrak s}\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathfrak s}(GL_2(\mathbb Q_v)).
\end{equation}
For the blocks in Equation~\eqref{v4-arith:bcglue-core:eq:bernstein-decomp},
the ordinary scalar comparison and its precise domain are proved in
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:w10-e:prop:excursion-bernstein}.
Its image in the spectral categorical centre is the scalar subalgebra
of Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:w10-e:prop:scalar-centre-obstruction}.
Using a Bernstein centre as $Z_v$ in the formal pullback requires
an action over the chosen coefficient ring and on the actual module
$M_v$. A categorical comparison additionally needs a functor with
specified essential image. A local $(\varphi,\Gamma)$-module at
residue prime $v$ is not identified here with a deformation over
$W(k)$ of coefficient characteristic $p_0\ne v$.
\end{remark}

\section{Equivariant Isomorphisms on Formal Charts}
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:core-theorem}

The module $M_{p,q}^{\mathrm{res}}$ is constructed from local data.
Let $N$ be an independently specified global module.
The pairwise comparison asks for an isomorphism from the former
to the latter which intertwines their central actions.
Equal determinant laws alone do not solve this problem.

\begin{example}[extension data invisible to determinant laws]
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:ex:extension-data}
Let $R=k[[t]]$ and $M=N=R^2$. Give them actions of $R[z]$ by
\[
 z|_M=0,\qquad z|_N=J=\begin{pmatrix}0&t\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For every polynomial $f$, one has
$f(J)=f(0)1+f'(0)J$, so both actions have characteristic
polynomial $(X-f(0))^2$ for $f(z)$.
The determinant polynomial laws agree after every coefficient
extension. The actions also coincide modulo $t$.
An invertible intertwiner $U$ would satisfy $JU=0$, contradicting
$J\ne0$. These are reducible algebra representations.
They exhibit the loss of extension data for coherent objects;
they do not contradict reconstruction of a representation from an
absolutely irreducible residual determinant.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[the scheme of equivariant isomorphisms]
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:thm:pairwise-reduces-to-chenevier}
Let $(R,\mathfrak m,k)$ be a complete Noetherian local ring.
Let $M,N$ be free $R$-modules of rank $n$ with actions of an
$R$-algebra generated by $a_1,\ldots,a_s$.
Choose bases and let $P_i,Q_i\in\operatorname{Mat}_n(R)$ denote
the action matrices on $M,N$.
The functor of equivariant isomorphisms is represented by
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:eq:pairwise-chenevier-equivalent}
 \operatorname{Isom}(M,N)
 =\operatorname{Spec}
 \frac{R[u_{ab},d]}{
 (d\det(U)-1,\ (Q_iU-UP_i)_{ab}:i,a,b)}.
\end{equation}
Here $U=(u_{ab})$, with $1\le a,b\le n$ and $1\le i\le s$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Over any commutative $R$-algebra $A$, a matrix $U$ is invertible
exactly when its determinant is a unit. The variable $d$ specifies
the unique inverse of that determinant. Intertwining the generators
is equivalent to intertwining the whole algebra. Thus maps from
the coordinate ring in
Equation~\eqref{v4-arith:bcglue-core:eq:pairwise-chenevier-equivalent}
to $A$ are exactly the equivariant
isomorphisms $M\otimes_R A\to N\otimes_R A$, naturally in $A$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[successive lifting obstructions]
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:cor:part-a-domain-reduction}
In the preceding situation, put
$I_r=\mathfrak m^r/\mathfrak m^{r+1}$ for $r\ge1$.
Let $U_r$ be an invertible intertwiner modulo $\mathfrak m^r$.
Choose a lift $\widetilde U$ modulo $\mathfrak m^{r+1}$ and set
\[
 E_i=Q_i\widetilde U-\widetilde U P_i
       \in\operatorname{Mat}_n(I_r).
\]
Write bars for reduction modulo $\mathfrak m$, and define
\[
 L_r:\operatorname{Mat}_n(I_r)\longrightarrow
       \operatorname{Mat}_n(I_r)^s,
 \qquad V\longmapsto(\overline Q_iV-V\overline P_i)_i.
\]
The class $[(E_i)_i]\in\operatorname{coker}L_r$ is independent
of the lift. It vanishes precisely when this $U_r$ extends to
an invertible intertwiner modulo $\mathfrak m^{r+1}$.
A compatible sequence of such lifts determines an equivariant
isomorphism over $R$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Changing $\widetilde U$ by $V\in\operatorname{Mat}_n(I_r)$
changes the defects by $L_r(V)$, since $\mathfrak m I_r=0$.
Thus the class is independent of the lift and vanishes exactly
when a correction solves every intertwining equation.
The determinant of a corrected lift remains invertible modulo the
maximal ideal, hence is a unit. Completeness gives the matrix limit
of a compatible sequence. The intertwining equations pass to the
limit, and its determinant is a unit because its residue is.
Conversely an equivariant isomorphism over $R$ gives such a
compatible sequence by reduction.
\end{proof}

The obstruction depends on the specified partial lift $U_r$.
Vanishing for a compatible sequence, rather than separate choices
at unrelated orders, constructs the limit.
For general coherent complexes the comparison uses a derived
 equivariant Hom complex. The finite free criterion does not assert
that the intended arithmetic sheaves satisfy its hypotheses.

\section{Global Actions and Geometric Comparison}
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:global}

\begin{proposition}[the action generated by local centres]
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:thm:colimit-to-global-reduces-to-AG}
Let $E$ be a commutative ring and $Z_v$ commutative unital
$E$-algebras. For finite sets $S$ of places put
$A_S=\bigotimes_{v\in S,E}Z_v$, with transition maps inserting
units. Set
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:eq:global-bernstein-adelic}
 A=\varinjlim_{S\text{ finite}} A_S.
\end{equation}
Pairwise commuting unital actions
$\alpha_v:Z_v\to\operatorname{End}_E(M)$ on an $E$-module
induce a unique action
\[
 \alpha:A\to\operatorname{End}_E(M),\qquad
 \alpha(\otimes_{v\in S}z_v)=\prod_{v\in S}\alpha_v(z_v).
\]
Its image is $A/\ker\alpha$. Natural actions on an $E$-linear
category instead give a map to its ordinary centre.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The product of the commuting actions is multiplicative and
$E$-multilinear on each finite product. The tensor-product property
gives an action of $A_S$. Inserting a unit changes no endomorphism,
so these maps are compatible. The colimit in
Equation~\eqref{v4-arith:bcglue-core:eq:global-bernstein-adelic}
therefore gives $\alpha$.
The quotient assertion is the first isomorphism theorem.
Products of natural endomorphisms are natural, which proves the
categorical assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[faithfulness and generation for a global scalar centre]
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:thm:global-bernstein-chenevier}
Suppose the preceding actions define
$\alpha:A\to Z(\mathcal C)$ on an $E$-linear category.
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:eq:global-bernstein-chenevier}
 A/\ker\alpha\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{im}\alpha
       \subseteq Z(\mathcal C).
\end{equation}
This image is the entire ordinary centre exactly when every
central endomorphism is generated by the finite local actions.
The map from $A$ itself is an isomorphism exactly when it is
also faithful. No identification with $R^{\mathrm{Ch}}$ is
implicit in this statement.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The quotient isomorphism in
Equation~\eqref{v4-arith:bcglue-core:eq:global-bernstein-chenevier}
follows from the preceding proposition.
Surjectivity means precisely that every central endomorphism
belongs to this image. Injectivity means $\ker\alpha=0$.
Together these are exactly the isomorphism conditions.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[first-order fixed-determinant deformations]
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:rem:chenevier-tangent}
Let $V=k^2$ with action $\bar\rho$, and let
$\operatorname{ad}\bar\rho=\operatorname{End}_k(V)$ with conjugation
action. A lift over $k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$ has the form
$(1+\epsilon c(g))\bar\rho(g)$.
Multiplicativity is the continuous cocycle equation for $c$.
The fixed-determinant condition is $\operatorname{tr}c(g)=0$.
Strict change of basis changes $c$ by the coboundary of an element
of $\operatorname{ad}\bar\rho$. Thus the tangent space before
additional local conditions is
\[
 \frac{Z^1_{\mathrm{cts}}(G_{\mathbb Q,S},
                  \operatorname{ad}^0\bar\rho)}
 {d\,\operatorname{ad}\bar\rho}.
\]
The denominator consists of traceless cocycles.
When $2$ is invertible, scalar subtraction identifies it with
$d\,\operatorname{ad}^0\bar\rho$, giving the usual
$H^1_{\mathrm{cts}}(G_{\mathbb Q,S},\operatorname{ad}^0\bar\rho)$.
At characteristic two this simplification needs justification.
Additional deformation conditions restrict this tangent problem.
A tangent-space comparison does not identify the complete rings.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The Geometric Pairwise and Global Problems}

Choose a coefficient base $E$ for a geometric realization.
Suppose local spaces $X_p,X_q$, a global space $X$, and a morphism
$r:X\to X_p\times_E X_q$ have been constructed in a specified
formal, analytic, or derived category. Specify coefficient comparison
maps relating this diagram to the deformation rings above.
Let $\mathcal F_p,\mathcal F_q$ be the local coherent objects,
and let $\mathcal F_{\mathrm{glob}}^{\mathrm{prism}}$ be an
independently defined global object. The superscript records the
intended prismatic realization; its existence is an additional
construction. Work in a domain where the external product and
$Lr^*$ preserve the chosen category of coherent complexes, for
example on perfect complexes. Supply the local centre actions over
$E$ and their compatibility with the coefficient maps.

\begin{proposition}[external-product and pullback Tor-independence]
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:prop:ordinary-derived-comparison}
Suppose the local and global objects are coherent sheaves in
cohomological degree zero, in a geometric domain with the stated
derived external product and derived pullback.
Put $\mathcal M=\mathcal F_p\boxtimes_E\mathcal F_q$ and
$\mathcal M^{\mathbb L}=\mathcal F_p\boxtimes_E^{\mathbb L}\mathcal F_q$.
The external-product Tor-independence condition is that the
canonical augmentation $\mathcal M^{\mathbb L}\to\mathcal M$
is a quasi-isomorphism. The pullback Tor-independence condition is
that $Lr^*\mathcal M\to r^*\mathcal M$ is a quasi-isomorphism.
These are separate vanishing conditions on the higher derived
external product and the higher derived pullback.
Under both conditions, an equivariant ordinary isomorphism
$r^*\mathcal M\simeq\mathcal F_{\mathrm{glob}}^{\mathrm{prism}}$
induces an equivariant derived isomorphism
\[
 Lr^*\mathcal M^{\mathbb L}
       \simeq\mathcal F_{\mathrm{glob}}^{\mathrm{prism}}.
\]
For formal spaces the conditions refer to the derived completed
operations in the chosen realization.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The external-product condition gives
$Lr^*\mathcal M^{\mathbb L}\simeq Lr^*\mathcal M$.
The pullback condition identifies the latter with $r^*\mathcal M$.
Composition with the given ordinary isomorphism proves the result.
The augmentations are natural, so they intertwine the specified
central actions. Thus the composite is equivariant.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[ordinary descent without derived descent]
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:ex:external-tor}
Let $W=W(k)$ for a finite field $k$ of characteristic $p_0$.
Take all three spaces to be $\operatorname{Spf}W$, let $r$ be
the identity, and take
$\mathcal F_p=\mathcal F_q=\mathcal F_{\mathrm{glob}}=k$.
The ordinary external product and its pullback are $k$.
However, the free resolution
$[W\xrightarrow{p_0}W]$ of $k$, in degrees $-1,0$, gives
\[
 k\otimes_W^{\mathbb L}k\simeq k\oplus k[1].
\]
This is also the derived completed tensor product: the resulting
finite $p_0$-torsion modules are derived $p_0$-complete.
It is not quasi-isomorphic to $k$.
Here $r$ is flat and both local sheaves are perfect, but
external-product Tor-independence fails.
Thus perfectness and flatness of $r$ do not convert ordinary
pairwise descent into its derived enhancement.
\end{example}

\begin{conjecture}[pairwise and global Bernstein compatibility]
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:cj:core}
For the intended arithmetic geometric realization and every
admissible pair of distinct finite primes, the following comparisons
exist.
For coherent sheaves in degree zero, the ordinary pairwise
comparison is an equivariant isomorphism
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:eq:diagonal-is-external}
 r^*(\mathcal F_p\boxtimes_E\mathcal F_q)
       \simeq\mathcal F_{\mathrm{glob}}^{\mathrm{prism}}.
\end{equation}
The derived enhancement is the additional equivariant comparison
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:eq:derived-diagonal-is-external}
 Lr^*(\mathcal F_p\boxtimes_E^{\mathbb L}\mathcal F_q)
       \simeq\mathcal F_{\mathrm{glob}}^{\mathrm{prism}}.
\end{equation}
The ordinary and derived assertions are distinct.
Both hold together if ordinary descent and the two Tor-independence
conditions of
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:bcglue-core:prop:ordinary-derived-comparison}
hold. Each asserted family of isomorphisms must be compatible with
the geometric transition maps as the finite set of places varies.
Second, the local actions on the global automorphic category induce
an isomorphism onto its specified ordinary Bernstein centre
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:eq:colimit-to-global}
 A\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{glob}},
\end{equation}
and a specified comparison identifies the action in
Equation~\eqref{v4-arith:bcglue-core:eq:colimit-to-global}
with its spectral
counterpart. Thus the action must be faithful and generate the
intended centre. A comparison of higher centres must
be constructed in its category of derived algebras and is a
separate assertion from this degree-zero identity.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[the two comparison problems]
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:rem:both-halves}
The pairwise assertion compares coherent objects and their actions.
The global assertion identifies the image and range of the assembled
central action. The formal restriction diagram constructs neither
the first isomorphism nor the latter surjectivity.
The hypotheses of
Corollary~\ref{v4-arith:w5-h:cor:ch1-closes-pairwise} specify the
Selmer-algebra, geometric, trace and ordinary descent data.
The derived enhancement requires the additional derived descent
datum or the two Tor-independence conditions. Neither version
establishes the global centre map.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[local equations and gluing of the pairwise comparison]
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:thm:core-final-reduction}
Suppose, on a formal scheme $X$, the two objects in the pairwise
comparison are vector bundles of equal rank.
Choose an open cover trivializing both bundles.
Suppose the algebra of actions on each chart has specified finitely
many generators. The equivariant isomorphism exists if and only if
there are invertible matrices on the charts satisfying the
intertwining equations and agreeing under the transition matrices
on every overlap.
On a complete local chart, a specified residue isomorphism lifts
exactly when one can successively choose lifts with zero obstruction
classes from
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:bcglue-core:cor:part-a-domain-reduction}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
An isomorphism gives its invertible matrices in the chosen bases.
Equivariance gives the intertwining equations, and independence of
trivialization gives the overlap equalities.
Conversely the overlap equalities glue the local morphisms as
morphisms of sheaves. Their local inverses also agree on overlaps,
so the global morphism is invertible. Equivariance can be checked
on each chart, and hence holds globally.
The assertion on a complete local chart is precisely the successive
lifting proposition. It requires a compatible choice at every order.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the missing arithmetic comparisons]
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:rem:what-remains}
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:rem:honesty}
The remaining constructions are the geometric realization of the
formal restriction map, the local modules with their Bernstein
actions over a common coefficient base, and the independently defined
global prismatic object. Their equivariant comparison must preserve
the required filtrations and agree on overlaps and on larger finite
sets of places. To apply the vector-bundle criterion one must also
establish its local freeness hypotheses. Otherwise a derived
comparison is required. Identifying a kernel quotient or matching
traces does not construct these maps.
\end{remark}

\section{The Global Descent Problem}
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:lacd-consequence}

\begin{proposition}[data supplied by two-prime restriction]
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:cor:LACD-tightened}
For the fixed deformation problem, two-prime restriction supplies
the map $B_{p,q}\to R^{\mathrm{Ch}}$, its closed-image quotient,
and its formal fibres. Given finite local modules and the specified
local actions, it also supplies $M_{p,q}^{\mathrm{res}}$ with its
product action. These constructions do not require arithmetic
Arinkin--Gaitsgory or a prismatic comparison.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The restriction diagram and fibre follow from
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:bcglue-core:thm:prismatic-nygaard-transition}.
The module and action follow from
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:bcglue-core:prop:formal-pullback-action}.
Their proofs use the stated universal properties and finite module
presentations only.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[categorical and prismatic descent]
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:rem:domain-delta}
\label{v4-arith:bcglue-core:rem:realization-discipline}
Adelic categorical descent additionally requires the actual local
functors, their coefficient and family comparisons, the prismatic
and untilt descent maps, and the global centre comparison.
The ordinary scalar-centre theorem does not provide these functors.
The determinant rings do not specify a common Nygaard filtration
at distinct residue primes. A proposed arithmetic singular-support
construction must define its spaces and higher centre maps before
it can supply the global comparison. The algebraic and geometric
obstructions to that step are developed in
Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:w6-g:chapter}.
\end{remark}
