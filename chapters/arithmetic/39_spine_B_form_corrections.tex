\chapter{Sharp Hypotheses for the Four-Input RH Form}
\label{v4-arith:w3-comparison-B}

The six comparisons below have different mathematical inputs.
P1 requires an arithmetic evaluation, while P3 compares arithmetic
and Petersson pairings. K5 supplies a spherical Whittaker normalization,
and C1--C5 specify additional algebraic and analytic operations.
The Hilbert--P\'olya and positive-trace questions concern the spectral
realization.

Tate's Poisson formula gives the scalar functional equation.
The arithmetic P1 interpretation needs its defect and evaluation maps.
The local Whittaker norms and occupation/radial isometries retain their
arithmetic placement and adelic transport hypotheses. The Friedrichs
construction supplies an operator domain. Identifying its determinant
and trace with the required spectral object is a further comparison.

\subsection{Zero-Placement}
\label{v4-arith:w3-comparison-B:correction-23}

\begin{proposition}[one-sided zero-placement]
\emph{Conditional statement.} Assume P2, full P3, the
\(\mathrm{H}\text{-}\mathrm{P}^{\mathrm{Ar}}\) determinant-trace realisation,
and the positive \(\mathrm{VB}^{*}\) trace form.  Then
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:rh:thm:VB-implies-F-RH} gives the zero-placement clause
of F-RH.  The reverse implication is not a consequence of RH; it asks
for spectral regularity of the operator model.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The implication used in
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:rh:thm:VB-implies-F-RH} is the positive quadratic form
statement on the Bost--Connes regularised trace.  P2 and P3 identify
the test vectors and the Petersson form with the arithmetic trace
form.  \(\mathrm{H}\text{-}\mathrm{P}^{\mathrm{Ar}}\) identifies the
zero divisor and the Weil--Connes trace distribution.  Positivity then
forces the ordinates entering the Weil form to be real, hence the zeros
lie on the critical line.  None of these steps constructs the converse
positive form from zero-placement alone.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Arithmetic Positselski}
\label{v4-arith:w3-comparison-B:correction-25}

\begin{proposition}[two arithmetic comparison criteria]
Suppose the arithmetic comodule and contramodule recipients,
exact adjunction, and complete unit and counit cofibers satisfy
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:thm:arithmetic-positselski}.
Then the adjunction is an equivalence.

Suppose also that the arithmetic central classes have the
common defect and Mellin evaluation of
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:thm:P1-resolution}, and that the evaluation
detects that defect. Then their cancellation is equivalent
to the completed scalar functional equation.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The first assertion is the stated completion criterion applied
to the actual unit and counit. The second follows by evaluating
\(v-Jv=(\kappa+\kappa^!)w\) with \(E(w)\ne0\).
The two assertions require different maps.
Faithfulness of reduction modulo \(p\) is not used. It fails
already on the nonzero endomorphism \(p\) of \(\Lambda/(T)\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Koszul--Petersson Compatibility}
\label{v4-arith:w3-comparison-B:correction-26}

\begin{proposition}[Conditional transport of P3]
Assume the arithmetic placement, pairing maps and completed adelic
extension of Hypothesis~\ref{v4-arith:kms-gns:hyp:arithmetic-pairing}
on the specified normalized sph-fin cuspidal core. The transported
positive Petersson form then agrees with the arithmetic bilinear
pairing composed with its specified conjugation.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The model identities are \(H(v,w)=B(v,Jw)\). The occupation,
diagonal Hilbert--Schmidt and radial-function maps in
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:bcglue-k5:thm:K5-absorption-canonical}
verify these identities on their local carriers. Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:kp:thm:tate-assembly}
constructs their normalized restricted tensor form. The assumed
arithmetic maps identify that form on the stated completed domain.
No equality of local scalar factors alone supplies those maps.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}[The full pairing construction]
The normalized archimedean vacuum line and prime cyclic Gibbs model
are genuine local constructions. Their full arithmetic realization,
ramified factors and continuous-spectrum transport remain required.
The restriction to a cuspidal sph-fin core does not remove the
placement and pairing hypotheses even there.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Costello Refinements C1--C5}
\label{v4-arith:w3-comparison-B:correction-34}

\begin{proposition}[Costello refinements]
Assume the integral co--contra comparison, the central-defect
identity, and the detecting Mellin evaluation of
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:pos-costello:thm:P1-unconditional}.
Then the P1 biconditional holds on any common arithmetic domain
also satisfying the following C1--C5 requirements:
\begin{enumerate}
\item C1: local factorization coalgebras, compatible unital
transitions and the collision functors satisfy
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:pos-costello:thm:C1-closed}; the adelic
differential comparison of
Question~\ref{v4-arith:pos-costello:qn:adelic-factorization-comparison}
is supplied. Any required \(E_2\)-monoidal coefficient structures
are specified before taking their pointed colimit;
\item C2: the tame-weighted chiral carriers and degree-one operators of
Question~\ref{v4-arith:pos-costello:thm:C2-closed} are constructed,
with their full square-zero, norm and transition identities and
comparison maps. Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:pos-costello:thm:C2-strict-model}
supplies a proved ordinary local model;
\item C3: the completed arithmetic carrier and arithmetic/BV
comparison maps of Question~\ref{v4-arith:pos-costello:thm:C3-closed}
are constructed, preserving the observables, state and dynamics;
any pro-nilpotent presentation has the specified filtration and
compatible transition maps;
\item C4: the common coefficient complex and finite-place and real
realization maps of Question~\ref{v4-arith:pos-costello:thm:C4-closed}
are constructed, identifying the differential, central normalization
and pairing, including the real operators corresponding to the
Iwasawa and Gersten components;
\item C5: the morphism class is \(\Lambda\)-linear with trivial
\(\Gamma\)-action.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The P1 conclusion follows from the explicitly assumed evaluation
and defect identity by
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:pos-costello:thm:P1-unconditional}.
C1 supplies the local coalgebras, their compatible assembly, and
the required coefficient structures. C2 supplies the arithmetic-chiral
differential and comparison identities. C3 supplies the completed
carrier and state comparison. C4 identifies arithmetic and real
realizations through a common coefficient complex. C5 specifies
the allowed morphisms.

The integral co--contra criterion in
Section~\ref{v4-arith:w3-comparison-B:correction-25} requires
its own adjunction, complete cofibers, and reduced equivalences.
\end{proof}

\subsection{K5 Godement--Jacquet Normalisation}
\label{v4-arith:w3-comparison-B:correction-35}

\begin{proposition}[Local factors and the global continued value]
For a specified unramified tempered representation,
\(L_p(1,\operatorname{Ad}\pi_p)\) is finite and nonzero, and the
spherical norm is \(m_p(1+p^{-1})L_p(1,\operatorname{Ad}\pi_p)\).
The global continued value \(c_\pi=L(1,\operatorname{Ad}\pi)\ne0\)
and the arithmetic pairing comparisons are separate inputs to the
adelic normalization theorem.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The unit-circle parameters make each local denominator nonzero
because \(p^{-1}<1\); the shell-series proof computes the norm in
Lemma~\ref{v4-arith:bcglue-k5:lem:spherical-norm}. Neither assertion
uses global nonvanishing. Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:bcglue-k5:thm:K5-adelic-consistent}
rescales two already compared forms by the common supplied scalar
\(c_\pi^{-1}\). Finiteness of local factors supplies neither that
continued global value nor a projection excluding continuous spectrum.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The propagation of K5 to full Koszul--Petersson compatibility is
therefore the full P3 problem of
\S\ref{v4-arith:w3-comparison-B:correction-26}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{\(\mathrm{H}\text{-}\mathrm{P}^{\mathrm{Ar}}\) and the \(\mathrm{VB}^{*}\) Converse}
\label{v4-arith:w3-comparison-B:correction-36}

\begin{proposition}[Friedrichs domain and determinant divisor]
\emph{Conditional statement.} The Friedrichs construction of
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:hpvbconv:thm:L0-Friedrichs} gives a self-adjoint
operator on its quadratic-form domain.  The Hilbert--P\'olya input is
the further assertion that the associated normal zero-divisor operator has
determinant divisor \(\mathrm{div}\,\xi_{\mathbb R}\) and
Weil--Connes trace equal to the zero distribution.  This assertion is
not a choice of zero-mode weights.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Friedrichs theorem starts from a lower-semicontinuous positive
form and produces a self-adjoint operator.  It does not determine the
regularised determinant or the Weil--Connes trace of the scaling
action.  The Connes and Deninger forms have the required Lefschetz
shape only when the determinant divisor and the trace distribution are
the non-trivial zero divisor and distribution of \(\xi_{\mathbb R}\).
Thus the operator-domain theorem and the determinant-trace theorem are
distinct statements.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Conditional operator comparisons]
Under Hypothesis~\ref{v4-arith:hpvbconv:hyp:operator-quantitative-comparison},
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:hpvbconv:thm:VBstar-converse-spectral-regularity}
compares the predicates VB\(^{*}\), (Reg), (HB+) and (BD+).
(Reg) includes positivity and the specified spectral support as well
as determinacy. In the RH sector, supplying one of these predicates
with the comparison data establishes the VB\(^{*}\) converse;
the implication ``RH implies VB\(^{*}\)'' is not itself one of the
four equivalent predicates.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Item~1 of the stated hypothesis identifies operator positivity with
(Reg). Item~2 transports the operators and their domains by a unitary,
so that positivity is equivalent to (HB+). Item~3 supplies the separate
comparison of (Reg) with (BD+). Applying these three comparisons gives
the asserted equivalences. RH alone supplies none of these comparison
maps or the sharp approximation bound.
\end{proof}

\section{The Four-Input Conditional Conjunction}
\label{v4-arith:w3-comparison-B:four-input-branch}

\begin{theorem}[the four-input conditional conjunction]
\label{v4-arith:w3-comparison-B:thm:four-input-branch}
\emph{Conditional statement.} Assume the following four
statements.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(\mathrm{P}_{1}^{\mathrm{ArPos}}\): arithmetic Positselski for
the specified arithmetic domain, together with the central-defect
identity, Mellin evaluation, and detection condition of
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:thm:P1-resolution}.
\item \(\mathrm{P}_{3}^{\mathrm{KP}}\): full-sector
Koszul--Petersson compatibility, extending the normalized sph-fin
cuspidal identity by the three spectral hypotheses of
\S\ref{v4-arith:w3-comparison-B:correction-26}.
\item \(\mathrm{HP}^{\mathrm{Ar}}\): the
\(\mathrm{H}\text{-}\mathrm{P}^{\mathrm{Ar}}\) determinant-trace realisation,
including determinant divisor \(\mathrm{div}\,\xi_{\mathbb R}\) and
the Weil--Connes zero trace distribution.
\item \(\mathrm{VB}^{*}_{+}\): the positive Bost--Connes
regularised trace form giving the forward
\(\mathrm{VB}^{*}\Rightarrow\) zero-placement implication.
\end{enumerate}
Together with the Tate-Poisson direction, P2, and Hamburger
determinacy, these four statements imply the zero-placement clause of
F-RH.  Hence the arithmetic branch implies RH under precisely this
four-input conjunction.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first hypothesis includes the evaluation and defect identity
needed for Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:thm:P1-resolution}, so it supplies
P1 on that arithmetic domain.  Full P3 identifies the adelic
Petersson form with the arithmetic trace form on the whole primary
space.  \(\mathrm{HP}^{\mathrm{Ar}}\) supplies the zero divisor and
Weil--Connes trace distribution attached to that trace.  The positive
\(\mathrm{VB}^{*}_{+}\) form is then the Weil-positive form on the zero
distribution.  By the Platonic completion theorem
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:platonic:thm:RH-completion}, P1, P2, P3,
\(\mathrm{HP}^{\mathrm{Ar}}\), \(\mathrm{VB}^{*}_{+}\), and Hamburger
determinacy imply F-RH, hence RH.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[independence of directions]
\label{v4-arith:w3-comparison-B:rem:preserved}
The theorem is a sufficient implication.  It does not assert that RH
recovers arithmetic Positselski, full Koszul--Petersson compatibility,
the Hilbert--P\'olya determinant-trace realisation, or the positive
\(\mathrm{VB}^{*}\) trace form.  The
\(\mathrm{H}\text{-}\mathrm{P}^{\mathrm{Ar}}\) and
\(\mathrm{VB}^{*}\) residuals are separated by
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:hpvbconv:prop:HPAr-det-independent}; the Positselski
and Koszul--Petersson residuals are separated by
Section~\ref{v4-arith:pos:residual-domain} and
Remark~\ref{v4-arith:kp:rem:substantial-reestimate}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[hypothesis support]
\label{v4-arith:w3-comparison-B:rem:changed}
The P1 input is an arithmetic central-defect identity and an
equivariant Mellin evaluation. A converse additionally requires
detection of the specified defect vector. The coefficient and
co--contra hypotheses are separate requirements.  The P3 support is the
\(\mathrm{KMS}_{1}\)/GNS identification, the full Rankin--Selberg
extension, and the Eisenstein-sector calculation.  The
\(\mathrm{HP}^{\mathrm{Ar}}\) support is the determinant divisor
\(\mathrm{div}\,\xi_{\mathbb R}\) and the Weil--Connes trace
distribution.
The \(\mathrm{VB}^{*}_{+}\) support is positivity of the regularised
Bost--Connes trace.
\end{remark}

\section{Logical Form of the Essential Biconditional}
\label{v4-arith:w3-comparison-B:load-bearing}

The essential biconditional
\begin{equation*}
\kappa^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathsf{G}}+(\kappa^{!})^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathsf{G}}
\;=\;0\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)
\end{equation*}
(Theorems~\ref{v4-arith:thm:G-row-Riemann-functional-equation}
and~\ref{v4-arith:thm:P1-resolution}) holds under its specified
arithmetic evaluation and central-defect hypotheses.
Tate's Poisson formula proves the scalar functional equation.
The forward arithmetic implication follows by evaluating the defect
identity. The reverse implication additionally requires that
evaluation detect the defect. It does not reconstruct an entire
co--contra equivalence from a scalar equality.

In \(\mathbb C^2\), swapping coordinates preserves their sum,
although it does not fix \((1,0)\). This is the kernel
example following Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:thm:P1-resolution}.

\section{Consequences}
\label{v4-arith:w3-comparison-B:summary}

\begin{enumerate}
\item The scalar Tate identity is unconditional. Its arithmetic
interpretation requires the defect and evaluation maps. The converse
also requires detection.
\item P3 on the normalized sph-fin cuspidal core retains its actual
arithmetic placement, pairing and adelic extension hypotheses.
\item K5 has explicit local Hilbert models and a computed spherical
norm; the arithmetic action-compatible comparison remains to be constructed.
\item C1--C5 are sufficient Costello refinements on their common
restricted domain and inherit the arithmetic-Positselski hypotheses.
\item The Friedrichs theorem gives the operator domain for
\(\mathrm{H}\text{-}\mathrm{P}^{\mathrm{Ar}}\); the determinant divisor
and trace distribution are the Hilbert--P\'olya content.
\item The \(\mathrm{VB}^{*}\) converse is a spectral-regularity
comparison and is logically distinct from the positive forward
implication to zero-placement.
\end{enumerate}
