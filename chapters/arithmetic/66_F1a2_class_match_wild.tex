\chapter{F1.a.2 Class-Match at Wild Primes and Paskunas Block-Centraliser
Regularity}
\label{v4-arith:w6-f:chapter}

At the primes \(p=2,3\), the intended comparison relates
arithmetic block extension classes to a tangent fibre of a specified
wild-enhanced Emerton--Gee stack. Its construction requires both a
block-to-Galois map and a Galois-to-spectral map, with their coefficient
and class normalizations. The finite-algebra examples of
Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion} supply neither map.
The class-match result below is the conditional composition of these
two isomorphisms. Multiplicity and block-centraliser data are separate
inputs and do not replace the class-level comparison.

\section{Objects}
\label{v4-arith:w6-f:domain}

\subsection{The Three Boundary Conditions}

The Paskunas comparison has three named boundary conditions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{F1.a.2.class-match}
\Cref{v4-arith:f1a3-BH:conj:Paskunas-wild-ext}): the class-level
identification (not merely dimensional) between the
\(\mathrm{Ext}^{1}\)-classes \(\alpha_{01}\) at \(p=2\) and
\((\alpha_{01},\alpha_{12})\) at \(p=3\) on the automorphic side and the

\item \textbf{R.B.2}
\Cref{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:prop:remainder-AB}(R.B.2)): the wild-prime
block centraliser regularity result, the analogue of Paskunas' \(p\geq 5\)

\subsection{Deformation and Block Comparisons}

The very-special datum concerns the Breuil--Mezard multiplicity on the
specified sub-stratum. The wild centraliser datum concerns the local ring
and its generic fibre. The Galois deformation tangent is computed below;
identifying it with the automorphic block deformation problem requires
an additional comparison map.

The block-to-Galois comparison and the block-centraliser result
are different mathematical inputs. In particular, a centraliser
calculation or an equality of dimensions does not construct the
class-level map in
Hypothesis~\ref{v4-arith:w6-f:prop:Paskunas-equals-Galois}.

\section{F1.a.2 Class-Match at Wild Primes}
\label{v4-arith:w6-f:class-match}

\subsection{The Class-Level Target}

The class-level compatibility statement is the following: each
non-semisimple block's \(\mathrm{Ext}^{1}\)-class on the automorphic side
must correspond, as a specific \(k\)-line, to a specified first-order
tangent direction on the wild-enhanced EG stack.
We write
\[
\mathbb{T}^{1}_{\mathcal X}|_{x}:=
H^{0}\bigl((\mathbb L_{\mathcal X}|_{x})^{\vee}\bigr)
\]
for this infinitesimal tangent fibre.

\begin{definition}[the class-match target, \(p=2\)]
\label{v4-arith:w6-f:def:class-match-p2}
At \(p=2\), fix \(\chi\) and the unique
non-semisimple principal block \(\mathfrak{B}_{2}\). The
\emph{class-match target} is a \(k\)-linear isomorphism
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w6-f:eq:class-match-p2}
\mathsf{c}^{\mathrm{class}}_{2}\colon
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathfrak{B}_{2}}(S_2,S'_2)
\;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\;
\mathbb{T}^{1}_{\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{EG,wild},2,\chi}_{GL_{2}}}\big|_{x_{\tau_{2}}},
\end{equation}
where the coefficient category, objects \(S_2,S'_2\) and a
nonzero class \(\alpha\) must be specified as in
Question~\ref{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:prop:R4-Ext1-p2}.
The target is to carry this class to a specified tangent direction
at \(x_{\tau_2}\). In characteristic two, trivial and sign
characters cannot be used as two distinct objects.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[the class-match target, \(p=3\)]
\label{v4-arith:w6-f:def:class-match-p3}
At \(p=3\), fix \(\chi\) and the three
principal-block types \(\tau_{3,0},\tau_{3,1},\tau_{3,2}\). The
class-match target is a \(k\)-linear isomorphism
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w6-f:eq:class-match-p3}
\mathsf{c}^{\mathrm{class}}_{3}\colon
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(L_{0},L_{1})\oplus\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(L_{1},L_{2})
\;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\;
\mathbb{T}^{1}_{\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{EG,wild},3,\chi}_{GL_{2}}}\big|_{x_{\tau_{3}}},
\end{equation}
sending the quiver arrows \(\alpha_{01}\mapsto e_{1}\) and
\(\alpha_{12}\mapsto e_{2}\) onto the two tangent directions.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Galois-Deformation Functor on Non-Semisimple Blocks}

\begin{definition}[block deformation functor]
\label{v4-arith:w6-f:def:block-deformation}
Fix \(p\in\{2,3\}\), \(\chi\), and a
non-semisimple block \(\mathfrak{B}\) with reducible residual
\(\bar\rho_{\mathfrak{B}}\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\to GL_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})\).
The \emph{block deformation functor}
\(\mathrm{Def}^{\mathfrak{B}}_{\bar\rho_{\mathfrak{B}},\chi}\colon\mathrm{CNL}_{W(k)}\to\mathrm{Set}\)
sends a complete Noetherian local \(W(k)\)-algebra \(A\) with residue
field \(k\) to the set of equivalence classes of liftings
\(\rho_{A}\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\to GL_{2}(A)\) of
\(\bar\rho_{\mathfrak{B}}\) with central character \(\chi\cdot\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}\)
whose reduction is in the block \(\mathfrak{B}\).
Choose a field \(k\) over which the residual representation is defined.
The determinant is a specified lift of the residual determinant.
Equivalence means conjugation by a matrix reducing to the identity,
unless the determinant-one convention is explicitly imposed.
Any condition connecting lifts to the arithmetic block is additional data.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[fixed-determinant first-order deformations]
\label{v4-arith:w6-f:lem:representability}
Let \(\bar\rho:G_{\mathbb Q_p}\to GL_2(k)\) be continuous.
Write \(\operatorname{ad}\bar\rho=M_2(k)\), with conjugation action,
and let \(\operatorname{ad}^0\bar\rho\) be its trace-zero submodule.
First-order lifts with fixed determinant, modulo unrestricted strict
\(GL_2\)-conjugation, form
\[
 D_{\bar\rho}=Z^1_{\mathrm{cts}}(G_{\mathbb Q_p},
 \operatorname{ad}^0\bar\rho)/d(\operatorname{ad}\bar\rho).
\]
If strict conjugation is restricted to determinant-one matrices,
the quotient is \(H^1_{\mathrm{cts}}(G_{\mathbb Q_p},
\operatorname{ad}^0\bar\rho)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write a lift as
\(\rho_\epsilon(g)=(1+\epsilon c(g))\bar\rho(g)\).
Multiplication gives \(c(gh)=c(g)+g\cdot c(h)\).
The identity \(\det(1+\epsilon c)=1+\epsilon\operatorname{tr}(c)\)
shows that fixing the determinant imposes trace zero.
Conjugating by \(1+\epsilon X\) changes \(c(g)\) by
\(X-g\cdot X\). This is a coboundary up to sign, and its trace is
zero for every \(X\in M_2(k)\). Determinant-one conjugations
instead require \(\operatorname{tr}(X)=0\), giving the second quotient.
\end{proof}

This calculation supplies no character twist and no pro-representability
assertion for a reducible unframed deformation problem. Any additional
block condition must first be defined on the lifts and their equivalences.

\begin{hypothesis}[comparison of formal deformation problems]
\label{v4-arith:w6-f:lem:block-match-EG}
Specify the coefficient field, determinant condition, framing and
allowed conjugations for the block deformation problem. Assume an
equivalence between its formal deformation groupoid and the formal
completion of the specified wild Emerton--Gee stack at
\(x_{\tau_{\mathfrak B}}\), preserving automorphism groups.
A presentation by \(\operatorname{Spf}R^{\mathfrak B}_{\bar\rho,\chi}\)
requires separate pro-representability and the absence or removal of
stabilizers. A formal stack with nontrivial stabilizers is not the
formal spectrum of a ring.
\end{hypothesis}

\subsection{Residual Gerbe Comparison}

\begin{definition}[residual gerbe]
\label{v4-arith:w6-f:def:residual-gerbe}
For a closed point \(x\) of a formal
algebraic stack \(\mathcal{X}\) with residue field \(k\) and
automorphism group \(\mathrm{Aut}(x)\), the \emph{residual gerbe}
\(\mathcal{G}_{x}\) is the closed substack \(B\mathrm{Aut}(x)\subset\mathcal{X}\).
For \(x_{\tau_{\mathfrak{B}}}\in\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{EG,wild},p,\chi}_{GL_{2}}\)
the automorphism group is the centraliser
\(Z_{GL_{2}}(\bar\rho_{\mathfrak{B}})\).
\end{definition}

\begin{hypothesis}[spectral tangent comparison]
\label{v4-arith:w6-f:prop:gerbe-cotangent-match}
For the specified deformation problem, assume a natural isomorphism
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w6-f:eq:gerbe-cotangent}
 \mathbb T^1_{\mathcal X^{\mathrm{EG,wild},p,\chi}_{GL_2}}
 \big|_{x_{\tau_{\mathfrak B}}}
 \xrightarrow{\sim} D_{\bar\rho_{\mathfrak B}},
\end{equation}
where \(D\) is the first-order quotient in
Lemma~\ref{v4-arith:w6-f:lem:representability}. Use its determinant-one
variant only when that is the chosen equivalence relation.
Require compatibility with the specified arithmetic classes.
The notation \(\mathbb T^1\) above denotes \(H^0\) of the tangent
complex. It does not identify this space with \(H^1\) of that complex.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{hypothesis}[the arithmetic block-to-Galois comparison]
\label{v4-arith:w6-f:prop:Paskunas-equals-Galois}
For the specified arithmetic block \(\mathfrak B\), coefficient
field, objects and determinant condition at \(p\in\{2,3\}\),
suppose there is a natural isomorphism from the relevant block
extension space to
\(D_{\bar\rho_{\mathfrak B}}\), carrying the selected extension classes
to the specified deformation classes. The coefficient convention
and this compatibility are part of the hypothesis.
\end{hypothesis}

The finite-algebra resolutions in
Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion} do not establish this
arithmetic isomorphism. One must construct the block functor and
verify its induced map on extensions in the stated coefficient
category. At three, a comparison with the radical-square-zero
three-vertex model also carries a nonzero adjacent Yoneda product
in degree two; it cannot be discarded by the algebra's path relation.

\subsection{Class-Match Theorem}

\begin{theorem}[F1.a.2 class-match, \(p\in\{2,3\}\)]
\label{v4-arith:w6-f:thm:class-match}
Assume the wild-EG datum of
Conjecture~\ref{v4-arith:deg-f1a:conj:EG-wild-enhancement},
Hypothesis~\ref{v4-arith:w6-f:prop:Paskunas-equals-Galois}, and
the natural Galois-to-tangent isomorphism of
Hypothesis~\ref{v4-arith:w6-f:prop:gerbe-cotangent-match},
including its class compatibility. Then the class-match
isomorphisms \(\mathsf{c}^{\mathrm{class}}_{2}\) and
\(\mathsf{c}^{\mathrm{class}}_{3}\) of
Definition~\ref{v4-arith:w6-f:def:class-match-p2} and
Definition~\ref{v4-arith:w6-f:def:class-match-p3} exist and are natural in the
block \(\mathfrak{B}\). The automorphic \(\mathrm{Ext}^{1}\)-classes
\(\alpha\) (at \(p=2\)) and \(\alpha_{01},\alpha_{12}\) (at \(p=3\)) are sent
to the tangent directions \(e_{1}\) (at \(p=2\)) and \(e_{1},e_{2}\)
(at \(p=3\)) respectively.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Compose the stipulated block-to-deformation map with the inverse
of the stipulated spectral-to-deformation map:
\[
 \operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathfrak B}
 \xrightarrow{\sim}D_{\bar\rho_{\mathfrak B}}
 \xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb T^1_{\mathcal X}|_{x_{\tau_{\mathfrak B}}}.
\]
Both maps are natural and preserve the specified class labels by
hypothesis. Their composite has the same properties.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[F1.a.2 full essential surjectivity at \(p=2,3\)]
\label{v4-arith:w6-f:cor:f1a2-surjectivity}
Under all hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w6-f:thm:class-match}, and the block-generation
and fully-faithfulness hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w5-e:thm:f1a2}, the block-diagonal
functor \(\Phi^{\mathrm{BD}}_{p,\chi}\) of
Definition~\ref{v4-arith:w5-e:def:block-diagonal} is essentially surjective
at \(p\in\{2,3\}\), hence an equivalence.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w5-e:thm:f1a2} establishes fully-faithfulness
without the wild-EG hypothesis and essential surjectivity on the
semisimple blocks.
On each non-semisimple block the only obstruction to essential
surjectivity was the class-level match, which is now
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w6-f:thm:class-match}. Combining gives full essential
surjectivity at \(p\in\{2,3\}\), conditional on all the stated class-comparison and generation
hypotheses.
\end{proof}

\section{R.B.1: Very-Special Ordinary-Block Multiplicity}
\label{v4-arith:w6-f:R-B-1}

\subsection{The Paskunas 2015 Residual}

Pa\v skunas 2015 Theorem~1.2 computes the block
centraliser structure of the ordinary block at \(p\geq 5\) in terms of
Breuil--Mezard multiplicities \(\mu(\sigma,\bar\rho)\), except on one
explicit sub-stratum: the \emph{very-special} locus where the residual
\(\bar\rho|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}\) is reducible with scalar
semisimplification and cyclotomic twist \(\bar\rho\sim\chi\oplus\chi\omega\)
for a specific character \(\chi\) (Pa\v skunas 2013
Remark~10.5).

\begin{hypothesis}[very-special multiplicity input]
\label{v4-arith:w6-f:prop:HuTan-verysp}
\emph{Conjectural statement.} For \(p\geq 5\) and the very-special
residual \(\bar\rho\sim\chi\oplus\chi\omega\), let
\(\mu_{\mathrm{vs}}(\sigma,\bar\rho)\) denote the Breuil--Mezard
multiplicity of the Serre weight \(\sigma\). The missing
very-special input is the finite list of integers
\(\{\mu_{\mathrm{vs}}(\sigma,\bar\rho)\}_{\sigma}\), in the numerical
normalisation of Pa\v skunas 2015, satisfying the Breuil--Mezard cycle
identity for the scalar residual deformation ring.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{remark}[multiplicity convention]
The hypothesis is not a formula for the very-special multiplicities.
It names the exact numerical input required by the Pa\v skunas
centraliser argument.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[R.B.1 conditional implication]
\label{v4-arith:w6-f:cor:R-B-1-closed}
\emph{Conditional on}
Hypothesis~\ref{v4-arith:w6-f:prop:HuTan-verysp}. If the datum
Hypothesis~\ref{v4-arith:w6-f:prop:HuTan-verysp} holds, then residual (R.B.1)
of Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:prop:remainder-AB} follows.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The datum supplies exactly the very-special Breuil--Mezard
multiplicities requested in (R.B.1). It does not compute all ordinary-block
extension groups. The actual local algebra calculation is
\(\operatorname{Ext}^1_A(k,k)=
\operatorname{Hom}_k(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2,k)\), proved by the
connecting map and pushout in
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:prop:Ext1-ordinary-controlled}.
There is no subtraction by the number of relations. Transferring a
specified deformation direction to an extension between distinct
arithmetic simples still requires the explicit class comparison in
that proposition; the multiplicity datum alone does not supply it.
\end{proof}

\section{R.B.2: Wild-Prime Block Centraliser Regularity}
\label{v4-arith:w6-f:R-B-2}

\subsection{\(p=2\) centraliser datum}

\begin{hypothesis}[\(p=2\) wild block-centraliser datum]
\label{v4-arith:w6-f:thm:PaskunasTung-p2}
\emph{Conjectural statement.} Let \(p=2\), \(\chi\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\times},k^{\times})\),
and \(\mathfrak{B}\) a Pa\v skunas block of
\(\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{sm},\chi}_{GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{2})}\) with projective
cover \(P_{\mathfrak{B}}\). The block centraliser algebra
\(A_{\mathfrak{B}}:=\mathrm{End}_{\mathfrak{B}}(P_{\mathfrak{B}})\) is a
complete Noetherian local \(W(k)\)-algebra of Krull dimension \(2\).
Depending on \(\mathfrak{B}\):
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Supersingular blocks.} \(A_{\mathfrak{B}}\) is a regular
local ring of dimension \(2\).
\item \emph{Twisted-Steinberg block.} \(A_{\mathfrak{B}}\) is a
Cohen--Macaulay complete Noetherian local ring of dimension \(2\),
which is a quotient of a regular local ring of dimension \(3\) by a
single non-zero-divisor.
\item \emph{Principal-series blocks.} \(A_{\mathfrak{B}}\) is a
Cohen--Macaulay complete Noetherian local ring of dimension \(2\).
Regularity requires a separate datum; the existence of an extension
class does not decide it. For example, the regular ring \(k[[t]]\)
has \(\operatorname{Ext}^1_{k[[t]]}(k,k)=k\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{remark}[centraliser convention]
This is the \(p=2\) centraliser hypothesis in the complete Noetherian
\(W(k)\)-algebra convention of Pa\v skunas blocks.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Colmez--Dospinescu--Paskunas at \(p=3\)}

\begin{hypothesis}[\(p=3\) wild block-centraliser datum]
\label{v4-arith:w6-f:thm:CDP-p3-centraliser}
\emph{Conjectural statement.} Let \(p=3\), \(\chi\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{\times},k^{\times})\),
and \(\mathfrak{B}\) a CDP block. The block centraliser
\(A_{\mathfrak{B}}\) is a complete Noetherian local \(W(k)\)-algebra
satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Supercuspidal blocks: \(A_{\mathfrak{B}}\) is a regular local
ring of dimension \(2\).
\item Principal-series non-Steinberg blocks: \(A_{\mathfrak{B}}\) is
a Cohen--Macaulay complete Noetherian local ring of dimension \(3\).
\item Steinberg / twisted-Steinberg principal block: \(A_{\mathfrak{B}}\)
is a Cohen--Macaulay complete Noetherian local ring of dimension \(3\),
a quotient of a regular local ring of dimension \(4\) by a non-zero-divisor.
\end{enumerate}
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{remark}[centraliser convention]
This is the \(p=3\) centraliser hypothesis in the complete Noetherian
\(W(k)\)-algebra convention of Colmez--Dospinescu--Pa\v skunas blocks.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Unified Wild-Prime Block Centraliser Theorem}

\begin{theorem}[R.B.2 conditional implication: wild-prime block centraliser regularity]
\label{v4-arith:w6-f:thm:R-B-2-unified}
\emph{Conditional on}
Hypothesis~\ref{v4-arith:w6-f:thm:PaskunasTung-p2} and
Hypothesis~\ref{v4-arith:w6-f:thm:CDP-p3-centraliser}. Let \(p\in\{2,3\}\),
\(\chi\), and \(\mathfrak{B}\)
a block of \(\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{sm},\chi}_{GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}\).
The block centraliser \(A_{\mathfrak{B}}\) is a complete Noetherian
local \(W(k)\)-algebra of:
\begin{itemize}
\item Krull dimension \(2\) at \(p=2\);
\item Krull dimension \(3\) at \(p=3\) for principal-series blocks, and
\(2\) for supercuspidal blocks.
\end{itemize}
It is Cohen--Macaulay, and regular on supersingular / supercuspidal
blocks. If \(A_{\mathfrak B}\) is additionally reduced, its
total quotient ring is a finite product of fields. Reducedness is
a separate hypothesis for the other blocks.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At \(p=2\) apply Hypothesis~\ref{v4-arith:w6-f:thm:PaskunasTung-p2}: all blocks
are Cohen--Macaulay of dimension \(2\); supersingular blocks are
regular; the twisted-Steinberg block is a hypersurface, and the
principal-series blocks are Cohen--Macaulay by the stated datum.
At \(p=3\), the second datum gives the stated dimensions and
Cohen--Macaulay property, and regularity for supercuspidal blocks.
Being a hypersurface does not imply generic regularity: the ring
\(k[[t,u,\epsilon]]/(\epsilon^2)\) remains nonreduced at its
minimal prime. If the centraliser is reduced, its localizations at
minimal primes are fields, and its total quotient ring is their
finite product. This is the additional assertion under reducedness.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[conditional \(\mathrm{Ext}^{1}\)-vanishing at wild primes]
\label{v4-arith:w6-f:cor:Ext1-wild-unconditional}
Let \(p\in\{2,3\}\), and assume the centraliser datum of
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w6-f:thm:R-B-2-unified}. Assume in addition that
\(A_{\mathfrak B}\) is reduced and that the specified arithmetic
generic-fibre category is equivalent, by an exact localization or
Morita comparison, to modules over its total quotient ring
\(Q(A_{\mathfrak B})\). Suppose this comparison identifies localized
extension groups for the finite modules under consideration. Then
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathfrak B}(S,S')
 \otimes_{A_{\mathfrak B}}Q(A_{\mathfrak B})=0.
\end{equation}
Thus the specified generic-fibre category is semisimple. This does
not assert vanishing at regular closed points.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
A reduced Noetherian ring has total quotient ring a finite product
of fields. Every short exact sequence of modules over that ring
splits. The stipulated comparison therefore identifies the displayed
localized extension group with zero, exactly as in
Corollary~\ref{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:cor:Ext1-wild-conditional}.
Neither the hypersurface presentation nor a dimension equality
constructs this arithmetic comparison. In contrast,
\(\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{k[[t]]}(k,k)=k\) at the regular closed point.
\end{proof}

\section{Consequences for F1.a.3}

The intended comparison passes from extensions in the chosen
arithmetic block to determinant-fixed Galois deformation classes and

Hypothesis~\ref{v4-arith:w6-f:prop:Paskunas-equals-Galois}.

functor and preserve the selected classes. Once both maps are natural
isomorphisms, their composition is natural. Neither equal dimensions
nor a block-centraliser theorem alone gives these maps.

deformation ring of the scalar residual
