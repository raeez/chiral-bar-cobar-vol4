\chapter{Zero counts, Gaussian tests and formal completion}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:chapter}

Three calculations fix distinct parts of the arithmetic comparison.
The Riemann--von Mangoldt formula retains its argument term:
\[
N(T)=\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi e}+\frac78+S(T)+O(1/T),
\qquad S(T)=O(\log T).
\]
The balanced Weil explicit formula is evaluated on the Gaussian family
\(f_{\lambda}(x)=e^{-\pi x^{2}/\lambda}\). Its ordinary convergent
terms give the tested functional. Positivity is an additional assertion.

Artin--Rees controls induced filtrations on finite modules over the
Iwasawa algebra. Comparisons for formal mapping stacks require
additional constructions. The positivity and linear-growth assertions in
\(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\) are not consequences of these three
calculations.

\section{Primary Sources}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:sources}

The zero count and explicit formula have their classical analytic
normalizations. The argument estimate is imported in the precise form
named below. The finite-module filtration theorem is proved by its
Rees module. Positivity and moment determinacy require separate inputs.

\begin{description}
\item[Riemann and von Mangoldt.] Riemann's 1859 memoir introduces the
zero-counting problem. Von Mangoldt's 1895 paper gives a complete proof
of the asymptotic formula. The normalization below retains the
horizontal argument variation.
\item[Hasanalizade--Shen--Wong.]
\emph{Counting zeros of the Riemann zeta function},
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.06506}{arXiv:2107.06506},
Theorem~1.3 and Corollary~1.4 give the argument bound used here.
\item[Selberg, Avh.~Norske Vid.~Akad.~Oslo (1944) 1--27.] Refined
mean-square estimates on \(S(T)\).
\item[Weil.] \emph{Sur les formules explicites de la th\'eorie des
nombres premiers}, Lund, volume suppl\'ementaire
(1952), 252--265. The complete signed functional below uses both polar
values, the gamma term and every prime power.
\item[Tate.] PhD thesis, Princeton 1950 (printed in Cassels--Fr\"ohlich,
\emph{Algebraic Number Theory}, 1967, Ch.~XV). Adelic
Mellin-transform treatment of \(\xi\); self-dual Gaussian under the
archimedean factor.
\item[Atiyah--Macdonald.] \emph{Introduction to Commutative Algebra}
(Addison-Wesley 1969), Chapter~10: filtrations and completions of
Noetherian modules.
\item[Bourbaki, \emph{Alg\`ebre Commutative} (Ch.~III \S3, Masson
1961).] Artin--Rees and Noetherian filtered
rings.
\end{description}

\section{Riemann--von Mangoldt Density of Zeros}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:rvm}

\subsection{Statement}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:rvm-statement}

\begin{theorem}[Riemann--von Mangoldt zero count]
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:thm:rvm}
Let \(N(T)\) count the nontrivial zeros
\(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) with \(0<\gamma\le T\), including multiplicity.
For \(T\ge2\) away from zero ordinates,
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:rvm}
 N(T)=\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi e}
                   +\frac78+S(T)+R(T),\qquad R(T)=O(T^{-1}).
\end{equation}
Here
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:ST-def}
 S(T)=\frac1\pi\Delta_{\,2\to2+iT\to1/2+iT}\arg\zeta,
\end{equation}
with argument starting at zero at \(s=2\). At a zero ordinate,
the convention \(\gamma\le T\) uses the right limit of \(S\).
The formula is unconditional.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Proof}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:rvm-proof}

\begin{proof}
The completed function is
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:xi-factorisation}
 \xi(s)=\tfrac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s).
\end{equation}
It is entire, has no real zeros, and obeys
\(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\) and
\(\xi(\bar s)=\overline{\xi(s)}\).
These properties and its zero strip are proved in
Section~\ref{v4-arith:weil:theta}.

For a height \(T\) away from zero ordinates, use the rectangle
\(R_T=\{-1\le\operatorname{Re}s\le2,\ 0\le\operatorname{Im}s\le T\}\).
The argument principle gives
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:contour}
 \frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{\partial R_T}\frac{\xi'(s)}{\xi(s)}\,ds=N(T).
\end{equation}
The logarithmic derivative satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:log-der-sym}
 \frac{\xi'(s)}{\xi(s)}+
                 \frac{\xi'(1-s)}{\xi(1-s)}=0.
\end{equation}
The real bottom edge has zero argument change.

Reflection about \(\operatorname{Re}s=1/2\), together with conjugation,
makes the remaining change twice that on the path
\(C_T:2\to2+iT\to1/2+iT\). Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:vert-sum}
 N(T)=\frac1\pi\Delta_{C_T}\arg\xi.
\end{equation}
The horizontal part of \(C_T\) is retained in this expression.

Logarithmic differentiation gives
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:log-xi-prime}
 \frac{\xi'}\xi(s)=\frac1s+\frac1{s-1}-\frac12\log\pi
             +\frac12\frac{\Gamma'}\Gamma(s/2)+\frac{\zeta'}\zeta(s).
\end{equation}
At the endpoint of \(C_T\), the continuous arguments of \(s\) and
\(s-1\) sum to \(\pi\). Thus
\[
 N(T)=1+\frac{\vartheta(T)}\pi+S(T),\qquad
 \vartheta(T)=\operatorname{Im}\log\Gamma(1/4+iT/2)-\frac T2\log\pi.
\]
The logarithm of gamma is analytic on the right half-plane and real on
the positive axis.

Stirling's expansion gives
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:stirling-gamma}
 \vartheta(T)=\frac T2\log\frac{T}{2\pi}-\frac T2
                     -\frac\pi8+\frac1{48T}+O(T^{-3}).
\end{equation}
Combining the two identities proves the assertion away from zero
ordinates. Right limits give the stated convention at those heights.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The constant \(7/8\)]
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:rem:78}
The factor \(s(s-1)\) contributes \(1\) to \(N(T)\).
For \(z=1/4+iT/2\), the imaginary part of
\((z-1/2)\log z-z\) has constant term \(-\pi/8\).
Dividing by \(\pi\) gives \(7/8=1-1/8\).
This is an asymptotic argument calculation at large height.
The positive real number \(\Gamma(1/4)\) has real logarithm and does
not contribute that imaginary constant.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The unconditional argument bound}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:ST-bound}

\begin{theorem}[The classical argument estimate]
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:thm:ST-bound}
The function in \eqref{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:ST-def}, with its stated
one-sided convention, satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:ST-bound}
 |S(T)|\le C\log T\qquad(T\ge2)
\end{equation}
for an absolute constant \(C\). In particular, the number
\(\mathcal N(T,1)\) of zeros with ordinates in \((T-1,T+1]\) obeys
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:jensen-count}
 \mathcal N(T,1)=O(\log T).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The argument estimate is the classical analytic theorem.
A precise primary bound is Hasanalizade--Shen--Wong,
\emph{Counting zeros of the Riemann zeta function},
Theorem~1.3 and Corollary~1.4,
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.06506}{arXiv:2107.06506}.
The estimate remains valid under one-sided limits.
Subtract \eqref{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:rvm} at \(T-1\) and \(T+1\).
The change of its smooth main term is \(O(\log T)\), and the two
argument terms have the same bound. This proves the local count.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[\(N(T)\) to main term, \(\log T\) precision]
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:cor:NT-sharp}
Combining \eqref{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:rvm} and
\eqref{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:ST-bound},
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:NT-main-error}
N(T)\;=\;\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\!\frac{T}{2\pi e}+O(\log T),
\end{equation}
unconditionally, using the functional equation, Stirling expansion
and the classical argument estimate.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Substitute the \(S(T)\) bound into \eqref{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:rvm} and
absorb the \(7/8\) and \(O(1/T)\) into the \(O(\log T)\) bound.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Consequence for \(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\)}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:R-analytic-closure}

\begin{proposition}[Finite positive-power sums of ordinates]
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:prop:R-analytic-density}
Let \(N(T)\) count the positive ordinates \(\gamma\) of nontrivial
zeros with multiplicity. For each fixed integer \(n\geq0\), put
\(S_n(T)=\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}(1/4+\gamma^2)^n\). Then
\begin{align*}
 S_n(T)={}&\frac{T^{2n+1}}{2\pi(2n+1)}\log\frac{T}{2\pi}
 -\frac{T^{2n+1}}{2\pi(2n+1)^2}
 +O_n(T^{2n}\log T).
\end{align*}
In particular every \(S_n(T)\) diverges. Under RH these are the
corresponding positive-power sums of the real zero parameters.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For \(n=0\) this is the Riemann--von Mangoldt formula. For
\(n\geq1\), Stieltjes integration by parts gives
\[
 S_n(T)=(1/4+T^2)^nN(T)
 -\int_0^T2nt(1/4+t^2)^{n-1}N(t)\,dt.
\]
Write \(N(t)=F(t)+O(\log(2+t))\), where
\(F(t)=t\log(t/(2\pi))/(2\pi)-t/(2\pi)\), and absorb a fixed
initial interval in the error. The endpoint and integrated errors
are \(O_n(T^{2n}\log T)\). The main term is
\((2\pi)^{-1}\int^T(1/4+t^2)^n\log(t/(2\pi))\,dt\).
Replacing the polynomial by \(t^{2n}\) costs
\(O_n(T^{2n-1}\log T)\). Integrating this last monomial gives
the two displayed terms. Its leading term is positive and divergent.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}[Finite measures and the regularization problem]
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:rem:density-closure-domain}
The finite positive measure
\[
 \nu_T=\frac12\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}
 \left(\delta_{\sqrt{1/4+\gamma^2}}+
       \delta_{-\sqrt{1/4+\gamma^2}}\right)
\]
has odd moments zero and even moments \(S_n(T)\). It is compactly
supported and determinate, but its mass tends to infinity.
Consequently the unregularized integral over all ordinates has no
finite moment saddle. To obtain the proposed arithmetic Hamburger
functional one must specify a common linear regularization and prove
its positivity and growth. If that regularization gives moments
\(M_{2k}\) of \(\gamma\), its shifted moments must satisfy
\[
 \mu_{2n}=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk4^{-(n-k)}M_{2k}.
\]
This compatibility is a consequence of linearity, not a construction
of the regularization or an estimate for its moments.
\end{remark}

\section{Weil Explicit Formula at Gaussian Test Functions}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:weil-gaussians}

\subsection{The Weil Explicit Formula: Recall}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:weil-recall}

The arithmetic expression and the sum over zeros use different variables.
Their relation fixes the scale at which the prime powers sample a test
function. The following conventions make this relation explicit.

\begin{lemma}[The normalized explicit formula]
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:lem:weil}

For $b>\pi$, let
\[
 \mathcal T_b=\left\{f\in C^\infty(\mathbb R;\mathbb C):
 \int_{\mathbb R}e^{b|x|}|f^{(j)}(x)|\,dx<\infty
 \text{ for every }j\geq0\right\},\qquad
 \mathcal T=\bigcup_{b>\pi}\mathcal T_b.
\]
Use Lebesgue measure and define
\[
 \widehat f(t)=\int_{\mathbb R}f(x)e^{-2\pi ixt}\,dx,\qquad
 f^\sharp(x)=\overline{f(-x)},\qquad
 (f\star h)(x)=\int_{\mathbb R}f(y)h(x-y)\,dy.
\]
These operations make $\mathcal T$ a complex convolution
$*$-algebra. For $f\in\mathcal T_b$, the transform is holomorphic
on $|\operatorname{Im}t|<b/(2\pi)$.
Put $\psi=\Gamma'/\Gamma$ and $z_\rho=-i(\rho-1/2)$,
with nontrivial zeta zeros counted with multiplicity. Define
\begin{align}
 \Phi_\infty(f)
 &=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}\widehat f(t)
 \left[\operatorname{Re}\psi(1/4+it/2)-\log\pi\right]dt,
 \label{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:phi-arch}\\
 \Phi_{\mathrm{fin}}(f)
 &=-\frac1{2\pi}\sum_p\sum_{k\geq1}
 \frac{\log p}{p^{k/2}}
 \left[f\left(\frac{k\log p}{2\pi}\right)
       +f\left(-\frac{k\log p}{2\pi}\right)\right].
 \label{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:phi-fin}
\end{align}
These expressions converge absolutely. The arithmetic functional is
\[
 L_{2\pi}(f)=\widehat f(i/2)+\widehat f(-i/2)
                  +\Phi_\infty(f)+\Phi_{\mathrm{fin}}(f).
\]
The explicit formula states
\[
 L_{2\pi}(f)=\sum_\rho\widehat f(z_\rho),
\]
with absolute convergence. Consequently the balanced expression is
\begin{equation}
 W(f):=L_{2\pi}(f)-\sum_\rho\widehat f(z_\rho)=0.
 \label{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:weil-def}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Set $g_f(u)=(2\pi)^{-1}f(-u/(2\pi))$ and $a=b/(2\pi)>1/2$.
Every derivative of $g_f$ is integrable with weight $e^{a|u|}$.
The change of variables $u=-2\pi x$ gives
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}g_f(u)e^{itu}\,du=\widehat f(t),\qquad
 g_{f\star h}=g_f\star g_h,\qquad
 g_{f^\sharp}(u)=\overline{g_f(-u)}.
\]
Weighted Young's inequality and differentiation under convolution
prove closure of $\mathcal T$ under these operations.
Weighted integrability of $f'$ gives
$|f(x)|\leq C e^{-b|x|}$ by integrating from the appropriate infinity.
Integration by parts gives rapid transform decay in every closed
strip $|\operatorname{Im}t|\leq\eta<b/(2\pi)$.
The zero count $N(T)=O(T\log(T+2))$ therefore gives absolute
convergence of the zero sum.
The prime sum is bounded by a constant times
$\sum_{n\geq2}(\log n)n^{-1/2-a}$, which converges.
The gamma integral converges because its bracket is
$O(\log(2+|t|))$.

Here is the contour calculation in the rescaled variable.
Write $g=g_f$ and
\[
 F_g(s)=\int_{\mathbb R}g(u)e^{(s-1/2)u}\,du,
 \quad \Gamma_{\mathbb R}(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2),
 \quad H(s)=\frac{d}{ds}\log\bigl(\Gamma_{\mathbb R}(s)\zeta(s)\bigr).
\]
Thus $F_g(s)=\widehat f(-i(s-1/2))$.
Choose $1<c<\min(a+1/2,3/2)$.
The functional equation gives $H(1-s)=-H(s)$.
The residues of $H$ are the multiplicities of the nontrivial zeros
and $-1$ at each of $0,1$.
Integrate $H(s)F_g(s)$ on the counterclockwise rectangle with
vertical sides $1-c,c$ and heights $\pm T$.
Choose heights tending to infinity away from the zero ordinates.
For example, the bound $O(T\log(T+2))$ permits a choice in each
$[T,T+1]$ at distance at least $c_0/(T\log(T+2))$ from every
nearby ordinate, for sufficiently small fixed $c_0>0$.
The logarithmic derivative expansion of the Hadamard product of
$\xi$ then gives a polynomial bound for $H$ on these horizontal
segments. Its nearby terms are bounded by the zero count times
the inverse distance. For distant zeros the paired product terms
are $O(T/|\rho|^2)$ and summable.
The rapid decay of $F_g$ eliminates both horizontal integrals.
Reflection of the left side gives
\[
 \sum_\rho F_g(\rho)-F_g(0)-F_g(1)
 =\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{\operatorname{Re}s=c}
 H(s)\bigl(F_g(s)+F_g(1-s)\bigr)\,ds.
\]
On this line,
$\zeta'/\zeta(s)=-\sum_{n\geq2}\Lambda_{\mathrm{vM}}(n)n^{-s}$,
where $\Lambda_{\mathrm{vM}}(p^k)=\log p$ and is zero otherwise.
Absolute convergence permits termwise integration.
Fourier inversion gives
\[
 \frac1{2\pi i}\int_{\operatorname{Re}s=c}n^{-s}F_g(s)\,ds
 =n^{-1/2}g(\log n).
\]
The reflected test gives $n^{-1/2}g(-\log n)$.
Since $g(\pm\log n)=(2\pi)^{-1}f(\mp\log n/(2\pi))$,
these are exactly the stated prime terms with their negative sign.
For the gamma term, move the integration line to $1/2$.
Its logarithmic derivative is holomorphic between these lines,
and rapid test decay eliminates the horizontal integrals.
Combining the two reflected terms uses
\[
 2\operatorname{Re}\frac{\Gamma_{\mathbb R}'}{\Gamma_{\mathbb R}}(1/2+it)
 =\operatorname{Re}\psi(1/4+it/2)-\log\pi.
\]
The two pole values are $\widehat f(i/2)$ and $\widehat f(-i/2)$.
This proves the formula, including its constants and signs.
Equivalently, if $L_\zeta$ denotes the arithmetic functional for
the transform $\int g(u)e^{itu}\,du$, the comparison is
$L_{2\pi}(f)=L_\zeta(g_f)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Gaussian Test Family}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:gauss-family}

\begin{definition}[Gaussian test family]
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:def:gaussian-family}
For \(\lambda>0\), set
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:f-lambda}
f_{\lambda}(x)\;:=\;e^{-\pi x^{2}/\lambda}.
\end{equation}
This is a Schwartz function; its Fourier transform is
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:f-lambda-hat}
\widehat{f}_{\lambda}(y)\;=\;\sqrt{\lambda}\,e^{-\pi\lambda y^{2}}
\end{equation}
by the classical Gaussian-self-duality identity: indeed, at
\(\lambda=1\), \(\widehat{f}_{1}(y)=f_{1}(y)\) and the general case
follows by scaling \(x\mapsto x/\sqrt{\lambda}\) and the Plancherel
change-of-variables \(\widehat{f(\cdot/a)}(y)=a\widehat{f}(ay)\).
The transform is again a Gaussian with full support.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Gaussian admissibility]
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:rem:gauss-admissibility}
Every derivative of $f_\lambda$ is a polynomial times a Gaussian.
Thus $f_\lambda\in\mathcal T_b$ for every $b>\pi$.
Its Fourier transform has full support, as permitted by
Lemma~\ref{v4-arith:w5-i:lem:weil}.
All terms are ordinary convergent integrals or sums.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Closed-Form Evaluation of \(W(f_{\lambda})\)}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:weil-gauss-closed-form}

We evaluate each term of \(W(f_{\lambda})\) in closed form.

\begin{lemma}[pole and zero terms for Gaussians]
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:lem:pole-zero}
For the Gaussian \(f_{\lambda}\) of
Definition~\ref{v4-arith:w5-i:def:gaussian-family},
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:pole-zero}
\widehat{f}_{\lambda}(i/2)+\widehat{f}_{\lambda}(-i/2)\;=\;2\sqrt{\lambda}e^{\pi\lambda/4}.
\end{equation}
Moreover
\[
\sum_{\rho}\widehat{f}_{\lambda}(z_{\rho})
=\sqrt{\lambda}\sum_{\rho}e^{-\pi\lambda z_{\rho}^{2}},
\qquad z_{\rho}=-i(\rho-\tfrac12),
\]
and this zero sum is absolutely convergent.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:f-lambda-hat},
\(\widehat{f}_{\lambda}(\pm i/2)=\sqrt{\lambda}e^{\pi\lambda/4}\).
For \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) in the critical strip,
\(|e^{-\pi\lambda z_{\rho}^{2}}|
\leq e^{\pi\lambda/4}e^{-\pi\lambda\gamma^{2}}\). The
Riemann--von Mangoldt bound \(N(T)=O(T\log T)\) gives absolute
convergence.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The prime contribution on Gaussians]
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:lem:prime-side}

For $\lambda>0$,
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_{\mathrm{fin}}(f_\lambda)
 =-\frac1\pi\sum_p\sum_{k\geq1}\frac{\log p}{p^{k/2}}
       \exp\left(-\frac{(k\log p)^2}{4\pi\lambda}\right).
 \label{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:phi-fin-eval}
\end{equation}
The sum is absolutely convergent and smooth in $\lambda>0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Insert the even Gaussian into \eqref{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:phi-fin}.
On any compact interval $[\lambda_0,\lambda_1]\subset(0,\infty)$,
each parameter derivative is bounded by a polynomial in $\log n$
times $n^{-1/2}\exp[-(\log n)^2/(4\pi\lambda_1)]$.
This is summable over integers $n\geq2$.
For every fixed $M$, the exponential is at most $n^{-M}$
for all sufficiently large $n$.
Uniform domination proves convergence and differentiation term by term.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The archimedean contribution on Gaussians]
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:lem:arch}

For $\lambda>0$,
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_\infty(f_\lambda)=-\frac{\log\pi}{2\pi}+A_\Gamma(\lambda),
 \label{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:phi-arch-eval}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 A_\Gamma(\lambda)=\frac{\sqrt\lambda}{2\pi}
 \int_{\mathbb R}e^{-\pi\lambda t^2}
                \operatorname{Re}\psi(1/4+it/2)\,dt.
 \label{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:A-gamma}
\end{equation}
This ordinary integral is absolutely convergent.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Substitute $\widehat f_\lambda(t)=\sqrt\lambda e^{-\pi\lambda t^2}$
into \eqref{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:phi-arch}.
The Gaussian integral gives
$\int_{\mathbb R}\widehat f_\lambda(t)\,dt=f_\lambda(0)=1$.
The digamma factor is smooth on the real integration line and
is $O(\log(2+|t|))$ at infinity.
Gaussian decay gives absolute convergence.
The same bound with polynomial factors proves smooth parameter
variation on compact subintervals of $(0,\infty)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The Weil formula on Gaussians]
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:thm:weil-gauss-closed}

For $\lambda>0$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 W(f_\lambda)={}&2\sqrt\lambda e^{\pi\lambda/4}-\mathcal Z(\lambda)
 -\frac{\log\pi}{2\pi}\\
 &+A_\Gamma(\lambda)-\frac1\pi\mathcal P(\lambda)=0,
\end{split}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:weil-gauss-closed}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align}
 \mathcal Z(\lambda)
 &=\sqrt\lambda\sum_\rho e^{-\pi\lambda z_\rho^2},\qquad
 z_\rho=-i(\rho-1/2),\label{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:Z-lambda}\\
 \mathcal P(\lambda)
 &=\sum_p\sum_{k\geq1}\frac{\log p}{p^{k/2}}
       \exp\left(-\frac{(k\log p)^2}{4\pi\lambda}\right).
 \label{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:P-lambda}
\end{align}
The integral $A_\Gamma$ is defined in \eqref{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:A-gamma}.
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal Z(\lambda)=2\sqrt\lambda e^{\pi\lambda/4}
 -\frac{\log\pi}{2\pi}+A_\Gamma(\lambda)-\frac1\pi\mathcal P(\lambda).
 \label{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:weil-id-gauss}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Substitute Lemma~\ref{v4-arith:w5-i:lem:pole-zero},
Lemma~\ref{v4-arith:w5-i:lem:prime-side}, and
Lemma~\ref{v4-arith:w5-i:lem:arch} into
Lemma~\ref{v4-arith:w5-i:lem:weil}.
Gaussian admissibility was proved in
Remark~\ref{v4-arith:w5-i:rem:gauss-admissibility}.
Rearranging the balanced formula gives
\eqref{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:weil-id-gauss}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Poisson summation embedding]
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:rem:poisson}
The self-duality \(\widehat{f}_{1}=f_{1}\) of the standard Gaussian
at \(\lambda=1\) is the archimedean input to Tate's thesis. Poisson
summation for the standard Gaussian gives
\[
\theta(i\tau)\;:=\;\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\pi n^{2}\tau}\;=\;\tau^{-1/2}\theta(i/\tau),
\]
the classical modular transformation of the Jacobi theta. The
identity \eqref{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:weil-id-gauss} can be viewed as the
arithmetic Weil analogue: the zero sum \(\mathcal{Z}(\lambda)\) is the
arithmetic theta analogue, and the RHS is the corresponding
archimedean-plus-prime expansion.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Gaussian Convolutions and the Balanced Weil Functional}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:positivity-test}

The functional $W$ in \eqref{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:weil-def} is the
balanced difference, and it vanishes by the explicit formula.
The associated sesquilinear form is instead
\[
 B_{2\pi}(f,h)=L_{2\pi}(h^\sharp\star f),\qquad f,h\in\mathcal T.
\]
The change of variables $f\mapsto g_f$ preserves convolution and
involution, so this is the same form as
$L_\zeta(g_h^*\star g_f)$ in the logarithmic variable.
Its diagonal values involve the arithmetic functional $L_{2\pi}$,
rather than the identically zero balanced difference $W$.
Positivity on the full test algebra remains the Weil positivity
question. The Gaussian convolution identity below does not settle it.

\begin{definition}[Gaussian convolution sub-class]
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:def:gauss-conv-class}
The \emph{Gaussian convolution sub-class}
is the set
\[
\mathcal{G}\;:=\;\{f_{\lambda}\star f_{\mu}^{\sharp}\,:\,\lambda,\mu>0\}.
\]
Since \(f_{\lambda}\) is real and even, \(f_{\mu}^{\sharp}=f_{\mu}\), so
\(\mathcal{G}=\{f_{\lambda}\star f_{\mu}\,:\,\lambda,\mu>0\}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Gaussian convolution is Gaussian]
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:lem:gauss-conv}
For \(\lambda,\mu>0\),
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:gauss-conv}
f_{\lambda}\star f_{\mu}(x)\;=\;\sqrt{\frac{\lambda\mu}{\lambda+\mu}}\cdot f_{\lambda+\mu}(x).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, in Fourier:
\(\widehat{f_{\lambda}\star f_{\mu}}=\widehat{f}_{\lambda}\cdot\widehat{f}_{\mu}=\sqrt{\lambda\mu}\,e^{-\pi(\lambda+\mu)y^{2}}=\sqrt{\lambda\mu/({\lambda+\mu})}\cdot\sqrt{\lambda+\mu}\,e^{-\pi(\lambda+\mu)y^{2}}\),
consistent with \(\widehat{f_{\lambda}\star f_{\mu}}=\sqrt{\lambda\mu/(\lambda+\mu)}\cdot\widehat{f}_{\lambda+\mu}\). In particular,
\(f_{\lambda}\star f_{\lambda}(x)=\sqrt{\lambda/2}\cdot f_{2\lambda}(x)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\begin{align*}
f_{\lambda}\star f_{\mu}(x)
&\;=\;\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\pi t^{2}/\lambda}e^{-\pi(x-t)^{2}/\mu}\,dt.
\end{align*}
Completing the square in \(t\):
\begin{align*}
\frac{t^{2}}{\lambda}+\frac{(x-t)^{2}}{\mu}
&\;=\;\frac{\mu t^{2}+\lambda(x-t)^{2}}{\lambda\mu}\\
&\;=\;\frac{(\lambda+\mu)t^{2}-2\lambda t x+\lambda x^{2}}{\lambda\mu}\\
&\;=\;\frac{(\lambda+\mu)}{\lambda\mu}\!\left(t-\frac{\lambda x}{\lambda+\mu}\right)^{2}+\frac{x^{2}}{\lambda+\mu}.
\end{align*}
Hence
\begin{align*}
f_{\lambda}\star f_{\mu}(x)
&\;=\;e^{-\pi x^{2}/(\lambda+\mu)}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\pi(\lambda+\mu)u^{2}/(\lambda\mu)}\,du\\
&\;=\;e^{-\pi x^{2}/(\lambda+\mu)}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{\lambda\mu}{\lambda+\mu}}
\;=\;\sqrt{\frac{\lambda\mu}{\lambda+\mu}}\cdot f_{\lambda+\mu}(x),
\end{align*}
as claimed.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[balanced Weil identity on Gaussian convolutions]
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:prop:gauss-pos}
For every \(\lambda,\mu>0\),
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:gauss-pos}
W(f_{\lambda}\star f_{\mu})\;=\;\sqrt{\frac{\lambda\mu}{\lambda+\mu}}\cdot W(f_{\lambda+\mu})\;=\;0.
\end{equation}
This is an explicit-formula identity, not a proof of Weil positivity on
the Gaussian convolution sub-class \(\mathcal{G}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{v4-arith:w5-i:lem:gauss-conv} expresses
\(f_{\lambda}\star f_{\mu}\) as a scalar multiple of
\(f_{\lambda+\mu}\). The Weil functional \(W\) is linear, so
\[
W(f_{\lambda}\star f_{\mu})\;=\;\sqrt{\lambda\mu/(\lambda+\mu)}\cdot W(f_{\lambda+\mu}).
\]
By Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w5-i:thm:weil-gauss-closed} applied to
\(\lambda+\mu\) in place of \(\lambda\), and by the classical Weil
explicit-formula identity \(W(f_{\lambda+\mu})=0\), the result is zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The Gaussian pairing]
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:rem:gauss-pos-domain}
The Gaussian functions are real and even, so
\begin{equation*}
 B_{2\pi}(f_\lambda,f_\mu)
 =\sqrt{\frac{\lambda\mu}{\lambda+\mu}}\,
       L_{2\pi}(f_{\lambda+\mu}).
\end{equation*}
Thus \eqref{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:weil-id-gauss} evaluates this pairing
by ordinary convergent arithmetic terms.
The equality $W(f_\lambda\star f_\mu)=0$ expresses the balanced
explicit formula and supplies no sign inequality for $B_{2\pi}$.
Positivity on the full test algebra remains a separate assertion.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Gaussian identities and operator traces]
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:rem:H-closure}
The proposed arithmetic trace comparison in
Corollary~\ref{v4-arith:hpar-det:cor:wave3-structure} requires an operator realization,
a common domain and a trace or weight.
The identity \eqref{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:weil-id-gauss} computes its
arithmetic scalar on the Gaussian family.
It supplies no operator trace comparison and no positivity statement
for $B_{2\pi}$ on the full test algebra.
\end{remark}

\section{Finite modules and formal comparisons}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:formal-noetherian}

The arithmetic-loop comparisons in
Propositions~\ref{v4-arith:bzn-sub:prop:R1-formal-b-domain}
and~\ref{v4-arith:bzn-sub:prop:R1-formal-c-domain}
involve mapping objects, filtered colimits and formal completion.
Artin--Rees gives a precise theorem for a finite module and its
submodule. To apply it to those comparisons, one must construct the
modules and prove that they control the required maps.

\subsection{The Iwasawa coefficient ring}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:iwasawa}

\begin{definition}[The Iwasawa algebra]
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:def:iwasawa}
Choose \(\Gamma\cong\mathbb Z_p\) and a topological generator
\(\gamma\). Its completed group ring is
\[
 \Lambda=\mathbb Z_p[[\Gamma]]
  =\varprojlim_{r,n}(\mathbb Z/p^r)[\Gamma/p^n\Gamma]
  \cong\mathbb Z_p[[T]],\qquad \gamma\longmapsto1+T.
\]
The coefficient variable \(T\) is different from the zero-counting
height used earlier in this chapter.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Noetherian local structure]
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:lem:iwasawa-noeth}
The ring \(\Lambda\) is regular local and Noetherian of dimension two,
with maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m=(p,T)\) and residue field
\(\mathbb F_p\). Its \(\mathfrak m\)-adic topology is the topology
of the displayed finite group-ring quotients.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The formal power-series ring over the discrete valuation ring
\(\mathbb Z_p\) is Noetherian. Its maximal ideal is generated by
\(p,T\), and the chain \((0)\subset(p)\subset(p,T)\), together with
the power-series dimension theorem, gives dimension two.
The two generators form a regular system of parameters.

The finite group-ring quotient has defining ideal
\(J_{r,n}=(p^r,(1+T)^{p^n}-1)\).
For a fixed \(k\), take \(r\ge k\) and \(n\) sufficiently large.
For \(1\le j<k\),
\[
 v_p\binom{p^n}{j}\ge n-v_p(j),
\]
by \(\binom{p^n}{j}=p^n\binom{p^n-1}{j-1}/j\).
Every term of \((1+T)^{p^n}-1\) then lies in \(\mathfrak m^k\).
Thus \(J_{r,n}\subset\mathfrak m^k\).

Conversely, modulo \(J_{r,n}\), one has \(p^r=0\) and
\(T^{p^n}\in(p)\). Hence \((p,T)\) is nilpotent in that finite
quotient, and some \(\mathfrak m^k\) lies in \(J_{r,n}\).
The two ideal systems are cofinal, proving the topology and
completed-ring identification.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Induced filtrations on finite modules}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:artin-rees}

\begin{lemma}[Artin--Rees]
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:lem:artin-rees}
Let \(R\) be a Noetherian ring, \(\mathfrak a\subset R\) an ideal,
\(M\) a finite \(R\)-module, and \(N\subset M\) a submodule.
There is \(k\ge0\) such that, for all \(n\ge k\),
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:artin-rees}
 \mathfrak a^nM\cap N
       =\mathfrak a^{n-k}(\mathfrak a^kM\cap N).
\end{equation}
In particular, the induced filtration on \(N\) is cofinal with
its \(\mathfrak a\)-adic filtration.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The Rees algebra \(\mathcal R=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}\mathfrak a^nt^n\)
is generated over \(R\) by finitely many elements of degree one,
so it is Noetherian. The Rees module
\(\bigoplus_{n\ge0}\mathfrak a^nMt^n\) is finite over \(\mathcal R\).
Its graded submodule
\[
 \bigoplus_{n\ge0}(\mathfrak a^nM\cap N)t^n
\]
is therefore finitely generated. Choose homogeneous generators of
degrees at most \(k\).

In degree \(n\ge k\), a generator of degree
\(j\le k\) is multiplied by \(\mathfrak a^{n-j}\).
Factor this as \(\mathfrak a^{n-k}\mathfrak a^{k-j}\).
The second factor sends that generator into
\(\mathfrak a^kM\cap N\), proving one inclusion in the formula.
The reverse inclusion follows from \(N\) being a submodule.

Finally,
\(\mathfrak a^nN\subset\mathfrak a^nM\cap N
\subset\mathfrak a^{n-k}N\), which gives cofinality.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Arithmetic loops and filtered colimits}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:R1-formal-b-closure}

\begin{question}[The specified formal presentation]
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:prop:b2-closure}
For a derived formal arithmetic stack with presentation
\(\mathcal X=\operatorname{colim}_i\mathcal X_i\), determine the
coefficient, topology and transition hypotheses under which
\[
 \operatorname{colim}_i
       \mathrm{Map}(B\widehat{\mathbb Z},\mathcal X_i)
 \longrightarrow
       \mathrm{Map}(B\widehat{\mathbb Z},\mathcal X)
\]
is an equivalence. The question includes the
\(\mathfrak m\)-adically complete sector and its finite-type pieces.
\end{question}

A profinite presentation of \(\widehat{\mathbb Z}\) uses all finite
cyclic quotients with their divisibility transition maps.
The \(p\)-power quotients instead describe \(\mathbb Z_p\).
Finite cardinality of a group does not make its classifying space
compact in every category of targets. Nor does an inverse limit of
compact objects automatically remain compact.

\begin{question}[The mapping functor on a target category]
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:cor:b1-closure}
For which category \(\mathcal C\) of complete derived arithmetic
stacks does the internal functor
\(\mathrm{Map}(B\widehat{\mathbb Z},-)\) preserve the filtered
colimits under consideration?
\end{question}

Ordinary compactness of \(K\) in an infinity category means that the
space-valued functor \(\operatorname{Map}(K,-)\) preserves filtered
colimits. Preservation by an internal mapping object is a separate
assertion, to be checked after evaluation on all test objects.
Artin--Rees alone proves neither statement.

\begin{proposition}[A conditional arithmetic-loop comparison]
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:thm:R1-formal-b-closes}
Fix the category of targets and an internal arithmetic-loop functor
\(L^{\mathrm{Ar}}\mathcal Y=
\mathrm{Map}(B\widehat{\mathbb Z},\mathcal Y)\).
If this functor preserves a specified presentation
\(\mathcal X=\operatorname{colim}_i\mathcal X_i\), then
\[
 L^{\mathrm{Ar}}\mathcal X
       \simeq\operatorname{colim}_iL^{\mathrm{Ar}}\mathcal X_i.
\]
No preservation hypothesis is supplied merely by adic completeness
of the target.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The assumed comparison is exactly the displayed map after substituting
the definition of \(L^{\mathrm{Ar}}\).
\end{proof}

The order of completion and filtered colimit is a concrete issue even
for modules. Let \(R=\mathbb Z_p[[T]]\), \(\mathfrak m=(p,T)\), and
\(M_i=\bigoplus_{j=1}^iR\). Each \(M_i\) is adically complete, but
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{colim}_i\varprojlim_n M_i/\mathfrak m^nM_i
  =\bigoplus_{j\ge1}R
  \quad\subsetneq\quad
 \varprojlim_n\operatorname{colim}_i M_i/\mathfrak m^nM_i.
 \label{v4-arith:weil:eq:completion-counterexample}
\end{equation}

The right side is the space of sequences \((a_j)\in\prod_jR\) such
that, for every \(n\), all but finitely many \(a_j\) lie in
\(\mathfrak m^n\). Indeed, reduction modulo each power has finite
support, and compatibility recovers each coordinate by completeness
of \(R\). The sequence \(a_j=p^j\) belongs to this right side and
not to the direct sum.

Every fixed quotient has a finite support bound,
but no one bound works at every precision. This example isolates a
limit exchange that a formal-stack argument must justify. It is not
an obstruction to every possible arithmetic-loop comparison.

\subsection{The flat subcategory}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:R1-formal-c-closure}

\begin{question}[Flat objects in a formal colimit]
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:prop:c2-closure}
Construct the proposed flat object or full subcategory on
\(L^{\mathrm{Ar}}\mathcal X\), with its transition maps, and determine
when the comparison
\[
 \operatorname{colim}_i(L^{\mathrm{Ar}}\mathcal X_i)^{\mathrm{flat}}
       \longrightarrow(L^{\mathrm{Ar}}\mathcal X)^{\mathrm{flat}}
\]
is an equivalence. Specify whether the two sides are stacks,
categories of sheaves or categories of modules before forming the map.
\end{question}

A flat subcategory is not a submodule of a coordinate ring merely by
being full. Applying Artin--Rees requires an actual finite module
\(M\) and submodule \(N\), together with a theorem identifying their
induced filtrations with the proposed geometric condition.
A criterion using all adic truncations also requires its own
completeness and flatness hypotheses.

\begin{remark}[Two further geometric comparisons]
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:rem:c1-c3-open}
The extension of nilpotent singular support to the specified formal
stacks and its comparison with a Fargues--Scholze
\((\varphi,\Gamma)\)-module locus are additional geometric questions.
Neither is a consequence of the finite-module filtration theorem.
Their coefficient categories and functors must be specified separately.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The finite-module consequence}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:formal-conclusion}

\begin{proposition}[Adic completions of finite submodules]
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:thm:formal-summary}
For a finite \(\Lambda\)-module \(M\) and submodule \(N\), the
intrinsic \(\mathfrak m\)-adic completion of \(N\) agrees with its
completion for the induced filtration \(N\cap\mathfrak m^nM\).
The arithmetic-loop and flat-subcategory comparisons above remain
separate assertions. The categorical trace statement of
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:bzn-sub:thm:R1-formal-a-closes} retains its stated
categorical colimit hypothesis.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Artin--Rees makes the two filtrations cofinal, so their inverse-limit
completions are canonically isomorphic. The lemma concerns finite
modules and provides no mapping-stack or categorical colimit identity.
\end{proof}

\section{Consequences}
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:summary}

\begin{proposition}[three exact inputs]
\label{v4-arith:w5-i:prop:three-exact-inputs}

The following assertions hold.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The sharp Riemann--von Mangoldt identity
\eqref{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:rvm} with \(|S(T)|=O(\log T)\) is
unconditional
(Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w5-i:thm:rvm},
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w5-i:thm:ST-bound},
Corollary~\ref{v4-arith:w5-i:cor:NT-sharp}). This proves the density estimate
entering \(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{analytic}}\), but not the positivity or
linear-growth assertions.
\item The Gaussian specialization of the balanced Weil explicit formula
uses the ordinary convergent terms in
\eqref{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:weil-gauss-closed} and
\eqref{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:weil-id-gauss}. The convolution
identity \eqref{v4-arith:w5-i:eq:gauss-pos} is only the balanced
explicit formula and is not a Weil-positivity statement
(Remark~\ref{v4-arith:w5-i:rem:gauss-pos-domain}).
\item Artin--Rees identifies the intrinsic and induced adic
completions of a finite Iwasawa submodule
(Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:w5-i:thm:formal-summary}).
The arithmetic-loop and flat-subcategory maps require their
separate preservation and geometric hypotheses.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion is Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w5-i:thm:rvm} together with
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w5-i:thm:ST-bound}. The second assertion is
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w5-i:thm:weil-gauss-closed} and
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:w5-i:prop:gauss-pos}. The third assertion is
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:w5-i:thm:formal-summary}.
None of these assertions proves Weil positivity, a Hilbert--P\'olya
realization or the proposed formal arithmetic comparisons.
\end{proof}
