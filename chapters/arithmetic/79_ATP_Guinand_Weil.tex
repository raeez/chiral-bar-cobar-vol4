\chapter{A--TP through the Guinand--Burnol Co--Poisson Alternative}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:chapter}


The Weil distribution has additive and multiplicative descriptions,
related by the logarithmic coordinate. Its signed archimedean term
is a distribution, and its full positivity criterion retains both
polar evaluations and the prime term. The moment condition and
its finite-dimensional complement can be analyzed before a
co-Poisson or automorphic realization is constructed.

\section{The full pairing and logarithmic transport}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:orientation}
For $f\in\mathscr A=C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$, put
$f^*(t)=\overline{f(-t)}$ and $h_f(z)=\int f(t)e^{izt}dt$.
The arithmetic positivity condition is
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:eq:ATP-recall}
 W_\infty(f*f^*)\le P(f*f^*)-W_{\mathrm{fin}}(f*f^*),
\end{equation}
with the signed conventions of
\Cref{v4-arith:w5-a:thm:weil-explicit}.
Its difference is $q_W(f,f)=W(f*f^*)$.
On the full compact-test space this is equivalent to RH.
A restricted Petersson realization requires a specified map and
equality with this entire form. Rankin--Selberg coefficient
multiplication does not give that identity.
Indeed \Cref{v4-arith:w5-a:thm:prime-side} gives a negative
finite-prime square on compact tests.

\subsection{The multiplicative moment question}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:domain}
The substitution $x=e^t$ transports compact additive tests to
compact multiplicative tests with Haar measure $dx/x$.
It gives a convergent mean for a transported autocorrelation.
The additive expression evaluated directly against $dy/y$ instead
diverges at zero for a nonzero test. The mean-zero kernel below
has an explicit projection. Extending positivity from that kernel
requires its finite complement and the cross pairings with it.
A proposed co-Poisson identification must supply an actual transform
and the complete signed pairing on these carriers.

For this Sonine result use the cyclic Fourier convention
$\mathcal F_{2\pi}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{2\pi ixy}f(y)dy$.
On even $L^2(\mathbb R)$, normalized by
$\|f\|^2=\int_0^\infty|f(t)|^2dt$, put
\[
 K_\lambda=\{f:f=\mathcal F_{2\pi}f=0
                   \text{ on }(-\lambda,\lambda)\},\qquad\lambda>0.
\]
This is a closed subspace: it is the intersection of the kernels
of two bounded restriction maps. Thus it has an orthogonal
projection; Burnol's Theorem~4 computes it using the compressed
cosine transform. For $f\in K_\lambda$, the integral
$\widehat f(s)=\int_0^\infty f(t)t^{-s}dt$ converges for $\Re s>1/2$
by Cauchy--Schwarz. Its completed continuation
$\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\widehat f(s)$ is entire.
Burnol's Theorem~8 identifies their Hilbert space isometrically
with $\mathcal B(E_\lambda)$, using the critical line
$\Re s=1/2$ and measure $|ds|/(2\pi)$ in the Hardy-space definition.
This supplies a specific Fourier--Mellin Hilbert space.
A comparison with the complete Weil pairing in this chapter still
requires a map from the chosen compact-test domain to $K_\lambda$
and a proof of the identity of forms, including prime and polar terms.

Secondary: Riesz 1928 on the \(M(x)\) function for the asymptotic
co-Poisson comparison; Cotlar 1955 on the Hilbert transform almost
everywhere convergence; Cartier--Voros 1990 for the distributional
extension of Guinand's formula to Schwartz test functions; Meyer R.
2005 on distributional Selberg moments.

\section{The Guinand Alternative Explicit Formula}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:guinand-explicit}

The compact explicit formula admits a spectral-density notation
in which the gamma term occurs with a plus sign. Its relation to
the signed Weil density is a sign reversal, not a change in the
arithmetic functional.

\subsection{The completed function and Fourier tests}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:guinand:contour}
Let $\xi(s)=\tfrac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)$.
Its logarithmic derivative has a pole at each zero $\rho$ with
residue its multiplicity. It has no pole at $0$ or $1$, since
$\xi(0)=\xi(1)=1/2$.
By contrast, $\Lambda_\zeta(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)$
has simple poles there, and its logarithmic derivative has residue
$-1$. The identity
\[
 \frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)=\frac1s+\frac1{s-1}-\frac{\log\pi}{2}
                +\frac12\psi(s/2)+\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)
\]
holds meromorphically, with cancellations at $0,1$.

\begin{definition}[Fourier-pair conventions]
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:def:fourier-pair}
For an even $v\in\mathscr A$, use the pair
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:eq:g-check}
 h(t)=\int_{\mathbb R}v(u)e^{itu}du,\qquad
 v(u)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}h(t)e^{-itu}dt.
\end{equation}
The function $h$ is entire. Evenness alone does not imply reality;
when $v$ is real and even, $h$ is real and even on the real axis.
The letter $v$ distinguishes this inverse transform from the
auxiliary digamma density $g$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[the compact explicit formula in spectral-density form]
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:thm:guinand-explicit}
For the pair $(h,v)$ above and $z_\rho=-i(\rho-1/2)$,
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:eq:guinand}
 \sum_\rho h(z_\rho)=h(i/2)+h(-i/2)
 -2\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}v(\log n)
 +\int_{\mathbb R}h(t)\Phi_\infty^G(t)dt,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:eq:guinand-arch}
 \Phi_\infty^G(t)=\frac{\Re\psi(1/4+it/2)-\log\pi}{2\pi}.
\end{equation}
Zeros are counted with multiplicity. The zero sum is absolutely
convergent, and the prime sum is finite.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply \Cref{v4-arith:w5-a:thm:weil-explicit} to $v$.
Evenness combines the two prime evaluations. Its signed gamma
density is $-\Phi_\infty^G$, which gives the plus sign above.
This establishes the formula on the exact compact Fourier carrier.
A contour proof would use the test $h(-i(s-1/2))$, count the
actual poles of the chosen logarithmic derivative, and use the
Dirichlet series for $-\zeta'/\zeta$ only in $\Re s>1$ before
continuation. No direct critical-line Dirichlet-series expansion
is required here. Guinand~1948 and Patterson~1988, Ch.~3, treat
related explicit-formula conventions.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Signed and auxiliary gamma conventions}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:guinand:sign-convention}
\begin{proposition}[the exact convention dictionary]
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:prop:weil-vs-guinand}
With $\Phi_\infty^W(t)=k(t)/(2\pi)$ and
$\Phi_\infty^{\mathrm{aux}}(t)=g(t)/(2\pi)$, where
$k=\log\pi-\Re\psi(1/4+it/2)$ and $g=2\log\pi-k$,
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:eq:weil-guinand-dictionary}
 \Phi_\infty^G=-\Phi_\infty^W
 =\Phi_\infty^{\mathrm{aux}}-\frac{\log\pi}{\pi}.
\end{equation}
The constant difference pairs with $h$ as $-2\log\pi\,v(0)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The density identities follow by substitution. Fourier inversion
at zero gives $\int h(t)dt=2\pi v(0)$.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}[the constant term on convolution squares]
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:rem:constant-shift}
For $v=f*f^*$, the constant difference is
$-2\log\pi\|f\|_2^2$. It is not a multiple of $\int f$, and
it does not vanish merely because $f$ has mean zero.
Changing from the auxiliary density to the signed gamma density
therefore requires the complete norm correction. The full explicit
formula is unchanged only when this correction and its sign remain.
\end{remark}

\section{The Burnol Co--Poisson Transform}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:burnol-copoisson}

Ordinary Poisson summation exchanges a Schwartz function with
its Fourier transform. A reciprocal change of variables is a
different operation. The following calculation fixes that distinction
before posing a co-Poisson realization of the Weil form.

\subsection{Jacobi Theta and Co--Poisson}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:burnol:theta}

\begin{definition}[Jacobi theta function]
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:def:theta}
\ClaimStatusDefinitional. Let
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:eq:theta}
\theta(x)\;:=\;\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\pi n^{2}x}
\;=\;1+2\sum_{n\geq 1}e^{-\pi n^{2}x},\qquad x>0,
\end{equation}
with functional equation
\(x^{1/2}\theta(x)=\theta(1/x)\) (Jacobi 1828; modular \(S\)-transform).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[the mean-subtracted dilation sum]
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:def:copoisson}
Let $\varphi\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R)$ be real and even, with
$\varphi(0)=0$ and $\int_0^\infty\varphi=0$.
Use the cyclic Fourier transform
$\widehat\varphi_2(\xi)=\int\varphi(t)e^{-2\pi it\xi}dt$.
Set
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:eq:copoisson}
 (\mathcal C\varphi)(y)=\sum_{n\ge1}\varphi(ny)
                 -\frac1y\int_0^\infty\varphi(t)dt,\qquad y>0.
\end{equation}
The second term vanishes under the stated mean condition, and the
series converges absolutely for every $y>0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Poisson symmetry of the dilation sum]
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:thm:burnol-copoisson}
The cyclic transform preserves the stated even Schwartz class with
zero value and zero mean. On this class,
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:eq:copoisson-fe}
 (\mathcal C\varphi)(y)=y^{-1}
              (\mathcal C\widehat\varphi_2)(1/y).
\end{equation}
Both sides decrease faster than every power at zero and infinity.
Writing $M_+\varphi(s)=\int_0^\infty\varphi(t)t^{s-1}dt$, one has
\[
 M_+(\mathcal C\varphi)(s)=\zeta(s)M_+\varphi(s)
 \quad(0<\Re s<1),
\]
where the product is continued from $\Re s>1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The values $\widehat\varphi_2(0)=\int\varphi=0$ and
$\int\widehat\varphi_2=\varphi(0)=0$ follow from Fourier inversion.
Poisson summation gives
$\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\varphi(ny)
=y^{-1}\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\widehat\varphi_2(n/y)$.
Remove the zero terms and use evenness to obtain the identity.
Schwartz decay bounds each dilation sum by $C_N y^{-N}$ for
$y\ge1$. The identity gives arbitrary-power decay at zero.
For $\Re s>1$, absolute convergence permits termwise Mellin
integration and the change of variables $t=ny$, giving
$\zeta(s)M_+\varphi(s)$.
Because $\varphi(t)=O(t^2)$ at zero, $M_+\varphi$ is holomorphic
on $\Re s>-2$. Its value at one vanishes, cancelling the pole
of $\zeta$ there. Analytic continuation gives the asserted strip.
The Mellin transform of $\mathcal C\varphi$ itself is entire by
its two-sided rapid decay.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[reciprocal pullback and the native comparison]
For $J\varphi(t)=t^{-1}\varphi(1/t)$ on the positive line,
$M_+(J\varphi)(1-s)=M_+\varphi(s)$ wherever the integrals converge.
It is not a substitute for $\widehat\varphi_2$ in the Poisson identity.
Choose a nonzero smooth test supported away from zero with
$\int_0^\infty\varphi(t)dt=\int_0^\infty\varphi(t)dt/t=0$,
and extend it evenly. Such a test exists by solving two homogeneous
linear equations on three disjoint compact bumps. Then both
$\varphi$ and the even extension of $J\varphi$ belong to the
stated zero-mean class. If their reciprocal dilation-sum identity held, Mellin
continuation would give
$(\zeta(s)-\zeta(1-s))M_+\varphi(s)=0$.
The meromorphic factor is not identically zero, so Mellin uniqueness
would force $\varphi=0$, a contradiction.
Burnol's Sonine and co-Poisson spaces concern additional specified
Fourier-compatible constructions. Identifying a realization in one
of those spaces with the full signed Weil pairing remains a separate
map and pairing problem; the elementary Poisson identity alone
provides no zero-spectrum decomposition or positivity theorem.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The archimedean distribution in multiplicative coordinates}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:burnol:dual-kernel}

Let $Lf(x)=f(\log x)$ for $x>0$. The transported archimedean
functional is $T(\Phi)=W_\infty(L^{-1}\Phi)$ on
$C_c^\infty(\mathbb R_{>0})$ with its usual compact-test topology.
It has an explicit convergent subtraction formula.

\begin{proposition}[the exact transported gamma distribution]
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:prop:dual-kernel}
For $\Phi=Lf$, one has
\begin{align}
 T(\Phi)&=W_\infty(f)
 =\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}h_f(u)
     (\log\pi-\Re\psi(1/4+iu/2))du,
 \label{v4-arith:w7-i:eq:guinand-arch-recall}\\
 T(\Phi)&=(\log\pi+\gamma)\Phi(1)
 +\int_1^\infty
 \frac{x^{-1/2}[\Phi(x)+\Phi(x^{-1})]-2x^{-2}\Phi(1)}
      {1-x^{-2}}\frac{dx}{x}.
 \label{v4-arith:w7-i:eq:dual-kernel}
\end{align}
Here $\gamma$ is Euler's constant. Both expressions converge.
Away from $x=1$, its density relative to $dx/x$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:eq:tilde-g}
 K(x)=\frac{e^{-|\log x|/2}}{1-e^{-2|\log x|}}>0.
\end{equation}
At $x=1$ this density requires the subtraction in the preceding
formula and is not locally integrable by itself.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use the digamma identity already applied in
\Cref{v4-arith:wc:sign-test}:
$\psi(z)+\gamma=\int_0^\infty(e^{-v}-e^{-zv})/(1-e^{-v})\,dv$.
First truncate the $v$ integral away from zero and infinity.
Fourier inversion gives $f(0)$ from the constant term and
$[f(v/2)+f(-v/2)]/2$ from $\cos(uv/2)$.
Put $v=2t$. The result is
\[
 W_\infty(f)=(\log\pi+\gamma)f(0)
 +\int_0^\infty
 \frac{e^{-t/2}[f(t)+f(-t)]-2e^{-2t}f(0)}{1-e^{-2t}}dt.
\]
The numerator is $O(t)$ at zero and the denominator is asymptotic
to $2t$. At infinity the compact terms vanish and the remaining
term decays exponentially. Thus the integral converges.
The truncated digamma integrands, after integration, are bounded
by a constant times $1+\log(2+|u|)$, uniformly in the truncations:
near $v=0$, use $1-\cos(uv/2)\le\min(2,u^2v^2/8)$.
Rapid decay of $h_f$ permits dominated convergence.
The substitution $x=e^t$ gives the displayed formula.
For tests vanishing near one, the subtraction vanishes and the
change $x\mapsto x^{-1}$ gives the stated density on both sides.
Its asymptotic $K(x)\sim1/(2|\log x|)$ proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[support and sign]
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:rem:sign-tilde-g}
The off-diagonal density is positive on both $(0,1)$ and
$(1,\infty)$. The subtraction at one is part of the distribution.
There is no sign change at $1/\pi$ in this exact density.
Positivity of the off-diagonal density does not imply positivity
of its quadratic pullback, because the subtraction must remain.
\end{remark}
\section{Co--Poisson Diagnostic and Exact Restatement}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:copoisson-subclass}


The archimedean term on multiplicative tests is the distribution
$T$ above. Its application to a transported autocorrelation
retains the subtraction at one. Hilbert-transform parity gives a
separate elementary identity, but identifying a Hilbert-transform
pairing with $T$ requires an operator comparison.
\subsection{Hilbert Transform on Even Functions}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:copoisson:hilbert}

\begin{definition}[Hilbert transform]
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:def:hilbert}
\ClaimStatusDefinitional. For \(\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\), the Hilbert
transform \(H\phi\) is the Cauchy principal value
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:eq:hilbert}
(H\phi)(t)\;=\;\frac{1}{\pi}\,\mathrm{p.v.}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\phi(s)}{t-s}\,ds.
\end{equation}
The operator \(H\colon L^{2}\to L^{2}\) is isometric with
\(H^{2}=-\mathrm{id}\), and the pointwise convergence of the principal
value holds almost everywhere (Cotlar 1955).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Hilbert symmetry on even functions]
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:lem:hilbert-even}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere. For \(\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\) even, \(H\phi\)
is odd. For \(\phi\) odd, \(H\phi\) is even. The pairing
\(\langle\phi, H\chi\rangle\) vanishes whenever \(\phi\) and \(\chi\)
share the same parity.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
From \eqref{v4-arith:w7-i:eq:hilbert}, substitute \(s\mapsto-s\),
\(t\mapsto-t\): if \(\phi(-s)=\phi(s)\) then
\((H\phi)(-t)=\frac{1}{\pi}\,\mathrm{p.v.}\int\frac{\phi(s)}{-t-s}\,ds\)
\(=-\frac{1}{\pi}\,\mathrm{p.v.}\int\frac{\phi(s)}{t+s}\,ds
=-\frac{1}{\pi}\,\mathrm{p.v.}\int\frac{\phi(-u)}{t-u}\,du=-(H\phi)(t)\),
with \(u=-s\) and evenness. So \(H\phi\) is odd. The parity-orthogonality
follows from \(\int(\text{odd})\cdot(\text{even})=0\).
\end{proof}


\begin{proposition}[the full inequality after logarithmic transport]
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:prop:guinand-as-hilbert}
For $f\in\mathscr A$, put $\varphi=f*f^*$ and
$\Phi(x)=\varphi(\log x)$. Then archimedean Tate positivity for
this test is exactly
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:eq:guinand-hilbert}
 T(\Phi)\le P(\varphi)-W_{\mathrm{fin}}(\varphi).
\end{equation}
It is equivalent to $q_W(f,f)\ge0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By definition $T(\Phi)=W_\infty(\varphi)$.
The signed explicit formula gives
$W(\varphi)=P(\varphi)-W_{\mathrm{fin}}(\varphi)-T(\Phi)$.
Rearranging proves the assertion. No additional zero term is
added to the right-hand side of the inequality.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The moment condition in logarithmic coordinates}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:copoisson:subclass}

An additive autocorrelation cannot have a vanishing unregularized
$dy/y$ mean near its additive origin. This determines the necessary
change of variables before a multiplicative moment is imposed.
For $f\in\mathscr A$, put $\varphi=f*f^*$. Then
$\varphi(0)=\|f\|_2^2$. If $f\ne0$, continuity gives
$\Re\varphi(y)\ge\|f\|_2^2/2$ for sufficiently small positive $y$.
Consequently $\int_0^\infty\varphi(y)dy/y$ diverges at zero.

Define the logarithmic transport
\[
 (Lf)(x)=f(\log x),\qquad x>0.
\]
It is an isomorphism from $C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$ to
$C_c^\infty(\mathbb R_{>0})$. On the latter space use multiplicative
Haar measure $dx/x$, convolution
$(F*_\times G)(x)=\int_0^\infty F(y)G(x/y)dy/y$, and
$F^{*_{\times}}(x)=\overline{F(1/x)}$.
Changing variables $y=e^t$ gives
\[
 L(f*g)=Lf*_\times Lg,\qquad L(f^*)=(Lf)^{*_{\times}}.
\]
Thus the multiplicative autocorrelation is
$\Phi(x)=\varphi(\log x)$ and
\[
 \int_0^\infty\Phi(x)\frac{dx}{x}
 =\int_{\mathbb R}\varphi(t)dt=\left|\int_{\mathbb R}f(t)dt\right|^2.
\]
It is compactly supported away from zero, so the integral converges.

\begin{definition}[the mean-zero moment space]
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:def:copoisson-class}
Set $K_0=\{f\in\mathscr A:\int f=0\}$.
For real even tests, use its real even part $K_0^{\mathbb R,\mathrm{ev}}$.
The multiplicative mean of $L(f*f^*)$ vanishes exactly on $K_0$.
Give these spaces the compact-test topology. Write
\[
 \mathcal C_0=\{f\in K_0:q_W(f,f)\ge0\},
 \qquad q_W(f,g)=W(f*g^*).
\]
Membership in $\mathcal C_0$ is a quadratic inequality in addition
to the linear moment condition. No identification with a Sonine
space or a co-Poisson image is included in this definition.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[nonzero moment tests and their projection]
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:rem:CP-nonempty}
Fix a compact smooth $\eta$ of integral one. The map
$\Pi_0 f=f-(\int f)\eta$ is a continuous projection onto $K_0$,
which is closed of complex codimension one.
Choosing $\eta$ real and even restricts this projection to real
even tests. This restricted kernel contains nonzero functions.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The integral is continuous on compact tests and has value one on
$\eta$. Therefore $\Pi_0^2=\Pi_0$ and its image is exactly $K_0$.
For a nonzero real even compact smooth $b$, the derivative $b''$
is real and even and has integral zero. It is nonzero, since a
compactly supported affine function must vanish.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[closed positivity on the moment space]
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:thm:copoisson-closure}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:prop:BM-rule-out}
The set $\mathcal C_0$ is closed in $K_0$.
It is dense there if and only if $q_W(f,f)\ge0$ for every $f\in K_0$.
If $S\subset\mathscr A$ is dense, then $\Pi_0S$ is dense in $K_0$.
To deduce positivity from a dense linear span, the form must be
nonnegative on every finite linear combination from that span.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Convolution and $W$ are continuous on compact tests, so
$f\mapsto q_W(f,f)$ is a continuous real-valued function.
Its inverse image of $[0,\infty)$ is closed. A closed dense subset
of $K_0$ equals $K_0$, proving the equivalence.
Continuity and surjectivity of $\Pi_0$ prove the density assertion.
For two generators, positivity of their individual diagonals gives
no bound on the cross term $2\Re q_W(f,g)$ of their sum.
This proves the last distinction.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The finite complement and the full form}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:copoisson:explicit}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:burnol-residual}

Every compact test has the exact decomposition
$f=u+c\eta$ with $u=\Pi_0f\in K_0$ and $c=\int f$.
Thus
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:eq:CP3}
 q_W(f,f)=q_W(u,u)+2\Re\bigl(\overline c\,q_W(u,\eta)\bigr)
                         +|c|^2q_W(\eta,\eta).
\end{equation}
The missing part of a full positivity proof is determined by this
one-dimensional complement and its cross pairings.

\begin{theorem}[extension from the mean-zero kernel]
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:thm:burnol-reduction}
Assume $q_W(u,u)\ge0$ for all $u\in K_0$.
Then full Weil positivity is equivalent to
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:eq:burnol-reduction}
 q_W(\eta,\eta)\ge0,\qquad
 |q_W(u,\eta)|^2\le q_W(u,u)q_W(\eta,\eta)
 \quad(u\in K_0).
\end{equation}
In particular, if $q_W(\eta,\eta)=0$, the required condition is
$q_W(u,\eta)=0$ for every $u\in K_0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Full positivity implies these inequalities by Cauchy--Schwarz.
Conversely the cross bound and the preceding decomposition give
\[
 q_W(u+c\eta,u+c\eta)
 \ge\left(\sqrt{q_W(u,u)}-|c|\sqrt{q_W(\eta,\eta)}\right)^2\ge0.
\]
All compact tests have this decomposition.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[a sufficient norm bound]
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:cor:hilbert-sufficient}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:eq:hilbert-sufficient}
Under positivity on $K_0$, suppose the functional
$u\mapsto q_W(u,\eta)$ is bounded for the seminorm
$q_W(u,u)^{1/2}$ with squared bound at most $q_W(\eta,\eta)$,
and that $q_W(\eta,\eta)\ge0$.
Then full Weil positivity holds and hence RH holds.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The bound is exactly the cross inequality in the preceding theorem.
Apply the compact Weil criterion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[what Hilbert-transform isometry supplies]
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:prop:hilbert-rule-out}
The Hilbert transform on $L^2(\mathbb R)$ is skew-adjoint.
Thus $\Re\langle v,Hv\rangle=0$. Its norm isometry supplies no
identity with the cross pairing $q_W(u,\eta)$ above.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The Fourier multiplier of the Hilbert transform is purely
imaginary, of modulus one almost everywhere, and changes sign
under complex conjugation. Hence $H^*=-H$ and $H^*H=1$.
Taking the real part of its quadratic pairing gives zero.
The cross pairing instead uses the full Weil distribution.
Equating the two would require an additional operator identity.
\end{proof}

\section{The multiplicative comparison problem}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:consequences}
\begin{theorem}[moment transport and the positivity boundary]
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:thm:wave7-form}
The logarithmic map $L$ transports additive convolution and involution
to multiplicative convolution and involution. The multiplicative
mean-zero autocorrelations correspond exactly to $K_0$.
This kernel has the explicit continuous projection $\Pi_0$.
Its nonnegative Weil locus is closed. Positivity on $K_0$ extends
to all compact tests precisely under the complement inequalities
in \Cref{v4-arith:w7-i:thm:burnol-reduction}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The change-of-variables calculations, projection construction,
closedness argument, and quadratic decomposition above prove each assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the proposed co-Poisson realization]
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:rem:orthogonal-HFD}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:rem:orthogonal-obstruction}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:rem:honest-form}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:rem:compare-w6}
\label{v4-arith:w7-i:rem:beilinson}
A co-Poisson realization must specify a space, a transform, its
measure and domain, and an identity with the full signed Weil form.
The exact transport $L$ gives the requisite multiplicative test
algebra. It does not turn Fourier transformation into reciprocal
pullback, nor produce an ordinary time-domain kernel from a
spectral logarithmic derivative. Such a kernel requires a proved
distributional transform and a prescription at its singularities.
In particular the asserted split at $1/\pi$ cannot be used without
an exact kernel and sign proof. A bound on one auxiliary spectral
form also supplies no cross inequality for $q_W$.
The nonzero tests $b''$ establish that the linear moment kernel is
nonempty. Their full Weil positivity requires a separate proof.
Likewise density of a proposed co-Poisson image must be proved in
a topology controlling the complete Weil form. The explicit
projection and complement criterion state exactly what such a
density and positivity argument would have to establish.
\end{remark}
