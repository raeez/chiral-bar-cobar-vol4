\chapter{F1.a.1 Unramified Ideal, F1.a.2 Small-Prime Block Extension, and \(\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{ACD}}\) Cuspidal Descent}
\label{v4-arith:w5-e:chapter}

The local ordinary, small-prime block, and global cuspidal comparisons
have different carriers. Fixed-determinant Frobenius parameters and
spherical or Iwahori invariant vectors provide explicit local data.
An enhanced compact-generator criterion explains what additional maps
would make a categorical comparison an equivalence. The ordinary
essential image, the small-prime block functors and global cuspidal
descent remain separate arithmetic construction problems. Their
Eisenstein extension also requires normalized intertwiners and global
spectral synthesis.

\section{F1.a.1: The Unramified Ordinary Ideal}
\label{v4-arith:w5-e:f1a1}

\subsection{Hypotheses and Target}

\begin{setup}[Taylor--Wiles unramified setup]
\label{v4-arith:w5-e:setup:TW}
Fix a prime \(p\geq 5\), a level \(N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}\) with
\(\gcd(N,p)=1\), and a residual Galois representation
\[
\bar\rho\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to GL_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
\]
unramified at $p$ and at almost all $\ell\nmid Np$. Descend its
coefficients to a finite field $k$. Fix a full local central character
$\chi:\mathbb Q_p^\times\to k^\times$ using local reciprocity that
sends a uniformizer to geometric Frobenius. In the determinant-identified
DEG convention, its corresponding Galois character satisfies
$\det\bar\rho|_{G_{\mathbb Q_p}}=\chi\bar\varepsilon^{-1}$,
where $\bar\varepsilon$ is the residual cyclotomic character.
Thus both its inertia values and its value on a uniformizer are fixed.
A characteristic-zero comparison also chooses compatible lifts of the
central character and determinant, as in the construction of
Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:w6-e:chapter}. Assume:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textbf{(TW1)}] $p\ge5$.
\item[\textbf{(TW2)}] $\bar\rho|_{G_{\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)}}$ is
absolutely irreducible.
\item[\textbf{(TW3)}] The local residual representation at $p$ has
distinct characters in its semisimplification after the chosen
coefficient extension. This is local distinguishedness, not a claim
that the global representation has diagonal characters.
\item[\textbf{(TW4)}] Restrict to the intended ordinary component
$\mathrm{Block}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{\bar\rho|_{G_{\mathbb Q_p}}}$,
where the local representation has an unramified invariant line.
This line convention must be compared explicitly with the unramified
quotient convention of Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:w6-e:chapter}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{setup}

The central character in Setup~\ref{v4-arith:w5-e:setup:TW} is
ramified on units. Indeed, the residual representation is unramified
at $p$, so its determinant is trivial on inertia, whereas the geometric
reciprocity convention gives
$\bar\varepsilon(u)=\bar u$ for $u\in\mathbb Z_p^\times$ and
$\bar\varepsilon(p)=1$. Thus $\chi(u)=\bar u$. This is the convention of
Dotto--Emerton--Gee, Section~2.1.1, p.~9. A principal series induced
from two unramified characters has trivial central character on units
and cannot supply an object of this fixed-$\chi$ category.

\begin{proposition}[central-character-compatible smooth source objects]
\label{v4-arith:w5-e:local:ramified-source}
Fix a finite extension of $\mathbb Q_p$ with integer ring $\mathcal O$
and residue field $k$, a continuous lift
$\zeta:\mathbb Q_p^\times\to\mathcal O^\times$ of $\chi$, and the
compatible determinant $\delta=\zeta\varepsilon^{-1}$ under geometric
reciprocity, where $\varepsilon$ is the $p$-adic cyclotomic character. Let $A$ be a finite local Artinian $\mathcal O$-algebra
with residue field $k$. Write $\zeta_A$ for the induced character,
and choose an unramified character $\eta_A:\mathbb Q_p^\times\to A^\times$.
Put $G=GL_2(\mathbb Q_p)$ and let $B$ be its upper triangular subgroup.
The unnormalized smooth induction
\[
 I_A=\operatorname{Ind}_B^G(\zeta_A\eta_A^{-1}\otimes\eta_A)
\]
consists of locally constant $f:G\to A$ satisfying
\[
 f\left(\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&d\end{pmatrix}g\right)
 =\zeta_A(a)\eta_A(a)^{-1}\eta_A(d)f(g),
 \qquad (g_0f)(g)=f(gg_0).
\]
It is a nonzero smooth $p$-power-torsion $\mathcal O$-representation
with central character $\zeta$. It has no $GL_2(\mathbb Z_p)$-fixed
vectors. In particular, no square-root modulus factor is used over $A$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The finite coefficient characters have open kernels. Choose a principal
congruence subgroup $K_m\subset GL_2(\mathbb Z_p)$ so that the inducing
character $\theta_A$ of $B$ is trivial on $B\cap K_m$.
Define $f(bk)=\theta_A(b)$ on $BK_m$ and zero outside. Two
factorizations differ by $B\cap K_m$, so this is well-defined.
The image of $K_m$ in the Hausdorff space $B\backslash G$ is compact
and open, hence closed as well. The resulting zero extension is
locally constant and $f(1)=1$.
More generally, restriction to the compact group $GL_2(\mathbb Z_p)$
shows that every locally constant induced function is fixed on the
right by some sufficiently small open subgroup: a finite clopen
partition refines its local constancy neighbourhoods. This proves
smoothness. The ring $A$ is killed by a power of $p$, so the same is
true of $I_A$. A central scalar $x$ acts by
$\zeta_A(x)\eta_A(x)^{-1}\eta_A(x)=\zeta_A(x)$.
Choose a unit $u$ whose reduction is not $1$. The element
$\zeta_A(u)-1$ is a unit of $A$. If a vector were fixed by
$GL_2(\mathbb Z_p)$, it would be annihilated by this unit, hence vanish.
\end{proof}

A determinant twist cannot turn an unramified principal series into
this residual central-character family when $p$ is odd. On the cyclic
group $\mathbb F_p^\times$, such a twist changes the central exponent
by $2a$; the required exponent is $1$. The congruence
$2a=1\pmod{p-1}$ has no solution. This remains an obstruction after
coefficient extension and, by reduction, for the finite rings $A$.
The characteristic-zero unramified invariant-vector calculation below
therefore has a different central-character carrier.

\begin{definition}[data for the ordinary comparison subcategory]
\label{v4-arith:w5-e:def:unr-ord}
The representations $I_A$ are actual objects of the smooth
$p$-power-torsion category $\mathcal A_\zeta$ of
Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:w6-e:chapter}. Their stable colimit closure
inside $D(\mathcal A_\zeta)$ is a defined source subcategory, but is
not asserted to be the ordinary essential-image category.
The intended notation
$\mathrm D^{\mathrm{sph\text{-}fin,unr\text{-}ord}}
([GL_2(\mathbb Q_p)])_\chi$ requires the coefficient comparison with
$D(\mathcal A_\zeta)$, a choice of ordinary source objects, and a
proof of the required Hecke-finiteness or admissibility condition.
On the spectral side, construct the intended formal neighbourhood
$\mathcal X^{\mathrm{EG},p,\chi,\mathrm{unr\text{-}ord}}_{GL_2}$
with its determinant, ordinary flag and support condition. Identify
which compatible induced objects, ordinary subquotients or Hida-family
objects map to that support, and prove their generator property.
The local Frobenius and invariant-vector calculations do not by
themselves make these arithmetic identifications.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Statement}
The ordinary comparison requires stable presentable enhanced source and
target subcategories, a proof that the actual DEG functor restricts
between them, and a colimit-preserving restricted functor. The notation
$\mathrm{sph\text{-}fin}$ and $\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}$ here
names those intended categories; the framed local rings alone do not
construct them. The essential-image hypothesis below means that the
images of a specified generating family generate this target.


\begin{theorem}[F1.a.1 unramified ordinary ideal]
\label{v4-arith:w5-e:thm:f1a1-ordinary}
\ClaimStatusProvedHereConditional Under Setup~\ref{v4-arith:w5-e:setup:TW}
and the DEG essential-image theorem on the unramified ordinary
Emerton--Gee component, the
restriction of the DEG functor of
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:deg-f1a:thm:deg-fixed-chi}
\[
\Phi^{\mathrm{DEG}}_{p,\chi}\bigr|_{\mathrm{unr\text{-}ord}}\colon
\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{sph\text{-}fin,unr\text{-}ord}}([GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})])_{\chi}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{EG},p,\chi,\mathrm{unr\text{-}ord}}_{GL_{2}})
\]
is an equivalence. The Taylor--Wiles, Breuil--Mezard, Hida and

construct; the essential-image hypothesis is additional and is not
proved by the eigenvalue calculation below.
\end{theorem}

\section{Fixed Determinant, Frobenius Eigenvalues, and Invariant Vectors}

Fixing a determinant imposes a relation between the two Frobenius
roots. Their eigenlines also retain automorphisms. The resulting
parameter stack and the invariant vectors of a principal series give
explicit local constructions for a categorical comparison.

\subsection{The Frobenius parameter stack}

Let $E$ be a field of characteristic zero and let $d\in E^\times$.
For an $E$-algebra $A$, consider a rank-two projective $A$-module $V$
with an automorphism $F$ satisfying
$\bigwedge^2F=d\,\operatorname{id}_{\bigwedge^2V}$.
Isomorphisms intertwine $F$. No trivialization of $\bigwedge^2V$ is
part of the data. This defines the quotient stack
\[
 \mathcal U_d=[Y_d/GL_2],\qquad
 Y_d=\operatorname{Spec}
 E[x_{11},x_{12},x_{21},x_{22}]/(x_{11}x_{22}-x_{12}x_{21}-d),
\]
where $GL_2$ acts by conjugation. It is the algebraic parameter stack
for an unramified rank-two Weil representation with chosen generator
acting by $F$.

Put $t=\operatorname{tr}F$. The regular semisimple open substack
$\mathcal U_d^{\mathrm{rs}}$ is defined by $t^2-4d\ne0$.
The ordered eigenvalue scheme and diagonal torus are
\[
 T_d=\operatorname{Spec}E[a,a^{-1},(a^2-d)^{-1}],\qquad
 T=\mathbb G_m^2.
\]
The involution $w$ sends $a$ to $d/a$ and exchanges the factors of $T$.

\begin{proposition}[Eigenvalues and eigenline automorphisms]
\label{v4-arith:w5-e:local:frobenius-stack}
There is an equivalence
\[
 \mathcal U_d^{\mathrm{rs}}\simeq[T_d/(T\rtimes\langle w\rangle)],
\]
where $T$ acts trivially on $T_d$. The scheme of unordered eigenvalues is
\[
 U_d=\operatorname{Spec}E[t,(t^2-4d)^{-1}].
\]
It has dimension one. Over an algebraically closed field, each
geometric object of $\mathcal U_d^{\mathrm{rs}}$ has automorphism group
$\mathbb G_m^2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
After a finite \textrm{\'etale} base change, the polynomial
$X^2-tX+d$ has distinct roots $a,b$, with $b=d/a$.
The endomorphisms
\[
 e_a=\frac{F-b}{a-b},\qquad e_b=\frac{F-a}{b-a}
\]
are complementary idempotents. Their images are rank-one projective
modules, and $F$ acts on them by $a$ and $b$.
Conversely, two line bundles with these scalar actions give $(V,F)$.
Changing their frames gives the $T$-action. Exchanging their order
gives the involution. Descent gives the stated quotient stack.

The map $a\mapsto t=a+d/a$ identifies the unordered roots.
Its coordinate extension satisfies $a^2-ta+d=0$.
Inverting $t^2-4d$ makes this a finite \textrm{\'etale} double cover
with the stated involution. The invariant ring is
$E[t,(t^2-4d)^{-1}]$. Each automorphism preserves the two eigenspaces
and scales them independently.
\end{proof}

The atlas $Y_d$ has dimension three, since the determinant equation
has nonzero differential on invertible matrices.
Thus $\mathcal U_d$ has dimension $3-4=-1$ as an algebraic stack.
Its eigenvalue scheme has a different dimension and omits stabilizers.
If a trivialization of $\bigwedge^2V$ is required, the quotient uses
$SL_2$ instead. Its regular semisimple stabilizer is $\mathbb G_m$.
These are distinct moduli conventions.

\subsection{An unramified deformation ring}

Let $\mathcal O$ be a complete discrete valuation ring of mixed
characteristic, with finite residue field $k$ of characteristic $p$.
Fix a nonarchimedean local field $L$. Write $G_L$ for its absolute
Galois group and $I_L$ for inertia, so $G_L/I_L\simeq\widehat{\mathbb Z}$.
Fix $\overline F\in GL_2(k)$ and $d\in\mathcal O^\times$ lifting
$\det\overline F$. A test algebra is a local Artinian
$\mathcal O$-algebra $A$ with residue field $k$.
An unramified framed deformation sends arithmetic Frobenius to
$F_A\in GL_2(A)$, with $F_A\bmod\mathfrak m_A=\overline F$
and $\det F_A=d$.
Here arithmetic Frobenius acts on a residue field by its cardinality
power. Inertia acts trivially.

\begin{proposition}[Framed deformations and distinct residual roots]
\label{v4-arith:w5-e:local:framed-unramified}
Choose a matrix lift $F_0\in M_2(\mathcal O)$ of $\overline F$.
The framed deformation functor is represented by
\[
 R_d^\square=
 \mathcal O[[X_{11},X_{12},X_{21},X_{22}]]/
       (\det(F_0+X)-d).
\]
This ring is formally smooth of relative dimension three.
Suppose additionally that
$\overline F=\operatorname{diag}(\bar a,\bar b)$, with
$\bar a\ne\bar b$. Choose $a_0\in\mathcal O^\times$ lifting $\bar a$
and put $b_0=d/a_0$. Then
\[
 R_d^\square\simeq\mathcal O[[x,y,z]],\qquad
 F=\begin{pmatrix}
 a_0+x&y\\ z&(d+yz)/(a_0+x)
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Strict conjugacy classes of these deformations are represented by
$\mathcal O[[u]]$. The corresponding ordered eigenvalues are
$a=a_0+u$ and $b=d/a$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every test algebra is finite. A matrix in its finite group $GL_2(A)$
defines a unique continuous homomorphism from $\widehat{\mathbb Z}$.
Thus specifying $F_A$ is equivalent to specifying the unramified
representation. The matrix entries and determinant equation give the
first ring.

At least one cofactor of $\overline F$ is nonzero. The derivative in
the corresponding matrix coordinate is a unit. Solving for that
coordinate proves formal smoothness and leaves three parameters.
In the displayed diagonal case, $a_0+x$ is a unit and the determinant
equation solves directly for the lower-right entry.

For each $F_A$, Hensel lifting gives the unique characteristic root
$a$ reducing to $\bar a$. The other root is $b=d/a$.
Since $a-b$ is a unit, the projectors above split $A^2$ into two
free rank-one eigenspaces. Their bases can be chosen to reduce to the
standard residual bases. The resulting change of basis is the identity
modulo $\mathfrak m_A$, so it is a strict conjugation.
Two diagonal lifts are strictly conjugate exactly when their ordered
roots agree. The choice of $a-a_0\in\mathfrak m_A$ therefore represents
the set of strict conjugacy classes.
\end{proof}

The last ring represents classes, not the deformation groupoid.
For a diagonal lift, strict automorphisms contain
$(1+\mathfrak m_A)^2$. A quotient stack retains these automorphisms.
The construction concerns unramified lifts only. A crystalline lifting
condition or a larger Emerton--Gee component requires its own map and
coefficient conventions.

\subsection{Spherical and Iwahori invariant vectors}

Let $F$ now denote a nonarchimedean local field, with ring of integers
$\mathcal O_F$, uniformizer $\varpi$, and residue cardinality $q$.
Write $G=GL_2(F)$, $K=GL_2(\mathcal O_F)$, and let $B$ be the upper
triangular subgroup. Fix a characteristic-zero coefficient field $E$
containing a chosen square root $q^{1/2}$.
Let $\chi_1,\chi_2:F^\times\to E^\times$ be unramified characters.
Put $\alpha=\chi_1(\varpi)$ and $\beta=\chi_2(\varpi)$.

Normalized smooth induction is the space $I(\chi_1,\chi_2)$ of locally
constant functions $f:G\to E$ satisfying
\[
 f\left(\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&d'\end{pmatrix}g\right)
 =|a/d'|^{1/2}\chi_1(a)\chi_2(d')f(g),
\]
with right translation by $G$.
Let $J\subset K$ be the Iwahori subgroup whose reduction is upper
triangular. Choose
$w=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}$ and
$t_0=\operatorname{diag}(\varpi,1)$.

\begin{proposition}[Invariant spaces and their Hecke operators]
\label{v4-arith:w5-e:local:invariant-hecke}
Evaluation at $1$ identifies $I(\chi_1,\chi_2)^K$ with $E$.
Evaluation at $(1,w)$ identifies $I(\chi_1,\chi_2)^J$ with $E^2$.
With Haar measure giving $J$ volume one, define
$U=q^{-1/2}[Jt_0J]$ on the latter space. Its matrix is
\[
 U=\begin{pmatrix}
 \alpha&0\\ (1-q^{-1})\alpha&\beta
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Consequently $(U-\alpha)(U-\beta)=0$.
For the spherical Hecke operators, normalize the measure by
$\operatorname{vol}(K)=1$. Then
$T=[Kt_0K]$ and $S=[K\operatorname{diag}(\varpi,\varpi)K]$
act on the spherical line by
\[
 T=q^{1/2}(\alpha+\beta),\qquad S=\alpha\beta.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Iwasawa decomposition $G=BK$ identifies $K$-invariant functions
with their value at $1$. The two $B(k_F)$-orbits on
$K/J\simeq\mathbb P^1(k_F)$ give the representatives $1,w$.
This proves both dimension statements.

Conjugation by $t_0$ shows that $J\cap t_0Jt_0^{-1}$ consists of
matrices whose upper-right entry is divisible by $\varpi$.
Its index in $J$ is $q$. Representatives are the upper unipotent
matrices with entries $u\bmod\varpi$. Thus
a right-coset decomposition of $Jt_0J$ is
\[
 Jt_0J=\coprod_{u\in\mathcal O_F/\varpi\mathcal O_F}
 \begin{pmatrix}\varpi&u\\0&1\end{pmatrix}J.
\]
At $1$, every summand gives $q^{-1/2}\alpha f(1)$.
For a nonzero residue class $u$, choose a unit lift. Then
\[
 w\begin{pmatrix}\varpi&u\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
 =\begin{pmatrix}-\varpi/u&1\\0&-u\end{pmatrix}
  \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\\varpi/u&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The second factor belongs to $J$. Each of these $q-1$ terms contributes
$q^{-1/2}\alpha f(1)$ at $w$. The remaining term satisfies
$wt_0=\operatorname{diag}(1,\varpi)w$ and contributes
$q^{1/2}\beta f(w)$. Multiplication by $q^{-1/2}$ gives the matrix.

For $Kt_0K/K$, the same $q$ representatives are joined by
$\operatorname{diag}(1,\varpi)$. Evaluating the spherical function at
$1$ gives $q^{1/2}(\alpha+\beta)$. The central double coset for $S$
acts by the central character $\chi_1\chi_2$ at $\varpi$.
\end{proof}

If $\alpha\ne\beta$, the two projectors on the Iwahori invariant space are
\[
 P_\alpha=\frac{U-\beta}{\alpha-\beta},\qquad
 P_\beta=\frac{U-\alpha}{\beta-\alpha}.
\]
They have rank one and sum to the identity. If $\alpha=\beta$, the
matrix has a nonzero off-diagonal entry, so it is not diagonalizable.
The whole principal series is infinite-dimensional: locally constant
sections on arbitrarily many disjoint open subsets of $\mathbb P^1(F)$
are linearly independent. Its two-dimensional invariant space is a
different object.

Fixing the unramified central character fixes $\alpha\beta$.
Thus the two inducing parameters lie on the determinant relation.
The calculation constructs Hecke eigenvalues on invariant vectors.
A comparison with a specified Galois or coherent-sheaf category still
requires a functor and its action compatibility.

\begin{corollary}[A unit-root projector on invariant vectors]
\label{v4-arith:w5-e:local:ordinary-projector}
Suppose $E$ is a finite extension of $\mathbb Q_p$, with ring of
integers $\mathcal O_E$. Assume $\alpha\in\mathcal O_E^\times$
and $\beta\in\mathfrak m_E\setminus\{0\}$.
Then $P_\alpha$ is the projector onto the unique unit-eigenvalue line.
For any $U$-stable $\mathcal O_E$-lattice $\Lambda$ in $I^J$,
$P_\alpha$ preserves $\Lambda$, and
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}U^{n!}=P_\alpha
\]
in $\operatorname{End}_E(I^J)$ with its $p$-adic topology.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The difference $\alpha-\beta$ is a unit, so the projector is a
polynomial in $U$ with coefficients in $\mathcal O_E$.
The spectral decomposition gives
$U^{n!}=\alpha^{n!}P_\alpha+\beta^{n!}P_\beta$.
For each integer $m$, the image of $\alpha$ in
$(\mathcal O_E/\mathfrak m_E^m)^\times$ has finite order.
That order divides $n!$ for all sufficiently large $n$.
Thus $\alpha^{n!}\to1$, while $\beta^{n!}\to0$.
\end{proof}

This is a projector on a specified invariant space. It does not
construct a family of automorphic forms. If both eigenvalues are units,
the entire Iwahori invariant space is the unit-root part. In particular, the two unit
roots of the unramified integral deformation above do not determine
a unique ordinary line by their valuations.

\section{An Enhanced Generator Criterion}

Equality of scalar eigenvalues does not determine the image of a
functor. For a field $E$, let $A=E[t]$ and consider
\[
 F:D(A)\longrightarrow D(A)\times D(A),\qquad M\longmapsto(M,0).
\]
This functor is exact, preserves colimits, and is fully faithful.
The diagonal central $A$-action on the target is faithful.
The object $F(A)$ cannot generate $(0,A)$, since projection to the
second factor annihilates its localizing closure.

\begin{theorem}[Comparison on compact generators]
\label{v4-arith:w5-e:local:generator-criterion}
Let $\mathcal C,\mathcal D$ be presentable stable
$\infty$-categories, and let $F:\mathcal C\to\mathcal D$ be exact
and colimit-preserving. Let $\{P_i\}_{i\in I}$ be a set of compact
generators of $\mathcal C$.
Here generation means that their smallest full stable subcategory
closed under all small colimits is $\mathcal C$.
Assume each $F(P_i)$ is compact and that the maps of mapping spectra
\[
 \operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal C}(P_i,P_j)
 \longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal D}(F(P_i),F(P_j))
\]
are equivalences for all $i,j$. Then $F$ is fully faithful.
It is an equivalence if the objects $F(P_i)$ also generate
$\mathcal D$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $i$. Compactness and exactness imply that both mapping-spectrum
functors in the comparison, with second variable $X$, preserve
coproducts and finite colimits. They therefore preserve all colimits
between stable categories. The objects $X$ where the comparison is an
equivalence form a localizing subcategory of $\mathcal C$.
It contains every $P_j$, so it is all of $\mathcal C$.

Fix $X$ and vary the first argument. Mapping spectra convert colimits
in this argument into limits. Hence the objects $Y$ for which
\[
 \operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal C}(Y,X)
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal D}(F(Y),F(X))
\]
is an equivalence form a localizing subcategory containing all $P_i$.
This proves full faithfulness. Its essential image is closed under
colimits and finite cones, since diagrams in that image lift through
a fully faithful functor. If the image contains generators of
$\mathcal D$, it is all of $\mathcal D$.
\end{proof}

For an $E$-linear differential graded enhancement, the hypothesis is a
quasi-isomorphism on each derived Hom complex induced by the chosen
functor. An equality of dimensions or a match in degree one is weaker.
Use the augmentation modules $k=k[t]/(t)$ and
$k=(k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2))/(\epsilon)$. Then $k$ has
$\operatorname{Ext}^1(k,k)=k$ over both $k[t]$ and
$k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$. Over $k[t]$, its length-one free resolution
makes all higher Ext groups zero. Over the dual numbers, the periodic
resolution with differential multiplication by $\epsilon$ gives
$\operatorname{Ext}^n(k,k)=k$ for every $n\ge0$.

The local categorical Langlands question requires the actual source
subcategory, spectral support condition, comparison functor, and
objects $P_i$. The Frobenius and invariant-vector constructions above
supply parameters and operators for this question. A categorical
equivalence follows only after the derived Hom and generation
hypotheses have been established on those categories.

\section{\(\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{ACD}}\): Cuspidal Adelic Categorical Descent}
\label{v4-arith:w5-e:lacd}

\subsection{Cuspidal Locus}

Flath's 1979 theorem concerns cuspidal automorphic representations
of \(GL_{2}\): each such representation decomposes as a restricted
tensor product of local representations, and this decomposition is
defined after choices of nonzero spherical reference vectors at almost every finite place. Below the
cuspidal locus of the adelic quotient can be given an independent
description (Bernstein 1988; Mo{\oe}glin--Waldspurger 1995) which
we exploit.

\begin{definition}[cuspidal sph-fin comparison category]
\label{v4-arith:w5-e:def:cuspidal-sph-fin}
Fix a unitary central character
$\omega:\mathbb A^\times/\mathbb Q^\times\to\mathbb C^\times$.
The Hilbert carrier is the space of square-integrable automorphic
functions modulo the centre with $f(zg)=\omega(z)f(g)$.
Cuspidality means that the constant term
$\int_{N(\mathbb Q)\backslash N(\mathbb A)}f(ng)\,dn$ vanishes
for every $g$, where $N$ is the upper unipotent subgroup.
The intended category
$\mathrm D^{\mathrm{sph\text{-}fin}}([GL_2])^{\mathrm{cusp}}$
requires a chosen stable enhancement of its smooth admissible
cuspidal objects and a specified sph-finiteness condition. It is then
their stable colimit closure in that enhancement. Finite-dimensional
local Whittaker models are not a definition of cuspidality.
\end{definition}
\begin{remark}[cuspidal orthogonality and enhancement]
\label{v4-arith:w5-e:rem:cuspidal-orthogonality}
For the fixed central-character Hilbert quotient, the cuspidal space
is orthogonal to the residual and continuous spectral spaces and is
preserved by adelic Hecke operators and the archimedean
$(\mathfrak g,K_\infty)$ action. The spectral references are Jacquet
(1983), SLN~1041, and M\oe glin--Waldspurger (1995), Section~III.2;
Bernstein (1988) concerns Plancherel support. A corresponding orthogonal
decomposition of enhanced categories requires vanishing of cross
mapping spectra; Hilbert orthogonality alone does not provide it.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Flath's Theorem at Representation Level}

\begin{theorem}[Flath 1979 restricted tensor product]
\label{v4-arith:w5-e:thm:Flath}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere Every irreducible admissible
cuspidal automorphic representation \(\pi\) of
\(GL_{2}(\mathbb{A})\) with central character factoring through a
continuous character \(\omega_{\pi}\colon\mathbb{A}^{\times}/\mathbb{Q}^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}\)
decomposes, after choosing nonzero spherical reference vectors at almost all finite places, as a restricted tensor product
\[
\pi\;\simeq\;\bigotimes_{v}^{\prime}\pi_{v},
\]
where \(\pi_{v}\) is an irreducible admissible representation of
\(GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{v})\), unramified at almost all \(v\), with the
spherical vector at each unramified place playing the role of the
distinguished unit. Archimedean \(\pi_{\infty}\) is an irreducible
admissible \((\mathfrak{g},K_{\infty})\)-module (Jacquet--Langlands
SLN~114 \S 5).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Representation-level input]
Flath (1979), \emph{Decomposition of representations into tensor
products}, Proc.\ Symp.\ Pure Math.~33, Part~1, Section~2, gives the
factorization of an actual irreducible admissible representation with
its archimedean $(\mathfrak g,K_\infty)$ factor. The chosen reference
vectors define the restricted tensor construction. This is not a
canonical choice of those vectors and does not globalize arbitrary
local tuples.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Categorical Lift on the Cuspidal Locus}

\begin{theorem}[conditional cuspidal adelic categorical descent]
\label{v4-arith:w5-e:thm:LACD-cuspidal}
Suppose the intended globalizable restricted-tensor source
$\bigotimes_v^{\prime,\mathrm{glob}}\mathcal C_v$ and the target
$\mathrm D^{\mathrm{sph\text{-}fin}}([GL_2])^{\mathrm{cusp}}$
are specified compactly generated stable presentable categories.
The source must encode actual cuspidal globalization and exact
triangles, not only a list of globalizable irreducibles. Suppose an
exact colimit-preserving globalization functor
\[
 F:\bigotimes_v^{\prime,\mathrm{glob}}
 \mathrm D^{\mathrm{sph\text{-}fin}}([GL_2(\mathbb Q_v)])
 \longrightarrow\mathrm D^{\mathrm{sph\text{-}fin}}([GL_2])^{\mathrm{cusp}}
\]
is constructed, sends chosen compact generators to compact objects,
induces equivalences on their full mapping spectra, and has generating
image. Then $F$ is an equivalence. To obtain the structured
comparison, also require natural intertwining isomorphisms for:
\begin{enumerate}
\item finite-place Whittaker functors with their chosen additive characters;
\item Bernstein-centre and Hecke actions, compatible across places;
\item the archimedean $(\mathfrak g,K_\infty)$ action.
\end{enumerate}
Under these extra hypotheses the equivalence has those compatibilities.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The five parts of the construction have distinct roles.
First, the pointed restricted tensor product requires the actual local
categories and reference insertions of the restricted-tensor
construction in Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:tate-tensor}, together with its
archimedean and sph-finite hypotheses. Second, Flath factorizes each
actual cuspidal representation. It gives neither an enhanced source
category nor a map on $K_0$ until compatibility with exact triangles
has been proved. Third, the assumed enhanced globalization supplies
that functor; a possible construction uses a specified restriction
adjunction whose unit and counit are equivalences on generators.
Fourth, Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w5-e:local:generator-criterion} applies
to the supplied mapping-spectrum and generation data and proves that
$F$ is an equivalence. Fifth, the three specified natural
transformations express its Whittaker, centre and archimedean
compatibility. They extend from generators when their functors preserve
the relevant colimits. This proves precisely the stated conditional
comparison, without inferring those transformations from scalar
eigenvalues or from representation-level factorization.
\end{proof}

The arithmetic construction still asks for the globalizable enhancement,
its projective or compact generators, globalization and its adjunction,
and the actual Whittaker and centre maps. The relevant representation
references remain Bernstein--Zelevinsky (1976), Jacquet--Langlands,
SLN~114, Sections~3, 5 and~11, Bernstein (1988), Wallach (1988), and
M\oe glin--Waldspurger (1995), Section~III.3. Neither a product of
local scalar centres nor Hilbert spectral orthogonality supplies
this categorical equivalence.

\label{v4-arith:w5-e:cor:F1-cuspidal}
Assume the full local ordinary and non-ordinary comparisons, the

The reduction statements in
Corollary~\ref{v4-arith:cl:cor:F1-single-lemma} and

Residual modularity does not construct the Selmer stack, its support,

\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Eisenstein extension]
\label{v4-arith:w5-e:rem:LACD-Eisenstein-domain}
The Eisenstein extension requires:
\begin{itemize}
\item Explicit description of Eisenstein-series \(L^{2}\)-summands in

\end{itemize}
