\chapter{Paskunas Wild Block \(\mathrm{Ext}^{1}\)-Class Match at \(p\in\{2,3\}\)}
\label{v4-arith:w10-b:chapter}

The intended wild-prime class comparison can be factored through
Galois extension data or through a specified pseudo-character
tangent space. Either factorization requires actual maps in the
chosen coefficient category. A finite quiver model does not supply
these arithmetic maps, and equal dimensions do not identify classes.

\section{The Two Class-Comparison Routes}
\label{v4-arith:w10-b:domain}

The conditional composition of
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w6-f:thm:class-match} has the form
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w10-b:eq:ch66-colmez-arrow}
\mathrm{Ext}^1_{\mathfrak B}
 \xrightarrow{\sim}
 D_{\bar\rho_{\mathfrak B}}
 \xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb T^1_{\mathcal X}|_{x_{\tau_{\mathfrak B}}}.
\end{equation}
Here \(D_{\bar\rho_{\mathfrak B}}\) is the fixed-determinant tangent
\(Z^1_{\mathrm{cts}}(G_{\mathbb Q_p},\operatorname{ad}^0\bar\rho_{\mathfrak B})/
d(\operatorname{ad}\bar\rho_{\mathfrak B})\), as computed in
Lemma~\ref{v4-arith:w6-f:lem:representability}. Its determinant-one
conjugation variant is used only when that equivalence relation is chosen.
Its first arrow is the additional block-to-Galois hypothesis stated
there; the spectral tangent comparison is a separate input. The
pseudo-character route considered here likewise needs an actual
block-to-pseudo-character map and a spectral map, with any required
duals made explicit. Agreement of the routes requires a comparison
of their maps, not simply a common dimension.

\section{Block Centraliser Pseudo-Character Tangent}
\label{v4-arith:w10-b:centraliser-tangent}

\subsection{Pseudo-Character Tangent Space at Wild Primes}

\begin{lemma}[relative first-order tangent]
\label{v4-arith:w10-b:lem:ps-tangent}
Let \(k\) be a perfect field of characteristic \(p\), and let
\((R,\mathfrak m,k)\) be a complete Noetherian local \(W(k)\)-algebra.
The relative tangent space consists of continuous local
\(W(k)\)-algebra maps \(R\to k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)\) reducing to
the residue map. It is
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w10-b:eq:ps-tangent}
 T_{R/W(k)}=\operatorname{Hom}_k
 \bigl(\mathfrak m/(\mathfrak m^2+pR),k\bigr).
\end{equation}
Identifying this space with a specified pseudo-character deformation
tangent requires a pro-representing isomorphism \(R\simeq R_{\mathrm{ps}}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Such a map has the form \(r\mapsto\bar r+\epsilon d(r)\), where
\(d\) is a \(W(k)\)-linear derivation with values in the residue module.
On \(\mathfrak m\), the derivation kills \(\mathfrak m^2\) and \(pR\).
Conversely, reduce modulo \(p\). The resulting local \(k\)-algebra
splits as \(k\oplus\mathfrak m/pR\) as a vector space. A functional
on \(\mathfrak m/(\mathfrak m^2+pR)\), extended by zero on \(k\),
satisfies the Leibniz rule. These constructions are inverse.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the pseudo-character comparison]
\label{v4-arith:w10-b:rem:why-ps}
Pro-representability alone supplies neither a block-ring isomorphism
nor a map from block extensions to a deformation tangent.
At small primes one must specify the deformation object, whether a
trace or a pseudorepresentation, its coefficients and its determinant
condition. These choices precede any comparison with an arithmetic block.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Absolute Extensions and Relative Tangents}

\begin{proposition}[self-extensions of the residue field]
\label{v4-arith:w10-b:prop:block-ext-ps}
For a commutative Noetherian local ring \((R,\mathfrak m,k)\),
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w10-b:eq:block-ext-ps}
 \operatorname{Ext}^1_R(k,k)\simeq
 \operatorname{Hom}_k(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2,k).
\end{equation}
Every simple \(R\)-module is isomorphic to \(k\). Thus this formula
has no grading by pairs of distinct simple \(R\)-modules.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply \(\operatorname{Hom}_R(-,k)\) to
\(0\to\mathfrak m\to R\to k\to0\).
The restriction \(\operatorname{Hom}_R(R,k)\to
\operatorname{Hom}_R(\mathfrak m,k)\) is zero, and
\(\operatorname{Ext}^1_R(R,k)=0\). Exactness identifies the extension
space with \(\operatorname{Hom}_R(\mathfrak m,k)\).
Since \(\mathfrak m k=0\), these homomorphisms factor precisely through
\(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\). Every simple module is a quotient by
a maximal ideal, and a local ring has only the maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\).
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[regularity and the coefficient base]
For \(R=k[[t]]\), the resolution
\[
 0\longrightarrow R\xrightarrow{\ t\ }R\longrightarrow k
 \longrightarrow0
\]
gives \(\operatorname{Ext}^1_R(k,k)=k\), although \(R\) is regular.
For \(R=W(k)\), multiplication by \(p\) gives the same resolution
and absolute extension space \(k\). Its tangent relative to
\(W(k)\) is zero. Absolute residue-field extensions therefore do
not determine the relative deformation tangent without a comparison.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[duality for a specified deformation tangent]
\label{v4-arith:w10-b:prop:spectral-ps}
Let \(L_x\) be a complex of \(k\)-vector spaces, with
\(H^0(L_x)\) finite-dimensional. Suppose the specified
pseudo-character tangent \(T_{\mathrm{ps}}\) is identified with
\(H^0(R\operatorname{Hom}_k(L_x,k))\). Then
\(H^0(L_x)\simeq T_{\mathrm{ps}}^\vee\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Linear duality over a field is exact, so
\(H^0(R\operatorname{Hom}_k(L_x,k))=
\operatorname{Hom}_k(H^0(L_x),k)\).
Finite-dimensional biduality proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

The degree-one cotangent target of the arithmetic comparison requires
an additional shift and a comparison of complexes. In particular,
the required isomorphism
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w10-b:eq:spectral-ps}
 \mathbb L^1_{\mathcal X^{\mathrm{EG,wild},p,\chi}_{GL_2}}
 \big|_{x_{\tau_{\mathfrak B}}}
 \xrightarrow{\sim}T_{\mathrm{ps}}^\vee
\end{equation}
is an additional hypothesis, not a consequence of degree-zero duality.

\section{Wild-Prime Class-Match Theorem}
\label{v4-arith:w10-b:main-thm}

\begin{theorem}[wild block \(\mathrm{Ext}^{1}\)-class match, pseudo-character derivation]
\label{v4-arith:w10-b:thm:class-match}
\emph{Conditional statement.} Let \(p\in\{2,3\}\), \(\chi\) a central
character, and \(\mathfrak{B}\) a non-semisimple Pa{\v s}k{\=u}nas block.
Assume the wild-EG datum, actual natural isomorphisms of the
block extension space and the spectral cotangent fibre with the
same dual pseudo-character tangent, and preservation of the chosen
arithmetic classes. Then there is a
canonical \(k\)-linear class-level isomorphism
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w10-b:eq:class-match}
\mathsf{c}^{\mathrm{class},\mathrm{B}}_{p,\mathfrak{B}}\colon
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathfrak{B}}(S,S')\;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\;\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{EG,wild},p,\chi}_{GL_{2}}}\big|_{x_{\tau_{\mathfrak{B}}}}
\end{equation}
natural in \(\mathfrak B\). If the arithmetic classes in
Questions~\ref{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:prop:R4-Ext1-p2}
and~\ref{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:prop:R4-Ext1-p3} have been
constructed, the class-preserving factor maps carry them to the
specified cotangent directions. In characteristic two, trivial and
sign characters cannot label two distinct objects. At three, a
radical-square-zero model has a nonzero adjacent Yoneda product;
compatibility in higher degrees must retain it.
Agreement with the map of
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w6-f:thm:class-match} requires a specified tangent-to-cotangent identification, or maps to
a common target, followed by a comparison on each labelled line.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Compose the assumed block-to-dual-pseudo-character isomorphism
with the inverse of the assumed spectral-to-dual-pseudo-character
isomorphism. The composite is an isomorphism and is natural because
both factors are natural. Their stipulated compatibility transports
the selected classes. A comparison with the other factorization is
additional data; it does not follow from the existence of these maps.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[comparison on labelled lines]
\label{v4-arith:w10-b:cor:removes-disjoint}
Suppose both class-comparison routes are constructed and carry each
specified source line isomorphically onto the same specified target
line. Their restrictions to that line differ by a nonzero scalar.
For several lines these scalars may differ; there need not be one
scalar for the whole vector space.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Compose one line isomorphism with the inverse of the other. Every
automorphism of a one-dimensional vector space is multiplication by
a nonzero scalar. This statement applies to each labelled line
separately and assumes that neither map mixes those lines.
\end{proof}

\section{Derivation Comparison}
\label{v4-arith:w10-b:obstruction-analysis}

The pseudo-character comparison must specify its coefficient ring,
block objects, deformation condition and tangent/cotangent duality.
The finite-algebra examples of Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion}
do not establish these data in an arithmetic category. In particular,
the two local comparison questions contain no trace-pairing map
that could be imported as a class-level arithmetic isomorphism.

\section{Wild Class Boundary}
\label{v4-arith:w10-b:summary}

The two proposed routes remain conditional on their actual factor
maps and class compatibility. Once supplied, composition proves a
natural class isomorphism; agreement between the routes additionally
requires a commuting comparison, or specified linewise normalizations.
Neither a block-centraliser dimension nor a pro-representable
deformation functor alone supplies those identifications. Any
higher-degree arithmetic realization must also account for the
Yoneda products and global tensor terms computed in
Section~\ref{v4-arith:bzn-rcompletion:R4}.
