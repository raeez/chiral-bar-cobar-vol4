\chapter{Gaussian Tests for the Signed Gamma Form}
\label{v4-arith:w7-d:chapter}

A Gaussian decays fast enough for every term of the explicit formula,
although it is not compactly supported. This extension requires
exponential control in the logarithmic variable. Ordinary Schwartz
continuity cannot control the uncentered critical prime measure.

\section{An exponential test space}
\begin{definition}[rapid exponential decay]
Let $\mathscr G$ consist of all smooth functions $f$ such that
\[
 p_{a,N,j}(f)=\sup_{t\in\mathbb R}
 e^{a|t|}(1+|t|)^N|f^{(j)}(t)|<\infty
 \quad(a,N,j\in\mathbb N).
\]
Give $\mathscr G$ the topology of these seminorms. Use
$h_f(z)=\int f(t)e^{izt}dt$ and $f^*(t)=\overline{f(-t)}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[extension of the explicit formula]
\label{v4-arith:sc:gaussian-extension}
The space $\mathscr G$ is a convolution algebra with continuous
involution. Compact smooth functions are dense in $\mathscr G$.
The explicit formula, its signed pairing, and both polar moments
extend continuously to $\mathscr G$.
In particular, for $f,g\in\mathscr G$,
\[
 W(f*g^*)=\sum_\rho h_f(z_\rho)\overline{h_g(\bar z_\rho)}
 =P(f*g^*)-W_\infty(f*g^*)-W_{\mathrm{fin}}(f*g^*).
\]
The zero and prime sums are absolutely convergent. Positivity on
all squares in $\mathscr G$ is equivalent to positivity on all
squares in $\mathscr A$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The inequality $|t|\le|v|+|t-v|$, together with the corresponding
polynomial weight inequality, bounds every convolution seminorm
by a weighted supremum seminorm times a weighted integral seminorm.
An additional exponential weight bounds that integral. This proves
continuity of convolution. Reflection proves continuity of $*$.
For a compact smooth cutoff $\chi=1$ near zero, put
$f_R(t)=\chi(t/R)f(t)$. Leibniz's rule and one extra exponential
weight give $p_{a,N,j}(f-f_R)\to0$ for every seminorm.

Integration by parts gives rapid decay of $h_f$ in each fixed
horizontal strip, continuously in these seminorms. The zero count
then bounds the zero sum. The polar evaluations are continuous.
The gamma density has logarithmic growth, so its integral is
continuous. Finally, for example,
\[
 \sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}|f(\pm\log n)|
 \le p_{2,0,0}(f)\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\log n}{n^{5/2}}.
\]
The compact explicit formula therefore passes to the limit $f_R$.
Convolution continuity gives the paired identity and passage of
positivity. The reverse positivity implication uses
$\mathscr A\subset\mathscr G$.
\end{proof}

\section{The normalized scalar integral}
\begin{lemma}[Gaussian Fourier transform]
For $\lambda>0$ set $f_\lambda(t)=e^{-\lambda t^2/2}$. Then
\[
 h_{f_\lambda}(u)=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}}
 e^{-u^2/(2\lambda)},\qquad
 (f_\lambda*f_\lambda)(t)=\sqrt{\frac\pi\lambda}
 e^{-\lambda t^2/4}.
\]
Consequently, with $k(u)=\log\pi-\Re\psi_\Gamma(1/4+iu/2)$,
\[
 W_\infty(f_\lambda*f_\lambda)
 =\sqrt{\frac\pi\lambda}\,H(\lambda),\qquad
 H(\lambda)=\frac1{\sqrt{\pi\lambda}}
             \int e^{-u^2/\lambda}k(u)du.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Completing the square gives both Gaussian integrals.
Insert $|h_{f_\lambda}|^2=(2\pi/\lambda)e^{-u^2/\lambda}$
in the gamma pairing with its coefficient $1/(2\pi)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[convergent error-function series]
\label{v4-arith:sc:erfc}
Let $c_n=n+1/4$ and
$\operatorname{erfc}(x)=\frac2{\sqrt\pi}\int_x^\infty e^{-v^2}dv$.
For every $\lambda>0$,
\[
 H(\lambda)=k(0)-\sum_{n=0}^\infty
 \left[\frac1{c_n}-\frac{2\sqrt\pi}{\sqrt\lambda}
 e^{4c_n^2/\lambda}\operatorname{erfc}(2c_n/\sqrt\lambda)\right].
\]
Each bracket is nonnegative and at most $\lambda/(8c_n^3)$.
The series converges uniformly on compact $\lambda$-intervals.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The digamma series gives
$k(0)-k(u)=\sum_n (u^2/4)/(c_n(c_n^2+u^2/4))$.
Tonelli's theorem exchanges this nonnegative sum and the Gaussian
integral. For $a>0$,
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}\frac{e^{-u^2/\lambda}}{u^2+a^2}du
 =\sqrt\pi\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-a^2r}}{\sqrt{r+1/\lambda}}dr
 =\frac\pi a e^{a^2/\lambda}\operatorname{erfc}(a/\sqrt\lambda).
\]
The first equality follows by integrating
$\int_0^\infty e^{-r(u^2+a^2)}dr$ in either order.
The substitution $v=a\sqrt{r+1/\lambda}$ gives the second.
Set $a=2c_n$ and use
$\int e^{-u^2/\lambda}u^2du=\sqrt\pi\lambda^{3/2}/2$.
The estimate on each bracket follows by replacing
$c_n^2+u^2/4$ by $c_n^2$. It proves uniform convergence.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[the exact Gaussian sign range]
\label{v4-arith:w7-d:thm:gauss-ATP}
The continuous function $H$ is strictly decreasing on $(0,\infty)$,
with limits $k(0)>0$ at zero and $-\infty$ at infinity.
There is a unique $\lambda_c>0$ with $H(\lambda_c)=0$, and
\[
 W_\infty(f_\lambda*f_\lambda)\ge0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 0<\lambda\le\lambda_c.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
After $u=\sqrt\lambda v$,
$H(\lambda)=\pi^{-1/2}\int e^{-v^2}k(\sqrt\lambda v)dv$.
Strict radial decrease of $k$ gives strict decrease of this integral.
Logarithmic growth gives a common integrable majorant on each compact
$\lambda$-interval and on $0<\lambda\le1$.
Thus $H$ is continuous and tends to $k(0)$ at zero.
For each $v\ne0$, the nonnegative quantity
$k(0)-k(\sqrt\lambda v)$ increases to infinity.
Monotone convergence gives the limit at infinity.
The intermediate value theorem completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\section{The arithmetic inequality on Gaussian squares}
For these Gaussian tests the polar and prime contributions are
\[
 P(f_\lambda*f_\lambda)=\frac{4\pi}{\lambda}e^{1/(4\lambda)},
 \qquad
 W_{\mathrm{fin}}(f_\lambda*f_\lambda)
 =2\sqrt{\frac\pi\lambda}\sum_{n\ge2}
   \frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}e^{-\lambda(\log n)^2/4}.
\]
These expressions converge for every $\lambda>0$ and give the
complete value of $W(f_\lambda*f_\lambda)$ after subtracting the
prime and gamma terms from the polar term.
The gamma sign theorem alone does not determine that value.

\begin{proposition}[a single Gaussian parameter does not test a quadratic form]
For a Hermitian form, nonnegative values on a spanning collection
of vectors do not imply nonnegative values on its linear span.
In particular, Gaussian cross terms require their own comparison.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The Hermitian matrix
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&2\\2&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$
is positive on each coordinate vector but has value $-2$ on $(1,-1)$.
More generally, $q(f+g,f+g)=q(f,f)+q(g,g)+2\Re q(f,g)$.
The signed arithmetic representation determines these cross terms
on $\mathscr G$, but positivity of individual Gaussian squares
does not bound them.
\end{proof}

\section{The auxiliary digamma integral and Fourier normalization}

The signed kernel \(k\) and the auxiliary kernel
\(g(u)=\Re\psi_\Gamma(1/4+iu/2)+\log\pi\) satisfy
\(k(u)+g(u)=2\log\pi\). To compare scalar calculations, use a separate
parameter \(L>0\) and define
\[
 I(L)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-2\pi u^2/L}g(u)\,du,\qquad m_L=\sqrt{L/2}.
\]
Direct substitution in the definition of \(H\) gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{I(L)}{m_L}=2\log\pi-H\left(\frac{L}{2\pi}\right).
 \label{v4-arith:w7-d:eq:J-normalised}
\end{equation}
Thus the zero of \(I\) is a level \(H=2\log\pi\), distinct from the
signed threshold \(H=0\).

\begin{proposition}[the auxiliary error-function series]
\label{v4-arith:w7-d:prop:term-by-term}
\label{v4-arith:w7-d:thm:erfc-series}
With \(c_n=n+1/4\), put
\begin{align}
 J_n(L)&=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-2\pi u^2/L}
                 \frac{c_n}{c_n^2+u^2/4}\,du,
 \label{v4-arith:w7-d:eq:Jn-def}\\
 &=2\pi e^{8\pi c_n^2/L}
       \operatorname{erfc}\left(2c_n\sqrt{2\pi/L}\right).
 \label{v4-arith:w7-d:eq:Jn-closed}
\end{align}
Then the paired series
\begin{equation}
 I(L)=m_L(-\gamma_E+\log\pi)
       +\sum_{n\ge0}\left(\frac{m_L}{n+1}-J_n(L)\right)
 \label{v4-arith:w7-d:eq:I-erfc-series}
\end{equation}
converges absolutely. The accelerated series is
\[
 \frac{I(L)}{m_L}=g(0)+\sum_{n\ge0}
       \left(\frac1{c_n}-\frac{J_n(L)}{m_L}\right),\qquad
 0\le\frac1{c_n}-\frac{J_n(L)}{m_L}
       \le\frac{L}{16\pi c_n^3}.
\]
In particular its tail from \(n=N\ge1\) is at most
\(L/[32\pi(N-3/4)^2]\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The digamma series is
\begin{equation}
 g(u)=-\gamma_E+\log\pi+
 \sum_{n\ge0}\left(\frac1{n+1}-\frac{c_n}{c_n^2+u^2/4}\right).
 \label{v4-arith:w7-d:eq:g-hurwitz-recall}
\end{equation}
Subtract its value at zero. The resulting nonnegative summands
permit Tonelli's theorem. The error-function integral in
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:sc:erfc}, with \(\lambda=L/(2\pi)\), gives
\(J_n\) and the accelerated bound. The difference
\(1/(n+1)-1/c_n\) is absolutely summable, so regrouping gives
\eqref{v4-arith:w7-d:eq:I-erfc-series}.
Finally \(\sum_{n=N}^\infty(n+1/4)^{-3}
\le\int_{N-1}^\infty(x+1/4)^{-3}dx\) proves the tail estimate.
The unaccelerated paired summands are \(O_L(n^{-2})\), since
\(J_n(L)=m_L/c_n+O_L(c_n^{-3})\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the real and complex Gaussian integrals]
\label{v4-arith:w7-d:lem:erfc}
The integral used above, for \(a,\alpha>0\), is
\begin{equation}
 \int_{\mathbb R}\frac{e^{-\alpha u^2}}{a^2+u^2}\,du
 =\frac\pi a e^{\alpha a^2}\operatorname{erfc}(a\sqrt\alpha).
 \label{v4-arith:w7-d:eq:erfc-identity}
\end{equation}
Also
\begin{equation}
 \int_{\mathbb R}\frac{e^{-\alpha u^2}}{a-iu}\,du
 =\pi e^{\alpha a^2}\operatorname{erfc}(a\sqrt\alpha).
 \label{v4-arith:w7-d:eq:erfc-imag-axis}
\end{equation}
Indeed \((a-iu)^{-1}=(a+iu)/(a^2+u^2)\); the imaginary term is odd
and integrates to zero. The real integral was proved by a Laplace
integral in Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:sc:erfc}.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[the auxiliary limits and threshold]
\label{v4-arith:w7-d:prop:narrow}
\label{v4-arith:w7-d:prop:wide}
\label{v4-arith:w7-d:thm:lambda-c}
As \(L\downarrow0\),
\[
 I(L)=m_L\left(g(0)+\frac{L}{8\pi}g''(0)+O(L^2)\right),
 \qquad g(0)=\log\pi-\gamma_E-\frac\pi2-3\log2<0.
\]
As \(L\to\infty\), \(I(L)\sim\tfrac12m_L\log L\).
The normalized integral \(I(L)/m_L\) is strictly increasing and
has a unique zero \(L_g>0\), characterized by
\(H(L_g/(2\pi))=2\log\pi\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write \(u=\sqrt{L/(2\pi)}v\) and use the probability density
\(\pi^{-1/2}e^{-v^2}\). Its second moment is \(1/2\).
The even Taylor expansion of \(g\), with fourth-order remainder
near zero and logarithmic growth away from zero, gives the narrow
expansion. The Gaussian tail outside a fixed neighborhood is smaller
than every power of \(L\). The value at zero follows from the
reflection and duplication identities for \(\psi_\Gamma\).
For the wide limit, \(g(u)=\log|u|+O(1)\) for \(|u|\ge1\).
Split the scaled integral at \(|v|=\sqrt{2\pi/L}\).
On the complement, \(\log|v|\) is integrable against the Gaussian.
On the small interval, boundedness of \(g\) gives an
\(O(L^{-1/2}\log L)\) error after subtracting \(\tfrac12\log L\).
Thus \(I(L)/m_L=\tfrac12\log L+O(1)\).
Finally \eqref{v4-arith:w7-d:eq:J-normalised} and the strict decrease
of \(H\) give monotonicity and uniqueness.
\end{proof}

The time Gaussian \(F_L(x)=e^{-\pi Lx^2}\) has the Fourier transform
\begin{equation}
 \widehat F_{L,2\pi}(u)=\int F_L(x)e^{-2\pi iux}\,dx
       =L^{-1/2}e^{-\pi u^2/L}.
 \label{v4-arith:w7-d:eq:gaussian-FT}
\end{equation}
Since \(F_L=f_{2\pi L}\), its signed pairing is
\[
 W_\infty(F_L*F_L)=\frac1{\sqrt{2L}}H(2\pi L).
\]
More generally, \(\mathcal F_+\phi(2\pi u)=\widehat\phi_{2\pi}(-u)\),
so evenness of \(k\) and a change of variables give
\[
 W_\infty(\phi)=\int_{\mathbb R}\widehat\phi_{2\pi}(u)k(2\pi u)\,du.
\]
This conversion includes both the kernel argument and the measure factor.
The parameter in the auxiliary identity
\eqref{v4-arith:w7-d:eq:J-normalised} is therefore not the physical
Gaussian parameter in this last pairing.

\section{Cross terms and the positive Gaussian cone}
\begin{proposition}[the signed gamma form on positive Gaussian sums]
\label{v4-arith:w7-d:thm:positive-cone}
For \(\lambda,\mu>0\), put \(\nu=2\lambda\mu/(\lambda+\mu)\).
Then
\begin{align*}
 (f_\lambda*f_\mu)(t)
 &=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\lambda+\mu}}
           e^{-\lambda\mu t^2/[2(\lambda+\mu)]},\\
 W_\infty(f_\lambda*f_\mu)
 &=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\lambda+\mu}}H(\nu).
\end{align*}
Consequently, on the cone
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal C^+_{\mathrm{Gauss}}=
 \left\{\sum_{j=1}^N a_jf_{\lambda_j}:
 N\ge1,\ a_j\ge0,\ 0<\lambda_j\le\lambda_c\right\},
 \label{v4-arith:w7-d:eq:positive-cone}
\end{equation}
one has \(W_\infty(f*f^*)\ge0\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Complete the square in the convolution integral.
The Fourier product is
\((2\pi/\sqrt{\lambda\mu})e^{-u^2/\nu}\).
Integrating it against \(k(u)/(2\pi)\) gives the displayed pairing.
For \(\lambda,\mu\le\lambda_c\), their harmonic mean satisfies
\(\nu\le\lambda_c\), so every cross pairing is nonnegative.
Expand the finite sum with coefficients \(a_i a_j\ge0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[continuity and carrier boundaries]
\label{v4-arith:w7-d:lem:W-arch-continuous}
\label{v4-arith:w7-d:cor:gauss-dense}
\label{v4-arith:w7-d:prop:compare-HFD}
The signed gamma distribution extends continuously to the Schwartz
space. Its quadratic pairing is continuous there as well.
The linear span of the centered Gaussians lies in the closed even
subspace and is not dense in the full Schwartz space.
Every nonzero element of \(\mathcal C^+_{\mathrm{Gauss}}\) is positive
on all of \(\mathbb R\), so it is not compactly supported.
Its Fourier transform is not in \(\mathcal F_+\mathscr A\).
Both compact tests and these Gaussians belong to \(\mathscr G\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Logarithmic growth of \(k\) bounds its Fourier pairing by a Schwartz
seminorm. The Fourier transform, convolution and involution are
continuous on Schwartz space. Reflection is continuous, so its
fixed subspace is closed and excludes nonzero odd Schwartz functions.
Each Gaussian is strictly positive, which proves the support claim
for nonzero positive combinations. Fourier injectivity would force
a compact preimage to equal this noncompact function.
Membership in \(\mathscr G\) follows directly from the defining
seminorms. Continuity of the complete arithmetic functional uses
that exponential topology, as proved above.
\end{proof}

The positive Gaussian cone controls the signed gamma term only.
The full Weil form still subtracts this term and the prime term
from the polar term. Neither positivity of the gamma cone nor its
comparison with a compact or automorphic test class proves the
full arithmetic inequality.

\section{The auxiliary Schwartz form and its Gaussian cone}
\label{v4-arith:pres74:aux-form}

The auxiliary integral also defines a quadratic form, but it is not
the signed gamma form. On the complex Schwartz space use the convention
$\widehat f(u)=\int_{\mathbb R}f(x)e^{-2\pi iux}\,dx$ and set
\[
 \mathcal T_g(\phi)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}\widehat\phi(u)g(u)\,du,
 \qquad
 Q_g(f,h)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
              \widehat f(u)\overline{\widehat h(u)}g(u)\,du.
\]
Thus $\mathcal T_g$ is complex linear and $Q_g$ is Hermitian,
linear in its first argument, with $Q_g(f,h)=\mathcal T_g(f*h^*)$.
The value of $\mathcal T_g$ is real when its Fourier argument is real,
in particular for real even tests. No real-valuedness is claimed
for arbitrary complex tests.

\begin{proposition}[continuity and the two normalizations]
\label{v4-arith:pres74:aux-continuity}
The functional $\mathcal T_g$ and the sesquilinear form $Q_g$ are
continuous on Schwartz space. The function $I$ is smooth on
$(0,\infty)$. With $\langle f,h\rangle_2=\int f\bar h$, one has
\begin{align*}
 \frac1{2\pi}\int\widehat f(u)\overline{\widehat h(u)}k(u)\,du
   &=\frac{\log\pi}{\pi}\langle f,h\rangle_2-Q_g(f,h),\\
 W_\infty(f*h^*)&=\int\widehat f(u)\overline{\widehat h(u)}k(2\pi u)\,du.
\end{align*}
The second equality denotes the signed gamma extension; it does not
extend the full prime functional to arbitrary Schwartz tests.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The function $g$ is smooth at zero and has at most logarithmic growth.
Bound its integral by a weighted supremum seminorm of $\widehat\phi$;
for the form use the product of such seminorms of $\widehat f$ and
$\widehat h$. The integrable majorant can be taken proportional to
$(1+|u|)^{-2}$. Fourier transform is continuous on Schwartz space.
On each compact interval of positive $L$, every $L$-derivative of
$e^{-2\pi u^2/L}$ is bounded by a polynomial in $u$ times a fixed
Gaussian. Multiplication by $g(u)$ is integrable against this bound,
so differentiation under the integral proves $I\in C^\infty$.
The first identity uses $k+g=2\log\pi$ and Plancherel in the
$2\pi$ convention. The second uses
$h_f(2\pi u)=\widehat f(-u)$ and the change of measure
$du_{\mathrm{old}}=2\pi\,du$, followed by evenness of $k$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[auxiliary Gaussian pairings and the exact cone]
\label{v4-arith:pres74:aux-cone}
For $F_L(x)=e^{-\pi Lx^2}$, $L,M>0$, let $H_{LM}=2LM/(L+M)$.
Then
\[
 Q_g(F_L,F_M)=\frac{I(H_{LM})}{2\pi\sqrt{LM}},\qquad
 Q_g(F_L,F_L)=\frac{I(L)}{2\pi L}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \{L>0:Q_g(F_L,F_L)\ge0\}=[L_g,\infty),
 \qquad Q_g(F_L,F_L)=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ L=L_g,
\]
where $H(L_g/(2\pi))=2\log\pi$.
On the nonempty cone
\[
 \mathcal C_g^+=\left\{\sum_{j=1}^N a_jF_{L_j}:
                   a_j\ge0,\ L_j\ge L_g,\ N\ge1\right\}
\]
one has $Q_g(f,f)\ge0$. A nonzero element has zero value precisely
when every parameter with nonzero coefficient equals $L_g$.
The same nonnegativity holds on the closure of this cone in
Schwartz topology.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The product of the two Fourier Gaussians is
$(LM)^{-1/2}e^{-\pi u^2(1/L+1/M)}$, which gives the first identity.
The sign and equality statements follow from the strict increase of
$I(L)/m_L$ and its unique zero proved above.
If $L,M\ge L_g$, then
$H_{LM}^{-1}=(L^{-1}+M^{-1})/2\le L_g^{-1}$.
Thus every cross pairing is nonnegative. Expanding the square of a
finite sum gives
\[
 Q_g(f,f)=\sum_{i,j}a_i a_j
             \frac{I(H_{L_iL_j})}{2\pi\sqrt{L_iL_j}}\ge0.
\]
If some $a_j>0$ has $L_j>L_g$, its diagonal term is strictly
positive and none of the other terms can cancel it. Otherwise all
active parameters equal $L_g$ and every term vanishes.
The closure statement is continuity of $Q_g$. Nonemptiness follows
from any $F_L$ with $L\ge L_g$.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[two auxiliary Gaussian scales]
\label{v4-arith:pres74:two-gaussians}
For real $a,b$,
\[
 Q_g(aF_L+bF_M,aF_L+bF_M)
 =\frac1{2\pi}\left(\frac{a^2I(L)}L
   +\frac{2abI(H_{LM})}{\sqrt{LM}}+\frac{b^2I(M)}M\right).
\]
Choose $L_g/2<L<L_g$ and
$M>L_gL/(2L-L_g)$. Then $M>L_g$ and $H_{LM}>L_g$, while
$I(L)<0$. Taking $a=1$ and $b>0$ sufficiently small gives a
negative value by continuity in $b$. This example mixes one
negative and one positive Gaussian; it does not contradict the
positive-cone theorem, whose parameters all lie above $L_g$.
Nor does that theorem assert positivity for every complex linear
combination of its generators.
\end{example}

\subsection{High-frequency dominance on a specified carrier}
\label{v4-arith:pres74:hfd}
Let $t_g>0$ be the unique zero of $g$: existence follows from
$g(0)<0$, logarithmic growth and strict increase on $(0,\infty)$.
Indeed the differentiated Hurwitz series has positive summands
$c_nu/[2(c_n^2+u^2/4)^2]$ for $u>0$, uniformly convergent on compact
intervals. For $f\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R)$ define
\[
 A_g(f)=\int_{|u|\le t_g}|\widehat f(u)|^2(-g(u))\,du,\qquad
 B_g(f)=\int_{|u|>t_g}|\widehat f(u)|^2g(u)\,du.
\]
The high-frequency dominance condition, in this Fourier convention, is
\[
 2t_g|g(0)|\sup_{|u|\le t_g}|\widehat f(u)|^2\le B_g(f).
\]
It implies $A_g(f)\le B_g(f)$ and hence
$Q_g(f,f)=(B_g(f)-A_g(f))/(2\pi)\ge0$.
This is the auxiliary inequality associated with the condition in
\Cref{v4-arith:w6-a:def:HFD-class}; changing the Fourier convention
requires its explicit frequency rescaling, and does not turn $g$ into $k$.

For a specified restricted compact test space $E\subset\mathscr A$,
put $E_{\mathrm{HFD}}=\{f\in E:f\text{ satisfies this condition}\}$.
Every nonzero element of $\mathcal C_g^+$ is positive on all of
$\mathbb R$, so $\mathcal C_g^+\cap E=\{0\}$; both sets contain zero.
The assertion that $E_{\mathrm{HFD}}$ has a nonzero element requires
a construction within the actual restrictions defining $E$.
It is a separate question whether a chosen automorphic subspace
admits such tests and a norm comparison. A noncompact Gaussian is
not an example in that compact space.

\paragraph{Other positive classes and the full inequality.}
\label{v4-arith:pres74:routes}
One may ask for other positive subspaces of $Q_g$, including
nonconic combinations controlled by a de Branges or Connes--Deninger
construction, compact smooth tests, or a spectral-density comparison
with the Gaussian moments of $g$. No assertion that these exhaust
all possible methods is made. The cone is a proper subset of its
complex linear span: $-F_L$ belongs to that span for $L\ge L_g$
but cannot be a nonnegative sum of strictly positive Gaussians.
For comparison, if functions $\varphi_j$ are positive definite,
then $\sum_jc_j\varphi_j$ is positive definite for $c_j\ge0$:
each of its finite Gram forms is the same nonnegative combination
of the Gram forms of the $\varphi_j$. This elementary fact does
not identify positive-definite functions with vectors on which
$Q_g$ is nonnegative. Closure of a positive cone follows
from quadratic continuity; linear density of positive individual
vectors alone does not control cross terms.

The signed cone and $\mathcal C_g^+$ prove different gamma
inequalities. Both lie in $\mathscr G$, where the full arithmetic
identity is defined. For a specified $E\subset\mathscr G$ and
$S\subset E$, positivity of the full Weil form on $E$ is equivalent
to positivity on $E\setminus S$ only after positivity on $S$ is
established. This is the elementary partition of the quantifier
over $E$. Auxiliary positivity on $S$ does not supply that premise,
since the polar and prime terms remain. RH equivalence then requires
a proved comparison of $E$ with the full compact test criterion.
