\chapter{Arithmetic bar constructions and residue comparisons}
\label{v4-arith:arakelov-bar}

Continuous Galois cochains give an integral arithmetic input to the
coefficient bar. Their relative products and coefficient limits are
constructed in Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:coefficients:chapter}.
A comparison with chiral collision operations requires residue complexes
on the geometric carrier. It also requires compatible operations and
maps identifying them with the coefficient model.
The elementary residue and bar constructions below specify what is
available before making that comparison.

\section{Incidence of finite places}
\label{v4-arith:bar:configuration-space}

\begin{observation}
\label{v4-arith:obs:config-discrete}
The ordered \(n\)-tuples of distinct finite places of
\(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z\) form a countable set. Giving this
set the discrete topology produces a combinatorial configuration
model. It is not an identification with the scheme configuration
functor, and its distinct-place tuples contain no collision locus.
A topology describing collisions must be supplied separately.
\end{observation}

\begin{definition}[incidence poset of arithmetic places]
\label{v4-arith:def:arith-config}
Let \(\mathcal I_K\) be the poset of finite subsets of the finite
places of \(K\), ordered by inclusion. Its order complex has a simplex
for every finite strict chain of such subsets. The simplicial
cochains of this order complex are a combinatorial coefficient
complex. They do not by definition carry the differential forms or
residues of a geometric configuration space.
\end{definition}

A motivic fundamental group is another construction with a specified
base and category of motives. Relating its operations, or those of a
Grothendieck--Teichm\"uller Lie algebra, to this incidence complex
requires an actual map. Neither a common set of prime labels nor a
formal resemblance between relations provides that map.

\section{Steinberg symbols and reciprocity}
\label{v4-arith:bar:arnold-steinberg}

\begin{observation}[constant differences]
\label{v4-arith:obs:no-dlog-p-q}
For two distinct rational primes \(p,q\), the difference \(p-q\)
is a constant in \(\mathbb Q^\times\). Its relative differential
in \(\Omega^1_{\mathbb Q/\mathbb Q}\) is zero. Thus it does not
supply the nonzero logarithmic form associated with moving insertion
coordinates on a complex curve.
\end{observation}

\begin{theorem}[Steinberg relation and the rational tame symbols]
\label{v4-arith:thm:steinberg-replaces-arnold}
In Milnor \(K\)-theory, \(\{a,1-a\}=0\) for \(a\ne0,1\).
In particular \(\{p,1-p\}=0\) in \(K^M_2(\mathbb Q)\).
Tate's theorem identifies
\[
 K^M_2(\mathbb Q)\cong
 \{\pm1\}\oplus\bigoplus_p\mathbb F_p^\times
\]
by the real sign symbol together with the finite tame symbols.
The finite symbols alone have an order-two kernel.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The first assertion is a defining relation of \(K^M_2\).
The isomorphism is the external theorem stated in
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:coefficients:thm:tate}. The class
\(\{-1,-1\}\) has trivial finite tame symbols and real sign
\(-1\), and that theorem identifies it as the kernel generator.
\end{proof}

This theorem does not identify the Steinberg relation with an Arnold
relation on configuration-space forms. Such an identification would
need a map on the complexes carrying those relations and a comparison
of their induced operations.

\begin{theorem}[dimension-one residues and their range]
\label{v4-arith:thm:parshin-reciprocity}
For each finite place \(v\) of \(K\), the normalized tame symbol
is a homomorphism
\[
 \partial_v:K^M_2(K)\longrightarrow k_v^\times,
 \qquad \partial_v\{\pi_v,u\}=\bar u.
\]
There is no codimension-two term on
\(\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal O_K\). On \(\mathbb P^1_F\), the
normed tame symbols have common recipient \(F^\times\) and obey
\eqref{v4-arith:coefficients:eq:curve-reciprocity}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:coefficients:prop:tame} constructs the
local homomorphisms and checks their Steinberg relations.
The ring \(\mathcal O_K\) has dimension one, proving the second
assertion. The last assertion is the tame-symbol reciprocity theorem
for the projective line with its norm maps, cited in
\S\ref{v4-arith:coefficients:sec:symbols}.
\end{proof}

For a general degree \(n\), the dimension-one residue diagram has
terms \(K^M_n(K)\) and
\(\bigoplus_vK^M_{n-1}(k_v)\). No additional codimension-two
points are produced by writing a further arrow. A surface construction
instead starts from a regular arithmetic surface \(X\), a curve
\(C\subset X\), and a point \(x\in C\). Its successive symbols
require these flags and appropriate recipients and norm maps.
A sum or product of symbols in different fields is undefined until
those maps are specified. Exactness and a motivic comparison are
additional statements with their own coefficient hypotheses.

\section{The coefficient tensor coalgebra}
\label{v4-arith:bar:tensor-coalgebra}

Fix a procyclic group \(\Gamma\cong\mathbb Z_\ell\), with chosen
generator, and put \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z_\ell[[\Gamma]]\).
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:coefficients:prop:iwasawa} identifies it
with \(\mathbb Z_\ell[[T]]\). If the full cyclotomic group has a
finite factor, that factor must either be retained in the group ring
or removed by a stated choice. It is not part of the displayed
procyclic identification.

\begin{definition}[coefficient bar over \(\Lambda\)]
\label{v4-arith:def:lambda-tensor-coalgebra}
Let \(A\) be an augmented associative dg \(\Lambda\)-algebra, with
augmentation ideal \(I\). In cohomological grading put \(sI=I[1]\).
Its coefficient bar is
\[
 B_\Lambda A=\bigoplus_{n\geq0}(sI)^{\otimes_\Lambda n},
\]
with deconcatenation coproduct and differential \(b=b_1+b_2\), where
\[
 b_1(sa)=-s(da),\qquad
 b_2(sa\otimes sb)=(-1)^{|a|}s(ab).
\]
Extend these maps as coderivations with the Koszul tensor convention.
The empty word is the coaugmentation. Completion, when needed, is
specified as in \eqref{v4-arith:coefficients:eq:completed-bar}.
\end{definition}

The identities \(d^2=0\), Leibniz's rule, and associativity imply
\(b^2=0\), by
Proposition~\ref{v4-prop:bar-coefficient-identities}. This is an
associative coefficient construction. The primitive arithmetic
\(\mathcal V^{\mathrm{prim}}\) is a Hochschild trace class and is
not thereby an augmented algebra \(A\). Its identification with
such an input requires a separately constructed object, product,
and augmentation.

A proposed additional residue differential must act on a common
complex with the bar differential. If a residue complex is tensored
with \(B_\Lambda A\), the tensor is a complex. A coalgebra structure
on it also requires a compatible diagonal on that residue complex.
Names such as an Iwasawa differential or a tame-symbol weight do not
specify degree-one endomorphisms of this tensor.

\begin{question}[a mixed arithmetic differential]
\label{v4-arith:bar:conj:dAr-square-zero}
Construct a common trigraded coalgebra and coderivations
\(d_{\mathrm{Iw}},d_{\mathrm{res}},d_{\mathrm{loc}}\) of the three
coordinate degrees, realizing respectively the intended Iwasawa,
residue, and local bar operations. Do they satisfy
\[
 d_i^2=0,\qquad d_id_j+d_jd_i=0\quad(i\ne j)?
\]
With these tridegrees, the equations are equivalent to
\((d_{\mathrm{Iw}}+d_{\mathrm{res}}+d_{\mathrm{loc}})^2=0\), since
its six homogeneous components have distinct tridegrees.
The first requirement is construction of the displayed maps on the
same carrier. Nilpotence is the subsequent equation.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[archimedean comparison]
\label{v4-arith:thm:archimedean-reduction}
Choose an archimedean realization of the arithmetic coefficient
object and a chiral algebra on a specified complex curve. Construct
and test a map from the realized arithmetic bar to that chiral bar,
including its operations, scalar extension, and unit.
\end{question}

The elementary equality
\(\Omega^j_{\mathbb R/\mathbb R}=0\) for \(j>0\) supplies no such
comparison: the point \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb R\) is not the
complex curve on which chiral collisions take place. Real Galois
cochains also differ from a trivial complex at \(\ell=2\), by
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:coefficients:prop:real-two}.

\section{Graded Koszulness and arithmetic purity}
\label{v4-arith:bar:koszulness}

Let \(A=\bigoplus_{j\geq0}A_j\) be a connected graded algebra over
a field \(k\), so \(A_0=k\), with augmentation onto degree zero.
The internal weight \(j\) is distinct from resolution degree \(i\).
By definition, \(A\) is Koszul when
\(\operatorname{Tor}^A_i(k,k)_j=0\) for \(i\ne j\).
The coefficient bar resolves this Tor group under the hypotheses of
Proposition~\ref{v4-prop:bar-derived-tensor}.

\begin{theorem}[a conditional weight criterion]
\label{v4-arith:thm:arithmetic-koszulness}
Suppose \(k\) has characteristic zero and each \(A_j\) is finite-dimensional.
Assume the total groups
\(\operatorname{Tor}^A_i(k,k)\) are finite-dimensional, and a
bar-compatible operator \(F\) preserves internal weights.
Fix an embedding of its eigenvalue field into \(\mathbb C\) and
a real number \(q>1\). Assume that every eigenvalue on a nonzero
internal-weight-\(j\) bar term has absolute value \(q^{j/2}\).
Then \(A\) is Koszul if and only if every nonzero
\(\operatorname{Tor}^A_i(k,k)\) is pure of weight \(i\), meaning
that its eigenvalues have absolute value \(q^{i/2}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The differential preserves internal weight and commutes with \(F\).
Every eigenvalue on its weight-\(j\) cohomology therefore still has
absolute value \(q^{j/2}\), since eigenvalues on a subquotient come
from the original operator. If the Tor groups are diagonal, their
purity follows. Conversely, \(q^{j/2}\ne q^{i/2}\) when \(i\ne j\).
Purity in weight \(i\) thus forces every off-diagonal summand to
vanish, which is the Koszul condition.
\end{proof}

An arithmetic application requires the graded algebra, its realization,
and the operator. It must also identify internal grading with the
stated arithmetic weights. Frobenius weights,
Hodge filtrations, and Sen weights are attached to different objects.
An equivalence between their purity conditions is not supplied by
this graded argument. The proposed criterion for a \(p\)-adic
Simpson realization of \(\mathcal V^{\mathrm{prim}}\) therefore
remains a comparison problem, including existence of that realization.

\begin{remark}[comparison of realizations]
A de Rham--\'etale comparison theorem has a specified geometric
input and coefficient range. It does not identify arbitrary mixed
Hodge modules over \(\mathbb C\) with Frobenius modules over finite
fields. Any purity argument here must carry those realization
hypotheses before invoking a comparison.
\end{remark}

\section{The primary Mellin character}
\label{v4-arith:bar:heisenberg-zeta}

The positive integers under multiplication form the free commutative
monoid on the primes. Let \(V\) have a basis \(e_m\) indexed by
\(m\geq1\), with energy \(He_m=(\log m)e_m\).
Its Mellin character is the absolutely convergent sum
\(\chi_V(s)=\sum_{m\geq1}m^{-s}\) for \(\operatorname{Re}s>1\).
This grading records multiplicative labels, not homological degree.

\begin{theorem}[the multiplicative-label character]
\label{v4-arith:thm:heisenberg-bar-zeta}
For the displayed graded space,
\[
 \chi_V(s)=\prod_p(1-p^{-s})^{-1}=\zeta(s),
 \qquad \operatorname{Re}s>1.
\]
If a specified primary zero-mode quotient of an arithmetic bar is
isomorphic to \(V\) with these energies, it has the same Mellin
character. The existence of that quotient and isomorphism is an
additional hypothesis.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For finitely many primes, expansion of the geometric series and
unique factorization give the sum over integers supported on those
primes. Absolute convergence of \(\sum_m m^{-s}\) for
\(\operatorname{Re}s>1\) permits the increasing-prime limit.
This proves the Euler product. A grading-preserving isomorphism
preserves the summands and their energies, proving the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{question}[the Bost--Connes comparison]
\label{v4-arith:cor:koszul-dual-BC}
Construct an augmented arithmetic Heisenberg coefficient algebra and
its Koszul dual. Is there a comparison with the Bost--Connes system
that preserves the intended multiplication, module category, and time
evolution? A Mellin character equal to \(\zeta(s)\) is one scalar
condition on such a comparison. It does not determine these objects
or imply their equivalence.
\end{question}

\section{Complementarity and the functional equation}
\label{v4-arith:bar:G-row-complementarity}

Put \(\xi(s)=\tfrac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\),
using the meromorphic continuation of the zeta function. The classical
functional equation is \(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\).
To derive it from an arithmetic duality one needs an evaluation map
that turns the duality into reflection of the Mellin parameter.

\begin{question}[arithmetic complementarity]
\label{v4-arith:thm:G-row-complementarity}
Construct arithmetic Heisenberg and dual coefficient objects with
defined central parameters \(\kappa^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathsf G}\)
and \((\kappa^!)^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathsf G}\), together with an
evaluation to completed Mellin data. Can that evaluation identify
\[
 \kappa^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathsf G}
       +(\kappa^!)^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathsf G}=0
 \quad\text{with}\quad \xi(s)=\xi(1-s)?
\]
It must include the archimedean gamma factor and the polar terms,
and it must identify the two functions rather than merely their
uncompleted Dirichlet coefficients.
\end{question}

The numerical equation on central parameters has no Mellin variable.
Without the requested map it proves neither meromorphic continuation
nor a functional equation. An arithmetic comparison preserving the
full evaluation would explain these analytic operations. An equality
of characters in the half-plane of convergence leaves it open.

\section{Coalgebra duality with Iwasawa coefficients}
\label{v4-arith:bar:positselski-arith}

The coderived category of comodules and the contraderived category
of contramodules use different acyclicity conventions and different
infinite operations. A contramodule over a coalgebra \(C\) has a
contraaction from \(\operatorname{Hom}(C,P)\) to \(P\), obeying
contraassociativity and a counit identity. It is not a comodule with
the arrow reversed. A co--contra comparison requires a specified
coefficient category and the corresponding derived constructions.

\begin{question}[an arithmetic co--contra comparison]
\label{v4-arith:thm:arithmetic-positselski-preview}
For a specified dg coalgebra \(C\) over \(\Lambda\), construct the
comodules, contramodules, and derived categories appropriate to its
topology. Under what projectivity, conilpotence, and completeness
hypotheses does the comparison give an equivalence
\[
 D^{\mathrm{co}}(C\text{-comod})
 \simeq D^{\mathrm{ctr}}(C\text{-contramod})?
\]
Determine separately when the coalgebra unit
\(C\to B_\Lambda\Omega_\Lambda C\) and algebra counit
\(\Omega_\Lambda B_\Lambda A\to A\) are equivalences in the chosen
categories.
\end{question}

The bar takes an augmented algebra as input and the cobar takes a
coaugmented coalgebra. Thus the expression
\(\Omega_\Lambda B_\Lambda C\), with only a coalgebra \(C\) supplied,
is not the coalgebra unit. The coefficient adjunction and its algebra
resolution are constructed in Chapter~\ref{v4-ch:bar-cobar-topology}.
Their extension to the intended arithmetic categories remains
separate. Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:thm:arithmetic-positselski} gives
a lifting criterion once the required adjunction is constructed.

\begin{remark}[the module \(\mu\)-invariant]
For a finitely generated torsion \(\Lambda\)-module \(M\), the
Iwasawa invariant is
\(\mu(M)=\operatorname{length}_{\Lambda_{(\ell)}}M_{(\ell)}\).
An arbitrary coalgebra has no such invariant without choosing a
module to which the definition applies.
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:coefficients:prop:mu} gives a conilpotent
coalgebra with quotient \(\mu=1\), and a nonconilpotent one with
quotient \(\mu=0\). Thus this module invariant cannot replace the
coalgebra hypotheses in the displayed question.
\end{remark}

\section{A concrete arithmetic coefficient bar}
\label{v4-arith:bar:concrete-construction}

\begin{definition}[relative Galois coefficient bar]
\label{v4-arith:def:arith-bar-concrete}
For the strict Galois cochain cospan with specified multiplicative
local conditions over \(R_r\), let \(P_r\) be the complex
\eqref{v4-arith:coefficients:eq:selmer-complex}, with either endpoint
product. Its arithmetic coefficient bar is
\[
 \mathcal B_r=B_{R_r}(R_r\oplus P_r).
\]
The augmentation is the external scalar projection. Its coefficient
completion is \(\varprojlim_r\mathcal B_r\), with tensor target
\eqref{v4-arith:coefficients:eq:completed-bar}, when the local
conditions and maps form a compatible coefficient tower.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[properties of the coefficient construction]
\label{v4-arith:thm:bar-verifications}
The finite-stage bars have square-zero differential and coassociative
counital coproduct. They are conilpotent, and the two endpoint choices
are isomorphic by the integral all-arity comparison of
Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:gluing:chapter}. For a compatible tower that
comparison extends to the specified coefficient completion.
An interpretation as a derived tensor retains the resolution
hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{v4-prop:bar-derived-tensor}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The dg identities for the endpoint products are
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:gluing:prop:products}. The coefficient-bar
identities then apply. Finite words admit only finitely many reduced
cuts. The all-arity maps and their coefficient continuity are the
constructions of Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:gluing:chapter}.
\end{proof}

\section{The arithmetic comparison to be constructed}
\label{v4-arith:bar:summary}

The continuous-cochain bar and the local tame symbols are defined on
their displayed carriers. The first has a multiplication, augmentation,
and ordered bar coproduct. The second changes coefficient fields
along valuations. A chiral arithmetic comparison must relate these
operations, not just their prime labels.

The common residue carrier and mixed maps are asked for in
Question~\ref{v4-arith:bar:conj:dAr-square-zero}. Archimedean realization
requires Question~\ref{v4-arith:thm:archimedean-reduction}. The arithmetic
weight realization is the hypothesis needed for
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:thm:arithmetic-koszulness}.

The Mellin model has its stated character. Its identification with a
primary bar quotient, its Bost--Connes duality, and its completed
functional equation comparison remain separate.

Likewise, the proposed numerical complementarity values
\(0,8,13,250/3,25/3\) require actual arithmetic objects and computed
central parameters before they classify any examples. In particular,
a proposed K3--Mukai Borcherds realization of the value eight is its
own construction. Neither those numerical candidates nor the
Iwasawa module invariant settle the coefficient, geometric, and
co--contra comparisons above.
