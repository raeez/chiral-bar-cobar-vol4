\chapter{Bost--Connes Modular Spectrum and the Hilbert--P\'olya Comparison}
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:hp-operator}

The critical Bost--Connes state gives a self-adjoint modular generator
whose eigenvalues are $\tfrac12\log q$, $q\in\mathbb Q_{>0}$.
A specified reducing occupation sector instead has the simple spectrum
$\{\log m:m\geq1\}$. We construct both carriers and an oscillator
embedding in the same GNS space. The embedding preserves logarithmic
prime energies and positive squared-semigroup shifts. Its oscillators
are Hilbert-space operators, not operators affiliated with the
Bost--Connes observable algebra. Ordinary conformal energy is a separate
grading. These distinctions leave a precise arithmetic question:
construct a self-adjoint operator whose normalized determinant is
$\xi_{\mathbb R}$, together with the Petersson, chiral and trace
comparisons that connect it to the arithmetic Fock space.

\section{Bost--Connes $C^*$-Dynamical Setup}
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:bc-setup}

We use the global algebra and positive-temperature KMS convention of
\Cref{v4-arith:w5-direct:def:BC}. The coefficient algebra factors over
primes, but the isometries act across those factors.

\begin{definition}[global and local Bost--Connes algebras]
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:def:bc-algebra}
Put $D=C^*(\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z)$, with unitaries $e(r)$ satisfying
$e(r+s)=e(r)e(s)$ and $e(r)^*=e(-r)$. For $n\geq1$ set
\[
 \alpha_n(e(r))=\frac1n\sum_{nt=r}e(t),\qquad
 C^*_{\mathbb Q}=D\rtimes_\alpha\mathbb N^\times.
\]
The generators $\mu_n$ are isometries, with
\[
 \mu_m\mu_n=\mu_{mn},\quad \mu_n^*\mu_n=1,\quad
 \mu_n d\mu_n^*=\alpha_n(d),\quad \mu_n^*e(r)\mu_n=e(nr).
\]
In particular $P_n:=\mu_n\mu_n^*=n^{-1}\sum_{nt=0}e(t)$ is a
projection. The time evolution is
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:eq:bc-time}
 \sigma_t(\mu_n)=n^{it}\mu_n,\qquad
 \sigma_t(e(r))=e(r),\qquad \sigma_t(\mu_n^*)=n^{-it}\mu_n^*.
\end{equation}
For a prime $p$, let $D_p=C^*(\mathbb Q_p/\mathbb Z_p)$ and
$C^*_{\mathbb Q,p}=C^*(D_p,\mu_p)\subset C^*_{\mathbb Q}$.
The primary decomposition of $\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$ gives the
coefficient tensor product $D=\bigotimes_pD_p$, with local units.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[the coefficient tensor and the cross-prime action]
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:rem:wave4-ref}
The global crossed product is described in Laca--Raeburn,
arXiv:math/9911134, \S2, p.~3. If $r$ is $\ell$-primary and
$\ell\ne p$, multiplication by $p$ is invertible on that primary
group, and the relations give
\[
 \mu_p e(r)=e(p^{-1}r)\mu_p,\qquad
 \alpha_p(e(r))=P_p e(p^{-1}r).
\]
For example $\mu_2^*e(1/3)\mu_2=e(2/3)$, whereas commuting local
factors would give $e(1/3)$. In the critical state below,
$\|(e(1/3)-e(2/3))\Omega_1\|^2=2$, so these elements differ.
Thus the algebras $C^*_{\mathbb Q,p}$ do not assemble by a
generatorwise tensor product. The algebra generated only by the
isometries has the distinct Toeplitz tensor-product description of
Connes, \emph{Noncommutative Geometry}, V, \S11, Proposition~7,
pp.~530--531. Neither that description nor the coefficient tensor
asserts a product representation of the character-enlarged local
algebras.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[the critical KMS state]
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:lem:kms-1}
There is a unique KMS$_1$ state $\varphi_1$ on $C^*_{\mathbb Q}$.
It satisfies
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \varphi_1(e(r))&=\delta_{r,0},\\
 \varphi_1(\mu_a e(r)\mu_b^*)&=\delta_{a,b}a^{-1}\delta_{r,0},\\
 \varphi_1(\mu_n^*\mu_m e(r))&=\delta_{n,m}\delta_{r,0}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
In its GNS representation the state extends to a faithful normal state
on $M=\pi_1(C^*_{\mathbb Q})''$, a hyperfinite factor of type III$_1$;
the cyclic vector $\Omega_1$ is separating for $M$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Existence, uniqueness and the factor statement are Connes, V, \S11,
Theorem~5(a), p.~528; its character formula at $\beta=1$ is
$\delta_{r,0}$. Time invariance kills monomials of nonzero time
weight. At equal indices the KMS identity
$\varphi_1(xy)=\varphi_1(y\sigma_i(x))$ gives the factor $a^{-1}$
in the second formula, whereas $\mu_n^*\mu_n=1$ gives the third.
The faithful normal GNS extension and separating vector are the
modular realization used in
\Cref{v4-arith:w5-direct:thm:HPAr-elementary}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the critical partition value]
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:rem:partition-at-one}
The occupation partition function is $\zeta(\beta)$ for $\Re\beta>1$.
Its Laurent expansion is
$\zeta(\beta)=(\beta-1)^{-1}+\gamma+O(\beta-1)$, where $\gamma$
is the Euler--Mascheroni constant. Thus its Laurent finite part at
one is $\gamma$; the trace itself diverges there. Identifying this
finite part with a determinant normalization would require a
separate comparison. The full critical GNS heat operator is not
trace class, as its spectrum below shows.
\end{remark}

\section{GNS Representation and the Modular Operator}
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:gns-modular}

The rational time weights give an explicit basis that also records
the character action. It determines the modular sign and all
operator domains directly.

\begin{definition}[GNS triple]
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:def:gns}
Let $(\mathcal H_1,\pi_1,\Omega_1)$ be the GNS triple of $\varphi_1$.
Thus $\mathcal H_1$ is the completion of
$C^*_{\mathbb Q}/\{x:\varphi_1(x^*x)=0\}$ with inner product
$\langle x\Omega_1,y\Omega_1\rangle=\varphi_1(y^*x)$,
linear in the first variable; $\pi_1$ acts by left multiplication.
We suppress $\pi_1$ on algebra elements when acting on this space.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[rational-character basis and Hilbert tensor product]
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:lem:gns-restricted-tensor}
For $q=a/b\in\mathbb Q_{>0}$ in lowest terms and $r\in\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$,
put $v_{q,r}=\sqrt b\,\mu_a e(r)\mu_b^*\Omega_1$. These vectors are
an orthonormal basis. Consequently
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal H_1&\cong\ell^2(\mathbb Q_{>0})\widehat\otimes
 \ell^2(\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z)\\
 &\cong\bigotimes'_p\bigl[\ell^2(p^{\mathbb Z})\widehat\otimes
 \ell^2(\mathbb Q_p/\mathbb Z_p)\bigr].
 \end{aligned}
\]
with reference vectors $\delta_1\otimes\delta_0$ and
$\Omega_1=v_{1,0}$. For $d=\gcd(n,b)$ the generator actions are
\[
 e(s)v_{q,r}=v_{q,r+as},\qquad
 \mu_n v_{q,r}=d^{-1/2}\sum_{dt=r}v_{nq,t}.
\]
This is a tensor decomposition of Hilbert spaces, not of the local
BC representations.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Different $q$ have different time characters and are orthogonal by
state invariance. For fixed reduced $a/b$,
\[
 \langle v_{q,r},v_{q,s}\rangle
 =b\varphi_1(\mu_b e(-s)\mu_a^*\mu_a e(r)\mu_b^*)
 =\varphi_1(e(r-s))=\delta_{r,s}.
\]
Every monomial of ratio $a/b$ has indices $ac,bc$, and
$\mu_{ac}e(r)\mu_{bc}^*=\mu_a\alpha_c(e(r))\mu_b^*$ is a finite
linear combination of these basis vectors. Such monomials span a
dense algebra, proving completeness. Unique prime factorization of
positive rationals and the primary decomposition of $\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$
give the Hilbert tensor product. Finally
$e(s)\mu_a=\mu_a e(as)$ proves the first action. Reducing $na/b$
cancels $d$; expansion of $\alpha_d(e(r))$ and the normalization
$\sqrt{b/d}$ of the resulting basis give the coefficient $d^{-1/2}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Tomita conjugation and the modular operator]
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:lem:modular}
On the finite basis span the Tomita operator and its polar decomposition
$S_1=J_1\Delta_1^{1/2}$ satisfy
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:eq:Delta-basis}
 S_1v_{q,r}=q^{-1/2}v_{q^{-1},-r},\qquad
 J_1v_{q,r}=v_{q^{-1},-r},\qquad
 \Delta_1v_{q,r}=q^{-1}v_{q,r}.
\end{equation}
Here $J_1$ is conjugate-linear, and
\[
 \Delta_1^{it}\pi_1(x)\Delta_1^{-it}=\pi_1(\sigma_{-t}(x)),
 \qquad \Delta_1^{it}\Omega_1=\Omega_1.
\]
For $u=\sum c_{q,r}v_{q,r}$ the maximal domain of $\Delta_1$ is
$\sum q^{-2}|c_{q,r}|^2<\infty$; the domain of $S_1$ is
$\sum q^{-1}|c_{q,r}|^2<\infty$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Taking adjoints of $\sqrt b\mu_a e(r)\mu_b^*$ gives
$\sqrt b\mu_b e(-r)\mu_a^*=q^{-1/2}\sqrt a\mu_b e(-r)\mu_a^*$.
The resulting conjugate-linear weighted permutation has the stated
polar decomposition and domains; truncation in the basis gives its
closed extension. Equivalently, the modular sign is Connes, V, \S11,
Proposition~4, p.~527. Applying the displayed diagonal unitaries to
the two generator actions verifies that sign directly.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[BC log-modular generator]
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:def:N-BC}
Define $N_{\mathrm{BC}}=-\tfrac12\log\Delta_1$ by self-adjoint
functional calculus. Its action is
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:eq:N-BC}
 \begin{aligned}
 N_{\mathrm{BC}}v_{q,r}&=\tfrac12\log q\,v_{q,r},\\
 \operatorname{Dom}N_{\mathrm{BC}}
 &=\left\{\sum c_{q,r}v_{q,r}:\sum(\log q)^2|c_{q,r}|^2<\infty\right\}.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[self-adjointness and the full modular spectrum]
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:thm:N-BC-self-adjoint}
The finite basis span $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{BC}}$ is an essential
self-adjointness core for $N_{\mathrm{BC}}$. Its point spectrum is
$\tfrac12\log\mathbb Q_{>0}$, each eigenvalue has countably infinite
multiplicity, and its closed spectrum is $\mathbb R$. All its spectral
measures are pure point. The point spectrum of $\Delta_1$ is
$\mathbb Q_{>0}$, and its closed spectrum is $[0,\infty)$;
zero is not an eigenvalue.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a real diagonal sequence, the adjoint of the finite-span operator
has exactly the square-summability domain in
\eqref{v4-arith:w5-b:eq:N-BC}; truncations converge in its graph norm.
This proves self-adjointness of the closure. Each $q$ has the
countably infinite character basis. A diagonal operator's spectrum
is the closure of its diagonal values: outside that closure its
coordinatewise inverse is bounded, whereas coordinate vectors give
approximate eigenvectors at every limit point. Positive rationals
are dense in $(0,\infty)$, proving both closed-spectrum statements.
The spectral projections act coordinatewise, so all spectral
measures are atomic. All eigenvalues of $\Delta_1$ are positive.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[modular and occupation energies]
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:rem:N-BC-not}
The full generator has negative as well as positive energies, and
$e^{-tN_{\mathrm{BC}}}$ is unbounded for every $t>0$.
The weight $k\log p$ occurs on the vectors $v_{p^{2k},0}$.
Restricting to their positive-integer square sector gives the
occupation model below. It is this specified restriction, rather
than the full modular spectrum, that realizes the logarithmic
prime weights in \Cref{v4-arith:hpar-det:def:candidate-weights}.
Neither operator has the zeta-zero divisor by this construction.
\end{remark}
\section{Arithmetic Heisenberg Fock and Its Embedding into the GNS Space}
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:primary-heisenberg}

The occupation sector fixes the zero-mode normalization. An explicit
orthonormal family in the modular fixed-point algebra then supplies
oscillator multiplicities inside the same GNS representation.

\begin{definition}[complex arithmetic Fock carrier]
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:def:fock}
Choose positive integers $d_p$ and $d_\infty$ as component counts.
Let $J$ consist of the modes $(p,k,a)$, $k\geq1$, $1\leq a\leq d_p$,
and $(\infty,k,a)$, $k\geq1$, $1\leq a\leq d_\infty$.
Write $\mathcal C=\mathbb N_0^{(J)}$ for finite occupation configurations
and $\mathcal F_{\mathrm{osc}}=\ell^2(\mathcal C)$ for the complex
bosonic Hilbert space with orthonormal number states $|\mathbf n\rangle$.
Equivalently it is the restricted tensor product of the per-prime
Fock spaces and the archimedean complex Fock space, with vacuum
reference vectors. Put
\[
 L_w=\ell^2(\mathbb N^\times,m^{-1}dm),\qquad
 \epsilon_m=\sqrt m\,\delta_m,\qquad
 \mathcal F^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathrm{Heis}}
   =\mathcal F_{\mathrm{osc}}\widehat\otimes L_w.
\]
The primary core $\mathcal F^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathrm{Heis},\mathrm{prim}}$
is the finite span of $|\mathbf n\rangle\otimes\epsilon_m$.
This specifies a complex Hilbert realization of the oscillator
algebra. Comparing it with the native per-prime chiral objects of
\Cref{v4-arith:drinfeld-kapranov} and the archimedean theory of
\Cref{v4-arith:polyakov} requires complex realization and chiral
structure maps in addition to this Hilbert space.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[weighted occupation embedding]
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:lem:zero-mode-embeds-gns}
Let $K_2=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{\mu_{m^2}\Omega_1:m\geq1\}$.
The map
\[
 \iota_0:L_w\longrightarrow K_2,\qquad
 \iota_0\delta_m=m^{-1/2}\mu_{m^2}\Omega_1
\]
is unitary onto $K_2$. This space reduces $N_{\mathrm{BC}}$, and
$N_{\mathrm{occ}}:=N_{\mathrm{BC}}|_{K_2}$ is unitarily equivalent to
\[
 H_w\delta_m=(\log m)\delta_m,\qquad
 \operatorname{Dom}H_w
 =\left\{c:\sum_m\frac{(\log m)^2|c_m|^2}{m}<\infty\right\}.
\]
The isometries $R_n\delta_m=\sqrt n\,\delta_{nm}$ satisfy
\[
 R_n^*\delta_m=
 \begin{cases}n^{-1/2}\delta_{m/n},&n\mid m,\\0,&n\nmid m,\end{cases}
 \qquad \iota_0R_n=\pi_1(\mu_{n^2})\iota_0.
\]
Their Hilbert adjoints correspond to compressed BC adjoints.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The vectors $\mu_{m^2}\Omega_1=v_{m^2,0}$ are orthonormal eigenvectors
of energy $\log m$, whereas $\|\delta_m\|^2=1/m$ in $L_w$.
This proves the isometry, its range, and the maximal-domain identity
by the coordinate formula. Their span is invariant under every
spectral projection, so $K_2$ reduces the generator. The shift and
adjoint formulas follow on the normalized basis $\epsilon_m$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[symmetry-invariant BC--GNS Heisenberg lift]
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:def:bc-gns-heisenberg-lift}
Let $G=\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{\times}$ act by
$\beta_u(e(r))=e(ur)$ and $\beta_u(\mu_n)=\mu_n$.
The relations and the state formula show that this is a
state-preserving action. Hence
$U_u(x\Omega_1)=\beta_u(x)\Omega_1$ defines GNS unitaries.
They are strongly continuous: character orbits factor through finite
quotients on the dense monomial algebra, and unitarity extends
continuity to every vector. Define the Hilbert projection
\[
 P_G=\int_GU_u\,du
\]
using normalized Haar measure. Haar invariance gives
$P_G^*=P_G=P_G^2$ and $\operatorname{Ran}P_G=\mathcal H_1^G$.
A symmetry-invariant BC--GNS Heisenberg lift consists of a closed
$E\subset\mathcal H_1^G$, containing $K_2$, and a unitary
$\iota:\mathcal F^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathrm{Heis}}\to E$ with
$\iota(|\mathbf0\rangle\otimes\delta_m)=\iota_0\delta_m$,
transporting the oscillator annihilation and creation operators
with their maximal domains and primary core. This definition uses
the specified Galois symmetry projection $P_G$, not the occupation
projection $P_K$ onto $K_2$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[an explicit same-GNS Fock embedding]
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:lem:fock-embeds-gns}
Such a lift exists after a choice of an auxiliary prime $\ell$ and
an enumeration of $J$. Put $P_k=\mu_{\ell^k}\mu_{\ell^k}^*$ and
\[
 h_k=\frac{P_{k+1}-\ell^{-1}P_k}
 {\sqrt{\ell^{-k-1}(1-\ell^{-1})}},\qquad k\geq0.
\]
Enumerate $J=\{j_1,j_2,\ldots\}$ and let $r_i$ be the increasing
ordinary primes. Set
\[
 c(\mathbf n)=\prod_i r_i^{n_{j_i}},\qquad
 f_{\mathbf0}=1,\qquad f_{\mathbf n}=h_{c(\mathbf n)-2}
 \quad(\mathbf n\ne\mathbf0).
\]
The logarithmic finite-place energy is encoded by
\[
 t(\mathbf n)=\prod_{p,k,a}p^{k n_{p,k,a}}.
\]
Archimedean modes contribute no factor to $t$. The vectors
\[
 v_{\mathbf n,m}=\mu_{(m t(\mathbf n))^2}f_{\mathbf n}\Omega_1
\]
are orthonormal, and the unitary onto their closed span $E$ is
$\iota(|\mathbf n\rangle\otimes\epsilon_m)=v_{\mathbf n,m}$.
Moreover $E$ reduces $N_{\mathrm{BC}}$, and
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:consumer56:logarithmic-energy}
 \iota^*N_{\mathrm{BC}}|_E\iota
 =I\otimes H_w+\sum_{p,k,a}k\log p\,K_{p,k,a}\otimes I,
\end{equation}
where $K_j|\mathbf n\rangle=n_j|\mathbf n\rangle$.
In normalized coordinates its maximal domain is
\[
 \left\{z:\sum_{\mathbf n,m}
 (\log m+\log t(\mathbf n))^2|z_{\mathbf n,m}|^2<\infty\right\}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The projections satisfy $P_jP_k=P_{\max(j,k)}$ and
$\varphi_1(P_k)=\ell^{-k}$. Direct expansion gives
$\varphi_1(h_k)=0$ and $\varphi_1(h_k^*h_k)=1$.
If $j<k$, both projections defining $h_k$ lie under $P_{j+1}$;
therefore $h_jh_k$ is a scalar multiple of $h_k$ and has expectation
zero. Thus $1,h_0,h_1,\ldots$ is orthonormal. Unique factorization
makes $\mathbf n\mapsto c(\mathbf n)$ injective, with value one
only at the vacuum, so the $f_{\mathbf n}$ are orthonormal too.
They are fixed by time evolution and by $G$.

The vector $v_{\mathbf n,m}$ has energy $\log(m t(\mathbf n))$.
Different energies are orthogonal. At equal values
$m t(\mathbf n)=m't(\mathbf n')$, cancellation of the common isometry
reduces the inner product to
$\varphi_1(f_{\mathbf n'}^*f_{\mathbf n})=\delta_{\mathbf n,\mathbf n'}$;
then $m=m'$. This proves the isometry and its vacuum normalization.
Every vector is $G$-fixed. The closed span of these vectors is invariant
under all spectral projections, proving reduction and the stated
diagonal maximal domain. The choices of $\ell$ and the enumeration
are part of the construction; no canonical chiral identification is
asserted.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[closed oscillators and their normalization]
\label{v4-arith:consumer56:CCR}
On $E$ define
\[
 b_jv_{\mathbf n,m}=\sqrt{n_j}\,v_{\mathbf n-\mathbf e_j,m},\qquad
 b_j^*v_{\mathbf n,m}=\sqrt{n_j+1}\,v_{\mathbf n+\mathbf e_j,m},
\]
with the first vector zero when $n_j=0$. Their maximal domains are,
respectively, $\sum n_j|z_{\mathbf n,m}|^2<\infty$ and
$\sum(n_j+1)|z_{\mathbf n,m}|^2<\infty$. They are closed mutual
adjoints and have the common invariant core
$\mathcal D_E=\operatorname{span}\{v_{\mathbf n,m}\}$.
On this core $[b_j,b_{j'}^*]=\delta_{jj'}I_E$ and like operators
commute. Every $\mu_{m^2}\Omega_1$ is annihilated by every $b_j$.
For $j=(p,k,a)$, including $p=\infty$, set
\[
 A_k^{(p,a)}=\sqrt{k}\,b_{p,k,a},\qquad
 A_{-k}^{(p,a)}=\sqrt{k}\,b_{p,k,a}^*.
\]
Then $[A_k^{(p,a)},A_{-l}^{(q,b)}]=k\delta_{kl}\delta_{pq}\delta_{ab}I_E$.
The normalized creation monomial is
\[
 \mathrm{Asc}(\mathbf n)=
 \prod_{p,k,a}\frac{(A_{-k}^{(p,a)})^{n_{p,k,a}}}
 {\sqrt{k^{n_{p,k,a}}n_{p,k,a}!}},\qquad
 \iota(|\mathbf n\rangle\otimes\delta_m)
 =m^{-1/2}\mathrm{Asc}(\mathbf n)\mu_{m^2}\Omega_1.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Taking inner products of coordinate vectors gives the adjoint
formulas. Square summability with the indicated weights gives the
closed maximal operators; finite coordinate truncations converge in
their graph norms. Their commutators follow by applying both orders
to each number vector. Iteration of creation gives
$\sqrt{n_j!}$, and $A_{-k}=\sqrt{k}b_j^*$ supplies the further
$k^{n_j/2}$ factor. This proves the displayed normalization.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[symmetry, semigroup shifts and observable obstructions]
\label{v4-arith:consumer56:observable-obstruction}
The projections $P_K$, $P_E$ and $P_G$ are proper Hilbert-space
projections, and none belongs to $M$.
The oscillators extended by zero on $E^\perp$ commute with $P_G$,
including their domains; their commutator on
$\mathcal D_E\oplus E^\perp$ is $P_E$.
The positive squared-semigroup action satisfies
\[
 \pi_1(\mu_{a^2})v_{\mathbf n,m}=v_{\mathbf n,am},\qquad
 \pi_1(\mu_{a^2})|_E=\iota(I\otimes R_a)\iota^*.
\]
Its Hilbert adjoint is the compressed adjoint. Compression to $E$
does not give a representation of all BC observables. No nonzero
closed densely defined operator affiliated with $M$, with
$\Omega_1$ in its domain, can annihilate $\Omega_1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $E\subset\mathcal H_1^G$, the zero extensions preserve the
$P_G$ decomposition. The vector $(e(1/3)-e(2/3))\Omega_1$ has
squared norm two and Galois average zero, so $P_G\ne I$.
Also $e(1/2)\Omega_1$ is fixed by $G$ and orthogonal to $K_2$:
its expectation is zero, and all other occupation vectors have
nonzero energy. Thus $P_G\ne P_K$. A nonvacuum oscillator vector
is orthogonal to $K_2$, so $P_E\ne P_K$.
In particular $P_Kb_j^*\Omega_1=0$ but
$b_j^*P_K\Omega_1=b_j^*\Omega_1\ne0$; the occupation projection
cannot replace the symmetry projection in the lift.

Multiplication of the positive isometries proves the shift formula.
For $a>1$, however,
$\|\mu_{a^2}^*\Omega_1\|^2=a^{-2}$ and its modular energy is
$-\log a$. It is orthogonal to $E$, whose energies are nonnegative,
whereas the compressed adjoint kills $\Omega_1$.
For a prime $r\ne\ell$, every $P_k$ is an average of characters
of $\ell$-power order, hence $\varphi_1(P_ke(1/r))=0$.
Only energy-zero vectors of $E$ can pair with $e(1/r)\Omega_1$;
those pairings reduce to the same character averages, including
$\varphi_1(e(1/r))=0$. Thus $P_Ee(1/r)\Omega_1=0$.
A compressed unitary kills a unit vector, which rules out
multiplicativity of compression.

Finally, if $B$ is affiliated with $M$, its kernel projection
$Q$ belongs to $M$. If $B\Omega_1=0$, then
$(I-Q)\Omega_1=0$; separation gives $Q=I$, hence $B=0$.
The same argument says that a projection in $M$ fixing $\Omega_1$
is $I$. Each of the proper projections above fixes $\Omega_1$,
so none belongs to $M$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Hilbert realization and additional arithmetic structure]
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:rem:embedding-domain}
The weighted occupation embedding and the chosen complex oscillator
lift are explicit. They identify neither an Eisenstein or ramified
summand nor a native chiral algebra inside the GNS space. Such
identifications require representation maps, coefficient
realizations and compatible pairings. Likewise, the external
oscillator operators are not affiliated BC observables. The
positive squared shifts commute with them on the primary core,
but the full BC action and its adjoints do not preserve that
oscillator representation. These are substantive restrictions on
any arithmetic determinant-trace comparison using $E$.
\end{remark}
\section{The Primary-Heisenberg Twist and the Determinant-Trace Operator}
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:twist}

The occupation and oscillator constructions supply a Hilbert space
and several gradings. The zero-divisor problem asks for a different
operator, with a prescribed determinant. An isometry of Hilbert
spaces does not identify these generators.

\subsection{Statement of the Twist}
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:twist:statement}

\begin{definition}[twist data and the separate conformal grading]
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:def:twist}
On $\mathcal F_{\mathrm{osc}}$ let
\[
 L_0|\mathbf n\rangle=\Bigl(\sum_{p,k,a}k n_{p,k,a}\Bigr)|\mathbf n\rangle,
 \qquad G_F=L_0\otimes I+I\otimes H_w,
\]
where the sum includes the archimedean modes. These are nonnegative
self-adjoint diagonal operators on their maximal square-summability
domains, with the grading convention of
\Cref{v4-arith:hpvbconv:def:L0-domain}. With $[A_k,A_{-k}]=kI$, the contribution to $L_0$ is
$A_{-k}A_k=k b_k^*b_k$, with no additional factor of $k$.

A primary-Heisenberg twist consists of the following comparison data.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A complex Hilbert realization $\mathcal H_\xi$ of the proposed
arithmetic primary sector, and a specified isometry into the
cuspidal Petersson Hilbert space of
\Cref{v4-arith:thm:P3-resolution}, with fixed central character and
Tamagawa normalization. Compatibility with its native Koszul pairing
is an additional pairing identity, as in
\Cref{v4-arith:cl:cj:koszul-Petersson-compat}; the positivity of the
Petersson integral alone does not construct this realization.
\item A unitary $U:\mathcal F^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathrm{Heis}}\to\mathcal H_\xi$
whose restriction to the primary core represents the proposed
arithmetic comparison. Put $\mathcal D_\xi=U\mathcal F^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathrm{Heis},\mathrm{prim}}$.
\item A self-adjoint operator $A$ with full domain
$\operatorname{Dom}A\subset\mathcal H_\xi$, for which
$\mathcal D_\xi\subset\operatorname{Dom}A$ is an invariant core.
Its domain is not identified with that core.
\item The transported grading $G_\xi=UG_FU^*$, with
$\operatorname{Dom}G_\xi=U\operatorname{Dom}G_F$. Thus
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:eq:twist-intertwine}
 G_\xi U=UG_F\quad\hbox{on }\operatorname{Dom}G_F.
\end{equation}
Any chain-level, chiral or arithmetic-observable interpretation of
$U$ requires the corresponding maps and identities as further data.
The grading identity does not assert $A=G_\xi$ or intertwine $A$
with $N_{\mathrm{BC}}$.
\end{enumerate}
Define
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:eq:H-HP-def}
 H_{\mathrm{HP}}:=A,
\end{equation}
with its full self-adjoint domain, and
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:eq:Theta-xi-def}
 \Theta_\xi:=\tfrac12I+iA,\qquad
 \operatorname{Dom}\Theta_\xi=\operatorname{Dom}A.
\end{equation}
The same-GNS representative is
$A_E=\iota U^*AU\iota^*$ on $E$, with domain
$\iota U^*\operatorname{Dom}A$. This is a domain-preserving unitary
transport of $A$, not an identification with the modular restriction.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[two spectral obstructions]
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:rem:what-iv-says}
The logarithmic energy in \eqref{v4-arith:consumer56:logarithmic-energy}
uses $k\log p$, whereas the conformal grading uses positive integers
$k$. An intertwiner carrying a nonzero integer-energy one-particle
state into the same modular representation would require
$k=\tfrac12\log q$ for $q\in\mathbb Q_{>0}$. This is impossible.
Here is an elementary proof for the integer case needed here.
For fixed positive integer $k$ and $n\geq1$, set
\[
 F_n(x)=\frac{x^n(2k-x)^n}{n!},\qquad
 I_n=\int_0^{2k}e^xF_n(x)\,dx.
\]
All endpoint derivatives of $F_n$ are integers: derivatives of order
less than $n$ vanish, and expansion at either endpoint makes every
remaining derivative an integer multiple of $j!/n!$, $j\geq n$.
Repeated integration by parts therefore gives
$I_n\in\mathbb Z+e^{2k}\mathbb Z$. But
\[
 0<I_n\leq 2k e^{2k}\frac{k^{2n}}{n!}\longrightarrow0.
\]
If $e^{2k}$ were rational, clearing its fixed denominator would
produce positive integers tending to zero. Thus ordinary finite
or archimedean oscillator energy cannot be the modular generator.
Archimedean modes in the construction above have zero modular
weight and a separate positive conformal grading; their multiplicities
also preclude a simple occupation partition identity on all of $E$.

A second obstruction prevents retaining the occupation vacuum as a
zero eigenvector of $A$: then $\Theta_\xi$ would have eigenvalue
$1/2$ and its determinant would vanish there. On the other hand,
$\eta(1/2)=\sum_{n\geq1}((2n-1)^{-1/2}-(2n)^{-1/2})>0$,
and $\zeta(1/2)=\eta(1/2)/(1-\sqrt2)\ne0$. All remaining factors
in $\xi_{\mathbb R}(1/2)$ are nonzero. Hence the zero-divisor
operator cannot literally inherit that vacuum energy.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Determinant-Trace Requirement: Hadamard Inversion}
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:twist:hadamard-inversion}

Write $\rho=\beta_\rho+i\gamma_\rho$ for the nontrivial zeros,
with multiplicity. The completed function is
$\xi_{\mathbb R}(s)=\tfrac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)$.
Its genus-one factorization is
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:eq:hadamard}
 \xi_{\mathbb R}(s)=\frac12e^{Bs}\prod_\rho(1-s/\rho)e^{s/\rho},
 \qquad B=-1-\frac\gamma2+\frac12\log(4\pi).
\end{equation}
Here $\gamma$ is the Euler--Mascheroni constant. The prime side
appears in the logarithmic derivative, for $\Re s>1$:
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:eq:log-deriv-xi}
 \begin{aligned}
 -\frac{\xi_{\mathbb R}'(s)}{\xi_{\mathbb R}(s)}
 &=-\frac1s-\frac1{s-1}+\frac12\log\pi\\
 &\quad-\frac12\frac{\Gamma'(s/2)}{\Gamma(s/2)}
 +\sum_{n\geq2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^s}.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The product and the explicit formula relate the zero and prime
distributions; they do not give a unitary conjugacy between the
occupation generator and a zero-spectrum operator. The arithmetic
problem is to construct the latter from the specified primary
sector and prove its determinant and trace comparisons.

\subsection{The Hilbert--P\'olya Hypothesis and Its Consequences}
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:twist:consequences}

\begin{hypothesis}[arithmetic determinant-trace realization]
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:hyp:twist-existence}
There exist twist data as in \Cref{v4-arith:w5-b:def:twist}, together
with a complete orthonormal eigenbasis $(u_\rho)$ indexed by every
zero occurrence, such that
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:eq:spec-A}
 \begin{gathered}
 Au_\rho=\gamma_\rho u_\rho,\qquad
 (\tfrac12I+iA)u_\rho=\rho u_\rho,\\
 \operatorname{Dom}A=
 \left\{\sum c_\rho u_\rho:\sum\gamma_\rho^2|c_\rho|^2<\infty\right\}.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Thus the original zero multiplicities and zero placement are part
of the requirement, not merely the multiset of ordinates.
Use the normalized determinant prescription
\eqref{v4-arith:sc:normalized-det-formula} under the unitary
$u_\rho\mapsto\delta_\rho$.
The zero symmetry is represented by an involutive unitary $R_\xi$
which exchanges the occurrences $\rho$ and $1-\rho$, preserves
$\operatorname{Dom}A$, and satisfies $R_\xi A R_\xi=-A$.
Identifying $U^*R_\xi U$ with a native arithmetic $S$-modular
operation is an additional specified comparison, including its
carrier and domain.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{remark}[the arithmetic comparison problem]
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:rem:equals-HPAr}
The eigenvalue condition implies RH and gives the abstract normalized
Hilbert--P\'olya realization of
\Cref{v4-arith:w5-direct:thm:HP-form}. Conversely, under RH the
diagonal zero-occurrence construction gives that abstract operator.
It does not construct the Petersson realization, native chiral maps,
or arithmetic symmetry comparison in the hypothesis. Those are the
additional requirements of the arithmetic formulation
\Cref{v4-arith:rh:hyp:HPAr}. Transport by $\iota U^*$ places a
specified realization in the same GNS Hilbert space while retaining
its domain, but supplies no equality with $N_{\mathrm{BC}}$ or
$N_{\mathrm{occ}}$.
\end{remark}

\section{Determinant Identity: Hadamard Factorisation}
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:determinant}

\subsection{The normalized genus-one determinant}
\label{v4-arith:sc:determinant}

Let $Z$ contain each zero $\rho$ of $\xi$ with its multiplicity.
On $K_Z=\ell^2(Z)$, define
\[
 (\Theta_Zu)_\rho=\rho u_\rho,\qquad
 \operatorname{Dom}\Theta_Z=\{u:\sum_\rho|\rho u_\rho|^2<\infty\}.
\]
This is a closed normal operator. Its inverse is bounded, compact,
and Hilbert--Schmidt: $\xi(0)=1/2$, the zeros are discrete, and
$\sum_\rho|\rho|^{-2}<\infty$.

\begin{theorem}[complete determinant normalization]
\label{v4-arith:sc:normalized-det}
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:prop:det-formal}
Define the second regularized Fredholm determinant of the diagonal
Hilbert--Schmidt operator $-s\Theta_Z^{-1}$ by
\[
 D_2(s)=\det{}_2(1-s\Theta_Z^{-1})
       :=\prod_{\rho\in Z}(1-s/\rho)e^{s/\rho}.
\]
With $B=-1-\gamma/2+\tfrac12\log(4\pi)$, set
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_Z(s):=\tfrac12e^{Bs}D_2(s).
 \label{v4-arith:sc:normalized-det-formula}
\end{equation}
This entire function equals $\xi(s)$.
It is the unique entire function of order at most one with this
zero divisor, $\Delta_Z(0)=1/2$, and $\Delta_Z'(0)/\Delta_Z(0)=B$.
Off the zero divisor it satisfies
\[
 (\log\Delta_Z)''(s)
       =-\operatorname{Tr}(\Theta_Z-s)^{-2}.
\]
The trace is an ordinary absolutely convergent trace.
Unitary conjugacy of $\Theta_Z$, including its domain, preserves
this determinant prescription.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $|w|\le1/2$, $\log(1-w)+w=O(|w|^2)$.
The inverse-square sum proves normal convergence of the product
on every compact set, independently of the enumeration of zeros.
Its value and derivative at zero are $1$ and $0$.
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:pm:constant} gives the product for $\xi$
with exactly the factor $e^{Bs}/2$. This proves the identity.
Hadamard factorization writes every other entire function of order
at most one with this divisor as $e^{a+bs}D_2(s)$.
The two initial conditions give $e^a=1/2$ and $b=B$.
Twice differentiating the normally convergent logarithmic product
gives $-\sum_\rho(\rho-s)^{-2}$.
The inverse-square estimate proves absolute trace convergence.
Unitary conjugacy preserves the diagonal eigenvalues and their
multiplicities, hence every factor in the prescribed product.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[the divisor leaves an exponential ambiguity]
\label{v4-arith:sc:det-ambiguity}
For every $c\in\mathbb C$, the function $e^{cs}\xi(s)$ has the same
divisor, the same value at zero, and order at most one.
Its logarithmic derivative at zero is $B+c$.
Thus a divisor condition and a value normalization do not imply
the determinant identity. Both normalizations in
\eqref{v4-arith:sc:normalized-det-formula} are necessary.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The exponential has no zero, has value one at zero, and contributes
$c$ to the logarithmic derivative. Multiplication preserves the
stated order bound.
\end{proof}

The prescription above uses no complex powers of $s-\Theta_Z$.
A determinant defined by such powers requires a spectral cut and
meromorphic continuation of its trace. Agreement with $\Delta_Z$
requires the same second logarithmic derivative and the two stated
initial conditions. These conditions determine equality locally
near zero by integration, and globally by analytic continuation.

\begin{remark}[operator comparison and determinant normalization]
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:rem:det-does-not-buy}
For a normal operator with a complete orthonormal eigenbasis indexed
by the same zeros, use the prescription
\begin{equation}
 \det_{\infty}(s-\Theta)
 :=\tfrac12e^{Bs}\det_2(1-s\Theta^{-1})=\xi(s).
 \label{v4-arith:w5-b:eq:det-identity}
\end{equation}
The unitary eigenbasis map proves equality with $\Delta_Z$.
This fixes the determinant notation in the determinant-trace
hypothesis. The construction of such an operator from an arithmetic
primary sector requires an additional domain-preserving comparison.
The diagonal operator $\Theta_Z$ itself uses the zero divisor as input.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[dilation and occupation models]
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:rem:berry-keating-contrast}
Berry--Keating's $xp$ model concerns zero-density asymptotics
(\emph{SIAM Review} 41 (1999), 236--266). A basic dilation
realization illustrates the separate operator question. The map
$f(x)\mapsto f(e^u)$ is unitary from
$L^2(\mathbb R_{>0},dx/x)$ to $L^2(\mathbb R,du)$ and sends
$-ix\partial_x$ to $-i\partial_u$, with domain transported from
$H^1(\mathbb R)$. Fourier transformation gives spectrum $\mathbb R$.
This self-adjoint dilation generator does not have a discrete
zeta-zero divisor. Likewise the self-adjoint occupation operator
$N_{\mathrm{occ}}$ with eigenvalues $\log m$ supplies no such divisor.
A density comparison or a logarithmic weight must be supplemented
by the actual determinant-trace realization; see
\Cref{v4-arith:hpvbconv:rem:berry-keating} for the corresponding
spectral question.
\end{remark}

\section{Trace Distribution: the Full Explicit Formula}
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:trace}

\begin{proposition}[ordinary trace on compact Fourier tests]
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:prop:weil-trace}
Assume \Cref{v4-arith:w5-b:hyp:twist-existence}. For
$f\in\mathcal A:=C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$ set
$h(z)=\mathcal F_+f(z):=\int_{\mathbb R}f(t)e^{izt}\,dt$.
Then $h(A)$ is trace class and
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:eq:trace-identity}
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Tr}h(H_{\mathrm{HP}})
 &=\sum_\rho h(\gamma_\rho)\\
 &=h(i/2)+h(-i/2)
   +\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}h(u)
       \bigl(\Re\psi_\Gamma(1/4+iu/2)-\log\pi\bigr)\,du\\
 &\hspace{1em}-\sum_{m\geq2}\frac{\Lambda(m)}{\sqrt m}
       \bigl(f(\log m)+f(-\log m)\bigr),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\psi_\Gamma=\Gamma'/\Gamma$. The prime sum is finite.
Equivalently the right side is $P(f)-W_\infty(f)-W_{\mathrm{fin}}(f)$,
with signed archimedean density
$\log\pi-\Re\psi_\Gamma(1/4+iu/2)$ and polar functional
$P(f)=h(i/2)+h(-i/2)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Repeated integration by parts gives
$|h(u)|\leq C_N(1+|u|)^{-N}$ for real $u$ and every $N$.
The zero-count estimate of \Cref{v4-arith:pm:zero-count} is
$O(T\log(2+T))$. Summing over dyadic intervals, with $N>2$,
therefore gives $\sum_\rho|h(\gamma_\rho)|<\infty$.
The specified orthonormal eigenbasis makes $h(A)$ a diagonal
trace-class operator with exactly this ordinary trace.
The determinant spectral condition gives
$\rho=1/2+i\gamma_\rho$. The compact-test explicit formula
\Cref{v4-arith:pm:weil} now gives all terms displayed, with its
Fourier convention and both prime-test values. The gamma integral
converges by logarithmic growth of the density. Unitary transport
of this trace-class operator to $E$ preserves the ordinary trace;
no unspecified regularization is inferred from an isometric
embedding of unrelated operators.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[the full Weil-positive cone and a Petersson comparison]
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:cor:weil-positive}
Under the same hypothesis, put $f^*(t)=\overline{f(-t)}$.
For every $f\in\mathcal A$ the full Weil value satisfies
\[
 W(f*f^*)=\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(h_f(A)h_f(A)^*\bigr)
           =\sum_\rho|h_f(\gamma_\rho)|^2\geq0,
 \qquad h_f=\mathcal F_+f.
\]
Thus Weil positivity and Li positivity hold.
To obtain this conclusion instead from a proposed Petersson
realization, one must construct a complex-linear map $J$ on all
of $\mathcal A$ with
$\langle Jf,Jg\rangle_{\mathrm{Pet}}=W(f*g^*)$, or construct it on
a dense subspace in a topology for which the full form is
continuous. Positivity only on an unspecified restricted cone
supplies neither condition.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Fourier transformation sends convolution and involution to
$h_fh_f^\#$, where $h_f^\#(z)=\overline{h_f(\bar z)}$.
On the real spectrum this is $|h_f|^2$. The preceding absolute
trace argument applies to $f*f^*$. This proves positivity on the
full compact test algebra, hence the Weil criterion and the Li
criterion of \Cref{v4-arith:w5-direct:thm:weil-positivity} and
\Cref{v4-arith:w5-direct:thm:li-positivity} give the conclusions.
For a Petersson map the stated pairing identity gives positivity
directly; if its domain is dense, continuity passes the inequality
to limits. The full compact-test form is continuous in the
inductive-limit topology by \Cref{v4-arith:pm:weil}.
\end{proof}

\section{Unconditional Constructions and the Arithmetic Comparison}

\subsection{Bost--Connes and Oscillator Carriers}
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:form:layer-1}

The global BC algebra, critical state and rational-character GNS
basis give the maximal self-adjoint generator with point spectrum
$\tfrac12\log\mathbb Q_{>0}$ and closed spectrum $\mathbb R$.
The finite basis span is a core, not its full self-adjoint domain.
Its restriction $N_{\mathrm{occ}}$ to $K_2$ has simple eigenvalues
$\log m$. The weighted zero-mode embedding, explicit oscillator
lift, closed CCR operators and logarithmic energy identity are
unconditional constructions after the stated auxiliary choices.
They do not identify the oscillator algebra with affiliated BC
observables or identify conformal energy with modular energy.

\subsection{Consequences of a Zero-Divisor Realization}
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:form:layer-2}

Given the eigenbasis and domain in
\Cref{v4-arith:w5-b:hyp:twist-existence}, the prescribed normalized
genus-one determinant is $\xi_{\mathbb R}$ by
\Cref{v4-arith:w5-b:prop:det-formal}. On compact Fourier tests the
ordinary trace is the full polar, signed-gamma and prime formula
\eqref{v4-arith:w5-b:eq:trace-identity}. It is positive on all
convolution squares, and Li positivity follows. These statements
use the zero-divisor realization; they are not occupation-trace
identities. The domain-preserving unitary $\iota U^*$ transports
them to $E$ for $A_E$, while leaving its distinction from
$N_{\mathrm{BC}}|_E$ intact.

\subsection{Hilbert--P\'olya and the Other Trace Models}
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:form:layer-3}

The remaining arithmetic problem is to construct the native
primary-sector realization, its Petersson pairing and chiral maps,
and an operator $A$ satisfying the normalized zero-divisor
condition. The zero-input diagonal operator proves no such
arithmetic origin. Connes' adele-class-space absorption trace and
Deninger's motivic determinant are further comparison targets.
As in \Cref{v4-arith:hpvbconv:thm:HPAr-equivalence}, a comparison
requires explicit Connes/Fock and Deninger/Fock intertwiners.

\begin{theorem}[conditional determinant and trace comparison]
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:thm:precise-obstruction}
Assume the arithmetic twist hypothesis. Its normalized determinant
and full compact-test trace are those of
\Cref{v4-arith:w5-b:prop:det-formal} and
\Cref{v4-arith:w5-b:prop:weil-trace}.
For a Connes comparison, additionally specify a Hilbert realization
$H_C$ of the relevant adele-class-space representation, a
self-adjoint scaling generator $A_C$, and a unitary
$V_C:\mathcal H_\xi\to H_C$ with
\[
 V_C\operatorname{Dom}A=\operatorname{Dom}A_C,\qquad
 A_CV_C=V_CA.
\]
The absorption-trace prescription must also agree on these tests
with $\operatorname{Tr}h(A_C)$, with its stated sign and normalization.
For a Deninger comparison, additionally specify a Hilbert
realization $H_D$ of the proposed motivic cohomology, a closed normal
generator $\Phi=\tfrac12I+iA_D$ with $A_D$ self-adjoint, and a unitary
$V_D:\mathcal H_\xi\to H_D$ intertwining $\Theta_\xi$ and $\Phi$
on their full domains. Its determinant prescription must have the
same genus-one normalization, or be proved to agree with it.
Under these hypotheses the normalized determinant and trace
statements transfer to both models and back. Without the maps and
prescriptions they are comparison questions, not equivalences of
the constructed operators.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The first assertion follows from the stated eigenbasis map and the
ordinary trace-class argument. A domain-preserving unitary
intertwiner preserves the spectral calculus, eigenvalue
multiplicities and trace-class traces. It also preserves the
normalized determinant prescription, as proved in
\Cref{v4-arith:sc:normalized-det}. The further trace/determinant
agreements in the hypotheses identify these transported quantities
with the respective geometric prescriptions. Applying the inverse
unitaries proves the reverse transfers. In the motivic notation,
self-adjointness concerns $A_D$, while the completed zero generator
is $\Phi=1/2+iA_D$; these are different operators. This makes
explicit the intertwiner hypotheses retained in
\Cref{v4-arith:hpvbconv:thm:HPAr-equivalence}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[logarithmic weights and their normalization]
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:rem:W-honest}
On the specified carrier $K_2$, the positive squared shifts satisfy
$N_{\mathrm{occ}}\mu_{n^2}=\mu_{n^2}(N_{\mathrm{occ}}+\log n)$
on their maximal domains, and the vacuum has energy zero.
These two requirements force the eigenvalue $\log m$ at
$\mu_{m^2}\Omega_1$. They also give
$\operatorname{Tr}_{K_2}e^{-\beta N_{\mathrm{occ}}}=\zeta(\beta)$
for $\Re\beta>1$.
KMS alone does not select $K_2$: for integers $s\geq1$, the sectors
$K_s=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{\mu_{m^s}\Omega_1\}$ have
energies $(s/2)\log m$. Thus the weight question of
\Cref{v4-arith:hpar-det:cor:wave3-structure} has this explicit
occupation realization after the carrier, covariance and vacuum
normalization are fixed. It does not solve the arithmetic
zero-divisor comparison.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[the conditional arithmetic conclusion]
\label{v4-arith:w5-b:rem:honest-conditional}
There are explicit self-adjoint modular and occupation generators,
and an explicit symmetry-invariant Hilbert Fock embedding in the
critical GNS space. The conformal grading can be transported
unitarily as a separate operator. A native arithmetic
Hilbert--P\'olya realization still requires its own operator,
domain, pairing and normalized zero-divisor data. Once these are
constructed, the determinant and full trace identities follow.
Comparisons with Connes' absorption trace, Deninger's determinant,
or a native chiral operation retain the additional maps and
normalizations stated above.
\end{remark}
