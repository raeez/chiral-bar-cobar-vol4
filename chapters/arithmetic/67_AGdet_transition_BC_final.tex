\chapter{Global Centre Actions and the Arithmetic Comparison}
\label{v4-arith:w6-g:chapter}

Local central actions generate an algebra of endomorphisms of a
global object. Identifying that algebra with a global Bernstein
centre requires faithfulness and generation. Identifying it with a
derived spectral centre also requires a comparison of higher
operations. A complete local determinant ring, an ordinary category
centre, and a derived centre are different objects in these problems.

Two calculations constrain a proposed arithmetic construction.
The regular nilpotent orbit in $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ has codimension
one, not two. Moreover, a complete local ring cannot split as a
product of two nonzero Bernstein components, although its generic
fibre can split. These calculations leave concrete geometric and
algebraic maps to be constructed before a global comparison follows.

\section{The Formal and Geometric Data}
\label{v4-arith:w6-g:domain}

Use the fixed finite coefficient field $k$, Witt ring $W=W(k)$,
finite ramification set, residual determinant and closed deformation
conditions of Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:bcglue-core}.
For distinct residue primes $p,q$ different from the coefficient
prime, the proved formal construction is
\[
 R_p^{\mathrm{Ch}}\widehat\otimes_W R_q^{\mathrm{Ch}}
 \longrightarrow R^{\mathrm{Ch}}.
\]
Its kernel quotient is the closed image, and its completed base
changes represent the fibres of local restriction.
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w5-h:thm:ch1-closure} supplies this construction.
The actual formal pullback module and its commuting local actions
are constructed in
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:bcglue-core:prop:formal-pullback-action}.
Their use in the ordinary pairwise geometric comparison requires
all the Selmer-algebra, geometric, trace and equivariant-descent data
of Corollary~\ref{v4-arith:w5-h:cor:ch1-closes-pairwise}.
The derived enhancement requires the separate derived datum or
both external-product and pullback Tor-independence.
The trace comparison alone does not identify the coherent objects.

For the categorical problem, choose a common coefficient base $E$
and specify all coefficient maps from the local constructions.
Write $Z(\mathcal C)$ for the ordinary centre of an $E$-linear
category. When a derived centre is used, write $\mathfrak Z(\mathcal D)$
for that independently specified derived algebra.
Its definition, algebra structure and functorial maps are part of
the construction. In particular, an $E_2$-structure on a category
does not by notation alone identify its centre with an ordinary
commutative deformation ring.

\section{The Rank-Two Nilpotent Calculation}
\label{v4-arith:w6-g:agdet-transition}

\begin{proposition}[the nilpotent cone in rank two]
\label{v4-arith:w6-g:prop:codim-2}
Let $K$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.
The nilpotent cone in $\mathfrak{sl}_2(K)$ has dimension two.
Its nonzero locus is the regular nilpotent orbit, of codimension
one in $\mathfrak{sl}_2(K)$. The zero orbit has dimension zero.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write
\[
 X=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&-a\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then $X^2=(a^2+bc)1$, so nilpotency is the equation
$a^2+bc=0$ in $\mathbb A_K^3$.
On $b\ne0$ it is parametrized by $a,b$, with $c=-a^2/b$.
The quadric is irreducible: a factorization into linear forms would
give a quadratic form of rank at most two, whereas $a^2+bc$ has
rank three. Thus the cone has dimension two.
Every nonzero nilpotent matrix has one Jordan block and is
conjugate to $\begin{psmallmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{psmallmatrix}$.
A conjugating matrix can be rescaled to determinant one because
$K$ is algebraically closed. The nonzero locus is therefore one
$SL_2(K)$-orbit of dimension two. The remaining orbit is $\{0\}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the arithmetic local model]
\label{v4-arith:w6-g:rem:codim-2-role}
This calculation does not determine the dimension of an arithmetic
Selmer space. Such a conclusion requires a constructed local-model
morphism and its relative dimension. A nonempty transition space
cannot be a subspace of both a closed locus and its open complement,
since their intersection is empty.
In singular-support theory the support condition lies in the scheme
of singularities of a quasi-smooth derived space. It is not, by
definition, a locus where the underlying representations have
unipotent monodromy. The relevant constructions are
Arinkin--Gaitsgory, arXiv:1201.6343, \S\S2.3, 4.1 and 8.2;
\S11.1 defines the global nilpotent cone for the geometric
local-system stack. An arithmetic analogue requires its own
space, singular-support subset and functors.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Restriction of Centres}

\begin{proposition}[restriction to a full subcategory]
\label{v4-arith:w6-g:thm:gkrw-2-convergence}
Let $\mathcal D'\subseteq\mathcal D$ be a full $E$-linear
subcategory. Restriction of natural endomorphisms gives a unital
$E$-algebra homomorphism
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w6-g:eq:gkrw-centre-restriction}
 Z(\mathcal D)\longrightarrow Z(\mathcal D').
\end{equation}
Its kernel consists of central endomorphisms vanishing on every
object of $\mathcal D'$.
This construction imposes no cellular-filtration or codimension
bound on that kernel.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A natural endomorphism on $\mathcal D$ has an endomorphism as
its component at every object of $\mathcal D'$.
Fullness places these components in $\mathcal D'$, and naturality
restricts to its morphisms. Addition, composition and the identity
restrict componentwise, giving the algebra map in
Equation~\eqref{v4-arith:w6-g:eq:gkrw-centre-restriction}.
A natural transformation is zero exactly when all its components
are zero, which proves the kernel description.
\end{proof}

A derived centre comparison needs the corresponding coherent maps
of derived algebras. A cellular presentation can be used only after
it has been constructed for the actual category and related to
those maps. The ordinary restriction homomorphism proves no such
cellular or higher-centre statement.

\subsection{Filtration Length and Geometric Codimension}

\begin{example}[a Nygaard filtration on a point]
\label{v4-arith:w6-g:thm:bl-2-convergence}
On the prism $(\mathbb Z_p,(p))$, take the Frobenius lift
$\varphi=\mathrm{id}$. The Nygaard submodules defined by
$\varphi(x)\in p^i\mathbb Z_p$ are
\[
 N^{\ge i}\mathbb Z_p=p^i\mathbb Z_p\quad(i\ge0),\qquad
 \operatorname{gr}_N^i\mathbb Z_p=\mathbb F_p.
\]
Every nonnegative graded degree is nonzero, although
$\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb F_p$ has dimension zero.
Thus a geometric codimension bound does not by itself truncate
the Nygaard filtration in degree two.
\end{example}

For a filtered complex $C$ with decreasing filtration $F$, the
usual cohomological spectral sequence begins with
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w6-g:eq:bl-nygaard-ss}
 E_1^{a,b}=H^{a+b}(\operatorname{gr}_F^a C).
\end{equation}
Convergence of Equation~\eqref{v4-arith:w6-g:eq:bl-nygaard-ss}
to $H^{a+b}(C)$ requires appropriate completeness,
exhaustiveness and convergence hypotheses on the filtered complex.
A comparison with a cellular filtration additionally requires a
filtered chain map and a proof of its effect on graded pieces.
The dimension calculation above supplies neither a map nor a
spectral-sequence degeneration statement.

\begin{remark}[an equivariant-cohomology comparison]
\label{v4-arith:w6-g:thm:bfm-equivariant}
Suppose a geometric model $Y$ with an algebraic group action $H$
has been specified for an arithmetic transition category
$\mathcal D_{01}$. An equivariant-cohomology construction would
require a morphism
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w6-g:eq:bfm-centre}
 H_H^\bullet(Y,E)\longrightarrow
 H^\bullet(\mathfrak Z(\mathcal D_{01})).
\end{equation}
Both the model $Y$ and this morphism must be constructed, with
coefficients, grading and products fixed. If $Y$ is to lie in an
affine Grassmannian, its embedding and the functor realizing the
arithmetic category must also be given. The rank-two nilpotent
calculation does not supply these data or an isomorphism of the
displayed algebras.
\end{remark}

\section{Central Actions on a Glued Category}

There is an elementary central action once an actual categorical
gluing diagram has been supplied. It illustrates which maps a
higher-centre comparison must refine.
Let $\mathcal D_0,\mathcal D_1,\mathcal D_{01}$ be $E$-linear
categories, and let $F_i:\mathcal D_i\to\mathcal D_{01}$ be
$E$-linear functors.
Their category of glued objects $\mathcal D$ has objects
$(x_0,x_1,\eta)$, where
$\eta:F_0x_0\xrightarrow{\sim}F_1x_1$.
A morphism is a pair $(u_0,u_1)$ satisfying
$F_1(u_1)\eta=\eta F_0(u_0)$.

\begin{proposition}[a central action from a fibre product of rings]
\label{v4-arith:w6-g:thm:agdet-transition-closure}
Let $A_0,A_1,A_{01}$ be commutative unital $E$-algebras with maps
$\theta_i:A_i\to A_{01}$ and central actions
$\alpha_i:A_i\to Z(\mathcal D_i)$ and
$\alpha_{01}:A_{01}\to Z(\mathcal D_{01})$.
Assume, for each $a\in A_i$ and $x\in\mathcal D_i$, that
\[
 F_i(\alpha_i(a)_x)
   =\alpha_{01}(\theta_i(a))_{F_i(x)}.
\]
Then there is a canonical homomorphism
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w6-g:eq:agdet-transition-compat}
 A_0\times_{A_{01}}A_1\longrightarrow Z(\mathcal D).
\end{equation}
The image of Equation~\eqref{v4-arith:w6-g:eq:agdet-transition-compat}
is the quotient by the kernel of this action.
No injectivity or surjectivity is asserted.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $(a_0,a_1)$ in the fibre product and a glued object
$(x_0,x_1,\eta)$, use the pair of endomorphisms
$(\alpha_0(a_0)_{x_0},\alpha_1(a_1)_{x_1})$.
Their images in $\mathcal D_{01}$ are components of the same
natural endomorphism, since $\theta_0(a_0)=\theta_1(a_1)$.
Naturality with respect to $\eta$ gives the equality required for
a morphism of glued objects.
Naturality on each factor proves naturality on $\mathcal D$.
Addition, multiplication and units act componentwise, so this is a
unital algebra homomorphism. The image quotient follows from the
first isomorphism theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[the additional conditions for an ordinary centre identity]
\label{v4-arith:w6-g:cor:agdet-closed}
The map in
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:w6-g:thm:agdet-transition-closure} is an
isomorphism if and only if the action is faithful and every central
endomorphism of $\mathcal D$ arises from a compatible pair in
$A_0\times_{A_{01}}A_1$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
These are respectively injectivity and surjectivity of the
constructed homomorphism.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the arithmetic higher-centre problem]
\label{v4-arith:w6-g:rem:agdet-domain}
For the proposed Selmer-derived category, the gluing spaces and
functors have not been constructed by the preceding ring arguments.
The required arithmetic statement must first specify an actual
categorical descent diagram. It must then construct the induced
maps of higher centres and prove that the relevant homotopy limit
agrees with the global higher centre. Even for the ordinary glued
category, the proposition gives an action rather than a centre
isomorphism. An arithmetic Arinkin--Gaitsgory comparison would
also have to match this spectral action with the automorphic one.
\end{remark}

\section{Local Deformation Rings and Bernstein Components}
\label{v4-arith:w6-g:bcglue-final}

\begin{proposition}[idempotents in a local ring]
\label{v4-arith:w6-g:thm:bc-bernstein-galois}
A local ring $R$ has no idempotents except $0,1$.
In particular a complete local deformation ring cannot be a product
of two nonzero rings.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $e^2=e$, then $e(1-e)=0$.
At least one of $e,1-e$ is a unit, because otherwise their sum $1$
would lie in the maximal ideal. Multiplying by its inverse shows
that the other is zero. A product of two nonzero rings contains
the nontrivial idempotent $(1,0)$, proving the last assertion.
\end{proof}

An idempotent $e$ in an arbitrary commutative ring does produce
a module decomposition
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w6-g:eq:bc-bernstein-galois}
 M=eM\oplus(1-e)M.
\end{equation}
To verify Equation~\eqref{v4-arith:w6-g:eq:bc-bernstein-galois},
write $m=em+(1-e)m$; the intersection is zero after
multiplication by $e$. Bernstein decompositions of a representation
category can use its categorical idempotents. They do not produce
nontrivial idempotents in a fixed complete local Galois ring.

\begin{example}[a split generic fibre of a local ring]
\label{v4-arith:w6-g:ex:generic-fibre-split}
Put $R=\mathbb Z_2[[x]]/(x(x-2))$.
It is local with residue field $\mathbb F_2$.
The relation $x^m=2^{m-1}x$ for $m\ge1$ reduces every series
uniquely to $a+bx$ with $a,b\in\mathbb Z_2$.
The reductions converge $2$-adically.
Uniqueness follows by evaluating at $x=0$ and $x=2$:
$a=0$ and $2b=0$ imply $a=b=0$.
After inverting $2$, these evaluations give
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w6-g:eq:bc-global-bernstein-galois}
 R[1/2]\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb Q_2\times\mathbb Q_2,
 \qquad a+bx\longmapsto(a,a+2b).
\end{equation}
The inverse in Equation~\eqref{v4-arith:w6-g:eq:bc-global-bernstein-galois}
sends $(u,v)$ to $u+(v-u)x/2$.
Integrally, the image consists of pairs in $\mathbb Z_2^2$ with
$v-u\in2\mathbb Z_2$, so $(1,0)$ is not in the image.
The generic-fibre splitting therefore does not split $R$.
\end{example}

This distinction is necessary before relating a Bernstein component
to a formal deformation space. Such a comparison must specify
whether it concerns the integral ring, its generic fibre, a quotient,
or an action image. A correspondence of closed points establishes
none of those ring identifications by itself.

\section{The Algebra Generated by Local Actions}

\begin{proposition}[finite-place tensor products and their action image]
\label{v4-arith:w6-g:thm:fg-factorization-gluing}
Let $E$ be a commutative ring and $Z_v$ commutative unital
$E$-algebras indexed by places $v$. Set
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w6-g:eq:fg-factorization-product}
 A=\varinjlim_{S\text{ finite}}\bigotimes_{v\in S,E}Z_v,
\end{equation}
The transition maps in
Equation~\eqref{v4-arith:w6-g:eq:fg-factorization-product} insert units.
Pairwise commuting actions on an $E$-module $M$ induce a unique
homomorphism $\alpha:A\to\operatorname{End}_E(M)$.
There is a canonical identification
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w6-g:eq:fg-global-bernstein}
 A/\ker\alpha\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{im}\alpha.
\end{equation}
For central actions on an $E$-linear category, the image in
Equation~\eqref{v4-arith:w6-g:eq:fg-global-bernstein} lies in
its ordinary centre.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On a finite set $S$, send $\otimes_{v\in S}z_v$ to the product
of its acting endomorphisms. Commutativity between the factors
makes this a homomorphism. The unit condition makes these maps
compatible when $S$ grows. The colimit gives the unique action,
and the kernel quotient is its image. The categorical assertion
follows by composing natural endomorphisms.
This is also
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:bcglue-core:thm:colimit-to-global-reduces-to-AG}.
\end{proof}

The displayed tensor colimit has a specified algebraic meaning.
A topological completion or a restricted tensor product with chosen
spherical data is a further construction. Its topology and transition
maps must be stated. A factorization interpretation additionally
requires the factorization object and its structural maps on a
specified base. The tensor formula alone supplies no arithmetic
factorization geometry.

\begin{proposition}[transport across a categorical equivalence]
\label{v4-arith:w6-g:thm:bcglue-across-charts}
Let $F:\mathcal C\to\mathcal D$ be an $E$-linear equivalence.
Suppose both categories carry local central actions from the same
algebras $Z_v$, and that $F$ intertwines each action.
Then the induced isomorphism $Z(\mathcal C)\simeq Z(\mathcal D)$
intertwines the assembled actions of $A$.
Their kernels coincide, and their action images are isomorphic.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Conjugation by $F$ transports natural endomorphisms, as in
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:bcglue-core:thm:local-bernstein}.
It intertwines every finite product of local central actions.
The colimit property therefore intertwines the actions of $A$.
An algebra isomorphism preserves zero, so the kernels agree.
Taking their common quotient identifies the two images.
\end{proof}

\begin{conjecture}[the arithmetic centre comparison]
\label{v4-arith:w6-g:conj:arithmetic-centres}
For the intended arithmetic categories, the comparison requires
local functors, coefficient and family maps, and prismatic and
untilt descent data.
The resulting automorphic and spectral central actions are expected
to be identified by the arithmetic comparison, with faithful local
action and generation of the specified global centres.
If $A_{\mathrm{aut}}$, $A_{\mathrm{fact}}$ and
$\mathfrak Z(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{Sel}})$ denote their proposed
higher enhancements in one specified category of derived algebras,
the intended comparison is
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w6-g:eq:three-centres-coincide}
 A_{\mathrm{aut}}\simeq A_{\mathrm{fact}}
       \simeq\mathfrak Z(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{Sel}}).
\end{equation}
Here the higher enhancements, their comparison morphisms, and the
proof that those morphisms are equivalences are part of the assertion.
An ordinary determinant ring is not substituted for a higher
algebra in Equation~\eqref{v4-arith:w6-g:eq:three-centres-coincide}.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{corollary}[what an equivariant arithmetic comparison would transport]
\label{v4-arith:w6-g:cor:bcglue-core-closed}
If an arithmetic categorical equivalence has been constructed and
intertwines the specified local central actions, it transports their
ordinary global action image. To obtain the ordinary-centre clause
of Conjecture~\ref{v4-arith:bcglue-core:cj:core}, those actions must also
be faithful and generate the intended centres.
The pairwise coherent-sheaf clause remains the separate equivariant
isomorphism in that conjecture.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:w6-g:thm:bcglue-across-charts} to the
ordinary actions. Faithfulness and generation are the additional
injectivity and surjectivity conditions. Transport of central
endomorphisms alone does not compare the independent coherent
objects occurring in the pairwise clause.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[the two comparisons required by Bernstein gluing]
\label{v4-arith:w6-g:cor:bcglue-final}
The formal determinant restriction constructs a pairwise pullback
with its local action, and the tensor colimit constructs the algebra
generated by all specified local actions.
To obtain BC-diamond-glue from these constructions, one must
construct the equivariant isomorphism with the global prismatic
object and the intended global centre equivalence, compatibly with
coefficients, finite-place transitions and untilt descent.
Neither construction asserts that these comparison maps exist.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The pullback and its action are
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:bcglue-core:prop:formal-pullback-action}.
The assembled algebra and image are
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:w6-g:thm:fg-factorization-gluing}.
The two missing comparisons have the precise domains and codomains
of Conjecture~\ref{v4-arith:bcglue-core:cj:core} and
Conjecture~\ref{v4-arith:w6-g:conj:arithmetic-centres}.
The nonunique Galois lifts and the extension example in
Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:bcglue-core} prevent replacing these maps
by determination from two primes or by equality of determinant laws.
\end{proof}

For adelic categorical descent, the remaining objects are the local

\begin{remark}[singular support, filtrations, and central actions]
\label{v4-arith:w6-g:rem:data}
The singular-support construction must specify the derived Selmer
space and the conical subset of its scheme of singularities.
A cellular or prismatic construction must specify its filtered
complex and the comparison to that category.
An equivariant-cohomology model must supply the morphism in
\eqref{v4-arith:w6-g:eq:bfm-centre}.
These data have different domains. Combining them requires actual
functors and compatible morphisms, beyond the existence of the

Chenevier's determinant deformation theorem is used with its
formal coefficient ring and residual data, as specified in
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:w5-h:prop:chenevier-moduli-via-det-trace}.

quasi-smooth derived domain. A transfer to an arithmetic Selmer
space must establish that domain and the required functoriality.
The ring and orbit calculations in this chapter do not make that
transfer.
\end{remark}

\section{The Next Arithmetic Construction}
\label{v4-arith:w6-g:summary}

On a common geometric chart, the pairwise problem consists of
the local pullback, the global sheaf, and the matrices or complexes
carrying their central actions. Its solution is an equivariant
isomorphism compatible with the overlap maps. The successive obstruction classes in
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:bcglue-core:cor:part-a-domain-reduction} perform
this calculation on finite free complete local charts.

The global problem requires automorphic and spectral central-action
maps from the same finite-place algebras. Their kernels and images
can then be compared using
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:w6-g:thm:fg-factorization-gluing}.
A higher-centre equivalence further requires its derived comparison
map and a proof that the cone of its underlying map of complexes
vanishes on the stated domain.
These constructions preserve the global arithmetic question while
keeping its formal algebra, coherent objects and higher centres
distinct.
