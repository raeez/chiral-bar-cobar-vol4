\section{Genus-\(g\) Arithmetic Data Recap}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r6:datum}

Fix \(g\geq 0\) and an arithmetic genus-\(g\) datum
\(\mathcal{D}_{g}=(\mathcal{B}_{g},\mathcal{E}_{g},\varphi_{g})\) as in
\Cref{v4-arith:ag:defn:datum}. Four structural features of
\(\mathcal{D}_{g}\) are used throughout.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Local Galois representation and Frobenius convention.}
Fix $\ell\ne p$ at a prime of good reduction. In the Jacobian row
let $V_\ell J=T_\ell J\otimes_{\mathbb Z_\ell}\mathbb Q_\ell$ and use
\[
 V_{g,p}=H^1_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(J_{\overline{\mathbb Q}_p},\mathbb Q_\ell)
       =\operatorname{Hom}(V_\ell J,\mathbb Q_\ell)
\]
as the carrier of $\rho_{g,p}$. Arithmetic Frobenius acts on the
residue field by $x\mapsto x^p$; geometric Frobenius is its inverse.
The Tate-module Weil pairing takes values in $\mathbb Q_\ell(1)$.
Its dual polarization on $V_{g,p}$ takes values in
$\mathbb Q_\ell(-1)$, on which geometric Frobenius acts by $p$.
Thus the cohomological geometric convention has paired eigenvalue
products $p$; the Tate-module geometric convention has products $p^{-1}$.
For another row, its realization, coefficient field, rank and
polarization must be supplied separately. A rank-$2g$ symplectic
realization of weight $w_{\mathcal D_g}-1$ uses a pairing into
$\mathbb Q_\ell(1-w_{\mathcal D_g})$, or its specified coefficient extension.
A prismatic crystal requires a comparison to the chosen realization.
In the Siegel row the standard rank is $2g+1$ and the spin rank is
$2^g$; neither is identified with a rank-$2g$ Jacobian realization.
\item \emph{Satake parameters.} In the chosen unramified realization,
let $\alpha_j^{(p)}$ be the eigenvalues of geometric Frobenius.
In the rank-$2g$ polarized case they may be paired so that
$\alpha_j^{(p)}\alpha_{2g+1-j}^{(p)}=p^{w_{\mathcal D_g}-1}$.
Indeed invariance of the pairing up to this scalar pairs each
nonzero eigenvalue with the scalar divided by it, with multiplicity.
The local factor is $\det(1-p^{-s}\rho_{g,p}(\mathrm{Frob}_p^{\mathrm{geom}}))^{-1}$.
For spin or other gauge labels the determinant uses that actual
representation. At good reduction inertia acts trivially, so its
trace is the rank, not generally the Frobenius trace.
For example $y^2=x^3-x$ over $\mathbb F_5$ has eight points:
the five affine fibres have respectively $1,1,2,2,1$ points.
The arithmetic Tate-module trace is $5+1-8=-2$ and determinant $5$;
its geometric trace is $-2/5$ and determinant $1/5$.
Dualizing recovers trace $-2$ and determinant $5$ for geometric
Frobenius on $H^1$. The pairing convention is the one in Milne,
\emph{Elliptic Curves}, second edition, V~\S8, p.~221.
\item \emph{Rank-\(2g\) Fock oscillators per place.} The local factor
\(\mathcal{H}^{(p),g}\) of
\(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim},g}_{\mathrm{Heis}}=\bigotimes_{p}'\mathcal{H}^{(p),g}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{(\infty),g}\)
is the rank-\(2g\) Fock whose character is the local Euler factor above.
The archimedean factor \(\mathcal{H}^{(\infty),g}\) carries the
rank-\(2g\) archimedean Heisenberg with \(\Gamma\)-factor
\(\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s)^{g}\) at weight \(1\) (Windows~I, V) or the
corresponding \(\Gamma\)-factor of
\Cref{v4-arith:ag:table:rows} at Dedekind or Siegel-spin weight.
\item \emph{Motivic functional-equation centre
\(w_{\mathcal{D}_{g}}\).} The completed \(L\)-function
\(\Lambda_{\mathcal{D}_{g}}(s)\) satisfies
\(\Lambda_{\mathcal{D}_{g}}(s)=\epsilon_{\mathcal{D}_{g}}\Lambda_{\mathcal{D}_{g}}(w_{\mathcal{D}_{g}}-s)\)
by \Cref{v4-arith:ag:thm:all-genus}; purity lies on
\(\mathrm{Re}(s)=w_{\mathcal{D}_{g}}/2\).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{remark}[degeneration at \(g=0\)]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r6:rem:g-zero}
The rank-zero local system has trace zero. Its symmetric algebra
is the scalar line with character one, and its empty Fock tensor
is \(\mathbb C\). The separately defined area form
\(\mu_{\mathrm{Ar},0}=0\) gives area scalar one.
A nontrivial Riemann Tate sector and a \(GL_2\)-valued Wilson label
are additional inputs. Their scalar completed character, when
specified, is
\(\xi_{\mathbb R}(s)=\tfrac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\).
The empty tensor contributes only the factor one to that product.
Its tensor-unit comparison is given in
\Cref{v4-arith:w9-r6:cor:g-zero}; a native Tate surface additionally
requires the quantization and primary data of
\Cref{v4-arith:pres90:quantization}, \Cref{v4-arith:pres90:primary-virasoro}.
\end{remark}
\section{Genus-\(g\) 3D Arithmetic Chern--Simons Theory}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r6:3d-acs-g}

Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:3d-acs-E1bar} builds the 3D arithmetic
Chern--Simons theory on \(\overline{\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbb{Z}}\) with
gauge group \(GL_{2}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})\) in the profinite limit,
level \(k^{\mathrm{Ar}}=\kappa^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathsf{G}}\), boundary WZW
\(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}\), and Wilson-loop axiom
\Cref{v4-arith:3d-acs:conj:qu-acs}(3). The datum \(\mathcal{D}_{g}\) determines
a rank-\(2g\) gauge group \(GL_{2g}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})\), a rank-\(2g\)
Koszul level \(k^{\mathrm{Ar},g}\), and a rank-\(2g\) boundary Heisenberg
\(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim},g}\); the following definition records these data
and inherits the conditional form of the \(g=0\) theory.

\begin{definition}[genus-\(g\) arithmetic Chern--Simons]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r6:def:acs-g}
\ClaimStatusDefinitional. The \emph{genus-\(g\) arithmetic Chern--Simons
theory} is a family of 3D topological gauge theories on an arithmetic
\(3\)-space \(\mathcal{B}_{g}^{(3)}\) parameterised by
\(\mathcal{D}_{g}\), with
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Arithmetic \(3\)-space.} For Window~II at totally imaginary
\(K\) of degree \(2g\):
\(\mathcal{B}_{g}^{(3)}=\overline{\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{O}_{K}}\) with
Artin--Verdier fundamental class
\([\mathcal{B}_{g}^{(3)}]\in H^{3}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathcal{B}_{g}^{(3)},\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})\)
(Mazur 1973). For Window~I at \(X\to\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{O}_{K}\):
\(\mathcal{B}_{g}^{(3)}=\overline{\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{O}_{K}}\) with
extra fibre cohomology in \(H^{2}\) of the generic fibre collected into
the archimedean boundary via the Arakelov K\"ahler form
\(\mu_{\mathrm{Ar},g}\) of Faltings 1984 (to be defined in
\Cref{v4-arith:w9-r6:def:arakelov-kahler-g}). For Window~VI, Shimura varieties
of \(\mathrm{GSp}_{2g}\) have their own Artin--Verdier-type fundamental
class assembled via \'etale cohomology of the special fibre plus
Deligne 1974 Weil cohomology.
\item \emph{Gauge group.}
\(G_{g,N}:=GL_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})\), with profinite limit
\(G_{g,\infty}:=GL_{2g}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})\).
\item \emph{Level.}
\(k^{\mathrm{Ar},g}:=\kappa^{\mathrm{Ar},g}_{\mathsf{G}}(\mathcal{D}_{g})\),
the rank-\(2g\) arithmetic Koszul level of
\Cref{v4-arith:ag:thm:all-genus}.
\item \emph{Boundary WZW at the archimedean fibre.}
\(\partial\,\mathrm{ACS}^{\mathrm{qu},g}_{k^{\mathrm{Ar},g}}=\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim},g}\)
in the sense of
\Cref{v4-arith:3d-acs:thm:bulk-boundary-Vprim} scaled to rank \(2g\) per
\Cref{v4-arith:3d-acs:genus-g}.
\end{enumerate}
The finite-level state sums
\(Z^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{G_{g,N},\alpha_{g,N}}\) extend
\Cref{v4-arith:3d-acs:thm:finite-level-ACS} unchanged at each finite
rank; the profinite renormalisation
\(\mathrm{ACS}^{\mathrm{qu},g}_{k^{\mathrm{Ar},g}}\) remains conditional
in the sense of \Cref{v4-arith:3d-acs:conj:qu-acs} extended to rank
\(2g\).
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[rank scaling and residual profinite limit]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r6:rem:rank-scaling}
The rank-change \(2\to 2g\) does not alter the closed finite-level
state-sum of \Cref{v4-arith:3d-acs:thm:finite-level-ACS}: the field
groupoid
\(\mathcal{F}_{G_{g,N}}(\mathcal{B}_{g}^{(3)})\)
is a finite groupoid for each \(N\), the action
\(S_{\alpha_{g,N}}^{\mathrm{Ar}}\) is the same Artin--Verdier pairing,
and the gluing formula follows from \'etale descent of
rank-\(2g\) local systems. The profinite limit
\(\mathrm{ACS}^{\mathrm{qu},g}\) shares the profinite-renormalisation
residual of \Cref{v4-arith:3d-acs:conj:qu-acs}.
\end{remark}

For the partition comparison, a homology object must first have a
scalar character. Let $H^i_g=H_i(B^{\mathrm{ord},g})$ have specified
Hilbert realizations and a holomorphic family of trace-class
endomorphisms $T_i(s)$ on an open half-plane $\Omega$.
Assume $\sum_i\|T_i(s)\|_1$ converges locally uniformly there, and set
$\chi_g(s)=\sum_i(-1)^i\operatorname{Tr}T_i(s)$.
The trace and the sum then define a holomorphic scalar on $\Omega$.
The required character comparison is
$\chi_g(s)=\Lambda_{\mathcal D_g}(s)$ on $\Omega$, with the exact
row-specific gamma, conductor and pole factors. If a regularized
character is used instead, its prescription and normalization must
be specified and this same scalar identity proved on a common domain.
Neither expression identifies homology itself with a function.

\begin{proposition}[partition function at genus \(g\)]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r6:prop:partition-g}
\ClaimStatusConditional \emph{(on the genus-\(g\) extension of
\Cref{v4-arith:3d-acs:conj:qu-acs}(2) and the genus-\(g\) BD/CG
comparison of \Cref{v4-arith:3d-acs:cor:BDCG-ordered-comparison})}.
Suppose the scalar character comparison just specified holds, and
the bulk-boundary comparison identifies the partition scalar with
$\chi_g(k^{\mathrm{Ar},g})$ on $\Omega$, with matching meromorphic
continuations when evaluation is outside $\Omega$ and away from poles.
Then the partition function of
\(\mathrm{ACS}^{\mathrm{qu},g}_{k^{\mathrm{Ar},g}}\) on
\(\mathcal{B}_{g}^{(3)}\) equals the completed \(L\)-function
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r6:eq:partition-g}
Z_{\mathrm{ACS}^{\mathrm{qu},g}}\bigl(\mathcal{B}_{g}^{(3)};k^{\mathrm{Ar},g}\bigr)
\;=\;\Lambda_{\mathcal{D}_{g}}\bigl(k^{\mathrm{Ar},g}\bigr),
\end{equation}
with \(\Lambda_{\mathcal{D}_{g}}\) the row-specific completed \(L\)-function
of \Cref{v4-arith:ag:table:rows} (Dedekind \(\xi_{K}\), Hasse--Weil
\(\Lambda(s,X_{K})\), or Siegel spin \(\Lambda(s,\pi,\mathrm{spin})\)).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The rank-\(2g\) ordered incidence bar \(B^{\mathrm{ord},g}_{\bullet}\) on
\(\mathbb{P}\cup\{\infty\}\) with per-place factor \(\mathcal{H}^{(p),g}\)
is the rank-\(2g\) generalisation of the bar of
\Cref{v4-arith:3d-acs:defn:E1-bar}. The local character at \(p\) is
\(\chi_{p}^{(g),\mathrm{std}}(s)=\prod_{j=1}^{2g}(1-\alpha_{j}^{(p)}p^{-s})^{-1}\)
for the standard Wilson observable. In the Siegel spin row the local
character is instead the spin Euler factor
\(\chi_{p}^{(g),\mathrm{spin}}(s)=\prod_{\epsilon}(1-\beta_{\epsilon}^{(p)}p^{-s})^{-1}\)
over the weights of the spin representation. The archimedean character
is the row-specific \(\Gamma\)-factor. For a finite diagonal symmetric-algebra factor, the character
$\prod_j(1-\alpha_j^{(p)}p^{-s})^{-1}$ follows by multiplying the
convergent geometric series, where $|\alpha_j^{(p)}p^{-s}|<1$.
Passing to the ordered bar homology and the infinite product requires
the specified scalar character comparison, including its convergence
and archimedean normalization. Under that hypothesis and the actual
bulk-boundary trace map, $Z(k)=\chi_g(k)=\Lambda_{\mathcal D_g}(k)$
on $\Omega$. Uniqueness of meromorphic continuation gives the claimed
extension wherever the prescribed scalar is finite.
\end{proof}

\section{Wilson-Line Frobenius Identity at Genus \(g\)}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r6:wilson-line-g}

The gauge group \(G_{g,\infty}=GL_{2g}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})\) of
\(\mathrm{ACS}^{\mathrm{qu},g}\) contains the prime knots \(K_{p}\) as
defect insertions. Each \(K_{p}\) carries a holonomy valued in the
rank-\(2g\) local Galois representation \(\rho_{g,p}\); the question is
whether the expectation of the associated defect operator recovers
geometric Frobenius \(\mathrm{Frob}_{p}^{\mathrm{geom}}\). Arithmetic
Frobenius is its inverse in this convention. The trace identity below is conditional on the specified Wilson
comparison, including compatible coefficient fields and local labels.

A Wilson label is a representation of the gauge group, whereas
$\rho_{g,p}$ is a representation of a local Galois group.
At finite level specify a representation $r_{g,N}$ of $G_{g,N}$
over a characteristic-zero coefficient field and a comparison of
its holonomy trace with the trace of $\rho_{g,p}$ at Frobenius.
The traces must be placed in a common specified coefficient field.
A profinite label requires compatible finite labels and convergent
expectations. A Hilbert-space interpretation additionally requires
a complex realization and its positive pairing. These are data of
the Wilson comparison, not consequences of equality of dimensions.

\begin{definition}[genus-\(g\) Wilson line]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r6:def:wilson-line-g}
\ClaimStatusDefinitional. Fix a prime \(p\) of good reduction for
\(\mathcal{D}_{g}\); let \(K_{p}\subset\mathcal{B}_{g}^{(3)}\) denote the
prime-knot associated to \(p\) under the Morishita dictionary. For a
continuous representation
\(\rho_{g}:G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\to\mathrm{GSp}_{2g}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})\)
of rank \(2g\), the \emph{genus-\(g\) Wilson line} along \(K_{p}\) is the
defect operator
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r6:eq:wilson-line-def}
W^{(g)}_{\rho_{g}}(K_{p})\;:=\;\mathrm{tr}_{r_g}\bigl(\mathrm{Hol}_{K_{p}}(\mathsf{A})\bigr),
\end{equation}
Here $\rho_g$ indexes the matched gauge label $r_g$.
The compatible bulk field is
$\mathsf A\in\mathcal F_{G_{g,\infty}}(\mathcal B_g^{(3)})$.
Its Frobenius-longitude holonomy, distinct from the inertia meridian,
and the comparison with the local
Bost--Connes Adams operation $\psi_p^{(g)}$ must be supplied by
the chosen arithmetic gauge construction.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[genus-\(g\) Wilson-line Frobenius identity]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r6:thm:wilson-frobenius-g}
\ClaimStatusProvedHereConditional. Fix the cohomological geometric
Frobenius convention above. At each finite gauge level use an actual
representation $r_{g,N}$ of $G_{g,N}$ and the normalized finite
Wilson expectation of its character on a specified longitude.
Assume the arithmetic gauge comparison identifies the limiting
expectation, with its scalar coefficient realization and topology,
with the character of $\rho_{g,p}(\mathrm{Frob}_p^{\mathrm{geom}})$.
For an $\ell$-adic construction the following proof specifies the
congruence and scalar comparison data required by this assumption.
The genus-$g$ Wilson axiom and the chosen unramified local Langlands
comparison remain the arithmetic input; equality of representation
ranks does not supply it. For every unramified prime \(p\) of
good reduction for \(\mathcal{D}_{g}\),
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r6:eq:wilson-frobenius-g}
\langle W^{(g)}_{\rho_{g}}(K_{p})\rangle_{\mathrm{ACS}^{\mathrm{qu},g}}
\;=\;\mathrm{tr}_{\rho_{g}}\bigl(\mathrm{Frob}_{p}^{\mathrm{geom}}\bigr)
\;=\;\sum_{j=1}^{2g}\alpha_{j}^{(p)}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the Wilson-line defect expectation equals the character value
\(\chi_{\rho_{g}}(\mathrm{Frob}_{p}^{\mathrm{geom}})\) in the standard representation.
The Siegel spin \(L\)-factor is obtained from the spin Wilson observable,
not from this rank-\(2g\) standard trace.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A longitude represents a lift of Frobenius in the local quotient
$G_{\mathbb Q_p}/I_p$; changing the lift does not change an unramified
representation. A meridian represents inertia and has trace $2g$
on this carrier. These two observables must therefore be distinguished
in the arithmetic-topology comparison of Mazur and Morishita.

For the congruence construction, choose a finite extension
$E/\mathbb Q_\ell$ over which the representation is defined and a
stable $\mathcal O_E$-lattice $L$ of rank $2g$. For a uniformizer
$\varpi$, the reductions act on $L/\varpi^nL$ and their traces are
compatible in $\mathcal O_E/\varpi^n$. Completeness identifies their
inverse limit with the trace of the original matrix in $\mathcal O_E$.
This is an $\ell$-power congruence tower; it supplies neither a
representation modulo every integer $N$ nor a complex character
of the finite gauge group $G_{g,N}$.

Those gauge characters are $\operatorname{tr}_{r_{g,N}}$ by
definition. To use their normalized finite field sums, one needs
specified coefficient realizations and a comparison of the scalar
expectations with the preceding congruence traces. If the final
trace is in a number field $K$, its embeddings into $E$ and into
$\mathbb C$ must be fixed; $\ell$-adic convergence alone does not
imply convergence of the complex expectations. The assumed limiting
Wilson comparison includes the required topology and complex scalar
identity. It gives the first equality in the theorem.
The second is the elementary matrix identity that the trace is the
sum of eigenvalues with algebraic multiplicity. An identification
with a Bost--Connes Adams operation further requires its actual
representation and intertwiner; Borger's $\lambda$-ring operations
do not themselves construct that map on this cohomological carrier.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[local normalizations and comparison data]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r6:rem:independence-wilson-line}
The proposed local identifications involve Borger~2009, \S8,
on $\lambda$-rings, Fargues--Scholze~2021 on geometric local
Langlands, and Mazur~1973 and Morishita~2012 on arithmetic topology.
A comparison must specify how their objects define the gauge label
and the local arithmetic operation in the trace equality.

The Satake parameter normalisation is: unramified \(\rho_{g,p}\) has eigenvalues
\(\alpha_{j}^{(p)}\) with symplectic pairing
\(\alpha_{j}^{(p)}\alpha_{2g+1-j}^{(p)}=p^{w_{\mathcal{D}_{g}}-1}\); the
Frobenius trace is \(\sum_{j=1}^{2g}\alpha_{j}^{(p)}\), the character value of
\(\rho_{g}\) itself at \(\mathrm{Frob}_{p}^{\mathrm{geom}}\), not the character of
\(\bigwedge^{k}\rho_{g}\) for any \(k\geq 2\).

The Wilson-line defect at finite level lies in
\(\mathrm{Sh}_{\tau_{\Sigma}(\alpha_{g,N})}(\mathcal{F}_{G_{g,N}}(\Sigma))\)
for \(\Sigma=\partial(\text{tubular neighbourhood of }K_{p})\) per
\Cref{v4-arith:3d-acs:thm:finite-bulk-boundary}; the rank-\(2g\) setting is
structurally identical. The Wilson line is functorial under tensor product of
representations at the classical level:
\(W^{(g)}_{\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}}=W^{(g)}_{\rho_{1}}\cdot W^{(g)}_{\rho_{2}}\);
at the quantum level this uses the factorisation algebra structure of
\(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim},g}\) (rank-\(2g\) Heisenberg), consistent with
Ch.~\ref{v4-arith:all-genus-koszul} Window~V.

The residual hypotheses are: (a) profinite renormalisation of
\(\{Z^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{G_{g,N},\alpha_{g,N}}\}_{N}\) and (b) compatible tower
\(\{\alpha_{g,N}\}_{N}\), inherited from \Cref{v4-arith:3d-acs:residual}(2); and
(c) rank-\(2g\) local Langlands at \(p\), i.e.\ \Cref{v4-arith:ag:residual}(5).
The longitude/gauge comparison additionally requires the explicit
coefficient realizations, finite characters and limit topologies specified
in the theorem. The Artin--Verdier pairing of
\Cref{v4-arith:3d-acs:def:finite-field-groupoid} defines an action
only after its coefficient and fundamental-class data have been supplied;
it does not by itself identify a holonomy representation with $\rho_{g,p}$.
Good reduction makes the chosen $\ell$-adic representation unramified
for $\ell\ne p$.
\end{remark}

\section{Archimedean Wilson-Surface at Genus \(g\)}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r6:wilson-surface-g}

A surface area determines a scalar observable. Relating it to a
boundary scaling operator requires a quantization and an operator
comparison. The curve case supplies a precise area normalization.

\begin{definition}[normalized curve area]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r6:def:arakelov-kahler-g}
For a compact connected Riemann surface \(X\) of genus \(g\ge1\),
choose a basis \(\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_g\) orthonormal for
\(\langle\omega,\eta\rangle=(i/2)\int_X\omega\wedge\bar\eta\).
With the complex orientation, set
\begin{equation}
 \mu_{\mathrm{Ar},g}=\frac{i}{2g}
      \sum_{j=1}^g\omega_j\wedge\overline{\omega_j}.
 \label{v4-arith:w9-r6:eq:arakelov-kahler-g}
\end{equation}
This form is basis independent and has integral one.
For the rank-zero degeneration define \(\mu_{\mathrm{Ar},0}=0\)
separately. This is not the unit-area metric on a genus-zero curve.
A Minkowski torus or a Shimura variety requires its own specified
oriented two-cycle and two-form. Its Riemannian volume need not
have degree two.
\end{definition}

Basis independence follows from the unitary change-of-basis identity.
Each summand has integral \(1/g\), so their sum has integral one.
An arithmetic interpretation also requires a boundary object and a
map realizing this surface and metric there, as in
\Cref{v4-arith:pres90:quantization}.

\begin{definition}[area scalar and quantum surface data]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r6:def:wilson-surface-g}
For a specified oriented compact surface \(\Sigma_\infty\), real
integrable two-form \(\mu_{\mathrm{Ar},g}\), and real level
\(k=k^{\mathrm{Ar},g}\), define the area scalar
\begin{equation}
 A_g(k)=\exp\left(-k\int_{\Sigma_\infty}\mu_{\mathrm{Ar},g}\right).
 \label{v4-arith:w9-r6:eq:wilson-surface-g-def}
\end{equation}
A quantum realization requires an arithmetic boundary Hilbert space
\(H_{\Sigma,g}\), its pairing, and a densely defined closed normal
operator \(A_{\Sigma,g}\) obtained from the boundary quantization.
When its spectral exponential is bounded, write
\begin{equation}
 F^{(g)}_\infty(k)=e^{-kA_{\Sigma,g}}.
 \label{v4-arith:w9-r6:eq:wilson-surface-g-op}
\end{equation}
The area scalar does not define \(A_{\Sigma,g}\). The fields, gauge
normalization, curvature coupling and boundary conditions are part
of the required quantization.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[rank-\(g\) Mellin unitarity and domains]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r6:lem:mellin-unitary-g}
For \(g\ge1\), set
\[H_{c,g}=L^2((\mathbb R_{>0})^g,\prod_j dx_j/x_j).\]
The transform
\begin{equation}
 (\mathcal M_g f)(u)=(2\pi)^{-g/2}\int f(x)
          \prod_j x_j^{-iu_j}\prod_j\frac{dx_j}{x_j}
 \label{v4-arith:w9-r6:eq:mellin-g}
\end{equation}
extends from compact smooth logarithmic tests to a unitary
\(H_{c,g}\to L^2(\mathbb R^g,du)\).
Let \(D_j=-i\partial_{t_j}\), where \(t_j=\log x_j\), on its
maximal weak-derivative domain. Then
\[
 \mathcal M_gD_j\mathcal M_g^{-1}=M_{u_j},\qquad
 S_g=\mathcal M_g^{-1}M_{u_1+\cdots+u_g}\mathcal M_g,
 \quad
 \operatorname{Dom}S_g=\{f:(\textstyle\sum_j u_j)\mathcal M_gf\in L^2\}.
\]
The operator \(S_g\) is the self-adjoint closure of \(\sum_jD_j\)
on compact smooth logarithmic tests. For real \(k\),
\[
 C_g(k):=e^{-ikS_g},\qquad
 (C_g(k)f)(x)=f(e^{-k}x_1,\ldots,e^{-k}x_g).
\]
For a real centre \(c\), the shifted Mellin kernel
\(\prod_jx_j^{c-iu_j}\) is unitary instead from
\(L^2(\prod_jx_j^{2c}dx_j/x_j)\) to \(L^2(du)\), with the same
factor \((2\pi)^{-g/2}\). It diagonalizes
\(-i(\partial_{t_j}+c)\) as \(M_{u_j}\) on the domain where
\(e^{c\sum t_j}f(e^t)\) has its \(j\)-th weak derivative in \(L^2(dt)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Logarithmic coordinates identify Haar measure with Lebesgue measure.
Fourier Plancherel and integration by parts prove the first identities,
including the maximal multiplication domains. For the sum, rotate
coordinates so that \(\sum_j\partial_{t_j}\) is a multiple of one
coordinate derivative. Compact smooth functions form a core by
cutoff and convolution approximation in its graph norm.
Fourier multiplication by \(e^{-ik\sum_j u_j}\) gives the displayed
translation. Multiplication by \(e^{c\sum t_j}\) is an isometry from
the weighted source to \(L^2(dt)\); differentiating this product
proves the shifted formula. With unweighted Haar measure a kernel
\(\prod_j x_j^{c+iu_j}\) instead has squared norm
\(\int |f(x)|^2\prod_jx_j^{2c}dx_j/x_j\), so it is not unitary
there when \(c\ne0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[surface transport and the primary comparison]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r6:thm:virasoro-conjugacy-g}
For $g\ge1$, assume the quantum data above and a unitary
\(V_g:H_{\Sigma,g}\to H_{c,g}\) with
\[
 V_g\operatorname{Dom}A_{\Sigma,g}=\operatorname{Dom}S_g,
 \qquad V_g A_{\Sigma,g}=iS_gV_g.
\]
Then \(F^{(g)}_\infty(k)=V_g^{-1}C_g(k)V_g\) is bounded and unitary
for every real \(k\).
Suppose also that a specified primary Hilbert space \(H_{\mathrm{prim},g}\)
has a closed normal operator \(A_{\mathrm{prim},g}\) and a unitary
\(T_g:H_{\mathrm{prim},g}\to H_{\Sigma,g}\) with
\(T_g\operatorname{Dom}A_{\mathrm{prim},g}=\operatorname{Dom}A_{\Sigma,g}\)
and \(T_gA_{\mathrm{prim},g}=A_{\Sigma,g}T_g\). Then
\begin{equation}
 F^{(g)}_\infty(k)=T_g e^{-kA_{\mathrm{prim},g}}T_g^{-1}.
 \label{v4-arith:w9-r6:eq:virasoro-conjugacy-g}
\end{equation}
For \(k\ne0\) and \(g\ge1\), neither \(iS_g\) nor \(C_g(k)\)
has nonzero eigenvectors. Thus ordinary Fock energy cannot supply
these intertwining data.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The domain identities give unitary equivalence of the normal
operators. Spectral calculus gives both exponential identities.
Under \(\mathcal M_g\), an eigenvector of \(S_g\) would be supported
on a hyperplane, which has Lebesgue measure zero. For \(C_g(k)\),
each eigenvalue preimage is a countable union of parallel hyperplanes,
also of measure zero. A Fock vacuum is a nonzero eigenvector,
so its image under an asserted intertwiner would contradict this fact.
\end{proof}

A native primary realization must specify the pairing used to prove
\(T_g\) and the arithmetic operations it preserves.
If Virasoro operators \(L_n^{(g)}\) are intended, their common
invariant domain and commutators must also be constructed. Central
charge \(g\) is a separate representation assertion. Tensor Mellin
unitarity does not imply it. If \(L_0^{(g)}\) is realized as self-adjoint conformal energy,
it cannot be unitarily conjugate to the nonzero skew-adjoint
operator \(iS_g\). Formal Virasoro adjoint relations on a common
domain alone do not prove self-adjointness.
The relation of \(A_{\mathrm{prim},g}\) to \(L_0^{(g)}\) therefore
requires an explicit normalization and cannot identify these two
operators directly. These are the rank-\(g\) versions of
\Cref{v4-arith:pres90:primary-virasoro}.

\begin{remark}[level differentiation]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r6:rem:wilson-surface-realisation}
For a finite measure \(d\nu\) and real measurable area \(a\), suppose
\(Z(k)=\int e^{-ka}\,d\nu\) and differentiation under the integral
is justified. Then \(Z'(k)=-\int a e^{-ka}\,d\nu\).
When \(Z(k)\ne0\), the normalized area expectation is
\(-\partial_k\log Z(k)\). This is a scalar insertion identity.
It is not an equality between \(Z'(k)\) and a boundary operator.
For bounded \(a\), dominated convergence justifies the formula
on every compact real \(k\)-interval.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[normalization and the arithmetic boundary]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r6:rem:independence-wilson-surface}
The centered translation exponential is \(C_g(k)\).
Adding a shift \(c_j\) to each factor's normal generator gives
\(e^{-k\sum_jc_j}C_g(k)\). Thus a motivic normalization requires
specifying every \(c_j\), not merely naming a critical line.
For the normalized curve, \(A_g(k)=e^{-k}\) is scalar.
When \(k\ne0\), a compact logarithmic test with sufficiently small
support has disjoint support from its translate by \(k(1,\ldots,1)\).
Consequently \(C_g(k)\) is nonscalar and cannot equal that area scalar.
The arithmetic geometry, quantization, primary pairing and generator
comparisons remain necessary to realize the continuous formulas on
the intended boundary objects.
At finite gauge level the proposed native surface-defect carrier is
$\mathrm{Sh}_{\tau(\alpha_{g,N})}
(\mathcal F_{G_{g,N}}(\Sigma_\infty))$.
Constructing it requires a defined field groupoid on the arithmetic
archimedean surface, a transgressed twist $\tau(\alpha_{g,N})$ in its
specified coefficient theory, and the resulting category of twisted
sheaves. Its Hilbert realization and observable comparison are
additional data. The rank-one tensor question asks for an equivalence
from a specified tensor product of $g$ rank-one defect categories to
this category, compatible with their twists, pairings and Wilson
observables. A decomposition of the Hodge vector space alone is not
such a categorical equivalence. If the surface geometry, action and
measure split and these categorical and Hilbert comparison maps are
constructed, the tensor product of the realized rank-one observables
is the proposed genus-$g$ observable. For Jacobian and Minkowski
rows these compatibilities must be proved; the Siegel row additionally
requires its actual Siegel--Maass metric and normalization.
\end{remark}
\section{Global Frobenius at Genus \(g\) and the Deninger Identification}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r6:deninger-g}

The Wilson-line and Wilson-surface operators at each place are local.
To obtain the Deninger character identity
\(\chi(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim},g})(s)=\Lambda_{\mathcal{D}_{g}}(s)\), one
must assemble them into a single global operator on the conditional
Deninger cohomology \(H^{\bullet}_{\Delta,g}\). The restricted tensor
product over all places is therefore a formal global Frobenius until
the descent and spectral-flow hypotheses are imposed.

\begin{definition}[global genus-\(g\) Frobenius]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r6:def:global-F-g}
For a fixed real level \(k\), the formal global symbol is
\begin{equation}
 F^{(g)}:=F^{(g)}_\infty(k)\otimes\bigotimes_p'\psi_p^{(g)}.
 \label{v4-arith:w9-r6:eq:global-F-g}
\end{equation}
Its operator realization requires local Hilbert spaces, reference
vectors and boundedness or convergence conditions. A sufficient
construction is given in the following theorem. Identifying that
Hilbert tensor with the primary arithmetic trace sector requires
its native pairing and realization map. Its action on Deninger
cohomology further requires a complex and an action-compatible
chain comparison, with the hypotheses in
\Cref{v4-arith:w8-a:thm:all-genus-deninger}.
\end{definition}
\begin{theorem}[genus-\(g\) Wilson assembly of the global Frobenius]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r6:thm:global-F-g-wilson}
Fix $g\ge1$. Assume the finite-place Wilson trace comparisons of
\Cref{v4-arith:w9-r6:thm:wilson-frobenius-g} and the native surface
comparison of \Cref{v4-arith:w9-r6:thm:virasoro-conjugacy-g}.
For each prime choose a Hilbert space \(H_{p,g}\), a unit reference
vector \(e_{p,g}\), and a bounded contraction \(\psi_p^{(g)}\)
fixing that vector. Then, for real \(k\),
\[
 F^{(g)}(k)=F_\infty^{(g)}(k)\otimes\bigotimes_p'\psi_p^{(g)}
\]
defines a contraction on
\(H_{\Sigma,g}\widehat\otimes\bigotimes_p'(H_{p,g},e_{p,g})\).
Its archimedean factor is the transported continuous operator,
and its finite-place Wilson traces are the specified Frobenius traces.
To regard this as an action on the arithmetic primary trace sector
requires a unitary realization of that sector in this tensor product
intertwining its local operations. Descent to a Deninger complex
requires an action-compatible chain map and the stated completion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
On tensors equal to \(e_{p,g}\) at all but finitely many primes,
apply \(\psi_p^{(g)}\) at each exceptional prime. Since every other
factor fixes its reference vector, this is independent of the chosen
finite set. The finite tensor maps are contractions and therefore
extend to the Hilbert completion. Tensoring with the unitary surface
factor preserves the norm bound. The local trace comparisons are
the assumed Wilson identities. They identify scalar traces and do
not themselves supply the primary unitary or the cohomological map.
\end{proof}
The following arithmetic identification assumes the native
cohomological comparisons and determinant identity stated in
\Cref{v4-arith:w8-a:thm:all-genus-deninger} on the specified row.
The surface and tensor constructions above do not construct them.
For its purity clause, one must additionally identify the native
middle-degree generator, with its domain and multiplicities, with
the operator whose eigenvalues are the nontrivial zero occurrences.
Its Frobenius must be the corresponding exponential $e^{-\Theta^{(g)}}$.
An equality of local scalar traces does not give these operator data.

\label{v4-arith:w9-r6:thm:deninger-g-full}
\ClaimStatusProvedHereConditional \emph{(on
\Cref{v4-arith:w8-a:thm:all-genus-deninger}(1)--(4),
\Cref{v4-arith:w9-r6:thm:wilson-frobenius-g},
\Cref{v4-arith:w9-r6:thm:virasoro-conjugacy-g}, and

\Cref{v4-arith:w9-r6:thm:global-F-g-wilson})}.
For every $g\ge1$ and every
arithmetic genus-\(g\) datum
\(\mathcal{D}_{g}=(\mathcal{B}_{g},\mathcal{E}_{g},\varphi_{g})\):
\begin{enumerate}

\label{v4-arith:w9-r6:eq:character-g}

\end{equation}

\section{Uniform \(g=0\) Compatibility}
\label{v4-arith:w9-r6:g-zero-recovery}

The empty genus tensor is the tensor unit. To compare it with a
nontrivial arithmetic theory, the latter must first be specified.

\begin{corollary}[rank-zero tensor comparison]
\label{v4-arith:w9-r6:cor:g-zero}
At \(g=0\), the empty Fock tensor is \(H_0=\mathbb C\), its
centered generator is zero, and its exponential is \(1_{H_0}\).
Let \(H_T\) be a separately specified Tate Hilbert space with closed
normal generator \(A_T\). The map
\[
 Q:\mathbb C\widehat\otimes H_T\longrightarrow H_T,
 \qquad Q(z\otimes h)=zh
\]
is unitary. It maps \(\mathbb C\otimes\operatorname{Dom}A_T\)
onto \(\operatorname{Dom}A_T\) and intertwines
\(1\otimes A_T\) with \(A_T\). If \(e^{-kA_T}\) is bounded,
\[
 Q(1\otimes e^{-kA_T})Q^{-1}=e^{-kA_T}.
\]
A nonzero rank-two Wilson label is independent of the rank-zero
trace. A native Tate surface/Virasoro comparison holds only with
the geometry, pairing, quantum observable and generator maps of
\Cref{v4-arith:pres90:quantization}, \Cref{v4-arith:pres90:primary-virasoro}.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The empty tensor and sum define \(H_0\) and its generator.
The Hilbert tensor norm satisfies \(\|z\otimes h\|=|z|\|h\|\),
so \(Q\) is an onto isometry. Its inverse is \(h\mapsto1\otimes h\).
The domain and operator identities hold on \(1\otimes h\) and
hence on the full stated domain. Functional calculus proves the
exponential identity. The trace of the zero-dimensional identity
is zero, whereas its symmetric-algebra character is one.
Neither operation constructs a rank-two representation.
\end{proof}

If the supplied Tate character is \(\Lambda_\zeta(s)\), adjoining
the empty tensor leaves it unchanged. Multiplication by
\(s(s-1)/2\) gives \(\xi_{\mathbb R}(s)\).
This scalar identity does not construct an arithmetic cohomology
complex or its determinant map. Those require the additional data
of \Cref{v4-arith:pres90:cohomology}. In particular, a choice of
\(H_T\), rather than the substitution \(g=0\), supplies any
nontrivial continuous scaling or arithmetic zero operator.

\label{v4-arith:w9-r6:rem:hw-ss-rh}

\item \(w_{\mathcal{D}_{g}}=1\) (Dedekind, Window~II at totally imaginary

\item \(w_{\mathcal{D}_{g}}=2\) (Hasse--Weil, Windows~I, V at the Jacobian

\item \(w_{\mathcal{D}_{g}}=w_{\pi,\mathrm{spin}}\) (Siegel spin, Window~VI at cusp form

\end{itemize}

\end{remark}
