\chapter{Quantum Arithmetic Chern--Simons: Bulk Extension and Residual Obstruction}
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:chapter}

\emph{Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:3d-acs-E1bar} states
\Cref{v4-arith:3d-acs:conj:qu-acs}, the profinite
\(GL_{2}(\widehat{\mathbb Z})\) quantum arithmetic Chern--Simons
theory on \(\overline{\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbb{Z}}\), as three linked
bulk-boundary axioms. Every formal consequence depends on its
resolution: Ch.~\ref{v4-arith:w9-r1:wilson-frobenius}'s continuous
Wilson-Frobenius identity at \(\varprojlim_{N}^{\mathrm{ren}}\),
Ch.~\ref{v4-arith:w9-r2:archimedean-wilson-surface}'s specified
unitary conjugacy between \(F_{\infty}^{\mathrm{WS}}\) and
\(F_{\infty}\), the partition formula
\(Z_{\mathrm{ACS}^{\mathrm{qu}}}=\xi\) of
\Cref{v4-arith:3d-acs:cor:partition-bar}, and the triangle of
\Cref{v4-arith:w8-a:cor:triangle}. The bulk measure is examined through
five constructions: (i) state-sum renormalisation along the congruence
tower; (ii) Reshetikhin--Turaev theory for a modular trace lift of the
boundary trace class; (iii) Costello--Gwilliam factorization algebras on the
\'etale site of \(\overline{\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbb{Z}}\); (iv) Lurie
cobordism hypothesis on an \'etale arithmetic cobordism category;
(v) Scholze six-functor on a conjectural arithmetic diamond. For a specified finite field groupoid and action, its weighted sum
is defined. Coherent tower measures and an open-site factorization
structure require their own maps and descent. The archimedean Hilbert
realization, modular trace lift and arithmetic diamond add further
comparison data.}

\section{Setup and Axiomatic Target}
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:setup}

\begin{definition}[arithmetic space and boundary data]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:def:3space}
Write \(M=\overline{\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbb Z}\) for the intended
arithmetic compactification. An ACS realization must specify a site
\(\mathscr X\) for \(M\), a boundary object \(\mathscr B_\infty\),
and a boundary morphism \(j:\mathscr B_\infty\to\mathscr X\).
It must state a dualizing coefficient object \(\mathcal L\), its
cohomological degree and a trace map for pairing the action with
an arithmetic fundamental class.
At infinity it must also specify a smooth oriented surface
\(\Sigma_\infty\), a comparison to \(\mathscr B_\infty\), and a
metric whose curvature pulls back to a specified real two-form
\(\omega_\infty\). The smooth disk in
\Cref{v4-arith:w9-r2:def:Sigma-infty} supplies one local metric
model with area \(1/2\). Its existence does not construct the
arithmetic boundary morphism or its metric pullback.
For that disk, boundary integrals use its counterclockwise orientation;
a closed surface requires a separately specified surface.
\end{definition}
\begin{definition}[congruence tower and gauge tower]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:def:tower}
\ClaimStatusDefinitional. The \emph{gauge tower} is the inverse
system of finite groups
\[
\mathcal{T}:=\Bigl\{
G_{N}:=GL_{2}(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z),\qquad N\in\mathbb Z_{\geq 1}
\Bigr\}
\]
ordered by divisibility \(M\mid N\) giving reductions \(G_{N}\twoheadrightarrow G_{M}\), with
\[
\varprojlim_{N\in\mathcal{T}}G_{N}\;=\;GL_{2}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}).
\]
A \emph{level datum} is a compatible system
\(\alpha_{\bullet}=\{\alpha_{N}\in H^{3}(BG_{N},\mathbb C^{\times})\}_{N}\)
whose inverse-limit class is the arithmetic level
\(k^{\mathrm{Ar}}=\kappa^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathsf G}\) of
Ch.~\ref{v4-arith:3d-acs-E1bar}.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[the three axioms recalled]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:rem:axioms}
\Cref{v4-arith:3d-acs:conj:qu-acs} asks for a renormalised profinite
limit
\[
\mathrm{ACS}^{\mathrm{qu}}_{k^{\mathrm{Ar}}}
:=\varprojlim_{N}^{\mathrm{ren}}Z^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{G_{N},\alpha_{N}}
\]
satisfying:
\textbf{(WZW)}
\(\operatorname{HH}_{0}
(\partial\,\mathrm{ACS}^{\mathrm{qu}}_{k^{\mathrm{Ar}}})
\simeq\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}\) on the archimedean boundary, where
\(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}\) is the Hochschild trace class of the
identity on the global arithmetic Iwahori--Hecke category of \(GL_{2}\)
(Ch.~\ref{v4-arith:3d-acs-E1bar});
\textbf{(Z)} \(Z_{\mathrm{ACS}^{\mathrm{qu}}}(\overline{\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbb Z};k^{\mathrm{Ar}})=\xi(k^{\mathrm{Ar}})\),
where \(\xi\) is the completed Riemann zeta function
(\Cref{v4-arith:3d-acs:cor:partition-bar});
\textbf{(W)} \(\langle W_{\rho}(K_{p})\rangle=\mathrm{tr}\,\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_{p})\),
where \(W_{\rho}(K_{p})\) is the Wilson-loop observable at the prime knot \(K_{p}\subset\overline{\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbb{Z}}\)
in representation \(\rho\) (Ch.~\ref{v4-arith:w9-r1:wilson-frobenius}).
\end{remark}

\section{Construction I: Tower Renormalisation via Kan Extension}
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:construction-I}

The most direct construction is the one inherited from the finite-level
state-sum construction of
\Cref{v4-arith:3d-acs:thm:finite-level-ACS}. For specified finite field groupoids and compatible action phases,
each $Z^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{G_N,\alpha_N}$ is the corresponding finite sum. The obstruction is the existence of a profinite
functor compatible with (WZW), (Z), (W).

\subsection{Finite-level Kan tower}
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:kan-tower}

\begin{lemma}[finite reduction and lifting mass]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:lem:reduction}
Let $\pi:G_N\twoheadrightarrow G_M$ be a surjection of finite groups.
It induces pullback of level classes and extension of structure group
on field groupoids. Suppose the chosen arithmetic space $M'$ has
finite field groupoids, with compatible phases
$\alpha_N=\pi^*\alpha_M$.
For a $G_M$ field $b$, write $\mathcal L_b$ for the homotopy fibre
of its lifting groupoid, including the isomorphism to $b$.
If all these fibres have the same finite cardinality $m_{N,M}(M')$,
then
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:eq:reduction-compat}
 Z^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{G_N,\alpha_N}(M')
 =m_{N,M}(M')Z^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{G_M,\alpha_M}(M').
\end{equation}
For a central abelian kernel $K$, if a lift exists and the lifting
fibre is the groupoid of $K$-torsors, its cardinality is
$|H^1(M',K)|/|H^0(M',K)|$, when these groups are finite.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Finite-groupoid integration weights each isomorphism class by the
reciprocal order of its automorphism group. Fubini over homotopy
fibres gives the sum over $b$ of its phase times
$|\mathcal L_b|/|\operatorname{Aut}b|$.
Constant fibre mass gives the displayed identity.
For the stated torsor groupoid, the isomorphism classes form
$H^1(M',K)$ and each automorphism group is $H^0(M',K)$.
Their cardinality ratio proves the second assertion.
A nonempty lifting fibre is a hypothesis: centrality does not
remove an obstruction to lifting a $G_M$ field.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the measure in a reduction tower]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:rem:divergence-factor}
For $K\cong(\mathbb F_\ell)^d$ with trivial action, the torsor
mass above is $|K|^{b_1-b_0}$, where
$b_i=\dim_{\mathbb F_\ell}H^i(M',\mathbb F_\ell)$.
For example, over a point the lifting fibre is $BK$, of mass
$1/|K|$, although $b_1=0$. Thus central-abelian reduction alone
does not give the factor $|K|^{b_1}$.
The profinite construction must determine the actual lifting
obstructions, stabilizers, phases and normalization at every transition.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[a chosen tower normalization]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:def:renorm-tower}
For a specified finite Euler characteristic $\chi(M')$, define
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:eq:renorm-tower}
 \widetilde Z_N(M')=|G_N|^{-\chi(M')}Z_N(M').
\end{equation}
This scalar definition does not assert transition compatibility.
The cohomology theory, coefficients and any compact-support or
boundary conditions used to define $\chi$ are part of the datum.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[the normalized transition factor]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:prop:renorm-compat}
Under the constant-mass hypothesis of the lemma, with $K=\ker\pi$,
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:eq:renorm-compat}
 \widetilde Z_N(M')=|K|^{-\chi(M')}m_{N,M}(M')\widetilde Z_M(M').
\end{equation}
Thus the equality of normalized scalar values follows when
$|K|^{-\chi}m_{N,M}=1$.
This scalar condition is additional to the coherent transition maps
required for state spaces and bordism assignments.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use $|G_N|=|K||G_M|$ in the defining normalization and the preceding
mass identity. No division by a possibly zero partition value is needed.
\end{proof}
For a connected torsor model with $b_1=0$ and $\chi=0$, the factor
is $|K|^{-1}$, not one. A different arithmetic measure or a
renormalization yielding compatible values must therefore be specified.
Nonabelian congruence kernels additionally require their full lifting
fibres. The arithmetic cohomology and duality needed to compute
these quantities are separate inputs; no numerical value of
$\chi(\overline{\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbb Z})$ is needed here.

\begin{theorem}[a coherent inverse tower defines a pro-object]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:thm:profinite-exists}
Suppose a cofiltered small category indexes actual state-sum functors
and coherent transition natural transformations in a specified target
category. This inverse diagram defines a pro-object in that functor
category. It does not by itself define a represented Hilbert limit
or a topological arithmetic field theory.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
An object of the pro-category is precisely such a cofiltered diagram.
An ordinary limit or a right Kan extension in a chosen target requires
that target to admit the relevant limits. Representation by a Hilbert
space is a further assertion, not part of the pro-object definition.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[tower and descent requirements]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:rem:construction-I-domain}
The arithmetic construction must supply the lifting measures and
coherent transition maps just specified. A topological state functor
further requires a represented limit and compatible gluing.
Its boundary trace must be identified with the designated primary
trace realization. Neither an inverse diagram nor the name of a
Kan extension constructs these data.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Sources and Hypotheses for Construction I}
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:ah:construction-I}

Kim's arithmetic action and its nonabelian extensions motivate
the finite field groupoids. Dijkgraaf--Witten and Freed--Quinn give
finite-groupoid integration models. The lifting-mass computation
above states the transition datum needed in the arithmetic tower.
A condensed or Tate realization additionally requires its topology,
transition maps and pairing. The finite sum and the represented
Hilbert limit are distinct constructions.

\begin{theorem}[Construction I conditional closure]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:thm:construction-I-closure}
\ClaimStatusProvedHereConditional \emph{(on a choice of condensed
topology on the Kan-limit and its descent to a concrete
topological state-functor)}. Under the profinite-Hilbert descent
hypothesis and a coherent tower as specified above, the represented limit of
\Cref{v4-arith:w10-a:thm:profinite-exists} defines
\(\mathrm{ACS}^{\mathrm{qu}}_{k^{\mathrm{Ar}}}\) satisfying the
reduction-coherence portion of
\Cref{v4-arith:3d-acs:conj:qu-acs}. The three axioms (WZW), (Z),
(W) reduce to: (WZW) restriction of the Kan-limit boundary functor
at \(\Sigma_{\infty}\) identifies with
\(\mathcal V^{\mathrm{prim}}\); (Z) evaluation of the Kan-limit on
the closed class \([M]\) reproduces \(\xi(k^{\mathrm{Ar}})\);
(W) evaluation on prime-knot insertions reproduces Frobenius traces.
Each of (WZW), (Z), (W) becomes a \emph{comparison statement} at the
Kan-limit, not a new existence statement.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The assumed coherent tower defines the pro-object of
\Cref{v4-arith:w10-a:thm:profinite-exists}.
Under a chosen condensed topology descending the pro-vector-space
structure to a Hilbert-space functor, (WZW), (Z), (W) become
comparison claims at the limit. Each is in principle checkable by
comparing the limit with the right-hand sides of the three axioms
using the finite-level comparisons of Chs.~\ref{v4-arith:w9-r1:wilson-frobenius}
and \ref{v4-arith:3d-acs-E1bar}. The existence of the descent is
hypothetical.
\end{proof}

\section{Construction II: Reshetikhin--Turaev from an Arithmetic Boundary Category}
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:construction-II}

The Reshetikhin--Turaev construction (Turaev 1994
\emph{Quantum Invariants of Knots and 3-Manifolds}, de Gruyter;
Reshetikhin--Turaev 1991 \emph{Invent.~Math.}~103, 547--597)
constructs topological invariants and the associated bordism functor
from a modular tensor category (MTC), with the required framing or
anomaly data. An arithmetic bordism interpretation requires an
additional comparison functor. In the arithmetic problem the input
cannot be the category of modules over \(\mathcal V^{\mathrm{prim}}\),
because \(\mathcal V^{\mathrm{prim}}\) is a Hochschild trace class, not
a vertex algebra. The possible Reshetikhin--Turaev input is instead a
modular tensor category whose Hochschild trace and regularised
character have \(\mathcal V^{\mathrm{prim}}\) as their boundary shadow.

\subsection{The arithmetic MTC candidate}
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:arith-MTC}

\begin{definition}[arithmetic modular tensor category candidate]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:def:arith-MTC}
\ClaimStatusDefinitional. The \emph{arithmetic modular tensor
category candidate} is
\[
\mathcal{MTC}^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{k^{\mathrm{Ar}}}
\;:=\;
\mathcal B^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{k^{\mathrm{Ar}}},
\]
a finite semisimple ribbon category equipped with a trace comparison
\[
        \operatorname{HH}_{0}
        \bigl(\mathcal B^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{k^{\mathrm{Ar}}}\bigr)
        \simeq
        \mathcal V^{\mathrm{prim}}
\]
and with Frobenius-labelled simple objects at the finite places. This
definition is a lifting problem from a Hochschild trace class to a
boundary category; it is not a module category over
\(\mathcal V^{\mathrm{prim}}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[the modular trace lift is conditional]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:rem:modularity-cond}
Modularity of \(\mathcal{MTC}^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{k^{\mathrm{Ar}}}\)
requires a non-degenerate braiding, a spherical trace, and compatibility
of the categorical trace with the Iwahori--Hecke Hochschild trace.
The completed-zeta character and finite-prime Fock characters are trace
statements. They do not construct a braided category whose trace is
\(\mathcal V^{\mathrm{prim}}\).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Reshetikhin--Turaev state-sum}
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:RT-state-sum}

\begin{theorem}[transport of a specified Reshetikhin--Turaev theory]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:thm:RT-construction}
Suppose the modular category and anomaly data define an RT functor
$Z_{\mathrm{RT}}$ on its topological bordism category $\mathsf B_{\mathrm{RT}}$.
Suppose an arithmetic bordism category $\mathsf B_{\mathrm{Ar}}$ and a
symmetric monoidal comparison $C:\mathsf B_{\mathrm{Ar}}\to\mathsf B_{\mathrm{RT}}$
are constructed. Then
$Z_{\mathrm{RT}}^{\mathrm{Ar}}=Z_{\mathrm{RT}}\circ C$
is a symmetric monoidal arithmetic assignment.
It has the intended primary boundary trace and Frobenius Wilson values
if its boundary trace maps and label comparisons identify those values.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Composition preserves the functorial and monoidal identities.
The last assertion is the stated equality of the trace and observable
maps under that composition. The ordinary RT construction does not
supply $C$ or those arithmetic equalities.
\end{proof}
The trace of a boundary category, the state space of a surface and
a scalar closed-bordism invariant have different types. Their proposed
arithmetic comparison must specify the maps between these objects.

\begin{remark}[closure of (WZW) and (W) modulo modularity]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:rem:RT-closes-WZW-W}
Under the RT and arithmetic bordism data just specified, and the trace
comparison of \Cref{v4-arith:w10-a:def:arith-MTC},
\Cref{v4-arith:w10-a:thm:RT-construction} gives the categorical
form of (WZW). The Wilson-line/Frobenius identity is the
Reshetikhin--Turaev quantum-trace formula only after the finite-place
label comparison is supplied. The remaining axiom (Z) is not
automatic: the partition function of an RT TQFT is the
\emph{Reshetikhin--Turaev invariant}, which is a finite complex
number, not an entire function of the level. Matching it with
\(\xi(k^{\mathrm{Ar}})\) requires a separate analytic input.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Partition Identification via the Regularised Boundary Character}
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:zhu-mod}

\begin{proposition}[conditional arithmetic partition identification]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:prop:RT-Zhu}
Assume the preceding RT transport data. Suppose the closed arithmetic
object has a specified decomposition whose state-space endomorphism
has trace $\chi_{\mathrm{reg}}(k^{\mathrm{Ar}})$, and its RT evaluation
is that trace. If the arithmetic character comparison identifies
$\chi_{\mathrm{reg}}(k)=\xi(k)$, then its partition value is
$\xi(k^{\mathrm{Ar}})$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Compose the assumed evaluation-to-trace and trace-to-character
identities. A topological Heegaard formula applies only after the
corresponding arithmetic bordism and decomposition maps have been given.
\end{proof}
The existence of this decomposition, regularized trace prescription
and analytic level family is part of the partition comparison problem.
Modularity of a finite category alone supplies none of these data.

\subsection{Sources and Hypotheses for Construction II}
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:ah:construction-II}

Reshetikhin--Turaev theory supplies a 3D TQFT from a modular tensor
category. Huang--Lepowsky theory is the complex-analytic model in
which a VOA produces such a category. In the arithmetic case the input
is not a module category over \(\mathcal V^{\mathrm{prim}}\), but a
modular trace lift
\(\operatorname{HH}_{0}(\mathcal{MTC}^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{k^{\mathrm{Ar}}})
\simeq\mathcal V^{\mathrm{prim}}\), together with non-degenerate
braiding, spherical trace, finite-place Frobenius labels, and the
regularised character comparison.

\begin{theorem}[Construction II conditional implication]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:thm:construction-II-closure}
\ClaimStatusProvedHereConditional \emph{(on a modular trace lift,
finite-place Frobenius labels, and the regularised-character
comparison, together with the arithmetic bordism transport data)}.
Under these hypotheses, the Reshetikhin--Turaev functor
of \Cref{v4-arith:w10-a:thm:RT-construction} gives the boundary trace
condition, the Wilson-line trace condition, and the scalar partition
identity of \Cref{v4-arith:3d-acs:conj:qu-acs}. Modularity alone does
not imply the zeta partition identity.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(WZW) is the MTC-boundary identification of
\Cref{v4-arith:w10-a:def:arith-MTC}. (W) is the RT quantum-trace
formula evaluated at the Frobenius conjugacy class via Morishita.
(Z) is \Cref{v4-arith:w10-a:prop:RT-Zhu}. The hypotheses are exactly
the modular trace lift, the finite-place label comparison, and the
regularised-character comparison.
\end{proof}

\section{Construction III: Costello--Gwilliam Factorization on \texorpdfstring{\((\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{\acute et}}\)}{the etale site}}
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:construction-III}

The Costello--Gwilliam formalism
(Costello--Gwilliam 2017 \emph{Factorization Algebras in Quantum
Field Theory I}, Cambridge Univ.~Press; vol.~II 2021) builds
perturbative QFTs as factorization algebras on a site. The
\'etale site of \(\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbb Z\) is a natural substitute
for the usual differential-geometric site in the arithmetic
setting.

\subsection{\'Etale factorization algebra}
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:CG-etale}

\begin{definition}[an arithmetic factorization candidate]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:def:CG-prefactor}
Let $X=\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbb Z$. Specify a category of arithmetic
regions with disjoint embeddings and a chosen factorization coverage.
A prefactorization candidate assigns a complex $\mathcal F(U)$ to each
region and maps $\bigotimes_i\mathcal F(U_i)\to\mathcal F(V)$ for
specified disjoint embeddings $U_i\to V$, with the required unit,
symmetry and composition coherences. A factorization object must also
satisfy descent for the chosen coverage. An arithmetic construction
must state this coverage and its relation to the etale site.
Ordinary descent of local systems does not replace this specification.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[finite field functions and their action functional]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:def:CG-Kim-prefactor}
For each specified finite field groupoid $\mathcal F_{G_N}(U)$, let
$\mathcal V_N(U)$ be the complex functions on its isomorphism classes.
Given an isomorphism-invariant phase $a_N(b)$, define
\[
 \mathcal I_{N,U}(v)=\sum_{[b]\in\pi_0\mathcal F_{G_N}(U)}
       \frac{a_N(b)v(b)}{|\operatorname{Aut}b|},\qquad
 Z_N(U)=\mathcal I_{N,U}(1).
\]
When the arithmetic action is defined, $a_N(b)$ is its exponential.
This requires the action's coefficient object, boundary conditions
and trace pairing from \Cref{v4-arith:w10-a:def:3space}.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[finite products and weighted integration]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:thm:CG-open}
Suppose disjoint unions of specified arithmetic regions give products
of their finite field groupoids, and their phases multiply.
Then
\[
 \mathcal V_N(U\sqcup V)\cong\mathcal V_N(U)\otimes\mathcal V_N(V),
 \qquad \mathcal I_{N,U\sqcup V}=\mathcal I_{N,U}\otimes\mathcal I_{N,V}.
\]
In particular $Z_N(U\sqcup V)=Z_N(U)Z_N(V)$.
The vector space $\mathcal V_N(U)$ has dimension
$|\pi_0\mathcal F_{G_N}(U)|$; its scalar integral is not that vector space.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Delta functions on isomorphism classes give a basis. The product
classes are pairs, and their automorphism orders multiply.
The tensor basis gives the first isomorphism; finite Fubini gives
the identity of integration functionals and the partition values.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the open-site factorization problem]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:rem:construction-III-uncond}
The disjoint-product calculation is a part of the proposed
Costello--Gwilliam construction on the arithmetic site.
A factorization algebra additionally needs covariant structure maps
for disjoint embeddings, their coherences, and the appropriate
factorization descent condition. Descent of field groupoids does
not by itself prove these assertions after passing to functions
or weighted pushforwards. The action pairing on an open arithmetic
region also requires its own compact-support or relative duality.
Constructing these data on $X=\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbb Z$ is a
separate problem from the archimedean extension. The finite-product
identity neither localizes every obstruction at infinity nor gives
an unconditional factorization algebra.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Archimedean extension}
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:CG-infty}

\begin{proposition}[data for an archimedean operator extension]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:prop:CG-infty-obstruction}
Suppose a specified arithmetic factorization object has an extension
to the boundary geometry of \Cref{v4-arith:w10-a:def:3space}.
A Hilbert realization of its primary boundary sector requires a
complex vector space \(E_\infty\) extracted in a specified degree
and completion from that trace sector, a Hermitian positive
semidefinite pairing \(q_\infty\), linear in its first variable,
and a complex-linear realization map
\[
 I_\infty:E_\infty\to H_B,\qquad
 \langle I_\infty v,I_\infty w\rangle=q_\infty(v,w),
 \qquad\overline{I_\infty E_\infty}=H_B.
\]
It identifies \(H_B\) with the Hilbert completion of
\(E_\infty/\{v:q_\infty(v,v)=0\}\).
Boundary operations must preserve this nullspace to descend.
Their asserted closed generators require domains and closures in
\(H_B\); a quantum surface comparison further requires the data of
\Cref{v4-arith:pres90:quantization}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Cauchy--Schwarz for a positive semidefinite Hermitian form makes
its nullspace orthogonal to every vector. The displayed identity
therefore induces an isometry on the quotient, which extends to
its completion. The dense-range assumption makes this extension
onto. If an operation does not preserve the nullspace, equivalent
representatives have inequivalent images, so it cannot descend.
The construction specifies neither an unbounded operator domain nor
an exponential until the corresponding operator data are supplied.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[native boundary realization]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:rem:construction-III-stop}
A finite field groupoid supplies finite-place state data. The passage
to an arithmetic boundary Hilbert space additionally uses the geometry,
native pairing and realization map above. A Hilbert completion alone
does not supply the factorization gluing maps or a quantum observable.
The desired boundary is a realization of a primary Hochschild trace
sector, not a vertex algebra specified by that trace class.
A proposed identification with an ordinary Fock representation must
state the operations it preserves. Its occupation energy has
eigenvectors and cannot be intertwined unitarily with continuous
Mellin scaling, by \Cref{v4-arith:w9-r2:lem:L0-Mellin}.
Consequently Hilbert descent, native pairing, boundary extension and
surface transport are separate required comparisons.
\end{remark}
\subsection{Sources and Hypotheses for Construction III}
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:ah:construction-III}

Costello--Gwilliam provides the factorization-algebra framework.
Finite field groupoids and a specified Kim action give the functions
and finite integration above. To use them as a factorization object
one must construct its structure maps and descent, including the
arithmetic support conditions. Its boundary realization then requires
\Cref{v4-arith:w10-a:prop:CG-infty-obstruction}.

\begin{theorem}[conditional factorization and tower assignment]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:thm:construction-III-closure}
Suppose the finite field-function assignments have been equipped with
factorization structure and descent, and their scalar integration
maps agree with the specified arithmetic action and Wilson insertions.
Suppose also that coherent reduction maps define an inverse diagram
of these factorization objects. Then this diagram is a pro-object
in the stated factorization category, and its levelwise scalar and
Wilson values are the assumed arithmetic ones.
A represented limit and passage of global sections to that limit
require additional continuity and descent theorems.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The supplied coherent diagram defines its pro-object. The integration
identities hold at each level by hypothesis. No exchange of an
inverse limit with global sections is used in this conclusion.
\end{proof}

\section{Construction IV: Cobordism Hypothesis}
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:construction-IV}

Lurie's cobordism hypothesis (Lurie 2008 arXiv:0905.0465, Thm.~2.4.6)
classifies extended \(n\)-dimensional TQFTs as fully dualizable
objects in a symmetric monoidal \((\infty,n)\)-category. For
\(n=3\) and a target
\(\mathcal{C}=\mathrm{ArithTC}\) the arithmetic tensor category, a
quantum ACS is specified by a fully dualizable object of \(\mathcal{C}\).

\subsection{Fully dualizable object candidate}
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:cobord-candidate}

\begin{definition}[arithmetic target \((\infty,3)\)-category]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:def:arith-target}
\ClaimStatusDefinitional. The \emph{arithmetic target} is the
symmetric monoidal \((\infty,3)\)-category
\(\mathrm{ArithTC}\) of arithmetic braided tensor categories:
objects are braided tensor categories equipped with a trace functor to
arithmetic Hochschild trace classes; 1-morphisms are bimodule
categories compatible with trace; 2-morphisms are bimodule functors;
3-morphisms are natural transformations; with symmetric monoidal
structure from the Tate restricted tensor of
Ch.~\ref{v4-arith:tate-tensor}.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[duality data for a specified arithmetic target]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:prop:fully-dualizable-candidate}
Suppose the proposed target $\mathrm{ArithTC}$ is constructed as a
symmetric monoidal $(\infty,3)$-category, with its trace-compatible
composition and coherences. An object is fully dualizable when it
has a dual with evaluation and coevaluation and the required adjoints
through the three morphism levels, satisfying their coherence identities.
Supplying these data for the modular trace candidate is an additional
arithmetic requirement.
\end{proposition}
The result of Douglas--Schommer-Pries--Snyder concerns fusion
categories of nonzero global dimension in their tensor-category
Morita setting. It does not construct the proposed arithmetic target
or prove that a trace-compatible lift preserves the requisite dualities.
This comparison must specify a monoidal functor and transport the
actual evaluation, coevaluation and adjunction data.

\begin{theorem}[the framed cobordism output on specified data]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:thm:cobord-output}
Given such a target and a fully dualizable object $A$ in it,
the framed cobordism hypothesis gives a symmetric monoidal functor
$Z_A:\mathrm{Bord}_3^{\mathrm{fr}}\to\mathrm{ArithTC}$ with $Z_A(pt)=A$,
classified by this fully dualizable datum.
An arithmetic assignment follows if a compatible arithmetic bordism
category and comparison functor into $\mathrm{Bord}_3^{\mathrm{fr}}$
are supplied. An oriented version requires the corresponding
homotopy fixed-point structure.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply the framed cobordism hypothesis to the stated fully dualizable
object, then compose with the assumed arithmetic comparison functor.
The oriented extension uses its extra tangential structure.
Equality with a particular RT theory further requires agreement
of their extended data in a common target; it does not follow
merely because both theories use a modular category.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[arithmetic cobordism category]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:rem:arith-cobord}
The cobordism hypothesis applies to a cobordism category
of smooth manifolds. Its arithmetic counterpart requires an
\emph{arithmetic cobordism category} whose objects are
arithmetic 2-surfaces and morphisms are arithmetic 3-spaces.
Construction of such a category is \Cref{v4-arith:3d-acs:residual}
(item 1 of the residual list in Ch.~\ref{v4-arith:3d-acs-E1bar}):
finite arithmetic cobordism functoriality. This is the Construction IV
analogue of Construction III's archimedean extension obstruction.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Sources and Hypotheses for Construction IV}
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:ah:construction-IV}

The cobordism-hypothesis presentation uses Lurie's theorem and the
Douglas--Schommer-Pries--Snyder dualizability theorem in its
specified tensor-category setting. It also uses the Reshetikhin--Turaev state sum on
the resulting modular tensor category. The required inputs are an
arithmetic cobordism category, the modular trace lift, finite-place
label compatibility, and the regularised-character comparison.

\begin{theorem}[Construction IV conditional closure]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:thm:construction-IV-closure}
\ClaimStatusProvedHereConditional \emph{(on the arithmetic cobordism
category, modular trace lift, finite-place label comparison, and
regularised-character comparison, the fully dualizable target data
and the arithmetic bordism comparison above)}. Given these hypotheses, the
cobordism hypothesis produces a unique extended 3D TQFT
\(Z_{\mathrm{coh}}^{\mathrm{Ar}}\) whose trace, Wilson, and scalar
partition data satisfy the axioms of
\Cref{v4-arith:3d-acs:conj:qu-acs}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply \Cref{v4-arith:w10-a:thm:cobord-output} and the specified
arithmetic comparison. The assumed trace, Wilson and regularized
character maps give the three scalar and boundary clauses.
Identification with an RT theory requires the extended-data
comparison stated in that theorem.
\end{proof}

\section{Construction V: Six-Functor Formalism on an Arithmetic Diamond}
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:construction-V}

Scholze's six-functor formalism (Scholze 2020--2022
\emph{Lectures on Condensed Mathematics}, Bonn; Scholze 2022
\emph{Six-Functor Formalisms} lecture notes) applies to diamonds and
to a broad class of stacks. A quantum field theory on a space is
given by a ``theory'' in the sense of six-functor formalism:
an association of \(\infty\)-categories with the six operations
\(\otimes,\operatorname{Hom},f^{*},f_{*},f_{!},f^{!}\). For 3D
CS on a diamond, Scholze et al.\ have outlined the six-functor
formalism as the natural home (Fargues--Scholze 2021
arXiv:2102.13459).

\subsection{Diamond of \texorpdfstring{\(\overline{\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbb Z}\)}{Spec Z bar}}
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:diamond}

\begin{conjecture}[arithmetic diamond]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:conj:arith-diamond}
\ClaimStatusConjectured. There exists a diamond
\(\overline{\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbb Z}^{\diamond}\) in the sense of
Scholze 2020 \emph{Etale Cohomology of Diamonds}
(arXiv:1709.07343, \S11) which is a globalisation of the
Fargues--Fontaine curves at each prime, with an archimedean
completion at \(\{\infty\}\) given by a continuous version of
\(\Sigma_{\infty}\). The diamond carries a six-functor formalism
with values in condensed \(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\)-sheaves.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[form of the arithmetic diamond]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:rem:diamond-form}
\Cref{v4-arith:w10-a:conj:arith-diamond} is a conjecture with no
known construction in the cited literature. The closest object is the
Clausen--Scholze analytic \(\mathbb{Z}\)-spectrum
(Clausen--Scholze 2020 \emph{Condensed Mathematics} lecture notes),
which builds the condensed structure on the arithmetic side but does
not supply the diamond-plus-archimedean-completion we would need.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Six-functor ACS}
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:six-fun-ACS}

\begin{proposition}[a specified six-functor trace expression]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:prop:six-fun-ACS}
Suppose the proposed arithmetic space $X^\diamond$ has a specified
six-functor theory $\mathcal C(-)$ and structure map $f:X^\diamond\to pt$
for which $f^!$ and $f_!$ are defined. Choose an object
$B\in\mathcal C(pt)$; relating it to a boundary category or its
Hochschild trace requires a specified realization functor.
Then $\mathcal T=f^!B$ and $f_!\mathcal T\in\mathcal C(pt)$ are defined.
If $f_!\mathcal T$ is dualizable, its identity has a trace in
$\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal C(pt)}(1)$.
A scalar value requires a specified map from this endomorphism ring
to the scalar coefficient field.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply the two assumed functors in order. Dualizability supplies
evaluation and coevaluation, whose trace composite is the stated
endomorphism of the unit. Applying the chosen scalar map gives
its scalar trace. Without dualizability or a separate regularized
trace prescription this final operation is not defined.
\end{proof}
Identifying this trace with $\xi(k^{\mathrm{Ar}})$ requires the
local-global trace comparison, including the archimedean factor
and pole normalization. The arithmetic diamond, boundary object,
coefficient realization and this trace identity are separate inputs.
Local geometric Langlands does not supply them by formal application
of $f^!$.

\begin{remark}[geometric and trace inputs for Construction V]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:rem:construction-V-form}
Write $B_{k^{\mathrm{Ar}}}\in\mathcal C(pt)$ for the specified coefficient realization of the boundary datum $\mathscr B_{k^{\mathrm{Ar}}}$. Construction V places \Cref{v4-arith:3d-acs:conj:qu-acs} inside
the arithmetic diamond, a coefficient and boundary trace object,
the requisite traceability data, and
the local-global trace formula. The diamond is
\Cref{v4-arith:w10-a:conj:arith-diamond}; the trace object lifts
\(\mathcal V^{\mathrm{prim}}\); the trace formula supplies the completed
Euler product.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Sources and Hypotheses for Construction V}
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:ah:construction-V}

The six-functor presentation uses Scholze diamonds, Clausen--Scholze

places. Its inputs are the arithmetic diamond, a boundary trace object
\(\mathscr B_{k^{\mathrm{Ar}}}\), the local-global trace formula, and

\(\operatorname{Tr}(f_!f^!B_{k^{\mathrm{Ar}}})\).

\label{v4-arith:w10-a:thm:construction-V-closure}
\ClaimStatusProvedHereConditional \emph{(on the arithmetic diamond,
boundary and coefficient realization, traceability, local-global
trace formula, and finite-place Frobenius compatibility)}. Under these hypotheses, the six-functor ACS
\(\mathcal T_{k^{\mathrm{Ar}}}=f^{!}B_{k^{\mathrm{Ar}}}\)
satisfies (WZW), (Z), (W) of
\Cref{v4-arith:3d-acs:conj:qu-acs}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(WZW) is the trace comparison
\(\operatorname{HH}_{0}(\mathscr B_{k^{\mathrm{Ar}}})
\simeq\mathcal V^{\mathrm{prim}}\). (Z) is the local-global trace
formula for
\(\operatorname{Tr}(f_{!}f^{!}B_{k^{\mathrm{Ar}}})\). (W) is
the finite-place spectral-Hecke compatibility producing the Frobenius
trace on local Galois representations.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Common Archimedean Term}
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:common-residual}

\begin{theorem}[comparison of two boundary Hilbert realizations]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:thm:common-residual}
Write \(H_c=L^2(\mathbb R_{>0},dx/x)\) and
\(\Theta_c=1/2+iD_c\), with \(D_c\) and its domain as in
\Cref{v4-arith:w9-r2:def:scaling}.
Let \((E_\infty,q_\infty)\) be a specified positive semidefinite
primary trace realization as in
\Cref{v4-arith:w10-a:prop:CG-infty-obstruction}.
Suppose a descended state space \(H_B\) and a primary Hilbert space
\(H_{\mathrm{prim}}\) have complex-linear dense-range maps \(I_B,I_{\mathrm{prim}}\)
from \(E_\infty\), both preserving \(q_\infty\).
Then there is a unique unitary \(T:H_B\to H_{\mathrm{prim}}\)
with \(TI_B=I_{\mathrm{prim}}\).
Suppose closed normal generators \(\Theta_B,\Theta_{\mathrm{prim}}\)
have cores \(I_B\mathcal D,I_{\mathrm{prim}}\mathcal D\), for a
specified \(\mathcal D\subset E_\infty\), and their actions agree
under \(T\) on these cores. Then
\[
 T\operatorname{Dom}\Theta_B=\operatorname{Dom}\Theta_{\mathrm{prim}},
 \qquad T\Theta_B=\Theta_{\mathrm{prim}}T.
\]
If a further unitary \(V_{\mathrm{prim}}:H_{\mathrm{prim}}\to H_c\)
intertwines \(\Theta_{\mathrm{prim}}\) with the continuous generator
\(\Theta_c\), including domains, then
\(V_\Sigma=V_{\mathrm{prim}}T\) gives the surface comparison in
\Cref{v4-arith:w10-a:thm:wilson-surface-compat}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The formula \(I_Bv\mapsto I_{\mathrm{prim}}v\) is well defined:
both kernels are the nullspace of \(q_\infty\). It is an isometry
on dense subspaces and extends uniquely to an onto unitary.
The graph of each generator is the closure of its graph on the
stated core. Applying \(T\oplus T\) to these graphs proves domain
equality and intertwining. Composition with \(V_{\mathrm{prim}}\)
proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[boundary comparisons for the different constructions]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:rem:aliases}
The inverse-limit, factorization and six-functor constructions each
ask for a native boundary realization. They can be identified through
the preceding theorem when they realize the same trace space,
pairing and operator cores. Establishing such identifications
requires actual maps from their respective boundary objects.
A topology on a pro-vector space does not define those maps.
Likewise a modular trace lift requires its own trace-to-boundary
realization before it supplies this Hilbert comparison.
No implication between the modular lift, the arithmetic diamond and
the Hilbert descent follows from their common intended trace class.
\end{remark}
\subsection{Relation to the Hilbert--P\'olya/Connes--Deninger obstruction}
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:HP-relation}

\begin{remark}[Hilbert descent and arithmetic spectral realization]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:rem:weaker-than-RH}
Hilbert descent asks for a limit state space and its native pairing.
The arithmetic Hilbert--P\'olya realization further specifies an
arithmetic middle-cohomology carrier, a self-adjoint operator
\(H_{\mathrm{HP}}\), and the determinant and trace normalization of
\(\Theta_\xi=\tfrac12+iH_{\mathrm{HP}}\).
These data are not clauses of Hilbert descent.
In particular, the zero-occurrence generator cannot be realized as
the restriction of continuous \(\Theta_c\) to an invariant Hilbert
subspace by an injective generator intertwiner: each occurrence has
a nonzero eigenvector, whereas \(\Theta_c\) has none.
An arithmetic cohomology comparison instead uses its own carrier,
as in \Cref{v4-arith:pres90:cohomology}.
\end{remark}

The purity condition depends on the operator and its carrier.
For continuous scaling, set
\[
 H_c=L^2(\mathbb R_{>0},dx/x),\qquad
 (U_a f)(x)=f(e^{-a}x),\qquad a\in\mathbb R.
\]
In the logarithmic coordinate \(t=\log x\), the self-adjoint generator is
\[
 D_c=-i\partial_t,\qquad
 \operatorname{Dom}D_c=\{f\in H_c:f(e^t)\in H^1(\mathbb R,dt)\}.
\]
Thus \(U_a=e^{-iaD_c}\),
\(\Theta_c=\tfrac12+iD_c\) has the same domain, and
\(F_\infty=e^{-\Theta_c}=e^{-1/2}U_1\).
Transport to a descended Hilbert space \(H_B\) requires a specified
unitary \(V:H_B\to H_c\). For a generator \(\Theta_B\), the required
comparison is
\[
 V\operatorname{Dom}\Theta_B=\operatorname{Dom}\Theta_c,
 \qquad V\Theta_B=\Theta_c V
 \quad\hbox{on }\operatorname{Dom}\Theta_B.
\]
It then gives \(e^{-\Theta_B}=V^{-1}F_\infty V\).

The arithmetic operator instead uses the multiset \(Z\) of nontrivial
zeta zeros, with one coordinate for each occurrence. On \(K_Z=\ell^2(Z)\), set
\[
 (\Theta_Z v)_\rho=\rho v_\rho,\qquad
 \operatorname{Dom}\Theta_Z=
 \left\{v\in\ell^2(Z):\sum_{\rho\in Z}|\rho v_\rho|^2<\infty\right\}.
\]
Its bounded exponential is \((F_Zv)_\rho=e^{-\rho}v_\rho\).
These operators distinguish continuous purity from arithmetic purity.

\begin{theorem}[Clausen--Scholze condensed-Hilbert descent plus A-TP \(\Rightarrow\) RH]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:thm:descent-plus-ATP-equals-RH}
\ClaimStatusProvedHereConditional \emph{(on the archimedean
condensed-Hilbert descent of
\Cref{v4-arith:w10-a:conj:condensed-descent} and the
A-TP archimedean positivity of
Ch.~\ref{v4-arith:w5-a:conj:archimedean-tate-positivity})}. Under
both the Clausen--Scholze condensed-Hilbert descent and A-TP, the Riemann
Hypothesis holds.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \(f\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)\), with
\(f^*(t)=\overline{f(-t)}\), A-TP states
\[
 W_\infty(f*f^*)\le P(f*f^*)-W_{\mathrm{fin}}(f*f^*).
\]
The signed explicit formula therefore gives \(W(f*f^*)\ge0\) for
every such \(f\). By Weil's criterion, all nontrivial zeros satisfy
\(\Re\rho=1/2\), which is RH.
On the coordinate vector at \(\rho\), the absolute value \(|F_Z|\)
has eigenvalue \(e^{-\Re\rho}\). Hence the same conclusion is precisely
\( |F_Z|=e^{-1/2}1_{K_Z}\), the arithmetic purity of
\Cref{v4-arith:w9-r2:thm:purity-ATP-RH}.
Conversely this equality forces \(\Re\rho=1/2\) at every occurrence,
and Weil's criterion recovers A-TP.

On \(H_c\), unitarity gives
\(F_\infty^*F_\infty=e^{-1}1_{H_c}\) without A-TP.
The specified unitary \(V\) transports this identity to \(H_B\)
by functional calculus, including the stated generator domains.
Thus descent provides the continuous Hilbert realization when its
comparison data exist. A-TP alone supplies the zero-placement
implication used above.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the archimedean Hilbert descent does not imply RH alone]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:rem:what-resolves}
Resolving the archimedean Hilbert descent alone does not
resolve RH: it constructs
\(\mathrm{ACS}^{\mathrm{qu}}_{k^{\mathrm{Ar}}}\) only on the
descended Hilbert-trace clauses (WZW), (Z), and (W); it does not
supply the full cobordism functor or the full Weil inequality A-TP.
The continuous scaling exponential is already pure. Under both descent
and A-TP, RH follows through the separate arithmetic operator \(F_Z\).
The archimedean Hilbert descent is the bulk-construction
obstruction; A-TP is the separate positivity obstruction. After the
descent, the remaining bulk gluing assertions stay separate; the
RH-equivalent condition in this construction is A-TP.
\end{remark}

\section{Refinement: The Condensed-Hilbert Bulk}

\subsection{Condensed-Hilbert setup}
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:condensed-setup}

\begin{definition}[condensed Hilbert pro-system]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:def:condensed-pro-system}
Specify finite-level boundary Hilbert spaces
$\mathcal H^{(\infty)}_{G_N,\alpha_N}$ and coherent bounded linear
transition maps, including their normalization.
Their associated condensed complex vector spaces form an inverse
diagram, denoted $\mathcal H^{(\infty)}_\bullet$.
Here $\mathrm{Cond}_{\mathrm{Hilb}}(\mathbb C)$ denotes the chosen
category of condensed realizations of complex Hilbert spaces with
bounded linear maps. A limit in condensed vector spaces need not be
represented by a Hilbert space. Such representability, its norm and
its compatibility with the native pairing are the additional descent
requirements below. A scalar normalization of partition values does
not by itself define transition maps on these state spaces.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[target archimedean Hilbert realization]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:def:target-Hilbert}
Specify a primary trace space \(E_\infty\), its Hermitian positive
semidefinite form \(q_\infty\), and its arithmetic operations.
The target \(H_{\mathrm{prim}}\) is the completion of its quotient
by the nullspace, with its quotient map \(I_{\mathrm{prim}}\).
Its condensed realization is the condensed object associated with
this Hilbert topology. Specifying the trace degree, the form and
its native realization is part of the arithmetic construction in
\Cref{v4-arith:pres57:native-pairing}.
An identification with a Heisenberg representation additionally
requires the operators, domains, central action and pairing map of
\Cref{v4-arith:pres90:primary-virasoro}.
\end{definition}
\subsection{The Condensed Hilbert--Trace Package}
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:named-obstruction}

 H_B\xrightarrow{\ T\ } H_{\mathrm{prim}},\qquad

 \label{v4-arith:w10-a:eq:condensed-descent}

The arithmetic boundary operations descend and agree on specified

\section{Wilson-Observable Compatibility}
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:wilson-compat}

\begin{theorem}[Wilson-line compatibility]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:thm:wilson-line-compat}
\ClaimStatusProvedHereConditional \emph{(on
\Cref{v4-arith:w10-a:conj:condensed-descent})}. Under the
condensed-Hilbert descent, the Wilson-line observable at a prime
knot \(K_{p}\) in the bulk \(\mathrm{ACS}^{\mathrm{qu}}_{k^{\mathrm{Ar}}}\),
evaluated on the limit state space
\(H_B\xrightarrow{T}H_{\mathrm{prim}}\),
is exactly the Frobenius trace \(\mathrm{tr}\,\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_{p})\)
of \Cref{v4-arith:w9-r1:thm:main}(2), as a Wilson clause of the
descent.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{v4-arith:w10-a:thm:descent-equivalent} supplies the Wilson
clause of the chosen descended ACS model. Axiom (W) in that model is
the Wilson-line Frobenius identity. Here the finite-place equality is expressly part of the descent
conjecture, including its label and trace comparison. A finite-state
sum alone would not give that identification.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Wilson-surface compatibility]
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:thm:wilson-surface-compat}
Assume the condensed-Hilbert descent of
\Cref{v4-arith:w10-a:conj:condensed-descent}, and write
\(H_B=\mathcal H^{(\infty)}_\infty\).
Suppose the arithmetic boundary quantization of
\Cref{v4-arith:pres90:quantization} constructs a closed normal
\(\Theta_B\) and its surface observable
\(F_\infty^{\mathrm{WS}}=e^{-\Theta_B}\).
Suppose a unitary \(V_\Sigma:H_B\to H_c\) satisfies
\[
 V_\Sigma\operatorname{Dom}\Theta_B=\operatorname{Dom}\Theta_c,
 \qquad V_\Sigma\Theta_B=\Theta_cV_\Sigma.
\]
Then the exponential is bounded and
\[
 F_\infty^{\mathrm{WS}}=V_\Sigma^{-1}F_\infty V_\Sigma,
 \qquad |F_\infty^{\mathrm{WS}}|=e^{-1/2}1_{H_B}.
\]
The quantization must contain this Tate normalization: a unitary
curvature phase alone has absolute value one.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The domain and operator equalities identify the normal spectral
measures. The function \(e^{-s}\) is bounded on
\(1/2+i\mathbb R\). Functional calculus proves the conjugacy, and
\(F_\infty=e^{-1/2}U_1\) proves the absolute-value formula.
This transports an already constructed quantum observable.
Defining a transported operator on an abstract Hilbert space alone
does not identify it with the arithmetic quantization.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[native pairing and the full Weil form]
\label{v4-arith:consumer94:native-Weil}
Let \(I_B:E_\infty\to H_B\) realize the native positive pairing
\(q_\infty\). Suppose a complex-linear map
\(R:\mathscr A=C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)\to E_\infty\) satisfies
\[
 q_\infty(Rf,Rh)=P(f*h^*)-W_\infty(f*h^*)-W_{\mathrm{fin}}(f*h^*)
 \qquad(f,h\in\mathscr A),
\]
with \(h^*(t)=\overline{h(-t)}\) and the Fourier and measure
conventions of \Cref{v4-arith:w5-a:thm:weil-explicit}.
Then \(J=I_BR\) realizes the full Weil form on \(H_B\), and RH follows.
If the identity is specified only on a complex subspace
\(E\subset\mathscr A\), it proves positivity only on \(E\).
It extends to the full criterion if \(E\) is dense in the compact
test topology. A mere density of its span in a different Hilbert
norm does not give that implication.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The native isometry and the signed explicit formula give
\(\langle Jf,Jh\rangle=W(f*h^*)\). Taking \(h=f\) gives
nonnegativity, and Weil's criterion implies RH. For a restricted
subspace, continuity of convolution, involution and \(W\) in the
compact test topology permits passage from approximating tests to
all compact tests. No such limit follows from an unspecified norm.
\end{proof}

This comparison asks for an arithmetic map \(R\), including all
polar, gamma and prime terms. The positivity of the target Hilbert
space and the continuous surface comparison do not construct \(R\).
The Gaussian extension in \Cref{v4-arith:sc:gaussian-extension}
provides another valid test topology only when the complete pairing
identity is transported there; a gamma sign alone does not do so.

\section{Comparison of the Five Presentations}
\label{v4-arith:w10-a:ah:synthesis}

\Cref{v4-arith:w10-a:thm:common-residual} gives a comparison theorem
for boundary Hilbert realizations carrying the same native pairing.
The inverse-limit construction must supply its transition maps,
renormalization and represented limit. The factorization construction
must supply boundary restriction and gluing. The categorical
constructions must supply their trace-to-boundary maps, and the
six-functor construction its arithmetic space and trace formula.

When these constructions realize a common \((E_\infty,q_\infty)\),
the theorem constructs their Hilbert unitary. Agreement of generator
cores gives operator transport. Their existence and arithmetic
identification are additional to this implication. In particular,
a passage from vector spaces to pro-objects and then to a condensed
Hilbert space does not specify the native pairing or the quantum
surface observable.
