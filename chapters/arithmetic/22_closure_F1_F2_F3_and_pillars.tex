\chapter{Closure of F1--F3 and Resolution of the Three Pillars}
\label{v4-arith:closure}

The three conjectures F1, F2, F3 and the three pillars P1, P2, P3 record what the arithmetic branch requires in order to complete the Riemann Hypothesis reformulation of Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:rh-reformulation}. F1 asserts global categorical arithmetic Langlands for \(GL_{2}/\mathbb{Q}\); F2 asserts that the global Hecke category is \(E_{2}\)-monoidal; F3 identifies its Hochschild trace with \(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}\). P1 is the essential equivalence between \(\kappa^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathsf{G}}+(\kappa^{!})^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathsf{G}}=0\) and the Tate functional equation for \(\Lambda_{\zeta}\), equivalently for the pole-cleared \(\xi_{\mathbb R}\); P2 is the Tate functional equation and explicit formula; P3 is unitarity. Each conjecture and each pillar is subjected to the seven-obstruction criterion below and either reduced to named components or proved conditionally. The residual obstructions at the end of this chapter are precisely stated, independently testable claims.

\section{Conditions for an Arithmetic Comparison}
\label{v4-arith:cl:criterion}

A conjecture \(C\) is a structured reduction of \(C_{0}\) if
\(C_{0}\Leftrightarrow C\) and \(C\) is a conjunction of named inputs,
each of which is either a theorem in the literature, a conditional
with identified literature gap, or a conjecture with a precise
statement. The seven obstructions are the structural tests for an
equivalence claim. For each condition they are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Specified comparison.} State the carriers and the map
relating the two constructions.
\item \emph{Sign / convention check.} Do chain-level signs and
\(q\)-conventions match across cited sources?
\item \emph{Ambient category check.} Do LHS and RHS live in
compatible \(\infty\)-categories?
\item \emph{Missing hypothesis.} Is a hidden hypothesis required
(for instance sph-finiteness, perfectoid compatibility, \(\mu=0\))?
\item \emph{False functoriality.} Does the claimed equivalence
preserve all relevant structure?
\item \emph{Unproved equivalence.} Is any internal step itself a
currently-open conjecture?
\item \emph{Numerical normalization.} Specify each constant and
the calculation identifying its normalization on the two sides.
\end{enumerate}
Each obstruction produces either a valid implication, a correction (reformulation
that survives), or a named residual obstruction. The result is a
structured reduction, not necessarily a full closure.

\section{F1: Structural Closure}
\label{v4-arith:cl:F1-closure}

\subsection{Statement}

F1 (Conjecture~\ref{v4-arith:conj:F1-global-AG}) asserts
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:cl:eq:F1-statement}
\mathrm{D}([GL_{2}])^{\mathrm{sph\text{-}fin}}\;\simeq\;\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}\bigl(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{Sel}}_{\bar\rho,\det,\mathcal{L}}\bigr),
\end{equation}
the global categorical arithmetic Langlands equivalence for
\(GL_{2}/\mathbb{Q}\), with \(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}\)
corresponding to the structure sheaf on the spectral side.

\subsection{Mathematical Inputs and Comparison Conditions}

\paragraph{Local geometric inputs.}
Fargues--Scholze arXiv:2102.13459 supplies local geometrisation on the
Fargues--Fontaine curve; Arinkin--Gaitsgory arXiv:1201.6343 supplies
the nilpotent-singular-support setting. The Emerton--Gee stack
arXiv:1908.07185 is a different deformation carrier. Applying these
constructions together requires the local categorical comparison
specified in (F1.a).

\paragraph{Obstruction 2 (conventions).} Singular-support conventions
in Arinkin--Gaitsgory follow the de~Rham shift; Fargues--Scholze
follows the pro-\'etale shift on diamonds. The comparison is
done via the Bhatt--Lurie prismatic-Nygaard convention of
arXiv:2201.06120, which bridges both. \emph{The comparison is mediated by prismatic Nygaard.}

\paragraph{Obstruction 3 (ambient categories).} LHS is a stable
presentable \(\infty\)-category of D-modules on an adelic quotient;
RHS is an \(\infty\)-category of ind-coherent sheaves with nilpotent
singular support on a derived formal algebraic stack. Via
Gaitsgory--Rozenblyum's AMS DAG monographs the categories sit
in the same ambient \((\infty,2)\)-category of correspondences.

\paragraph{Obstruction 4 (missing hypothesis).} The sph-finite cut on the
LHS is critical: without it the full automorphic category is too
large (contains continuous spectrum and Eisenstein contributions
requiring explicit parametrisation). Fargues--Scholze supply local
geometrisation and spectral action; they do not identify this
sph-finite automorphic category with
\(\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}\) on the Emerton--Gee stack.
\textbf{Residual: local categorical comparison.}

\paragraph{Obstruction 5 (false functoriality).} Functoriality under
Hecke operators \(T_{p}\) at every prime is the key test. At each
\(p\), Fargues--Scholze provide Hecke correspondences and spectral
action. Functoriality of a categorical comparison with the
Emerton--Gee side is a separate local input. Globally,
Hecke-functoriality is required place-by-place and across places
(composition of Hecke operators at distinct primes). The cross-prime
compatibility is the global gluing obstruction. \textbf{Residual:
local comparison plus global gluing.}

\paragraph{Obstruction 6 (unproved equivalence).} The local categorical
comparison
\(\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{sph\text{-}fin}}([GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})])
\simeq
\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{EG},p}_{GL_{2}})\)
and the global restricted tensor product matching the global
automorphic category are not in the literature as of 2026. Partial
change: Caraiani--Scholze
arXiv:1511.02418 for signature \((1,n-1)\) unitary Shimura varieties;
Ben-Zvi--Chen--Helm--Nadler arXiv:2010.02321 for coherent Springer
theory. Neither closes \(GL_{2}/\mathbb{Q}\) in full generality.
\textbf{Residual: local comparison and adelic-descent gluing.}

\paragraph{Obstruction 7 (numerical comparison).} No numerical comparison
currently backs F1; no synchronisation concern arises.

\subsection{Structured Reduction}

\begin{theorem}[F1 reduction]
\label{v4-arith:thm:F1-reduction}
F1 is equivalent to the conjunction of:
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbf{(F1.a)}] \emph{Local categorical comparison at every prime.}
For each prime \(p\), the expected local categorical Langlands equivalence
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{sph\text{-}fin}}([GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})])\;\simeq\;\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}\bigl(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{EG},p}_{GL_{2}}\bigr)
\end{equation}
holds, with local Hecke-functoriality.
\textbf{Form: conditional theorem}. Fargues--Scholze arXiv:2102.13459
supplies the local geometrisation, spectral action, and parameter
spaces. Emerton--Gee arXiv:1908.07185 supplies the
\((\varphi,\Gamma)\)-module stack. The displayed equivalence is not a
theorem of either source; Dotto--Emerton--Gee arXiv:2603.26887 gives
only a fully faithful fixed-central-character functor for
\(GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\), \(p\geq 5\).
\item[\textbf{(F1.b)}] \emph{Adelic descent of the automorphic side.}
The global automorphic category satisfies
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{D}([GL_{2}])^{\mathrm{sph\text{-}fin}}\;\simeq\;\bigotimes_{v\in|\overline{C}|}^{\prime}\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{sph\text{-}fin}}([GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{v})])
\end{equation}
as a restricted tensor product over places, with spherical vectors
at almost all unramified places.
\textbf{Form: conjectural, reduces to Tate--Weil restricted
tensor product for automorphic representations} (Tate 1950,
Weil 1967, Jacquet--Langlands 1970 for the representation-level
statement; the categorical lift is conjectural, cf.~Gan--Savin 2022
for partial change).
\item[\textbf{(F1.c)}] \emph{Spectral-side global assembly.}
The spectral side is the Selmer-derived global deformation stack
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{Sel}}_{\bar\rho,\det,\mathcal{L}}
\;:=\;
\mathrm{Map}_{\det,\bar\rho}(G_{\mathbb{Q},S},GL_{2})
\times^{\mathbf{R}}_{\prod_{v\in S}\mathrm{Map}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_{v}},GL_{2})}
\prod_{v\in S}\mathcal{X}^{\mathcal{L}_{v}}_{v},
\end{equation}
with \(\mathcal{X}^{\mathcal{L}_{v}}_{v}\) the prescribed local
condition inside the full Weil/\((\varphi,\Gamma)\) local parameter
stack. This derived fibre product imposes the global Galois
relation and Selmer conditions; it is not a product or colimit of
local Emerton--Gee stacks.
\textbf{Form: definitional here; derived and Noetherian
properties are conditional/conditional on the local deformation
conditions and the chosen Selmer problem.}
\item[\textbf{(F1.d)}] \emph{Local-global compatibility under the
equivalence.} For each finite set \(S\) of primes, the diagram
\begin{equation}
\begin{tikzcd}
\bigotimes_{p\in S}\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{sph\text{-}fin}}([GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})]) \ar[r, "\simeq"] \ar[d] & \mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}\bigl(\prod_{p\in S}\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{EG},p}_{GL_{2}}\bigr) \ar[d] \\
\bigotimes_{p\in S'}\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{sph\text{-}fin}}([GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})]) \ar[r, "\simeq"] & \mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}\bigl(\prod_{p\in S'}\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{EG},p}_{GL_{2}}\bigr)
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation}
commutes for \(S\subseteq S'\), where the vertical arrows are
restriction and extension-by-zero on the automorphic side, pull-back
and push-forward on the spectral side.
\textbf{Form: conjectural.}
\end{itemize}
Under this reduction, F1 closes given (F1.a) + (F1.b) + (F1.c) +
(F1.d); (F1.c) supplies the correct spectral target but does not
itself prove the categorical equivalence. The residual obstructions
are local comparison, adelic descent, derived Selmer Noetherianity,
and local-global compatibility.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Forward direction: assume F1. Restricting to local automorphic
categories at each prime gives (F1.a). The
global automorphic decomposition on the LHS (F1.b) follows from
the tensor structure of the categorical equivalence applied to
the automorphic side. The spectral decomposition (F1.c) is the
explicit description of the Emerton--Gee stack. Compatibility
(F1.d) follows from functoriality of the equivalence under
inclusion of prime sets.

Reverse direction: assume (F1.a)+(F1.b)+(F1.c)+(F1.d). By (F1.c),
the spectral target is the derived Selmer fibre product imposing
the fixed residual representation, determinant, local conditions,
and global Galois relation. The expected local comparison (F1.a)
identifies each local automorphic category with the corresponding
local spectral factor, while (F1.d) asserts that these local
comparisons respect the derived Selmer pullback rather than a
naive product of local stacks. By (F1.b), the restricted tensor
product of local automorphic categories is the global automorphic
category. Composing these conditional identifications gives
(\ref{v4-arith:cl:eq:F1-statement}).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[open content after reduction]
\label{v4-arith:cl:rem:F1-form}
The four items of Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:thm:F1-reduction} isolate the open
content of F1: local categorical comparison (F1.a), adelic categorical
descent (F1.b), the Selmer-derived spectral target (F1.c, definitional
here with derived-Noetherian properties conditional), and
local-global diagram commutativity (F1.d). The Selmer spectral target
is correctly formulated; the categorical equivalence depends on
conditional and conjectural inputs in (F1.a) and (F1.b).
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[F1 consequence of local comparison plus adelic descent]
\label{v4-arith:cl:cor:F1-single-lemma}
\emph{Comparison hypothesis.} F1 holds if the following two inputs are
true:
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbf{(F1.a)}] \emph{Local categorical comparison at every
prime.} The Fargues--Scholze local geometrization and the
Emerton--Gee stack are compared by a categorical equivalence of the
form required in Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:thm:F1-reduction}.
\item[\textbf{(L\(_{\mathrm{ACD}}\))}] \emph{Adelic categorical descent.}
Write
\(\mathcal C_v=\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{sph\text{-}fin}}([GL_2(\mathbb Q_v)])\).
Supply a coherent diagram \(S\mapsto\bigotimes_{v\in S}\mathcal C_v\)
over finite sets of places containing a fixed finite exceptional set.
Its transition functors insert specified maps from the categorical
tensor unit at the added places. These maps and all composition
coherences are part of the datum.

Require an equivalence from this diagram's categorical colimit
to the global automorphic category. It must preserve the Hecke
operations and the supplied local categorical comparisons.
\end{itemize}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\(\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{ACD}}\) combines (F1.b) and (F1.d) into a single
statement; (F1.c) supplies the correct Selmer-derived spectral
target and (F1.a) is a named local comparison input. Applying
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:thm:F1-reduction} reduces F1
to (F1.a) plus \(\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{ACD}}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[substantial estimate]
\label{v4-arith:cl:rem:F1-substantial}
Proving \(\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{ACD}}\) is of the same order as the
function-field geometric Langlands work of
Gaitsgory--Rozenblyum--Raskin--Lin (collectively 2019--2025),
adapted to the number-field setting. The non-formal theorem condition of
App.~C still applies.
\end{remark}

\section{F2: Structural Closure}
\label{v4-arith:cl:F2-closure}

\subsection{Statement}

F2 (Conjecture~\ref{v4-arith:conj:F2-perfectoid-E2}) asserts that each local
Iwahori Hecke category is \(E_{2}\)-monoidal via the perfectoid BD
Grassmannian, and the restricted tensor product is globally
\(E_{2}\).

\subsection{Mathematical Inputs and Comparison Conditions}

\paragraph{Local geometric inputs.} The equal-characteristic setting
uses Mirkovi\'c--Vilonen arXiv:math/0401222 and Zhu
arXiv:1603.05593; the mixed-characteristic setting uses
Bhatt--Scholze arXiv:1507.06490 and Fargues--Scholze arXiv:2102.13459.
The required local \(E_2\)-structure is the hypothesis specified below.

\paragraph{Local geometric comparison.} A complex formal-disk
model and a mixed-characteristic perfectoid chart have different
coefficient and geometric data. Their comparison must specify
functors preserving convolution and the required collision maps.
Scholze's tilting equivalence compares perfectoid algebras over
a perfectoid field with algebras over its tilt
(arXiv:1111.4914, Definition~1.6 and Theorem~1.7).
It does not by itself identify the complex formal-disk model
with the intended arithmetic Hecke category.

\paragraph{Ambient categories.} Write
\(\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm L}_{\mathrm{st}}\) for stable presentable
\(\infty\)-categories and colimit-preserving functors.
Its tensor product represents separately colimit-preserving
functors. Its unit is the category \(\mathrm{Sp}\) of spectra,
whose unit object is the sphere \(\mathbb S\).
The formal construction uses
\(\mathrm{Alg}_{E_2}(\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm L}_{\mathrm{st}})\).

Filtered colimits in its underlying categorical tensor category
are preserved by tensoring in each variable. Thus HA~3.2.3.1
constructs the filtered algebra colimit on that underlying
colimit. No presentability assertion about
\(\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm L}_{\mathrm{st}}\) itself is needed.

\paragraph{Pointing maps.} At each place that may be inserted,
one needs an actual morphism
\(\eta_v:\mathrm{Sp}\to\mathcal C_v\) of \(E_2\)-algebras.
Its value on the sphere is the monoidal unit. Identifying this
value with a spherical object is additional local data.
Existence of an invariant vector or a spherical subcategory
alone does not give that identification and its operadic maps.

\paragraph{Cross-place coherence.} The finite-stage transition
functors tensor identities with the supplied maps \(\eta_v\).
Tensor associativity gives their composition homotopies and
all higher compatibilities. The coherent diagram is therefore
constructed from the pointings. It is not inferred from
commutation of numerical Hecke eigenvalues.

\paragraph{Formal assembly and geometric comparison.}
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:tate:thm:F2c-main} proves the restricted-tensor
theorem under these hypotheses. The remaining comparison with
an independently defined global Hecke category requires an
actual functor and a proof that it is an equivalence.
The filtered-colimit theorem supplies neither that functor nor
the local geometric or categorical inputs.

\paragraph{Scope.} F2 requires the categorical structures and their
compatibility; no numerical equality supplies those maps.

\subsection{Structured Reduction}

\begin{theorem}[F2 assembly with supplied local data]
\label{v4-arith:thm:F2-reduction}
Consider the following local data and formal construction:
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbf{(F2.a)}] \emph{Local geometric input.} At each prime,
supply a local geometric model for the intended
\(\mathrm{Gr}^{\mathrm{BD}}_{GL_2,\mathrm{Spa}\,\mathbb Z_p}\),
with the required perfectoid realization and \(E_2\)-factorization properties.
The geometric frameworks include Fargues--Scholze
arXiv:2102.13459, Zhu arXiv:1603.05593 and
Bhatt--Scholze arXiv:1507.06490. The properties of the chosen
model are hypotheses here, not consequences of categorical
filtered colimits.
\item[\textbf{(F2.b)}] \emph{Local categorical input.} Supply the
local Iwahori categories
\(\mathrm{Hk}^{\mathrm{Iw}}_{GL_2,v}\), their colimit-compatible
\(E_2\)-monoidal structures and the cofinite pointing maps below.
This includes the archimedean category. A comparison with an
equal-characteristic model, such as the setting of Bezrukavnikov
arXiv:1209.0403, requires its own coefficient and categorical
functors. Neither a local geometric space nor a spherical vector
alone supplies these monoidal categories and maps.
\item[\textbf{(F2.c)}] \emph{Formal restricted-tensor construction.}
Let \(\{\mathcal C_v\}_{v\in V}\) satisfy
Setup~\ref{v4-arith:tate:setup:data} with \(\mathcal O=E_2\).
In particular, actual
\(E_2\)-algebra maps \(\eta_v:\mathrm{Sp}\to\mathcal C_v\)
are supplied on a cofinite subset \(V^{\mathrm{sph}}\).
Write \(u_v=\eta_v(\mathbb S)\).

Define
\begin{equation}
 \bigotimes_v'(\mathcal C_v,u_v)
 :=\operatorname*{colim}_{\substack{S\subseteq V\text{ finite}\\V\setminus V^{\mathrm{sph}}\subseteq S}}
       \bigotimes_{v\in S}\mathcal C_v,
\end{equation}
with transitions given by these pointings. This colimit is an
\(E_2\)-algebra, by
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:tate:thm:F2c-main}. Its uniqueness is
relative to the complete underlying colimit cocone.
\end{itemize}
Under the supplied local categorical and pointing hypotheses,
(F2.c) constructs the restricted-tensor \(E_2\)-category.
If a specified equivalence identifies its underlying category
with a global Hecke category, transport gives that category an
\(E_2\)-structure. Existence of this comparison is a separate
arithmetic hypothesis. No converse reconstructing the local
data from an arbitrary global category is asserted.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The supplied pointings form a diagram of finite tensor products
in \(\mathrm{Alg}_{E_2}(\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm L}_{\mathrm{st}})\).
Its indexing poset is filtered. HA~3.2.3.1 gives the stated
algebra colimit, as proved in
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:tate:thm:F2c-main}.
Transport along the specified equivalence gives the final
assertion. The geometric input (F2.a), the local enhancement
(F2.b) and that equivalence are not constructed by this proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[formal assembly and the remaining inputs]
\label{v4-arith:cl:rem:F2-open-lemma}
The formal theorem applies to supplied pointed families in any
set of places. Its proof uses HA~3.2.3.1 and separate tensor
preservation of filtered colimits. The local arithmetic
\(E_2\)-enhancements, their pointings and an independent global
comparison remain separate constructions. Singleton maps obey
(M2) of Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:tate:thm:F2c-main} and need not
be fully faithful.
\end{remark}

\section{F3: Conditional Closure}
\label{v4-arith:cl:F3-closure}

\subsection{Statement}

F3 (Conjecture~\ref{v4-arith:conj:F3-arith-BZN}) asserts
\(\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}(\mathrm{Hk}^{\mathrm{Iw}}_{GL_{2},\mathrm{glob}})\simeq\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}\)
as \(E_{1}\)-algebras.

\subsection{Mathematical Inputs and Comparison Conditions}

\paragraph{Carriers and conventions.} The comparison concerns
\(E_1\)-algebras in presentable \(\infty\)-categories on the sph-finite
domain of F1. Ben-Zvi--Nadler arXiv:0904.1247, Bezrukavnikov,
and the mixed-characteristic geometry of Fargues--Scholze give the
respective settings. Their arithmetic comparison must preserve these
carriers and the stated prismatic Nygaard conventions.

\paragraph{Obstruction 5 (false functoriality).} The Hochschild trace is
functorial on monoidal \(\infty\)-categories. The identification
with \(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}\) requires that the global
automorphic side (F1 + F2) and the Hochschild trace be compatibly
identified via Ben-Zvi--Nadler's categorical-character--Hecke--trace
triangle (Ben-Zvi--Nadler arXiv:0904.1247 \S1). This compatibility is
the residual content. \textbf{Residual: arithmetic Ben-Zvi--Nadler
compatibility.}

\paragraph{Obstruction 6.} Ben-Zvi--Nadler over \(\mathbb{C}\) is a theorem.
The arithmetic extension requires replacing the complex inertia
circle \(S^{1}=B\mathbb{Z}\) by an arithmetic target. The
inertia-only candidate \(B\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}=B\prod_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\)
records only the procyclic shadow and loses Frobenius, Weil-group,
and \((\varphi,\Gamma)\) data. \textbf{Residual: arithmetic
Ben-Zvi--Nadler extension for the full Weil/\((\varphi,\Gamma)\)
target.}

\paragraph{Scope.} The conclusion requires the stated categorical comparison.

\subsection{Conditional Closure}

\begin{theorem}[F3 conditional closure]
\label{v4-arith:thm:F3-reduction}
\emph{Conditional statement.} Assume (F1.a)--(F1.d) and
(F2.a)--(F2.c). Then F3 is equivalent to the single statement:
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbf{(F3.BZN\(^{\mathrm{Ar}}\))}] \emph{Arithmetic
Ben-Zvi--Nadler identification.} There is an \(E_{1}\)-algebra
equivalence
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}\bigl(\mathrm{Hk}^{\mathrm{Iw}}_{GL_{2},\mathrm{glob}}\bigr)
\;\simeq\;
\mathcal{O}\bigl(\mathrm{LocSys}^{W,\varphi\Gamma}_{GL_{2}}\bigr)^{\mathrm{flat}}
\end{equation}
arithmetising Ben-Zvi--Nadler arXiv:0904.1247, where
\(\mathrm{LocSys}^{W,\varphi\Gamma}_{GL_{2}}\) is the full
Weil/\((\varphi,\Gamma)\) arithmetic local-system target, with flat
structure sheaf pulled back from the global Emerton--Gee morphism.
The map through \(\mathrm{LocSys}_{GL_{2}}^{\mathrm{Ar}}(B\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})\)
is only the procyclic inertial shadow.
\textbf{Form: conjectural.}
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Given F1 + F2, the Hochschild trace of
\(\mathrm{Hk}^{\mathrm{Iw}}_{GL_{2},\mathrm{glob}}\) is an
\(E_{1}\)-algebra (via Dunn additivity applied to the \(E_{2}\)-structure
from F2). By F1, the global Hecke category matches
\(\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{Sel}}_{\bar\rho,\det,\mathcal{L}})\).
The Hochschild trace of
\(\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}(\mathcal{X})\) for a derived stack
\(\mathcal{X}\) is classically \(\mathcal{O}(L\mathcal{X})\) where
\(L\mathcal{X}=\mathrm{Map}(S^{1},\mathcal{X})\) is the free loop
space (Ben-Zvi--Nadler Thm~1.1). Arithmetically,
\(B\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\) records the procyclic inertial shadow, while
Frobenius and the Weil quotient are carried by the strengthened
Weil/\((\varphi,\Gamma)\) target
\(\mathrm{LocSys}^{W,\varphi\Gamma}_{GL_{2}}\).

The comparison with \(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}\) is controlled by
the six conditional windows of
Conjecture~\ref{v4-arith:thm:six-window-placement}: \(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}\)
is defined as the Hochschild trace class, and the other windows are
conditional characterisations conditional on F1--F3. Under this definition,
F3 reduces to (F3.BZN\(^{\mathrm{Ar}}\)), the arithmetic extension of
Ben-Zvi--Nadler Thm~1.1 from the complex setting to the full
Weil/\((\varphi,\Gamma)\) target.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[arithmetic inertia circle]
\label{v4-arith:cl:rem:F3-inertia}
The arithmetic inertia circle \(B\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\) is the
classifying stack of the profinite abelian group
\(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}=\prod_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\). Under the
Mazur--Kapranov--Reznikov dictionary, it is the arithmetic
avatar of the inertia circle of a knot complement; its geometric
realisation sits inside \(\overline{\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbb{Z}}\)
via the primes-as-knots correspondence (Morishita
arXiv:0904.3399).
\end{remark}

\section{P1: \texorpdfstring{\(\mathsf{G}\)}{G}-Row Complementarity and the Functional Equation}
\label{v4-arith:cl:P1-resolution}

\subsection{The Arithmetic Comparison}

Question~\ref{v4-arith:thm:G-row-complementarity} asks for arithmetic
coefficient objects and a comparison between central complementarity
and Mellin reflection. With the completed function
\(\Lambda_\zeta(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\), its intended form is
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:cl:eq:G-row}
 \kappa^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathsf G}
 +(\kappa^!)^{\mathrm{Ar}}_{\mathsf G}=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Lambda_\zeta(s)=\Lambda_\zeta(1-s).
\end{equation}
The right-hand identity is classical. The left-hand parameters require
arithmetic objects and a definition of their central levels. Relating
the two sides requires an evaluation that detects the central defect.
A scalar character alone does not provide such detection.

\subsection{The Character and Its Missing Identifications}

The elementary positive model has one bosonic occupation number
\(n_p\geq0\) at each prime, with only finitely many nonzero entries.
Define its energy by
\[
 H(n)=\sum_p n_p\log p.
\]
Unique factorization identifies the tuple \((n_p)_p\) with the integer
\(m=\prod_p p^{n_p}\), and its energy with \(\log m\).
The partition function is therefore
\begin{equation}
 Z(\beta)=\sum_{m\geq1}m^{-\beta}
         =\prod_p(1-p^{-\beta})^{-1}=\zeta(\beta),
 \qquad \beta>1.
\end{equation}
The same absolutely convergent series gives the complex Mellin
character for \(\operatorname{Re}s>1\), as proved in
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:thm:heisenberg-bar-zeta}.

A full oscillator Fock space has additional modes. If the mode at
\((p,r)\) has energy \(r\log p\), its partition function is
\[
 \prod_p\prod_{r\geq1}(1-p^{-r\beta})^{-1}
 =\prod_{r\geq1}\zeta(r\beta)\qquad(\beta>1).
\]
Absolute convergence follows by bounding the logarithm by a constant
multiple of \(\sum_p p^{-\beta}\).
Selecting the single occupation mode and identifying it with a primary
quotient of an arithmetic bar are separate operations. The latter
requires a map preserving the energy grading, local conditions,
and coefficient completion.

The Bost--Connes system also has the zeta partition function in its
convergent temperature range. Identifying an arithmetic Koszul dual
with that system is the additional construction of
Question~\ref{v4-arith:cor:koszul-dual-BC}.
Its algebra, time evolution, and state comparison are not determined
by equality of partition functions. Meromorphic continuation of the
scalar \(\zeta\) outside this range is not a convergent KMS trace.

A possible bar--cobar comparison would have to identify its pairing
with the completed Mellin expression
\[
 \Lambda_\zeta(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s).
\]
The gamma factor, polar contributions, and reflection then belong to
the comparison. The scalar residue
\(2\operatorname{Res}_{s=1}\zeta(s)=2\) remains valid, but its
interpretation as a determinant rank needs the determinant-line
hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:thm:c-as-residue}.

\subsection{A Criterion That Detects Complementarity}

Let \(V\) be a complex vector space with an involution \(J\),
and let \(\mathscr M(\mathbb C)\) denote meromorphic functions on
\(\mathbb C\). Fix a linear evaluation
\(E:V\to\mathscr M(\mathbb C)\), a vector \(v\), and a vector
\(w\) on which \(E\) is nonzero. Let \(\kappa,\kappa^!\in\mathbb C\).
The following criterion separates the construction of arithmetic
objects from a precise implication once their comparison data exist.

\begin{theorem}[a conditional complementarity criterion]
\label{v4-arith:thm:P1-resolution}
Suppose
\begin{equation}
 v-Jv=(\kappa+\kappa^!)w,
 \qquad
 E(v)(s)=\Lambda_\zeta(s),
 \qquad
 E(Jv)(s)=\Lambda_\zeta(1-s).
\end{equation}
Then \(\kappa+\kappa^!=0\) if and only if
\(\Lambda_\zeta(s)=\Lambda_\zeta(1-s)\).
The same equivalence holds with
\(\xi(s)=\tfrac12s(s-1)\Lambda_\zeta(s)\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Applying \(E\) to the first identity gives
\[
 \Lambda_\zeta(s)-\Lambda_\zeta(1-s)
   =(\kappa+\kappa^!)E(w)(s).
\]
Since \(E(w)\) is not the zero meromorphic function, this identity
vanishes exactly when \(\kappa+\kappa^!=0\). The polynomial
\(s(s-1)/2\) is nonzero and invariant under reflection.
Multiplying by it preserves equality of meromorphic functions.
\end{proof}

For an arithmetic application, \(V,J,v,w,E\) and the two levels
must be constructed from the proposed dual objects. In particular,
the displayed defect equation and the nonvanishing of \(E(w)\)
are substantive hypotheses. They are not consequences of a
co--contra equivalence considered without an evaluation.

The nonvanishing condition cannot be dropped. For
\(V=\mathbb C^2\), let \(J(x,y)=(y,x)\) and \(E(x,y)=x+y\).
The evaluations of \((1,0)\) and \(J(1,0)\) agree, while their
difference \((1,-1)\) is nonzero. Thus equality after evaluation
need not imply invariance of the object or cancellation of a central
parameter. The criterion asks only for detection of the specified
one-dimensional defect, not injectivity on all of \(V\).

\begin{remark}[arithmetic hypotheses]
\label{v4-arith:cl:rem:P1-conditional}
The arithmetic duality question includes its actual coefficient
objects, continuous duals, completions, and unit and counit maps.
The character comparison, Bost--Connes comparison, and detection of
the central defect are further requirements.
Question~\ref{v4-arith:thm:arithmetic-positselski-preview} keeps
these categories and their comparison separate from the
module-theoretic \(\mu\)-invariant.

An Iwasawa vanishing theorem concerns a specified arithmetic module.
Applying it to a Heisenberg construction requires an identification
with that module, including its topology and Galois action.
An unspecified cyclotomic deformation of a Fock space supplies no
such identification. The coalgebras of
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:coefficients:prop:mu} also show why
\(\mu=0\) cannot replace conilpotence.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Mellin Reflection and the Forward Direction}

For \(\theta(t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}e^{-\pi n^2t}\), Poisson
summation gives \(\theta(t)=t^{-1/2}\theta(1/t)\). Splitting its
Mellin integral at one gives the explicit continuation
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda_\zeta(s)=-\frac1s-\frac1{1-s}
 +\frac12\int_1^\infty(\theta(t)-1)
       \bigl(t^{s/2}+t^{(1-s)/2}\bigr)\frac{dt}{t}.
\end{equation}
The integral is entire and the full expression is invariant under
\(s\mapsto1-s\). This is the proof in
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:thm:arithmetic-modular-invariance}.
The polar terms are part of the formula. An unsubtracted theta
integral over all ideles is not a replacement for its convergence
and continuation argument.

\begin{corollary}[forward implication with an equivariant evaluation]
\label{v4-arith:cl:cor:P1-unconditional-forward}
Suppose \(E(Jx)(s)=E(x)(1-s)\) for all \(x\in V\), and
\(v-Jv=(\kappa+\kappa^!)w\). If \(\kappa+\kappa^!=0\), then
\(E(v)(s)=E(v)(1-s)\). If in addition \(E(v)=\Lambda_\zeta\),
this is the completed zeta functional equation.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The defect equation gives \(Jv=v\). Applying the equivariance
identity to \(v\) proves reflection of \(E(v)\), and the specified
identification gives the last assertion.
\end{proof}

This implication needs no co--contra theorem once the displayed
comparison maps have been supplied. Constructing those maps from
arithmetic duality remains the original problem. The converse requires
a detection hypothesis such as that of
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:thm:P1-resolution}.

\section{P2: Tate Functional Equation and Explicit Formula}
\label{v4-arith:cl:P2-resolution}

\subsection{Statement}

P2 asserts that the meromorphic Tate character
\(\Lambda_{\zeta}(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\) satisfies the
Mellin-theta presentation, the functional equation, the explicit
formula, and the residue identity. No global VOA or Zhu flat
connection is used for \(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}\).

\subsection{Mathematical Inputs and Comparison Conditions}

\paragraph{Analytic normalization.} Tate 1950 gives the Mellin-theta
presentation; the Riemann--von Mangoldt formula supplies the classical
zero-counting expression. The completed function is
\(\Lambda_{\zeta}(s)=\Gamma(s/2)\pi^{-s/2}\zeta(s)\), with boundary
poles at \(s=0,1\).

\subsection{Theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Tate functional equation and explicit formula]
\label{v4-arith:thm:P2-resolution}
The meromorphic completed zeta
\(\Lambda_{\zeta}(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\) is the Tate
character of the arithmetic Heisenberg trace and satisfies the
following four identities:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Mellin-theta presentation.} Initially for \(\Re s>1\),
\begin{equation}
\Lambda_{\zeta}(s)\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigl(\theta(t)-1\bigr)\,t^{s/2-1}\,dt,\qquad\theta(t)\;=\;\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\pi n^{2}t}.
\end{equation}
\item \emph{Logarithmic-derivative residue.} The logarithmic
derivative has contour integral
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\Lambda_{\zeta}'(s)/\Lambda_{\zeta}(s)\,ds,
\end{equation}
equal to the divisor count inside the contour, with the Tate boundary
poles included with sign.
\item \emph{Functional-equation invariance.} The \(\mathbb{Z}/2\)-action
\(s\mapsto 1-s\) on the arithmetic modular parameter acts on the
\(\Lambda_{\zeta}\)-line by \(\Lambda_{\zeta}(s)=\Lambda_{\zeta}(1-s)\).
\item \emph{Arithmetic rank residue.} The primary determinant has
rank residue
\(r_{\zeta}=2\cdot\mathrm{Res}_{s=1}\zeta(s)=2\)
(Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:thm:c-as-residue}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(1) For \(\Re s>1\), termwise integration of the positive Gaussian
series (and absolute convergence for complex \(s\)) gives
\(\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-s}\).
The displayed integral therefore equals \(\Lambda_\zeta(s)\) in that
half-plane; elsewhere the identity means meromorphic continuation
(Riemann 1859; Tate 1950, reprinted Cassels--Fr\"ohlich 1967 \S13.4).

(2) The contour integral is the argument-principle form of the
explicit formula of analytic number theory: for any test function \(h\) of
suitable growth,
\begin{equation}
\sum_{\rho}h(\rho)\;=\;h(0)+h(1)-\sum_{n\geq 1}\Lambda(n)\bigl[\widehat{h}(\log n)/\sqrt{n}+\overline{\widehat{h}(\log n)}/\sqrt{n}\bigr]-\text{archimedean},
\end{equation}
Riemann--von Mangoldt explicit formula (Riemann 1859; von Mangoldt
Crelle 114, 1895). No Virasoro zero-mode on
\(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}\) is used.

(3) The Jacobi theta functional equation \(\theta(t)=t^{-1/2}\theta(1/t)\)
plus change of variables \(t\mapsto 1/t\) in (1) gives
\(\Lambda_{\zeta}(s)=\Lambda_{\zeta}(1-s)\);
this is Riemann's 1859 proof of the functional equation. Multiplying
by \(\tfrac12s(s-1)\) gives the same involution
for \(\xi_{\mathbb R}\).

(4) Residue at \(s=1\) of \(\Lambda_{\zeta}(s)\): from \(\Lambda_{\zeta}(s)=\Gamma(s/2)\pi^{-s/2}\zeta(s)\)
and \(\mathrm{Res}_{s=1}\zeta(s)=1\), \(\Gamma(1/2)\pi^{-1/2}=\pi^{1/2}\pi^{-1/2}=1\),
giving \(\mathrm{Res}_{s=1}\Lambda_{\zeta}(s)=1\). Hence \(r_{\zeta}=2\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[P2 as Tate's thesis]
\label{v4-arith:cl:rem:P2-is-Tate}
The mathematical content of P2 is Tate 1950 (Mellin-theta symmetry,
adelic Poisson summation, the \(\Lambda_{\zeta}\) functional equation) plus the
Riemann--von Mangoldt explicit formula. The arithmetic chiral
interpretation: \(\Lambda_{\zeta}(s)\) as the Tate character of
\(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}_{\mathrm{Heis}}\), the functional equation
as Tate self-duality, and
\(\xi_{\mathbb R}(s)=\tfrac12s(s-1)\Lambda_{\zeta}(s)\) as the
pole-cleared determinant for zeros. The number \(r_{\zeta}=2\) remains
the residue. This
is the content of the arithmetic branch; the underlying analytic inputs
are classical. P2 closes unconditionally.
\end{remark}

\section{P3: Unitarity of \(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{prim}}\)}
\label{v4-arith:cl:P3-resolution}

\subsection{Statement}

Fix a complex state realization of the primitive arithmetic carrier.
P3 asks for a positive Hermitian form compatible with its proposed
Koszul duality. A Hochschild trace class alone does
not specify that state space or its automorphic realization.

\subsection{Local Positivity and Pairing Compatibility}

Let \(V\) be the chosen complex state space and \(W\) its proposed
dual coefficient space. A bilinear evaluation
\(e\colon V\otimes W\to\mathbb C\) becomes sesquilinear after
composition with a linear map \(\sigma\colon\overline V\to W\).
Positivity concerns the form \(e(v,\sigma\overline w)\). Neither
bilinearity nor the existence of a duality makes this form positive.

An automorphic comparison supplies an injective linear map
\(\iota\colon V\to\mathscr L\), where \(\mathscr L\) is a specified
automorphic \(L^2\)-space with fixed central character and Haar
normalization. The Petersson form on \(\mathscr L\) is denoted by
\(\langle\ ,\ \rangle_{\mathscr L}\), linear in its first variable.
The comparison must identify the two forms:
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:cl:eq:koszul-Pet-compat}
e(v,\sigma\overline w)
=\langle\iota v,\iota w\rangle_{\mathscr L}.
\end{equation}
In a Bost--Connes realization, \(\sigma\) must also agree with the
specified conjugation under the local comparison maps. These are
the placement and pairing requirements of
Hypothesis~\ref{v4-arith:kms-gns:hyp:arithmetic-pairing}.

\subsection{Theorem}

\begin{theorem}[positivity under an automorphic pairing comparison]
\label{v4-arith:thm:P3-resolution}
Suppose that \(V,W,e,\sigma\) and the injective placement \(\iota\)
satisfy \eqref{v4-arith:cl:eq:koszul-Pet-compat}. Then
\begin{equation}
h(v,w)=e(v,\sigma\overline w)
\end{equation}
is positive-definite and Hermitian. The Hilbert completion of \(V\)
for this form is isometric to \(\overline{\iota(V)}\subset\mathscr L\).
If the supplied evaluation is the arithmetic Koszul pairing, this
is the positive form compatible with that pairing.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The identity gives \(h(v,v)=\|\iota v\|^2\), which vanishes only
when \(v=0\). Sesquilinearity and Hermitian symmetry follow from
the same identity. Since \(\iota\) preserves norms, it extends
uniquely to an isometry of the completion onto the closure of its
image.
\end{proof}

Positive local models are available independently of this comparison.
For the archimedean Fock space
\(\mathcal H^{(\infty)}=\mathbb C[\alpha_{-1},\alpha_{-2},\ldots]|0\rangle\),
declare the occupation monomials orthogonal with
\[
\left\|\prod_{m\geq1}\alpha_{-m}^{r_m}|0\rangle\right\|^2
=\prod_{m\geq1}m^{r_m}r_m!.
\]
Only finitely many \(r_m\) are nonzero, so every displayed norm
is positive. The relation \(\alpha_m=m\partial_{\alpha_{-m}}\)
makes positive and negative modes adjoint for this form.

At each finite place choose a positive inner-product space
\(\mathcal H^{(p)}\) with a unit vector \(|0\rangle_p\).
Its algebraic restricted tensor product has the product form.
Indeed, every finite family of tensors lies in a finite tensor
product of finite-dimensional subspaces. Orthonormal bases of those
subspaces give a positive orthonormal tensor basis. Insertion of
\(|0\rangle_p\) is isometric, so the forms agree under enlargement
of the finite set of places.

The resulting Hilbert completion is
therefore well defined. Pointed \(E_0\)-data alone supply neither
these forms nor an identification with an arithmetic carrier.

\begin{conjecture}[Koszul--Petersson compatibility]
\label{v4-arith:cl:cj:koszul-Petersson-compat}
The primitive arithmetic carrier admits a complex state realization
\(V\), a dual coefficient space \(W\), and an injective automorphic
placement \(\iota\). Its Koszul evaluation and specified conjugation
satisfy \eqref{v4-arith:cl:eq:koszul-Pet-compat}.
The local comparisons preserve the chosen reference vectors,
intertwine the stated arithmetic actions, and extend to the
specified adelic completion as in
Hypothesis~\ref{v4-arith:kms-gns:hyp:arithmetic-pairing}.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[domain of the residual]
\label{v4-arith:cl:rem:P3-residual}
The local Fock and prime Gibbs forms give concrete positive models.
To apply the theorem to the arithmetic Hochschild construction,
one must first construct its state realization, the dual coefficient
map and the automorphic placement. Equality of pairings is then a
statement on those specified domains. It does not follow from
positivity of separately defined local models.
\end{remark}

\section{Closure Boundary}
\label{v4-arith:cl:summary}

\begin{enumerate}
\item F1 passes through \(\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{ACD}}\), but its present
table also contains the local categorical comparison, the global
restricted-tensor identification, and BC-diamond-glue
(Corollary~\ref{v4-arith:cl:cor:F1-single-lemma}).
\item F2.c supplies formal restricted-tensor assembly under actual
local \(E_2\) and pointing hypotheses. The local constructions
and the comparison with a global geometric category remain
additional inputs.
\item F3, conditional on F1 + F2, reduces to arithmetic Ben-Zvi--Nadler
(F3.BZN\(^{\mathrm{Ar}}\)).
\item P1 has a forward implication under the stated equivariant evaluation
(Corollary~\ref{v4-arith:cl:cor:P1-unconditional-forward}) and a conditional
full equivalence (Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:thm:P1-resolution}).
\item P2 closes unconditionally: the Tate functional equation is
framed via Mellin-theta and the explicit formula
(Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:thm:P2-resolution}).
\item P3 closes conditional on a single compatibility verification
(Conjecture~\ref{v4-arith:cl:cj:koszul-Petersson-compat},
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:thm:P3-resolution}).
\end{enumerate}

The residual obstructions are:
\begin{description}
\item[\((\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{ACD}})\)] Adelic categorical descent for
\(\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{sph\text{-}fin}}([GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{v})])\)
restricted over places. It is a non-formal theorem, number-field adaptation
of Gaitsgory--Rozenblyum function-field results.
\item[(F2)] The local arithmetic \(E_2\)-structures, actual
operadic pointings and comparison with the independently defined
global category must be supplied. The formal colimit step F2.c
is Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:tate:thm:F2c-main}.
\item[(F3.BZN\(^{\mathrm{Ar}}\))] Arithmetic Ben-Zvi--Nadler
identification: \(E_{1}\)-algebra equivalence
\(\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}(\mathrm{Hk}^{\mathrm{Iw}}_{GL_{2},\mathrm{glob}})\simeq\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{LocSys}^{W,\varphi\Gamma}_{GL_{2}})^{\mathrm{flat}}\)
(Ben-Zvi--Nadler arXiv:0904.1247 extended to the
Weil/\((\varphi,\Gamma)\) arithmetic target).
It is a non-formal theorem.
\item[(arith.~Positselski)] Construct the integral co--contra
recipients and adjunction. The lifting criterion of
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:thm:arithmetic-positselski} requires complete
unit and counit cofibers and their equivalences after reduction.
The arithmetic character and central-defect maps are additional.
\item[(Koszul--Pet compat)] Identification of arithmetic Koszul
pairing with adelic Petersson pairing
(Conjecture~\ref{v4-arith:cl:cj:koszul-Petersson-compat}). It is a non-formal theorem,
verification.
\end{description}

These five residual conditions cover the full domain of F1, F2, F3,
with two narrower items (\(\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{ACD}}\) and Koszul--Pet compat
each independently stated and substantial) and three items of
comparable domain to the original conjectures. P2 closes unconditionally;
P1 requires its arithmetic evaluation and defect detection.
P3 requires the specified arithmetic placement and pairing compatibility. The Riemann Hypothesis reformulation of
Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:rh-reformulation} proceeds from this basis.
