\chapter{Arithmetic Fibres and Pairing Comparisons}
\label{v4-arith:w6-i:chapter}

The constructions of Chapters~49--59 use Iwasawa coefficient rings,
local representation categories, adelic automorphic spaces and
complex analytic test spaces. Their relation with the constructions
of the preceding volumes requires specialization or realization maps.  Some proposed comparisons concern arithmetic fibres of
complex or Calabi--Yau assertions; others concern distinct objects.
A proposed fibre interpretation is a question until its functor is constructed.  A pairing comparison requires specified carriers and maps,
including the coefficient change and completion. The Weil pairing
comparison below states these data explicitly.

\section{The Fibre Dichotomy}
\label{v4-arith:w6-i:purpose}

There are two different questions: whether an arithmetic construction
is obtained from a common integral object by specialization, and whether
two constructions on different carriers admit a realization comparison.
Neither follows by replacing the names of their coefficient rings.
Statements on unrelated categories may have useful analogies without
a comparison functor. The following cases specify constructions and
obstructions needed to decide these questions.

\subsection{Comparison data}
\label{v4-arith:w6-i:comparison-criterion}
A fibre comparison specifies its base ring, a morphism of bases,
and a base-change or realization functor on the stated objects.
For paired vector spaces it must additionally give a map and an
identity of the transported forms. Agreement of scalar kernels
alone does not provide those data. The marked comparison of
Section~\ref{v4-marked:sec:data} retains the algebra, its module and the
specified functional; its functorial form also requires compatible
maps on changes of input.

\section{Three Basic Comparisons}

\subsection{Arithmetic Positselski and Vol~I Theorem~B}
\label{v4-arith:w6-i:W5-D-alignment}

The Vol~I theorem is the conditional continuous fixed-coalgebra
co--contra comparison: a strict Mittag--Leffler completed chiral
CDG-coalgebra must satisfy its CP1--CP3 package of finite-stage
comparisons, continuous structure maps, derived inverse-limit
identifications and preservation of second-kind acyclic objects.
The arithmetic comparison concerns the equivalence
\[
\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{co}}_{\Lambda_{p}}(C\text{-comod}_{\Lambda_{p}})
\simeq
\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{ctr}}_{\Lambda_{p}}(C^{\vee}\text{-contramod}_{\Lambda_{p}})
\]
over \(\Lambda_{p}=\mathbb Z_p[[G_p]]\), with \(\mu(C)=0\) and
bounded-below Tor against \(\mathbb F_p\).

The arithmetic constructions involve Pontryagin--Weil duality,
Greenlees--May completion, Matlis duality and Iwasawa coefficients;
the complex construction uses chiral coalgebras and field-coefficient
coderived and contraderived categories.

\begin{proposition}[coefficient obstruction and integral comparison]
\label{v4-arith:w6-i:thm:W5-D-alignment}
Reduction through the augmentation
$\Lambda_p\otimes_{\mathbb Z_p}\mathbb F_p\to\mathbb F_p$
has characteristic-$p$ target. It does not give a complex fibre.
A comparison with the characteristic-zero chiral equivalence requires
a common ring $R$ with specified maps $R\to\mathbb F_p$ and
$R\to\mathbb C$, categories $\mathcal C_R,\mathcal D_R$ and a functor
$F_R:\mathcal C_R\to\mathcal D_R$, together with base-change
functors to each fibre and natural isomorphisms
$F_k S_k^{\mathcal C}\cong S_k^{\mathcal D}F_R$
for $k=\mathbb F_p,\mathbb C$.
The fibre functors $F_k$ must themselves be identified with the
stated coderived--contraderived equivalences, using their actual
topologies, completions and derived base change.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A unital map $\mathbb F_p\to\mathbb C$ would send
$0=p1$ to the nonzero complex number $p$, a contradiction.
For the proposed common model, the displayed natural isomorphisms
are precisely the commuting base-change squares: at every object
$X$ they identify $F_k(S_k^{\mathcal C}X)$ with
$S_k^{\mathcal D}(F_RX)$. They provide a comparison only after
these categories, functors and isomorphisms have been constructed.
No equivalence after base change is asserted merely from a tensor
product of rings.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}[the two coefficient fibres]
\label{v4-arith:w6-i:rem:W5-D-overextension}
The common integral chiral/Iwasawa model and its compatibility with
Pontryagin duality remain open comparison data. Even the reduction
of a duality requires its chosen dualizing object and a base-change
map; identifying underlying vector spaces does not supply these.
The complex theorem retains its original characteristic-zero scope.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Bernstein Block-Additivity and Chiral Chains}
\label{v4-arith:w6-i:W5-F-alignment}

The block-additivity statement of
\Cref{v4-arith:w5-f:F1a3-AB:thm:unconditional-AB} concerns Hochschild
homology of the stated smooth $GL_2(\mathbb Q_p)$ representation
category. Its references include Bernstein, Bernstein--Deligne,
Bushnell--Henniart and Pa{\v s}k{\=u}nas. A comparison with chiral
chains needs an enhanced category and a common coefficient field;
it does not follow from the names of those references.

\begin{proposition}[a chain-level block comparison]
\label{v4-arith:w6-i:thm:W5-F-orthogonal}
Specify a field $k$, any required derived coefficient change, and
an enhanced $k$-linear realization $\mathcal C$ of the arithmetic
category. Suppose its Hochschild complex decomposes as
$C_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(\mathcal C)=\bigoplus_\nu C_\nu$ according to
its block idempotents. Choose a chiral object $A$ over $k$ with a
chain model $C_{\mathrm{ch}}(A)=\bigoplus_\nu B_\nu$ and chain maps
$\chi_\nu:C_\nu\to B_\nu$. If each $\chi_\nu$ is a
quasi-isomorphism, their direct sum transports block additivity to
this chiral complex. A mixed-complex comparison additionally
requires these decompositions to preserve the Connes operators
and $\chi_\nu B=B\chi_\nu$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Direct sums of complexes of $k$-vector spaces commute with
cohomology. Thus the direct sum of the isomorphisms
$H^*(\chi_\nu)$ is an isomorphism, respecting the block projectors.
Commutation with the differential gives a chain map; the additional
identity is exactly compatibility with the mixed operator.
\end{proof}
The categories, chiral object and maps in this proposition are
additional comparison data. Hochschild additivity by itself does
not construct the bar--cobar unit and counit or prove them to be
equivalences. In particular the conditional factorization restriction
of Vol~I Theorem~A retains its factorization and convergence packages,
and its properadic refinement requires its properadic package.
The conditional Verdier--bar comparison retains its finite regular
holonomic windows, biduality, tensor exchange and compatible
continuous bar/cobar data. The conditional Arnold comparison and
scalar companion values concern their specified connection data
and companion pairs, rather than a general bar--cobar equivalence.

\subsection{Chenevier--Bernstein Transport and \(E_2\)-Recognition}
\label{v4-arith:w6-i:W5-H-alignment}

Chenevier determinant-trace pseudocharacters, Bernstein transport
and the chiral $E_2$-centre concern different categorical data.
Their proposed relation asks for the following centralizer comparison.
\begin{proposition}[transport of a specified centralizer]
\label{v4-arith:w6-i:thm:W5-H-alignment}
Suppose $\mathcal C,\mathcal D$ are specified $(\infty,2)$-categories,
$X\in\mathcal C$, and $J:\mathcal C\to\mathcal D$ is a functor
that induces an equivalence on the endomorphism category of $X$.
Then it transports the endomorphisms of the identity $1_X$ to those
of $1_{JX}$, preserving their two compositions and hence the induced
$E_2$ structure. An arithmetic/chiral application additionally
requires these two endomorphism objects to be identified with the
claimed arithmetic transport and chiral centre.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The equivalence on endomorphism categories is fully faithful, so
it induces an equivalence of the mapping objects from $1_X$ to itself.
Functoriality preserves identity, vertical composition and the
horizontal composition inherited from the $(\infty,2)$-category.
These are the two compatible compositions defining the $E_2$ structure.
\end{proof}
The conditional factorization restriction and properadic envelope
of Vol~I Theorem~A is not an $E_2$-centre identification.
The functor $J$, the specified object $X$ and the two identifications
remain part of the arithmetic comparison question. A restricted
Hilbert tensor factor alone does not construct this categorical functor.

\subsection{Bost--Connes Dynamics and a Circle KMS Comparison}
\label{v4-arith:w6-i:W5-B-alignment}

\begin{proposition}[logarithmic dilation and an actual circle quotient]
\label{v4-arith:w6-i:thm:W5-B-alignment}
The map $Uf(t)=f(e^t)$ is unitary from
$L^2(\mathbb R_{>0},dr/r)$ to $L^2(\mathbb R,dt)$.
For $D_af(r)=f(e^{-a}r)$ it satisfies
$UD_aU^{-1}F(t)=F(t-a)$.
Fix $L>0$. The quotient group
$\mathbb R_{>0}/e^{L\mathbb Z}$ is instead identified with
$\mathbb R/L\mathbb Z$, with Haar measure $dt$ on $[0,L)$.
Its Hilbert space has orthonormal basis
$L^{-1/2}e^{2\pi int/L}$, $n\in\mathbb Z$; inverse translation
acts on this basis by $e^{-2\pi ina/L}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Substitution $r=e^t$ proves the norm identity and the intertwining.
The logarithm carries the subgroup $e^{L\mathbb Z}$ to $L\mathbb Z$,
so induces the quotient isomorphism. Orthogonality and completeness
are the Fourier-series theorem on $[0,L)$, with the stated norm.
No nonzero periodic function belongs to $L^2(\mathbb R,dt)$:
its squared integral over the line is the sum of identical integrals
over disjoint periods. Thus the circle space is a different carrier,
not a nonzero periodic subspace of the full logarithmic $L^2$ space.
\end{proof}
For the Bost--Connes dynamics $\sigma_a(\mu_r)=r^{ia}\mu_r$,
a circle action of period $L$ would require $L\log r\in2\pi\mathbb Z$
for every surviving nonzero generator. No such $L>0$ works for
both $r=2$ and $r=3$: otherwise $\log2/\log3$ is rational, which
would give $2^m=3^n$ for positive integers. A comparison with a
circle KMS theory must therefore specify a suitable quotient or
representation, its state, its periodic action and an intertwiner
with the chosen circle model. The logarithm alone supplies none of these.
\begin{remark}[finite-place and circle data]
\label{v4-arith:w6-i:rem:W5-B-finite-extension}
The proposed relation of the global Bost--Connes system and its
finite-place subalgebras to Costello--Gwilliam theory remains a
separate question. The closed-color statement in Volume~II
concerns an operadic comparison; it does not specify a KMS state
or periodic dynamics. The explicit
circle quotient above describes its analytic model, not an automatic
identification of the two quantum theories.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Koszul--Petersson Pairings and Arnold Comparison}
\label{v4-arith:w6-i:W5-C-alignment}

\begin{proposition}[a specified Petersson and chiral pairing comparison]
\label{v4-arith:w6-i:thm:W5-C-alignment}
Let $E$ be a complex vector space with bilinear form $B$ and
antilinear involution $\sigma$. Let $H_{\mathrm{cusp}}$ be a fixed
cuspidal automorphic $L^2$ space with specified central character
and quotient Tamagawa measure. Suppose complex-linear maps
$I:E\to H_{\mathrm{cusp}}$ and $J:E\to H_{\mathrm{ch}}$ have dense
images, where $H_{\mathrm{ch}}$ is a specified Hilbert realization
of a chiral bar pairing, and
\[
 B(v,\sigma w)=\langle Iv,Iw\rangle_{\mathrm{Pet}}
             =\langle Jv,Jw\rangle_{\mathrm{ch}}.
\]
Then $Iv\mapsto Jv$ extends uniquely to a unitary
$H_{\mathrm{cusp}}\to H_{\mathrm{ch}}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Both maps have kernel $\{v:B(v,\sigma v)=0\}$ by their norm
identities. The induced map is well-defined and isometric on the
dense image of $I$. Its extension has closed range containing the
dense image of $J$, hence is unitary.
\end{proof}
Constructing $E,B,\sigma,I,J$ for the native discrete Koszul sector
is the comparison problem. An Arnold or $\mathrm{KZ}^*$ connection
requires, in addition, bundles over an identified base and a
connection-preserving bundle map: the Hilbert identity alone does
not compare connections. The oscillator contraction is a local
model; it does not identify a Petersson integral with that contraction
without the actual automorphic realization and normalization.

\subsection{HP--Li Paley--Wiener and Theorem~H}
\label{v4-arith:w6-i:W5-A-alignment}


\begin{theorem}[realization of the restricted arithmetic pairing]
\label{v4-arith:w6-i:thm:W5-A-alignment}
Use $\mathscr A=C_c^\infty(\mathbb R;\mathbb C)$,
$f^*(t)=\overline{f(-t)}$ and the full signed Weil functional
$W=P-W_\infty-W_{\mathrm{fin}}$ in the angular Fourier convention
of \Cref{v4-arith:w5-a:thm:weil-explicit}.
Let $D\subset\mathscr A$ be a complex vector subspace and
$q_D(f,g)=W(f*g^*)$. A positive Hilbert realization of $q_D$
exists if and only if $q_D$ is positive semidefinite.
Its minimal realization is the completion of
$D/\{f:q_D(f,f)=0\}$.
For $D=\mathscr A$, existence is equivalent to RH.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The construction and equivalence are
\Cref{v4-arith:w5-a:thm:hpli-form}, restricted to $D$.
The nullspace is orthogonal to $D$ by Cauchy--Schwarz.
The quotient inner product is therefore well-defined.
\end{proof}

The conditional chiral Hochschild support and comparison statements
of Vol~I Theorem~H retain their completed Koszul-chart or
quotient-specific hypothesis packages and bounded-to-chart maps.
They do not themselves supply a Hilbert pairing.
An arithmetic fibre comparison requires a specified
complex fibre $E$, its bilinear pairing $B$, an antilinear involution
$\sigma$, and a complex-linear map $T:D\to E$ with
\[
 B(Tf,\sigma Tg)=W(f*g^*).
\]
Here $H_{\mathrm{aut}}=L^2_{\mathrm{cusp}}([GL_2],\chi;dh)$ for a fixed unitary central character $\chi$ and quotient Tamagawa measure $dh$.
A Petersson realization further requires a complex-linear map $I:E\to H_{\mathrm{aut}}$
and $B(v,\sigma w)=\langle Iv,Iw\rangle$ on the indicated image.
These are equalities of forms on specified carriers. If they hold,
$IT$ realizes $q_D$ and extends isometrically from its Hilbert
quotient to the closure of $IT(D)$.
Indeed its nullspace is exactly the nullspace of $q_D$, and the
pairing identity makes the induced map norm preserving.
This constructs the Hilbert comparison from the stated native data.
The scalar ratio $K_F^{\mathrm{prop}}$ of
\Cref{v4-arith:pres57:kernel-question} supplies neither $T$ nor $I$.
The actual cusp-form coefficient identity
\Cref{v4-arith:w5-a:eq:KF-kernel} is a different formula.
\subsection{Hermite--Biehler Positivity and the Phi Construction}
\label{v4-arith:w6-i:W5-J-alignment}

For $F(z)=\xi_{\mathbb R}(1/2+iz)$, the shift-family criterion of
\Cref{v4-arith:w5-direct:thm:dB-form} uses $E_h(z)=F(z+ih)$ for
all $h>0$, or a sequence tending to zero. One fixed shift is
insufficient by \Cref{v4-arith:consumer53:fixed-shift}.
The derivative criterion of \Cref{v4-arith:consumer53:derivative-HB}
allows real zeros of $F+iF'$; requiring no real zeros adds simplicity.
These are precise analytic criteria, not a construction of a
Calabi--Yau input or a chiral specialization.

\begin{proposition}[positive index and an auxiliary indefinite space]
\label{v4-arith:w6-i:thm:W5-J-orthogonal}
A positive Hermitian space of dimension greater than $p$ admits no
form-preserving injection into a finite-dimensional Hermitian space
of signature $(p,q)$. In particular an HB space need not embed
positively in a fixed plane of signature $(1,1)$.
For any positive Hermitian space $(H,h)$ there is instead the
auxiliary Hermitian form
\[
 Q((x,y),(x',y'))=h(x,x')-h(y,y')
\]
on $H\oplus H$, with positive isometric inclusion $x\mapsto(x,0)$.
In finite dimension $n$ its signature is $(n,n)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Projection to the positive summand of a space of signature $(p,q)$
is injective on every positive subspace: a nonzero vector in its
kernel would have nonpositive norm. Hence that subspace has
dimension at most $p$.
For an explicit HB example, take $E(z)=(z+i)^2$ and
$E^\#(z)=(z-i)^2$. For $z=x+iy$, $y>0$,
$|z+i|^2-|z-i|^2=4y>0$, and $E$ has no real zeros.
The polynomials $f=a+bz$ form a positive two-dimensional space with
\[
 \langle a+bz,c+dz\rangle_E
 =\int_{\mathbb R}\frac{(a+bx)\overline{(c+dx)}}{(1+x^2)^2}\,dx
 =\frac\pi2(a\bar c+b\bar d).
\]
The cross term is odd. Substitution $x=\tan\theta$ gives both
diagonal integrals $\pi/2$. These polynomials belong to the
associated de Branges space: both $f/E$ and $f^\#/E$ are rational
Hardy functions in the upper half-plane, with lower-half-plane
poles and decay $O(1/z)$. Their reproducing kernel is
\[
 K(w,z)=\frac2\pi(1+z\bar w)
 =\frac{E(z)\overline{E(w)}-E^\#(z)\overline{E^\#(w)}}
 {2\pi i(\bar w-z)}.
\]
The first expression follows from the orthogonal basis $1,z$;
expanding the numerator gives the second. Thus positive index one
already fails in this example, also over the real coefficient
subspace. Finally the formula for $Q$ directly proves the auxiliary
inclusion and, in finite dimension, its signature.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the required Mukai and Phi data]
\label{v4-arith:w6-i:rem:W5-J-signature}
For a one-dimensional positive space a chosen positive line of a
hyperbolic plane suffices. In general the auxiliary $H\oplus H$
is not a specified Mukai carrier. A comparison requires the actual
graded object, coefficient field, degree and sign of the Mukai
pairing, a real structure or conjugation, and a form-preserving map
from the chosen HB space to its Hermitian realization. Compatibility
with cyclic or factorization operations is additional data.

The two-stage Calabi--Yau-to-chiral construction in Vol~III also
requires a chosen CY input, formality data and a specialization
chart $(\sigma,\Sigma_{d-1},C,b,w)$. On its constructed loci its
output is $E_1$ for $d\geq3$; the $E_2$ enhancement at $d=2$ and
$E_\infty$ enhancement at $d=1$ are conditional. At $d=3$ the
framing, anomaly-cancellation, completion, TCFT-correction and
filtered Hochschild comparisons, together with the specified
pushforward and support comparisons, remain hypotheses.
Neither the HB inequality nor the auxiliary doubling supplies
these structures or their pairing comparison.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Zero Counting and Chevalley--Cousin Concentration}
\label{v4-arith:w6-i:W5-I-alignment}

\begin{proposition}[cohomological degree does not determine spectral counting]
\label{v4-arith:w6-i:thm:W5-I-orthogonal}
For an ordinary unital chiral algebra $A$ on a smooth curve $X$
over a characteristic-zero field, and a finite
nonempty set $I$, the Chevalley--Cousin complex of
Beilinson--Drinfeld, \S\S3.4.10--3.4.12, satisfies
\[
 H^n C(A)_{X^I}=0\quad(n\ne-|I|).
\]
This is concentration of cohomology sheaves on the finite Cartesian
power, with $C(A)_X=A[1]$ for a singleton. It is not a statement
of global Ran-space or genus-zero acyclicity.
Cohomological concentration alone does not determine eigenvalues
or a spectral counting function. A comparison with the
Riemann--von Mangoldt formula requires a Hilbert realization, a
specified operator with domain, finite-rank or trace-class spectral
projections, and a proved trace identity with the zero-counting
function $N(T)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The concentration assertion is Beilinson--Drinfeld,
\emph{Chiral Algebras}, Lemma~3.4.12, p.~183, with the complex
constructed in \S3.4.11. For the spectral assertion, put
$V=\mathbb C^n$ in degree zero with zero differential. For any
real numbers $a_1,\ldots,a_n$, the self-adjoint diagonal operator
$\operatorname{diag}(a_1,\ldots,a_n)$ has this same concentrated
complex as its carrier. Its finite spectrum is arbitrary. A
shift places the same example in any prescribed cohomological
degree. Thus concentration gives no restriction on the eigenvalues;
the spectral realization and trace identity are separate data.
\end{proof}

\section{Fibre Theorem}
\label{v4-arith:w6-i:comparison}
\label{v4-arith:w6-i:extent-update}


\begin{theorem}[arithmetic fibre compatibility and the Weil pairing]
\label{v4-arith:w6-i:summary}
Under the comparison hypotheses stated in
\Cref{v4-arith:w6-i:thm:W5-D-alignment}, \Cref{v4-arith:w6-i:thm:W5-H-alignment}, \Cref{v4-arith:w6-i:thm:W5-B-alignment}, \Cref{v4-arith:w6-i:thm:W5-C-alignment},
the integral base-change squares, centralizer transport, circle
quotient and paired Hilbert realization have the consequences proved
there. Their identifications with the named native arithmetic and
chiral theories remain the stated comparison questions. The Weil pairing has a positive Hilbert
realization exactly under the positivity criterion in
\Cref{v4-arith:w6-i:thm:W5-A-alignment}.
Its native arithmetic fibre comparison additionally requires the
maps and equalities specified there.
The Bernstein block-additivity, de Branges, and zero-density
comparisons concern the distinct domains stated above. Adelic descent
and wild-prime compact generation have their native arithmetic domains.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For the first four assertions use the explicit base-change squares,
endomorphism equivalence, logarithmic quotient and isometry construction.
None of these constructions asserts the missing native identifications.
For the Weil pairing use its explicit Hilbert quotient construction.
The block-chain comparison, positive-index obstruction with
auxiliary doubling, and spectral-concentration counterexample are
proved in
\Cref{v4-arith:w6-i:thm:W5-F-orthogonal}, \Cref{v4-arith:w6-i:thm:W5-J-orthogonal}, \Cref{v4-arith:w6-i:thm:W5-I-orthogonal}.
\end{proof}
