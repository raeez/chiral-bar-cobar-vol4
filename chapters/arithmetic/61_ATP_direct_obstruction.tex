\chapter{The Archimedean Tate Positivity Conjecture: A Direct Elementary Obstruction}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:ATP-direct-obstruction}

The digamma kernel
\(g(t)=\operatorname{Re}\psi(1/4+it/2)+\log\pi\) has one positive
zero. Splitting its quadratic integral at that zero gives two
non-negative energies \(A[f]\) and \(B[f]\), and the exact identity
\(G(f)=(B[f]-A[f])/(2\pi)\). An explicit high-frequency dominance
condition proves \(G(f)\geq0\) on a non-empty class of tests.

This auxiliary form differs from the signed archimedean term in the
Weil formula. With the Fourier convention of
Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:w5-a:chapter}, that term is
\(W_\infty(f*\widetilde f)=2\log\pi\|f\|_2^2-G(f)\).
Consequently full Weil positivity requires
\(A[f]/(2\pi)+2\log\pi\|f\|_2^2\leq B[f]/(2\pi)+R[f]\),
where \(R[f]\) retains the polar and finite-prime terms.
The kernel calculation proves neither this full inequality nor its
arithmetic comparison with a Koszul pairing.

\section{Kernel and Obstruction}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:context}

Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:w5-a:chapter} established the following. The
fixed-\(F\) Mellin--Rankin--Selberg comparison does not close the zeta
finite-prime term
(Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w5-a:thm:prime-side}). The polar terms
\(\widehat{f*\widetilde{f}}(\pm 1/(2i))\) are explicit finite Taylor
corrections (Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:w5-a:rem:H3-removable}). The irreducible
residual is A-TP
(Conjecture~\ref{v4-arith:w5-a:conj:archimedean-tate-positivity}), which
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w5-a:thm:ATP-equals-weil-positivity} establishes is
strictly equivalent to the Weil positivity form of RH.

\begin{definition}[the archimedean digamma kernel]
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:def:g}
Set
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:g-definition}
g(t)\;:=\;\mathrm{Re}\,\psi\!\left(\tfrac{1}{4}+\tfrac{it}{2}\right)+\log\pi,
\qquad t\in\mathbb{R},
\end{equation}
where \(\psi(z)=\Gamma'(z)/\Gamma(z)\) is the digamma function. The auxiliary quadratic form, with
\(\widehat f(t)=\int_{\mathbb R}f(u)e^{itu}\,du\), is
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:W-arch-as-g}
G(f)
\;=\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\widehat{f}(t)|^{2}\,g(t)\,dt.
\end{equation}
The signed Weil contribution in
\eqref{v4-arith:w5-a:eq:weil-archimedean} instead has density
\(k(t)=\log\pi-\operatorname{Re}\psi(1/4+it/2)\). Since
\(k+g=2\log\pi\), Plancherel gives
\[
W_\infty(f*\widetilde f)=2\log\pi\|f\|_2^2-G(f).
\]
\end{definition}

We analyse the sign of \eqref{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:W-arch-as-g} and
separate the positivity forced by the kernel \(g\) from the positivity
equivalent to RH.

\subsection{Domain}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:domain}

Work on \(\mathscr A=C_c^\infty(\mathbb R,\mathbb C)\), with the
Fourier transform fixed above and convolution involution
\(\widetilde f(u)=\overline{f(-u)}\). Its Fourier transforms are
entire and rapidly decreasing on the real axis, so all kernel
integrals below converge. If an arithmetic application imposes an
additional test class \(\mathrm{PW}^{\mathrm{hol}}_F\), use its
intersection with the classes constructed here. Non-emptiness of
that further intersection requires a separate check.

The elementary objects are:
\begin{itemize}
\item elementary analysis of \(g(t)\) via the Hurwitz series for \(\psi\)
and the Gauss summation at rational arguments;
\item decomposition of the auxiliary form \(G(f)\) into a
low-frequency core integral and a high-frequency tail integral;
\item unconditional non-negativity of \(G\) on an explicit
dominance sub-class;
\item reduction of full A-TP to a bounded-interval archimedean
inequality coupled to \(R[f]\).
\end{itemize}

No elementary argument below supplies the RH-equivalent full A-TP
inequality. The irreducible location is
\S\ref{v4-arith:w6-a:bounded-interval}.

\subsection{Analytic Ingredients}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:sources}

The elementary obstruction uses four classical sources.

\begin{description}
\item[Hurwitz 1882.] Hurwitz, A. \emph{Einige Eigenschaften der
Dirichlet'schen Functionen.} \emph{Zeitschrift f\"ur Mathematik und
Physik} 27 (1882). Hurwitz series representation
\(\psi(a)=-\gamma_{E}+\sum_{n\geq 0}[1/(n+1)-1/(n+a)]\); monotonicity of
individual terms.
\item[Gauss 1813.] Gauss, C.F. \emph{Disquisitiones generales circa
seriem infinitam \(1+\cdots\)}. \emph{Comment.~Soc.~Reg.~Sci.~Gottingensis
Recentiores} 2 (1813). Reflection relation
\(\psi(1-x)-\psi(x)=\pi\cot(\pi x)\) at rational \(x\); explicit values
\(\psi(1/4)=-\gamma_{E}-\pi/2-3\log 2\),
\(\psi(3/4)=-\gamma_{E}+\pi/2-3\log 2\).
\item[Whittaker--Watson 1927.] Whittaker, E.T.; Watson, G.N. \emph{A
Course of Modern Analysis} (4th ed., Cambridge 1927), Ch.~XII.
Stirling asymptotic for \(\psi\):
\(\psi(z)=\log z-1/(2z)-\sum_{k=1}^{N}B_{2k}/(2k z^{2k})+O(|z|^{-2N-2})\).
\item[Boas 1954.] Boas, R.P. \emph{Entire Functions}.
Academic Press 1954, Ch.~6. Paley--Wiener characterization of entire
functions of exponential type; the Plancherel--P\'olya theorem giving
\(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{f}(t)|^{2}\,dt=2\pi\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(u)|^{2}\,du\)
for \(f\in\mathrm{PW}\).
\end{description}

The kernel estimates use the Hurwitz series, the rational digamma
value, Stirling asymptotics and Plancherel. Their relation to the
full signed Weil form is the explicit identity stated above.

\section{The Digamma Kernel \(g(t)\)}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:digamma-kernel}

The sign structure of \(g\) controls whether
\(G(f)\) can be positive, negative, or zero.
All statements below are unconditional.

\subsection{Hurwitz Series Representation}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:digamma-kernel:hurwitz}

\begin{lemma}[Hurwitz series for \(g\)]
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:lem:g-hurwitz}
For every \(t\in\mathbb{R}\),
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:g-hurwitz}
g(t)\;=\;-\gamma_{E}+\log\pi+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\!\left[\frac{1}{n+1}-\frac{n+1/4}{(n+1/4)^{2}+t^{2}/4}\right].
\end{equation}
The series converges absolutely and uniformly on compact sets.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Hurwitz's series
\(\psi(a)=-\gamma_{E}+\sum_{n\geq 0}[1/(n+1)-1/(n+a)]\) (Hurwitz 1882;
Whittaker--Watson 1927 \S12.31) applied at \(a=1/4+it/2\):
\[
\frac{1}{n+1/4+it/2}=\frac{(n+1/4)-it/2}{(n+1/4)^{2}+t^{2}/4}.
\]
Real part: \((n+1/4)/[(n+1/4)^{2}+t^{2}/4]\). Adding \(\log\pi\) gives
\eqref{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:g-hurwitz}. Absolute convergence follows from
the large-\(n\) asymptotic
\(1/(n+1)-1/(n+1/4)=-(3/4)/[(n+1)(n+1/4)]=O(n^{-2})\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[strict monotonicity of \(g\) in \(|t|\)]
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:lem:g-monotone}
The map \(t\mapsto g(t)\) is even and
continuously differentiable on \(\mathbb{R}\), with
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:g-derivative}
g'(t)\;=\;\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(n+1/4)\,t/2}{\bigl[(n+1/4)^{2}+t^{2}/4\bigr]^{2}}.
\end{equation}
In particular \(g'(t)>0\) for \(t>0\), so \(g\) is strictly increasing in
\(|t|\) on \([0,\infty)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Evenness follows from \eqref{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:g-hurwitz}: each term
depends on \(t\) only through \(t^{2}\). For each \(n\geq 0\), with
\(c:=n+1/4\),
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\!\left[-\frac{c}{c^{2}+t^{2}/4}\right]
\;=\;-c\cdot\frac{-(t/2)}{[c^{2}+t^{2}/4]^{2}}
\;=\;\frac{c\cdot t/2}{[c^{2}+t^{2}/4]^{2}}.
\]
Summing over \(n\) and using uniform convergence on compact sets of
the differentiated series
(dominant bound \(\leq(n+1/4)\cdot|t|/2\cdot(n+1/4)^{-4}=O(n^{-3})\)
uniformly in \(|t|\leq T\)) gives
\eqref{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:g-derivative}. Each summand is non-negative
for \(t\geq 0\) and strictly positive for \(t>0\); hence \(g'(t)>0\) for
\(t>0\), giving strict monotonicity in \(|t|\) on \([0,\infty)\).
Continuity of \(g'\) follows from uniform convergence on compacts.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[boundary values of \(g\)]
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:prop:g-boundary}
The kernel \(g\) satisfies
\begin{align}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:g-at-zero}
g(0)&\;=\;-\gamma_{E}-\frac{\pi}{2}-3\log 2+\log\pi\;\approx\;-3.0827,\\
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:g-at-infinity}
g(t)&\;\sim\;\tfrac{1}{2}\log(t^{2}/4)+\log\pi\;=\;\log(|t|\pi/2),\qquad|t|\to\infty.
\end{align}
In particular \(g(0)<0<\lim_{|t|\to\infty}g(t)/\log|t|=1\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\eqref{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:g-at-zero} follows from the Gauss value
\(\psi(1/4)=-\gamma_{E}-\pi/2-3\log 2\) (Gauss 1813; Whittaker--Watson
\S12.31 ex.~1). Numerical evaluation:
\(-0.5772-1.5708-2.0794+1.1447=-3.0827\).

\eqref{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:g-at-infinity} follows from the Stirling
asymptotic \(\psi(z)=\log z-1/(2z)+O(|z|^{-2})\) applied at
\(z=1/4+it/2\): \(\log z=\tfrac{1}{2}\log(1/16+t^{2}/4)+i\arctan(2t)\),
\(\mathrm{Re}\,\log z\sim\tfrac{1}{2}\log(t^{2}/4)\) as \(|t|\to\infty\),
and \(-1/(2z)\to 0\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Unique Sign Change \(t_{\ast}\)}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:digamma-kernel:sign-change}

\begin{theorem}[existence and uniqueness of the sign change]
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:thm:t-star}
There is a unique \(t_{\ast}\in(0,\infty)\)
with \(g(t_{\ast})=0\). The map \(t\mapsto g(t)\) is strictly negative on
\((-t_{\ast},t_{\ast})\) and strictly positive on
\((-\infty,-t_{\ast})\cup(t_{\ast},\infty)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{v4-arith:w6-a:lem:g-monotone}, \(g\) is continuous, even, and
strictly increasing in \(|t|\) on \([0,\infty)\).
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:w6-a:prop:g-boundary} gives \(g(0)=-3.0827<0\) and
\(g(t)\to+\infty\). The intermediate value theorem and strict
monotonicity supply a unique \(t_{\ast}>0\) with \(g(t_{\ast})=0\); by
evenness \(g(-t_{\ast})=0\) as well.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[certified interval for the sign change]
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:prop:t-star-numerical}
The zero satisfies \(0.84<t_*<0.86\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Subtract the Hurwitz series at zero. With \(a=t^2/4\) and
\(c_n=n+1/4\), the positive-tail representation is
\[
 g(t)=g(0)+\sum_{n\geq0}\frac{a}{c_n(c_n^2+a)}.
\]
For \(S_N(t)=g(0)+\sum_{n=0}^Na/[c_n(c_n^2+a)]\), monotonicity
of \(x^{-3}\) gives
\[
 0\leq g(t)-S_N(t)
 \leq a\sum_{n>N}(n+1/4)^{-3}
 \leq \frac{a}{2(N+1/4)^2}.
\]
At \(N=20\), rational arithmetic with
\(0.577<\gamma_E<0.578\), \(3.1415<\pi<3.1416\),
\(0.6931<\log2<0.6932\) and \(1.1447<\log\pi<1.1448\)
gives
\(-0.024<S_{20}(0.84)<-0.020\) and
\(0.017<S_{20}(0.86)<0.020\). The positive tails are less than
\(0.00023\) at both endpoints. Thus the signs are opposite,
and strict monotonicity places the zero between them.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[numerical refinement]
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:rem:t-star-refinement}
Bisection with the same positive-tail bound can refine the
certified interval. The value \(t_*\approx0.85066\) is a numerical
approximation; all exact inequalities below use the zero defined
by \(g(t_*)=0\), or certified upper and lower bounds for it.
\end{remark}

\section{Decomposition of the Auxiliary Form}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:decomposition}

\begin{definition}[low-frequency core and high-frequency tail]
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:def:core-tail}
For \(f\in\mathscr A\), define
the non-negative quantities
\begin{align}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:A-core}
A[f]&\;:=\;\int_{|t|<t_{\ast}}|\widehat{f}(t)|^{2}\cdot(-g(t))\,dt,\\
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:B-tail}
B[f]&\;:=\;\int_{|t|>t_{\ast}}|\widehat{f}(t)|^{2}\cdot g(t)\,dt.
\end{align}
By Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w6-a:thm:t-star}, both \(A[f]\geq 0\) and \(B[f]\geq 0\)
with strict inequality whenever \(\widehat{f}\) does not vanish
identically on the respective domain.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[archimedean decomposition]
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:lem:decomposition}
For every \(f\in\mathscr A\),
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:decomposition}
G(f)\;=\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\bigl(B[f]-A[f]\bigr).
\end{equation}
In particular \(G(f)\geq 0\) if and only if
\(B[f]\geq A[f]\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Definition~\ref{v4-arith:w6-a:def:g} gives
\(G(f)=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{f}(t)|^{2}g(t)\,dt\).
Split the integral at \(|t|=t_{\ast}\):
\[
\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{f}(t)|^{2}g(t)\,dt
=\int_{|t|<t_{\ast}}|\widehat{f}(t)|^{2}g(t)\,dt+\int_{|t|>t_{\ast}}|\widehat{f}(t)|^{2}g(t)\,dt.
\]
By Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w6-a:thm:t-star}, \(g(t)<0\) on \(|t|<t_{\ast}\) and
\(g(t)>0\) on \(|t|>t_{\ast}\). The first integral equals \(-A[f]\) and the
second equals \(B[f]\), giving
\eqref{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:decomposition}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the exact split]
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:rem:why-not-more}
The identity \(G(f)=(B[f]-A[f])/(2\pi)\) implies
\(G(f)\geq0\) exactly when \(B[f]\geq A[f]\). It imposes no
restriction on finer subdivisions or stronger estimates. Full
Weil positivity requires the additional signed inequality of
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w6-a:thm:bounded-reduction}.
\end{remark}

\section{Auxiliary Positivity on the High-Frequency Dominance Class}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:HFD-subclass}

The condition \(B[f]\geq A[f]\) holds unconditionally whenever the
spectral mass of \(\widehat{f}\) above \(|t|>t_{\ast}\) dominates the
core supremum by an explicit factor. This dominance sub-class is
non-empty and admits a closed-form sufficient condition.

\subsection{The Sub-Class \(\mathscr A_{\mathrm{HFD}}\)}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:HFD-subclass:def}

\begin{definition}[high-frequency dominance class]
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:def:HFD-class}
Define the \emph{high-frequency dominance
sub-class} \(\mathscr A_{\mathrm{HFD}}\subset\mathscr A\)
as the subset of \(f\in\mathscr A\) satisfying
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:HFD-condition}
\sup_{|t|\leq t_{\ast}}|\widehat{f}(t)|^{2}\cdot 2t_{\ast}\cdot\|{-}g\|_{L^{\infty}[-t_{\ast},t_{\ast}]}
\;\leq\;
\int_{|t|>t_{\ast}}|\widehat{f}(t)|^{2}\,g(t)\,dt.
\end{equation}
The left-hand side is a crude upper bound for \(A[f]\); the right-hand
side is \(B[f]\).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[the Plancherel--P\'olya bound inside the core]
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:lem:PP-bound}
For \(f\in\mathscr A\) supported
in \([-T,T]\),
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:PP-inside-core}
\sup_{|t|\leq t_{\ast}}|\widehat{f}(t)|^{2}\;\leq\;2T\int_{-T}^{T}|f(u)|^{2}\,du.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\(|\widehat{f}(t)|=|\int_{-T}^{T}f(u)e^{itu}du|\leq(2T)^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{2}}\)
by Cauchy--Schwarz; squaring gives
\eqref{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:PP-inside-core}. This is the
Plancherel--P\'olya dimension bound (Boas 1954 \S6.8) applied inside
the core.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[unconditional \(G\)-positivity on \(\mathscr A_{\mathrm{HFD}}\)]
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:thm:HFD-ATP}
For every \(f\in\mathscr A_{\mathrm{HFD}}\),
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:HFD-ATP-conclusion}
G(f)\;\geq\;0.
\end{equation}
Full A-TP follows on the sublocus where, in addition,
\(R[f]+G(f)\geq2\log\pi\|f\|_2^2\) for \(R[f]\) as in
Equation~\ref{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:R-definition}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Condition \eqref{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:HFD-condition} combined with the
obvious bound
\(A[f]\leq\sup_{|t|\leq t_{\ast}}|\widehat{f}(t)|^{2}\cdot 2t_{\ast}\cdot\|{-}g\|_{L^{\infty}[-t_{\ast},t_{\ast}]}\)
and the identity \(B[f]=\int_{|t|>t_{\ast}}|\widehat{f}(t)|^{2}g(t)\,dt\)
gives \(A[f]\leq B[f]\). Lemma~\ref{v4-arith:w6-a:lem:decomposition} converts
to \(G(f)\geq 0\). The last sentence is
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w6-a:thm:bounded-reduction} on the sublocus
\(R[f]+G(f)\geq2\log\pi\|f\|_2^2\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[smooth high-frequency tests]
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:rem:HFD-nonempty}
Choose a nonzero real even \(\chi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)\)
with \(\int\chi\ne0\), and set \(f_M(u)=\chi(u)\cos(Mu)\).
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:HFD-concentration}
 \widehat f_M(t)=\tfrac12\bigl(\widehat\chi(t+M)+\widehat\chi(t-M)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Schwartz decay gives
\(\sup_{|t|\leq t_*}|\widehat f_M(t)|=O(M^{-K})\) for every
\(K>0\). Since \(\widehat\chi(0)\ne0\), choose \(\delta>0\)
on which its modulus is bounded below. On \(|t-M|<\delta\),
the other translate tends uniformly to zero, while
\(g(t)\sim\log M\). Hence \(B[f_M]\geq c\log M\) for some
\(c>0\) and sufficiently large \(M\). This proves the quantitative
HFD condition for nonzero smooth tests with fixed support.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[tests outside the dominance class]
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:rem:HFD-strict}
With the same \(\chi\), put \(f_L(u)=L^{-1/2}\chi(u/L)\),
\(L\geq1\). Then \(\widehat f_L(t)=\sqrt L\widehat\chi(Lt)\), so
\[
 G(f_L)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}|\widehat\chi(s)|^2g(s/L)\,ds
 \longrightarrow g(0)\|\chi\|_2^2<0.
\]
Dominated convergence applies because \(g\) has logarithmic growth
and \(\widehat\chi\) is Schwartz. Thus \(f_L\notin\mathscr A_{\mathrm{HFD}}\)
for large \(L\), proving that the dominance class is proper in
\(\mathscr A\). This construction widens spatial support and
concentrates Fourier mass near zero.
\end{remark}

\subsection{An Explicit Sufficient Condition}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:HFD-subclass:explicit}

The definitional condition \eqref{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:HFD-condition}
depends on \(\|{-}g\|_{L^{\infty}[-t_{\ast},t_{\ast}]}\), which equals
\(|g(0)|=\gamma_{E}+\pi/2+3\log 2-\log\pi\approx 3.0827\) by
Proposition~\ref{v4-arith:w6-a:prop:g-boundary} and strict monotonicity. We
reformulate the sufficient condition without the supremum.

\begin{corollary}[explicit sufficient condition]
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:cor:HFD-explicit}
Suppose \(f\in\mathscr A\)
satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:HFD-explicit}
2t_{\ast}\cdot|g(0)|\cdot\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{\infty}[-t_{\ast},t_{\ast}]}^{2}
\;\leq\;
\int_{|t|>t_{\ast}}|\widehat{f}(t)|^{2}\,g(t)\,dt.
\end{equation}
Then \(G(f)\geq 0\). For illustration only, with
\(t_{\ast}\approx 0.8507\) and \(|g(0)|\approx 3.083\), the prefactor
\(2t_{\ast}|g(0)|\approx 5.245\), the rounded display is
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:HFD-explicit-numerical}
\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{\infty}[-0.8507,0.8507]}^{2}\;\leq\;\frac{1}{5.245}\int_{|t|>0.8507}|\widehat{f}(t)|^{2}\,g(t)\,dt.
\end{equation}
This rounded display is not a certified sufficient condition; use
\eqref{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:HFD-explicit} with the exact constants.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\(A[f]\leq 2t_{\ast}\cdot|g(0)|\cdot\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{\infty}[-t_{\ast},t_{\ast}]}^{2}\)
by \(|g(t)|\leq|g(0)|\) on \(|t|\leq t_{\ast}\). Combine with
Lemma~\ref{v4-arith:w6-a:lem:decomposition}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[interpretation]
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:rem:HFD-interpretation}
The sufficient condition \eqref{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:HFD-explicit}
asserts: the peak core \(L^{\infty}\)-norm of \(\widehat{f}\), scaled by
a fixed constant depending only on \(t_{\ast}\) and \(g(0)\), is
dominated by the high-frequency tail \(L^{2}\)-mass weighted by \(g\).
Every \(f\in\mathscr A\) satisfying the displayed quantitative
dominance condition lies in \(\mathscr A_{\mathrm{HFD}}\), and
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w6-a:thm:HFD-ATP} gives \(G(f)\geq 0\).
\end{remark}

\section{The Full Signed Weil Inequality}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:bounded-interval}

\subsection{Core, Tail and Arithmetic Terms}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:bounded-interval:reduction}

\begin{theorem}[signed core-tail reduction]
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:thm:bounded-reduction}
On the test domain of the Weil formula, put
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:R-definition}
R[f]=\widehat{f*\widetilde f}(1/(2i))
 +\widehat{f*\widetilde f}(-1/(2i))-W_{\mathrm{fin}}(f*\widetilde f).
\end{equation}
Then \(W(f*\widetilde f)\geq0\) is equivalent to
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:bounded-reduction-star}
\frac{A[f]}{2\pi}+2\log\pi\|f\|_2^2
 \leq \frac{B[f]}{2\pi}+R[f].
\tag{\(\star\)}
\end{equation}
Thus A-TP on a specified full test class is the assertion that
\((\star)\) holds for every test in that class. Any equivalence with
RH uses the full test class in
Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w5-a:thm:ATP-equals-weil-positivity}; restriction
to one smaller class is not automatically an equivalent criterion.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
All integrals are defined on the stated test domain: the digamma
densities grow logarithmically and the Fourier transforms decrease
rapidly. The signed Weil formula and Plancherel give
\begin{equation}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:weil-rearranged}
\begin{split}
W(f*\widetilde f)
 &=R[f]-W_\infty(f*\widetilde f)\\
 &=R[f]-2\log\pi\|f\|_2^2+G(f)\\
 &=R[f]-2\log\pi\|f\|_2^2+\frac{B[f]-A[f]}{2\pi}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Rearranging this equality proves both directions of \((\star)\).
No positive zero-square sum is assumed in this calculation.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the zero-side functional]
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:rem:zero-sum-consequence}
The Weil functional is evaluated on the actual zero divisor
\(z_\rho=-i(\rho-1/2)\). Replacing it by
\(\sum_\rho|\widehat f(\gamma_\rho)|^2\) uses the critical-line
specialization \(z_\rho=\gamma_\rho\in\mathbb R\). The unconditional
identity used here is \eqref{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:weil-rearranged}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[the remaining inequality]
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:rem:bounded-residual}
The negative part of the auxiliary kernel is supported on
\([-t_*,t_*]\), but the signed Weil inequality also includes the
full norm term \(2\log\pi\|f\|_2^2\), the polar terms and the
finite-prime functional. Positivity of \(G(f)\) alone does not
control their sum. On the high-frequency dominance class it gives
\(W(f*\widetilde f)\geq R[f]-2\log\pi\|f\|_2^2\); a sufficient
additional condition is \(R[f]\geq2\log\pi\|f\|_2^2\).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Three Elementary Estimates and Their Limits}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:bounded-interval:ruled-out}

\begin{proposition}[projection to compact Fourier support]
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:prop:rule-out-projection}
If a Paley--Wiener Fourier transform vanishes for every real
\(t>t_*\), it is identically zero. Thus exact projection to
core-supported Fourier transforms leaves only the zero test in
this entire-function class.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The Fourier transform is entire. Its zeros on the open real interval
\((t_*,\infty)\) have accumulation points in its domain, so the
identity theorem applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[a majorant gives a sufficient bound]
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:prop:rule-out-majorant}
For any non-negative measurable \(h\geq-g\) on \([-t_*,t_*]\),
put \(A_h[f]=\int_{-t_*}^{t_*}|\widehat f(t)|^2h(t)\,dt\), assuming
this integral is finite. Then \(A[f]\leq A_h[f]\). Consequently
\[
\frac{A_h[f]}{2\pi}+2\log\pi\|f\|_2^2
 \leq\frac{B[f]}{2\pi}+R[f]
\]
is sufficient for \((\star)\). It is not asserted to be necessary.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Integrate the pointwise inequality against \(|\widehat f|^2\geq0\).
Replacing the left-hand side by a larger quantity proves the stated
implication. For the constant majorant \(h=|g(0)|\), this also gives
\(A[f]\leq2t_*|g(0)|\sup_{|t|\leq t_*}|\widehat f(t)|^2\),
the bound used in the high-frequency dominance condition.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[support bound]
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:prop:rule-out-pw-shrinkage}
If \(f\) is supported in \([-T',T']\), then
\(\sup_t|\widehat f(t)|^2\leq2T'\|f\|_2^2\). In particular
\(A[f]\leq4t_*T'|g(0)|\|f\|_2^2\).
These estimates alone give no bound on \(R[f]\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Cauchy--Schwarz gives the Fourier supremum bound, and substitution
in the preceding majorant estimate gives the bound for \(A[f]\).
The calculation has used only \(f\), its support and the kernel
\(g\); it has not estimated the polar or finite-prime terms in \(R[f]\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[scope of the estimates]
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:rem:three-rule-outs}
The projection statement is an obstruction to exact compact Fourier
support. The majorant and support estimates are useful sufficient
bounds. They do not exclude other elementary estimates or prove
that every approach to \((\star)\) must take a prescribed form.
\end{remark}

\section{Auxiliary Positivity and Full Weil Positivity}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:irreducible}

\begin{theorem}[auxiliary positivity and the remaining signed form]
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:thm:irreducible-ATP}
On \(\mathscr A_{\mathrm{HFD}}\), one has
\(G(f)\geq0\). On the full Weil test domain, positivity of
\(W(f*\widetilde f)\) is equivalent to \((\star)\), including its
norm, polar and finite-prime terms. The first assertion does not
establish the second.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The first assertion is Theorem~\ref{v4-arith:w6-a:thm:HFD-ATP}.
The second is the signed identity
\eqref{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:weil-rearranged}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[constructed quantities]
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:rem:honest-form}
The Hurwitz series proves monotonicity of \(g\), and its unique
positive zero defines the non-negative energies \(A[f]\) and
\(B[f]\). Their difference computes \(G(f)\) exactly. The
high-frequency dominance estimate proves positivity of this
auxiliary form on its stated class. The full inequality
\((\star)\) remains a separate question on the full Weil test class.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Further Comparison Problems}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:irreducible:open-obstructions}

\begin{remark}[zero distributions, Gaussian tests and spectral spaces]
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:rem:live-constructions}
Three natural further constructions concern the actual zero divisor,
Gaussian families, and Hermite--Biehler or de Branges spaces. A
zero-distribution argument must estimate the unconditional functional
in \eqref{v4-arith:w6-a:eq:weil-rearranged}, before specializing the
zeros to real ordinates. Gaussian calculations must retain the
finite-prime and polar terms and justify passage from their test
family to the desired full class. A spectral-space approach requires
an actual positive kernel and a map identifying its form with the
signed Weil functional; writing a kernel formula alone proves no
such identification. None of these comparisons is supplied by the
core-tail decomposition.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[relation to the full Weil form]
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:rem:compare-w5}
Chapter~\ref{v4-arith:w5-a:chapter} fixes the signed normalization.
The present kernel calculation adds explicit estimates for \(G\).
To use them for full positivity one must still establish
\(R[f]+G(f)\geq2\log\pi\|f\|_2^2\) on the required full test class.
\end{remark}

\section{The Remaining Construction}
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:summary}

\begin{remark}[the exact positivity problem]
\label{v4-arith:w6-a:rem:beilinson-closing}
The positive result is the explicit auxiliary-form estimate on the
high-frequency dominance class. The original problem is positivity
of the full signed Weil form, with its polar and finite-prime
terms, on the full test class. Its equivalence with RH does not turn
the auxiliary estimate into a proof. A further construction must
control \(R[f]+G(f)-2\log\pi\|f\|_2^2\) on that domain.
\end{remark}
